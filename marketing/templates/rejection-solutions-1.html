Hi {{ first_name }},

Rejection does not mean you are not good enough. It means the other person failed to notice what you offer.

You open your inbox and see a reply from a company you applied to. Your heart skips a beat. Is this it? Then you read: “Unfortunately, we decided not to move forward.” Even though you matched almost everything they asked for.

Suddenly the whole day feels different. We’ve all been there.

But the real problem isn’t the rejection. It’s letting one “no” make you doubt yourself and carrying that into your next application. A rejection lasts a moment. The meaning you attach to it lasts much longer.

Here’s how I see it: it’s the company’s loss, not yours. You did what you had to. If not them, someone else. Most companies filter candidates with automated screening anyway. If they don’t care, neither should you.

Keep upskilling, keep applying, keep taking your shot. Only one hit is enough.
