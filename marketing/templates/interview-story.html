A true placement story

Three candidates.
Three very different
interviews.

After two aptitude rounds and a communication round, three candidates finally made it to the interview at a technology and consulting firm.

All three had studied hard. They expected a relatively straightforward interview.

They got three completely different experiences.

Candidate 01

The Reunion

The first candidate got an interviewer who was an alumnus of his college.

They talked about cricket. Football. Even breakfast.

The interview felt more like catching up with an old friend than being evaluated for a job.

Candidate 02

The interviewer who helped

The second candidate was nervous and started panicking.

Her interviewer noticed and encouraged her to calm down. She was patient and friendly, helping the candidate feel more comfortable so she could give the interview.

Candidate 03

WTF Do You Want?

By all accounts, he was the most technically skilled of the three.

The interviewer asked him:

"Tell me about a challenge you've faced."

He had a well prepared answer on a technical challenge during his internship.

Interviewer: "I don't want something technical."

Fair.He told about a challenge he'd faced while managing a team.

Interviewer: "Not technical!!"

Calmly and confidently, he started again. About a challenge he'd faced as the sponsorship lead.

Interviewer: "I didn't like this. I wasn't looking for this."

The candidate, internally

"WHAT DO YOU WANT? The story I told my GF at 12 AM ?"

He asked a guesstimate—something never asked at the company. Still, the candidate calmly broke it down like he would do in a consulting interview.

Except he kept getting interrupted.

The interviewer seemed impatient and kept hurrying him along, almost as if he was on notice period.

And just like that, the interview ended.

Three candidates. The same company. The same placement drive.

One had a friendly conversation. Another was helped through her nerves. The third kept adapting his answers, but the interviewer remained unimpressed.

Sometimes, you can do everything right and still get unlucky.

You can't choose your interviewer. You can't control their expectations, their temperament, or whether your communication styles click.

Someone else might get the opportunity to show their best. You might get an experience that makes that almost impossible.

That's not to say preparation doesn't matter. It absolutely does. But hiring isn't always as predictable as we'd like to believe.

Prepare your skills. Practice communicating your experiences. Make sure your resume reflects what you can actually do.

You can control your preparation. You can't control the entire outcome.
