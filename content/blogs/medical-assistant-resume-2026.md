## Key Takeaways

- **Certification is the first filter.** CMA (AAMA), RMA (AMT), CCMA (NHA) and CMAA are different credentials — write the exact one you hold, with the awarding body.
- Medical assisting is two jobs in one. A resume that shows only clinical or only administrative work looks half-qualified.
- Name your EMR by product — Epic, Cerner, athenahealth. "EMR experience" matches nothing.
- Patient volume per day is the number that separates MA resumes. Almost nobody includes it.
- One page is correct here, unlike most healthcare roles.

Medical assistant postings attract high application volume, which means the first pass is fast and mechanical. Certification, EMR system and skill mix decide whether a resume is read by a person at all. This guide covers what to put where.

## Best Medical Assistant Resume Format for 2026

Reverse-chronological, **one page**, single column.

One page is right for this role — unlike a [nurse practitioner resume](/blog/nurse-practitioner-resume-2026), an MA resume does not carry licensure numbers, prescriptive authority or clinical rotation hours. If yours runs long, the usual cause is duty lists rather than achievements.

Single column, no tables, no text boxes. Clinics often run older ATS installations that mangle multi-column layouts — see [ATS formatting mistakes](/blog/ats-resume-formatting-mistakes).

Section order:

1. Header
2. Certification
3. Summary
4. Clinical and administrative skills
5. Experience
6. Education

## Certification Section

Put this directly under the header. It is the first thing screened.

```
CERTIFICATION
Certified Medical Assistant (CMA), AAMA — certified 2022, current through 2027
BLS for Healthcare Providers, American Heart Association — current through 04/2027
Phlebotomy Technician (CPT), NHA — 2021
```

The abbreviations are not interchangeable, and recruiters know the difference:

- **CMA (AAMA)** — American Association of Medical Assistants
- **RMA (AMT)** — American Medical Technologists
- **CCMA (NHA)** — National Healthcareer Association
- **CMAA (NHA)** — administrative only

Write the exact credential plus the awarding body. If a posting asks for "CMA or RMA" and you hold CCMA, apply anyway but do not relabel yourself — credential claims get verified.

If you are certification-eligible but not yet certified, say so plainly: *"CMA (AAMA) exam scheduled March 2026."* That is better than silence and better than implying you hold it.

## Professional Summary

Three lines: certification, setting, patient volume, and one specific strength.

> Certified Medical Assistant (AAMA) with 4 years in high-volume family practice, supporting 3 providers and 45-55 patients daily across clinical and front-office duties. Epic-proficient, with a 98% clean-claim rate on submitted encounters.

Most MA summaries read "compassionate and detail-oriented medical assistant seeking to leverage skills in a fast-paced environment." That describes every applicant. See [why weak bullets fail](/blog/weak-resume-bullets-fix).

## Show Both Halves of the Job

This is the mistake that costs interviews. Medical assisting is clinical *and* administrative, and postings usually want both. A resume weighted entirely to one side reads as half-trained.

Split the skills section explicitly:

**Clinical:** vital signs, phlebotomy and venipuncture, injections (IM, subcutaneous), EKG administration, specimen collection and processing, wound care and dressing changes, autoclave sterilisation, rooming and patient intake, medication administration, point-of-care testing (rapid strep, urinalysis, glucose)

**Administrative:** appointment scheduling, insurance verification and eligibility, prior authorisation, CPT and ICD-10 coding, claims submission, patient registration, referral coordination, medical records management, HIPAA compliance, phone triage

If your experience is genuinely one-sided, say so honestly and show the range you do have. Claiming injections you have never given fails at the first practical question.

## Name Your EMR

"EMR experience" is not a keyword. **Epic**, **Cerner**, **athenahealth**, **eClinicalWorks**, **NextGen**, **Allscripts**, **Meditech** and **Practice Fusion** are keywords, and postings name them specifically.

Write the systems you have used and roughly how long:

> Epic (3 years), athenahealth (1 year), eClinicalWorks (6 months)

This single detail moves match scores more than most people expect, because it is a hard requirement stated in the posting text. The principle is general — see [matching resume keywords to a job description](/blog/how-to-match-resume-keywords-to-job-description).

## Best ATS Keywords for a Medical Assistant Resume

**Clinical:** vital signs, phlebotomy, venipuncture, EKG, injections, specimen collection, sterilisation, wound care, patient intake, rooming, point-of-care testing, medication administration

**Administrative:** scheduling, insurance verification, prior authorisation, CPT coding, ICD-10, claims, patient registration, referrals, medical records, HIPAA

**Systems:** Epic, Cerner, athenahealth, eClinicalWorks, NextGen, Allscripts

**Setting:** family practice, internal medicine, pediatrics, urgent care, outpatient clinic, multi-provider practice

Take these from the posting itself where they apply to you. Do not list all of them.

## Writing Experience Bullets

Action, volume, result.

**Weak:**
> Responsible for taking patient vitals and rooming patients.

**Strong:**
> Roomed and took vitals for 45-55 patients daily across 3 providers, maintaining an average intake time under 6 minutes.

**Weak:**
> Handled insurance verification for the practice.

**Strong:**
> Verified insurance eligibility and secured prior authorisations for ~60 patients weekly, reducing claim denials from 12% to 4% over one year.

Numbers nearly every MA has: patients per day, providers supported, draws per week, average rooming time, denial rate, no-show rate after reminder calls, wait-time reduction. Our [guide to quantifying achievements](/blog/how-to-quantify-achievements-on-resume) covers finding them.

## Medical Assistant Resume Example (Experience Section)

> **Medical Assistant, Cedar Family Practice — Austin, TX · 2022-Present**
> - Support 3 providers in a family practice seeing 45-55 patients daily; handle rooming, vitals, and clinical documentation in Epic.
> - Perform 25-30 venipunctures weekly with a first-stick success rate above 95%; process and route specimens to LabCorp.
> - Administer immunisations and IM injections per protocol; maintain vaccine cold-chain logs with zero excursions in 3 years.
> - Verify insurance eligibility and obtain prior authorisations for ~60 patients weekly; cut claim denials from 12% to 4%.
> - Precept and onboard new medical assistants; wrote the clinic's current rooming checklist.

## New Graduate Medical Assistants

No clinical job yet is normal for this role, and hiring managers expect it.

**Lead with your externship**, with hours and specifics:

> **Clinical Externship, Riverside Internal Medicine — 200 hours, 2026**
> Roomed 20-25 patients daily under supervision; performed venipuncture, EKGs and vitals; documented in eClinicalWorks; assisted with minor procedure setup.

Then list your program's competencies — the skills lab covered injections, phlebotomy, sterilisation and EKG whether or not you have been paid for them. Prior customer service or retail work also counts: high-volume patient contact is a real transferable skill. ## Common Medical Assistant Resume Mistakes

**Writing "certified" without naming the credential.** CMA, RMA and CCMA are different. Name yours and the body.

**Saying "EMR" instead of the product.** The posting names Epic or athenahealth; match it if true.

**Listing only clinical or only admin duties.** Postings usually want both.

**Duty lists with no volume.** "Took vitals" tells a hiring manager nothing about scale.

**Running past one page.** Almost always duty padding.

**Omitting BLS.** It is frequently a hard requirement and takes one line.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long should a medical assistant resume be?**
One page. This role does not carry the licensure detail that justifies two.

**Which certification do employers prefer?**
CMA (AAMA) is most widely recognised, but RMA and CCMA are accepted in most postings. Write the one you hold.

**Do I need certification to apply?**
Not always — some states and employers hire uncertified MAs — but certified applicants are screened first where a posting lists it as preferred.

**Should I list clinical and administrative skills separately?**
Yes. It makes the both-halves point visible in the six seconds a screener spends.

**What if my externship is my only experience?**
Lead with it, include hours, and name the procedures you performed. That is normal for a new graduate.
