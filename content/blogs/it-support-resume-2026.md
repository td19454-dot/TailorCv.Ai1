## Key Takeaways

- IT support hiring managers want proof of volume and speed: tickets per week, first-contact resolution, response time and satisfaction scores.
- Name the tools: the ticketing system (ServiceNow, Jira Service Management, Zendesk), Active Directory, Microsoft 365, and the operating systems you support.
- Certifications (CompTIA A+, Network+, Microsoft) are real filters for help desk roles - list them near the top.
- Tailor to the tier: Tier 1 ads want customer handling and volume; Tier 2 ads want troubleshooting depth and escalation ownership.

An IT support resume gets interviews when it proves two things: you can close a lot of tickets without escalating them, and you keep users happy while you do it. That means numbers - tickets, resolution rate, satisfaction - plus the exact tools and certifications the job ad names.

## What the job ad is really asking for

| Job ad says | What it means for your resume |
|---|---|
| "Provide Tier 1/Tier 2 support" | Say which tier you worked and give a weekly ticket count. |
| "Experience with ticketing systems" | Name the system: ServiceNow, Jira Service Management, Zendesk, Freshservice. |
| "Active Directory and Microsoft 365 administration" | One bullet on account provisioning, password resets, group policy or licence management. |
| "Excellent customer service skills" | Show a satisfaction score (CSAT) or a compliment metric, not the phrase "people person". |

Technical support, help desk, Tier 1, Tier 2, troubleshooting, incident management, ticketing system, ServiceNow, Active Directory, Microsoft 365, Windows, macOS, remote support, hardware support, SLA, first-contact resolution, ITIL, customer service, documentation, knowledge base.

## Tailoring by Tier

- **Tier 1 / service desk ad:** lead with ticket volume, response time, CSAT and communication.
- **Tier 2 / desktop support ad:** lead with troubleshooting depth, imaging, Active Directory and escalations you owned.

## Common IT Support Resume Mistakes

1. **No numbers at all.** Help desk work is measurable - tickets, SLA, CSAT. Use them.
2. **Listing every OS ever released.** List the ones you supported in the last few years.
3. **Hiding certifications at the bottom.** For entry-level roles they are often the first filter.
4. **A fancy two-column template.** Many ATS systems misread it - see [ATS formatting mistakes](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/ats-resume-formatting-mistakes).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a certification to get a help desk job?
Not always, but CompTIA A+ is the most common filter for entry-level help desk roles. If you don't have it, show hands-on experience - home lab, volunteer IT, school IT desk - and list the certification as "in progress" only if you are actually studying for it.

### What metrics should an IT support resume include?
Tickets per week or month, first-contact resolution rate, average response or resolution time, SLA compliance and customer satisfaction (CSAT) scores. Even one or two of these makes your resume stronger than most.

### How do I write an IT support resume with no experience?
Lead with certifications and hands-on projects: building PCs, a home network or Active Directory lab, or helping a small business or school with IT. Describe them with the same metrics-first bullets you would use for a job.
