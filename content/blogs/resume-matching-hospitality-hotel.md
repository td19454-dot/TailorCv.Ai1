Hospitality and hotel job descriptions blend two very different priorities: measurable service standards (guest satisfaction scores, occupancy targets, upsell revenue) and softer interpersonal qualities (hospitality, composure under pressure, cultural awareness). A resume that leans too far into either direction, all metrics or all personality, misses the [match](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/resume-matching-with-job-description-complete-guide) that hiring managers in this industry are looking for.

Larger hotel groups and resort chains increasingly run applications through an [ATS](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/ats-score-guide), especially for high-turnover front-of-house roles, which means your resume needs to satisfy both the software and the human reviewer.

This guide covers how to match your resume to hospitality and hotel job descriptions, from entry-level front desk roles to hotel management positions.

## What Hospitality Job Descriptions Actually Prioritize

Most hospitality postings are structured around three pillars:

1. **Guest-facing skills** — communication, problem resolution, hospitality mindset
2. **Operational metrics** — occupancy rates, guest satisfaction scores (like NPS or GSS), upsell/revenue contribution, PMS system proficiency
3. **Physical and scheduling requirements** — shift flexibility, standing/lifting requirements, multilingual ability

Missing any one of these three pillars in your resume weakens your match, even if the other two are strong.

## Matching a Front Desk or Guest Services Resume

- Name the exact property management system (PMS) the posting requires, not a generic "hotel software" reference
- Quantify guest volume, resolution speed, or satisfaction scores wherever you have real numbers
- Mirror shift and availability language directly from the posting (overnight, weekend, holiday coverage)
- Reflect language ability if the posting mentions serving international guests

Read [how to quantify resume achievements](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-quantify-resume-achievements) for guidance on turning "handled guest complaints" into a measurable, matched bullet point.

## Matching a Hotel Management or Supervisory Resume

Management-level hospitality postings weigh operational and financial metrics more heavily:

- Lead with revenue, occupancy, or cost-control results tied to your management scope
- Match the exact department language used in the posting (rooms division, F&B, front office)
- Reflect team size managed and any staff training or scheduling responsibilities
- Mirror compliance or safety certification requirements exactly (food safety, OSHA, fire safety)

## Common Mistakes in Hospitality Resume Matching

### Listing "Customer Service" Without Specifics

"Customer service" alone is too generic to match a hospitality posting's specific language. Use the posting's own terms: "guest services," "front office operations," or "concierge services."

### Leaving Out Property Management System Experience

If a posting names a specific PMS and your resume does not mention it, you risk being filtered out even with strong general experience. Always name the systems you have used explicitly.

### Ignoring Shift and Availability Requirements

Hospitality postings are often explicit about schedule flexibility. If your resume signals a strict 9-to-5 background without addressing this, it can quietly hurt your match. Address availability directly if the posting asks for it.

### Formatting That Fails ATS Parsing

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do hotels really use ATS to screen entry-level resumes?

Larger hotel chains and resort groups often do, especially for high-volume front-of-house roles. Matching your resume to the posting's language improves your chances of passing an initial screen.

### Should I include soft skills like "friendly" or "team player" on a hospitality resume?

Yes, but back them with context. Instead of listing "friendly" alone, show it through a result: resolved guest complaints, achieved high satisfaction scores, or received guest recognition.

### How important is naming the specific PMS system?

Very important. If a posting requires Opera PMS experience and your resume only says "hotel software," you risk a keyword mismatch even with the right underlying skill.

### What if I am applying from outside the hospitality industry?

Focus on transferable skills like customer service, conflict resolution, and multitasking under pressure, and mirror the posting's specific terminology as closely as possible.
