## Key Takeaways

- Select all, copy, paste into a plain text editor - what appears is close to what a parser receives.
- Missing sections, scrambled columns and detached dates are the four failure signs to look for.
- A resume that fails this test cannot be fixed by adding keywords; the words are not arriving.
- Most failures trace to columns, text boxes, headers, footers or tables.
- The test takes half a minute and is worth running before every application round, not every application.

Before paying for a scan or rewriting a single bullet, spend thirty seconds finding out whether your resume is readable at all. This is the cheapest diagnostic in a job search and most people never run it.

## The Test

1. Open your resume as the file you actually send - the PDF, not the source document.
2. Select all (Ctrl+A / Cmd+A), copy (Ctrl+C / Cmd+C).
3. Paste into something with no formatting: Notepad, TextEdit in plain text mode, or any online plain-text box.
4. Read what appears.

That text is approximately what a parser receives. Not exactly - different parsers vary - but close enough to catch every serious problem.

## The Four Failure Signs

**1. A section is missing.** Your skills list is on the page and absent from the paste. The text lives inside a text box or graphic element that copies as nothing.

This is the most damaging failure, and the most invisible - the resume looks complete on screen.

**2. Columns are interleaved.** A two-column layout can paste as alternating fragments:

> Senior Analyst SQL, Python
> Acme Corp Tableau, dbt
> 2022-2025 Snowflake

Your job title, employer and skills have been shuffled into lines that mean nothing. A parser trying to identify your employment history from this will produce garbage.

**3. Contact details are gone.** Name, email and phone sitting in the document header often do not copy - and are frequently ignored by parsers even when they do. A resume that arrives without a phone number is a resume nobody calls.

**4. Dates are detached from jobs.** Your dates paste as a separate block, or land above the wrong role. The parser then computes your experience incorrectly - sometimes halving it.

Also worth noting, though less serious: bullet characters arriving as odd symbols, and ligatures breaking words apart.

## What Each Failure Means

| What you see in the paste | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Whole section missing | Text box or graphic element | Retype as normal body text |
| Interleaved fragments | Two-column layout | Switch to single column |
| No contact details | Details in the page header | Move into the document body |
| Dates in a separate block | Table or tab-stop layout | Put dates on the same line as the role |
| Nothing pastes at all | Resume exported as an image | Re-export as real text |

That last one is worth calling out. If nothing selects and nothing pastes, your resume is a picture. Some design tools export this way by default. No parser can read it, and no keyword work will change that.

## What a Pass Looks Like

A clean paste is boring, and that is the point:

> Priya Sharma
> priya.sharma@email.com | +44 7700 900123 | London, UK
>
> SUMMARY
> Data analyst with four years...
>
> EXPERIENCE
> Senior Data Analyst, Acme Corp
> March 2022 - Present
> - Rebuilt weekly reporting from manual spreadsheets to an automated dashboard...

Everything present, in order, each piece attached to the right thing. Ugly in plain text, correct in structure. That is a resume that arrives intact.

## When It Passes but the Score Is Still Low

A clean paste rules out the structural problem. If your score remains low after that, the cause is elsewhere - usually vocabulary differences between your resume and the posting, or a genuine mismatch in requirements.

That is a different problem with a different fix, and it is worth knowing which one you have before spending an hour editing.

## How Often to Run It

Once per resume version, not once per application. The test checks structure, and your structure does not change between applications unless you edit the layout.

Run it when you:

- Build a resume from a new template
- Switch editors, or export from a design tool
- Change the layout - add a column, move contact details, insert a table
- Have been applying for weeks with no responses at all

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is this as accurate as a real ATS?**
No, it is an approximation. Real parsers differ in how they handle edge cases. But every problem this test catches is a real problem, so a failure is always worth fixing.

**My paste looks messy but everything is there. Is that a fail?**
No. Plain text always looks plain. The question is whether the content is complete and correctly associated - not whether it looks tidy.

**Does this work for .docx files?**
Yes, though the more important test is on whatever file you actually submit. If you send PDFs, test the PDF.

**My contact details pasted fine from the header. Am I safe?**
Mostly, but header placement is still riskier than body placement - some parsers skip header regions entirely even when the text copies. Moving them into the body costs nothing.

**Should I use a plain-text resume instead?**
No. A normal single-column resume in a standard font parses reliably and looks professional. You want clean structure, not stripped design.

## After the Structure Check

Once the paste comes back clean, your resume is arriving intact and the remaining question is relevance - whether your wording matches what a given posting asks for.
