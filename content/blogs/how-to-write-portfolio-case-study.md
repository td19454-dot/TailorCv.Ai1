A case study turns a project into a story a hiring manager can follow and remember. It is the single highest-leverage thing you can add to a portfolio, because it converts "here is a thing I made" into "here is how I think and what I achieve." This template works for developers, designers, marketers, PMs, and analysts alike. Pair it with [How to Build a Professional Portfolio](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-build-professional-portfolio).

## The Universal Template
1. **Context** — what it was, your role, the timeline, the constraints.
2. **Problem** — the real challenge, framed sharply (not "build an app" but "users dropped off at checkout").
3. **Process** — research, the options you weighed, and the decisions you made. Show the messy middle.
4. **Solution** — what you actually built or delivered, with visuals.
5. **Outcome** — the measurable result and what you learned.

## Lead With the Outcome
Put the headline result at the very top: "Cut checkout drop-off 23%" or "Ranked #1 for a 12K-volume keyword." Skimmers decide in seconds whether to read on, so reward them immediately.

## Show Your Thinking
The decisions and trade-offs are what separate you from someone who merely executed. For each major choice, explain *why* — and what you rejected. This is exactly what [UX Designer Portfolio Guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/ux-designer-portfolio-guide) and [Product Manager Portfolio Guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/product-manager-portfolio-guide) hiring managers look for.

## Quantify Everything
Numbers create credibility. If you do not have business metrics, use proxies: usage, time saved, performance gains, reach. See [How to Quantify Resume Achievements](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-quantify-resume-achievements).

## Keep It Tight and Visual
- 2–4 strong case studies beat a dozen thin ones
- Add screenshots, diagrams, and before/after
- End with one clear takeaway and a link to the live work

## Common Mistakes
- Describing *what* without *why*
- No outcome or metric
- Too long, no summary for skimmers — see [Portfolio Mistakes to Avoid](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/portfolio-mistakes-to-avoid)


## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long should a case study be?

Long enough to show your thinking, short enough to skim: a headline outcome, a 30-second summary, then the detailed sections.

### What if I cannot share metrics?

Use rounded numbers, proxies (usage, performance), or describe the impact qualitatively with permission.

### How many case studies do I need?

Two to four strong ones. Lead with your best and tie each to a real outcome.
