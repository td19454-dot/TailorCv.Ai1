## Why Marketing Resumes Fail ATS More Than Most

Marketing job descriptions are highly specific - but candidates write generic resumes.

They write "managed social media" when the JD says "Meta Ads Manager, paid social campaigns, ROAS optimization."

They write "email campaigns" when the JD says "HubSpot lifecycle email, automated nurture sequences, segmented sends."

The gap between generic marketing language and JD-specific marketing language is wider than almost any other field.

## Step-by-Step: Matching Your Marketing Resume to a JD

### Step 1: Identify the Marketing Function

Is this a:
- Channel specialist role (SEO Manager, Paid Social Manager, Email Marketing Manager)?
- Generalist role (Marketing Manager, Growth Marketer)?
- Strategic role (VP Marketing, Director of Demand Generation)?
- Ops role (Marketing Operations, Revenue Operations)?

This determines which channel and tools keywords take priority.

### Step 2: Extract Channel and Platform Keywords

Pull every channel and platform name from the JD.
These are your highest-priority keywords.

If the JD says "HubSpot" - it must appear in your resume.
If it says "Google Ads" - it must be there.

### Step 3: Match Metrics to Your Experience

Find the 2-3 metrics the JD mentions or implies.
Rewrite 2-3 bullets to include those metrics from your actual experience.

**JD mentions:** ROAS, CPM, paid social
**Your bullet:** "Managed $350K annual Meta Ads budget across acquisition and retargeting campaigns, achieving average ROAS of 3.8x with CPM below industry benchmark"

### Step 4: Rewrite Your Summary for the Specific Marketing Discipline

**For an SEO Manager role:**
> "SEO Manager with 5 years of technical and content SEO experience for B2B SaaS companies. Expert in on-page optimization, technical audits (Core Web Vitals, site structure), keyword research (Ahrefs, Semrush), and content-led link building. Grew organic traffic by 180% and improved domain authority by 22 points over 2 years."

**For a Demand Generation role:**
> "Demand Generation Manager with 4 years driving pipeline through paid and organic channels for B2B SaaS companies. Experienced in HubSpot, Google Ads, [LinkedIn](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-use-linkedin-for-job-search) Ads, and ABM programs. Generated 40% of company ARR pipeline through multi-touch demand programs at an average CAC 25% below target."

Read [how to match your resume summary to a job description](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/resume-summary-match-job-description).

### Step 5: Update Skills Section with Exact JD Tools

Your [skills section](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/skills-to-add-to-resume-2026) should list every tool and channel from the JD that you have used.

**Before:**
```
Skills: Marketing, Content, Email, Analytics
```

**After (for Demand Generation role):**
```
Marketing Channels: SEO, SEM, PPC, Email Marketing, Paid Social, Content Marketing, ABM
Platforms: HubSpot (Expert), Google Ads, LinkedIn Campaign Manager, Meta Ads Manager
Analytics: Google Analytics 4, Salesforce, Tableau, SQL (intermediate)
Metrics: CAC, LTV, ROAS, MQL-to-SQL conversion, marketing attribution
```

## FAQ
### How important are platform certifications for marketing roles?

Very important for specialist roles (Google Ads certified, HubSpot certified, Meta Blueprint). List every relevant certification you hold.

### Do I need to include all metrics as acronyms?

Use the form the JD uses. If the JD writes "CAC" - use "CAC." If it writes "Customer Acquisition Cost" - use the full form. When unsure, include both: "Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)."

### What if I have channel experience but not the exact platform they list?

Mention your adjacent platform experience and note willingness to learn: "Google Ads (experienced) | Bing Ads (familiar)" shows honesty while signaling relevance.
