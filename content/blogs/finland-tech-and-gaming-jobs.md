## Key Takeaways

- Finland's **gaming industry is globally significant** relative to its population — Supercell, Rovio, Remedy, Housemarque and a long tail of studios.
- **Oulu is a serious telecommunications and wireless cluster**, built on Nokia's research legacy, and it is almost unknown internationally.
- **Helsinki and Espoo** hold the corporate technology, startups and the Aalto University ecosystem.
- These sectors **run in English** and hire internationally as standard, which makes them the accessible entry into Finland.
- Salaries are solid rather than spectacular, and **Finland's lower costs** make them stretch further than the Nordic comparison suggests.

## The gaming industry

Finland's games sector is the country's most internationally visible technology story, and it is genuinely substantial.

**Supercell** in Helsinki built Clash of Clans, Clash Royale, Brawl Stars and Hay Day, and operates a famously small-team structure where individual teams have unusual autonomy.

**Rovio** made Angry Birds and has remained a significant mobile studio.

**Remedy Entertainment** builds narrative-driven console and PC games — Max Payne, Alan Wake, Control — and is a genuinely different kind of studio from the mobile houses.

**Housemarque** in Helsinki built Returnal and has a long arcade-action heritage.

**And a long tail** of smaller studios, many founded by people who left the larger ones, concentrated overwhelmingly in the Helsinki region.

**Why here?** A combination of Nokia's collapse releasing a large pool of skilled mobile and embedded engineers, strong technical education, and early success creating a reinvestment cycle. Neogames and the industry association track the sector, and the density per capita is remarkable.

**What they hire:** engineers, artists, designers, producers, analytics and live-operations people. Mobile studios in particular need data and live-ops capability continuously.

## Oulu, and the telecommunications cluster

The most under-known technology location in [Northern Europe](/blog/southern-europe-vs-northern-europe-jobs).

**Nokia's research presence** in Oulu is substantial and long-standing, focused on radio, networks and wireless technology.

**The 5G and 6G research base** around the [University](/blog/how-to-list-education-on-resume) of Oulu is internationally significant — Oulu has been a centre of wireless research for decades and continues to be.

**A supply chain of smaller companies** in embedded systems, RF engineering, test and measurement, and IoT sits around it.

**For radio, embedded, signal processing and [networking](/blog/networking-tips-for-job-search) engineers**, Oulu offers work at a level that is genuinely hard to find elsewhere, with a fraction of Helsinki's competition and [housing](/blog/remote-job-search-guide) cost.

**The catch is honest:** it is a northern city with long dark winters and a small international community. People who like it stay for decades; people who need a big city do not.

## Helsinki, Espoo and the broader sector

**Espoo and Otaniemi** house Aalto University and a dense concentration of technology companies and startups around it. Nokia's headquarters is in Espoo.

**Startups cluster in Helsinki**, supported by an active ecosystem and Slush, which has grown into one of Europe's better-known startup events.

**Corporate technology teams** at KONE, Wärtsilä, Neste, the banks and the retail groups employ large numbers of engineers doing serious work that is invisible from outside.

**Public-sector technology** is unusually advanced — Finland has invested heavily in [digital government](/blog/estonia-jobs-and-e-residency) — and offers work that is stable and genuinely useful, though frequently requiring Finnish.

## How hiring runs

The process is recognisable, with a few local specifics.

1. **[Application](/blog/how-many-jobs-should-you-apply-to-per-day)**, usually direct to the company. Finnish [employers](/blog/how-recruiters-spot-generic-resumes) rely less on aggregator boards than the UK or Australia.
2. **Recruiter or [hiring manager](/blog/how-recruiters-read-resumes) call** — motivation, work rights, [salary](/blog/how-to-negotiate-salary-offer) expectations. Have a number ready; you will be asked.
3. **Technical assessment**, frequently a [take-home task](/blog/coding-interview-preparation-guide) rather than a live algorithmic grilling. Studios often ask for a small build or a design exercise.
4. **Technical [interviews](/blog/how-to-prepare-for-a-job-interview-in-2026-complete-guide-for-every-industry)**, often discussing your take-home or your shipped work rather than fresh puzzles.
5. **Team fit conversation**, weighted seriously — flat teams care how you disagree.
6. **Offer**, with the permit route worth raising explicitly if you need one.

**Take-home tasks are common**, which suits people who build well and disadvantages those who will not invest the time. Ask what the expected effort is and hold to it.

**[Portfolio](/blog/portfolio-from-resume-minutes) beats [CV](/blog/resume-optimization-guide) in games.** Shipped titles, a playable build, a level you designed, an art station page. Put the link at the top.

**Salary conversations happen early and directly**, in the Nordic pattern. "I am flexible" reads as unprepared.

## Compensation in Finnish technology

The structure, so you can read an offer properly.

**Collective agreements** cover a large share of employees, including in technology, setting floors and compressing ranges.

**Salaries are quoted monthly**, frequently with a holiday [bonus](/blog/how-to-negotiate-a-job-offer-email) paid alongside [annual leave](/blog/questions-to-ask-in-an-interview). Ask whether a figure includes it.

**Equity is uncommon** outside venture-backed startups and a small number of larger companies. Judge an offer on base.

**Bonus structures exist in games**, particularly in mobile studios where live performance is measurable, and can be a meaningful part of annual compensation.

**[Pension](/blog/first-90-days-new-job) contributions** are compulsory and split between employer and employee.

**Occupational healthcare** is a standard employer benefit and genuinely good — worth checking the tier.

**Holiday is generous**, and the July shutdown is real. Plan around it: hiring processes slow markedly in midsummer.

## Breaking in as a graduate

The routes that actually work when you have no shipped titles.

**Summer job programmes are the main pipeline.** Nokia, KONE, Wärtsilä, the banks and many studios run structured summer hiring that feeds permanent offers. If you are studying in Finland, this is the single highest-return application you will make.

**Game jams produce portfolios.** A finished jam game with a playable build is a genuine credential in this industry, and Finland has an active jam scene.

**Aalto and the Oulu ecosystem** run projects with industry partners, and those connections convert into jobs more often than cold applications do.

**Junior roles exist in live operations and analytics.** Mobile studios need people who can read data and run live events continuously, and it is a less contested entry than engineering or design.

**QA is a real door.** Several studio veterans started in testing, and in a small industry internal moves are common.

**Contribute something public.** An open-source tool, a technical blog, a modding project. In a sector this small, being visible works.

**And be honest about what you have done.** Overstating a contribution to a team project is easily unpicked in an industry where everyone knows each other.

## Living in Helsinki and Oulu

The practical comparison, since these are the two realistic destinations.

**Helsinki** is compact, well served by public transport, and expensive by Finnish standards while cheap by Nordic ones. Espoo and Vantaa are cheaper and well connected.

**Oulu** is dramatically cheaper, small, and cold. Housing is genuinely affordable on a technology salary, which is not true anywhere else in this guide.

**Winter is the honest variable.** Helsinki's winter is dark; Oulu's is darker and considerably colder, with proper snow for months.

**Summer compensates.** Long light evenings, lake and cottage culture, and a genuinely different country from May to August.

**English gets you through daily life** in both cities, and Finnish opens the social side.

**Both have small but real international communities**, larger in Helsinki, and organised activity is the way into either.

## The wider Finnish technology landscape

Beyond games and telecoms, three areas that employ more engineers than their profile suggests.

**Health technology.** Finland's health data infrastructure is unusually advanced, and a cluster of companies works on clinical software, diagnostics and medical devices around Helsinki, Turku and Oulu.

**Clean technology and energy.** Neste's renewable fuels business is globally significant, and Wärtsilä's energy division builds grid-balancing and marine power systems. Both are engineering-heavy and both hire internationally.

**Industrial software.** KONE, Valmet, Metso and Konecranes all run substantial software organisations building the control, monitoring and analytics layers on top of physical machines. This is genuinely interesting distributed-systems and embedded work and it is invisible from outside.

**Public digital services.** Finland has invested heavily in digital government, and the systems behind it are real engineering — though most of these roles require Finnish.

**Maritime and autonomous shipping.** A specialist Finnish strength, combining marine engineering with automation and sensing.

**The pattern:** Finland's best technology jobs are frequently inside companies that do not describe themselves as technology companies. Filtering for "tech" misses most of the market.

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: You want to work in games

Helsinki, and lead with what you have shipped. In this industry a playable build, a portfolio or a credited title outweighs any amount of CV prose, and studios hire internationally as standard.

### Scenario 2: You are a radio, embedded or signal processing engineer

Oulu, seriously. The wireless research base there is internationally significant and the competition is a small fraction of what you would face in a larger European technology city.

### Scenario 3: You need a permit

Target roles that qualify for the specialist permit — degree-level expert positions above the salary threshold. No labour market test and fast processing make it the route worth aiming at.

> **Tip:** If you build games, put a playable link at the top of your CV rather than describing your projects. Finnish studios will click it, and thirty seconds of actual play does more than a page of description ever will.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Show shipped or playable work
- Consider Oulu for wireless and embedded roles
- Look at corporate technology teams, not just startups
- Target the specialist permit route
- Compare salaries against Finland's lower costs
- Use summer programmes if you are studying in Finland

### Don't

- Assume gaming is only mobile — Remedy and Housemarque are console studios
- Restrict yourself to Helsinki
- Expect Finnish tech salaries to match the US
- Describe projects instead of linking them
- Ignore public-sector technology if you speak Finnish
- Overlook the industrial companies' engineering teams

## Common Mistakes

- **Ignoring Oulu.** A world-class wireless cluster with a fraction of the competition.
- **Describing rather than showing.** In games especially, a playable link is the application.
- **Only targeting startups.** KONE, Wärtsilä, Neste and the banks employ far more engineers.
- **Assuming all Finnish gaming is mobile.** Remedy and Housemarque are console-focused.
- **Not checking the specialist permit.** It is faster and skips the labour market test.
- **Comparing salaries without costs.** Finland is the cheapest Nordic country.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why is Finland's gaming industry so large?

Nokia's decline released a large pool of skilled mobile and embedded engineers, combined with strong technical education and early commercial success that created a reinvestment cycle.

### Which Finnish game studios hire internationally?

Supercell, Rovio, Remedy, Housemarque and a substantial tail of smaller studios, almost all concentrated in the Helsinki region and operating in English.

### What is in Oulu?

A significant wireless and telecommunications cluster built on Nokia's research presence and the University of Oulu's long record in radio research, plus a supply chain in embedded and RF engineering.

### Do these jobs run in English?

Yes, as standard. Gaming, technology and international companies operate in English because the teams are international.

### How do Finnish tech salaries compare?

Solid rather than spectacular by Nordic standards, but Finland's lower living costs mean they go further than a raw comparison against Norway or Denmark suggests.

### What should a games application include?

A playable link or a credited title at the top. In this industry showing the work outperforms describing it by a wide margin.
### What does the hiring process look like in Finnish tech?

Application direct to the company, a recruiter call where salary comes up early, a take-home technical task, interviews discussing that work, and a team fit conversation that is genuinely weighted.

### Is equity common in Finnish technology?

Not outside venture-backed startups and a few larger companies. Judge an offer on base salary plus the holiday bonus, and check whether a quoted monthly figure includes it.
### How does a graduate break into Finnish gaming or tech?

Summer job programmes at the large employers are the main pipeline, followed by game jams producing playable portfolios, university-industry projects, and less contested entries through live operations, analytics or QA.

### Helsinki or Oulu?

Helsinki for scale, variety and a larger international community. Oulu for wireless and embedded work, genuinely affordable housing, and far less competition — with a colder, darker winter.
### Where are the Finnish tech jobs outside gaming?

Health technology, clean energy at Neste and Wärtsilä, and the substantial software organisations inside KONE, Valmet, Metso and Konecranes — all invisible if you filter only for companies branded as technology.
