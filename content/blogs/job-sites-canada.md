## Key Takeaways

- Job Bank is the government platform and is particularly relevant for newcomers, since it also indicates roles connected to immigration streams.
- LinkedIn dominates professional hiring in Canada and functions as a recruiter-searched database as much as a job board.
- A large share of Canadian roles are filled through referrals, so applications alone are the hardest channel - especially for newcomers.
- Immigrant-serving organisations offer free employment services and employer connections that the people they exist for consistently underuse.
- Set your location to a Canadian city and use a Canadian phone number, because recruiters filter on both.

Canada's job market is smaller than the US market and more relationship-driven than its size suggests. That shapes the search: the boards tell you what exists, but a meaningful share of roles are filled through networks before or instead of open competition.

For newcomers this compounds the problem described in [the Canadian experience problem](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/canadian-experience-problem) - the channel that works best is the one you have least access to on arrival. Which is precisely why the free services built to bridge that gap matter so much.

This guide covers the platforms, how they screen you, and where to spend effort beyond them.

## The Major Platforms

### Job Bank

The Government of Canada's official job board, and the natural starting point for newcomers.

**Why it matters particularly:** beyond listings, it carries labour market information by occupation and region - wage data, outlook, and which provinces have demand. That is genuinely useful for deciding where to target, and it is free.

It is also where employers post when meeting LMIA advertising requirements, so it can surface roles where the employer is already engaged with hiring foreign workers - see [the LMIA job search guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/lmia-job-search-guide).

**Best for:** newcomers, labour market research, regional roles, and trades.

### LinkedIn

The dominant platform for professional hiring in Canada, and it does three jobs: listings, a database recruiters search, and the networking channel that referrals come through.

**Set up properly:** location set to your Canadian city - recruiters filter on this and a foreign location removes you from results - a headline naming your actual role, and a populated skills section. See [LinkedIn for Canada job search](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/linkedin-for-canada-job-search) and the [LinkedIn profile optimization guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/linkedin-profile-optimization-guide).

**Best for:** professional roles, recruiter contact, and finding people for referrals.

### Indeed

The largest by volume, aggregating from employer sites and carrying direct postings. Broad coverage including hourly and non-professional work that LinkedIn covers thinly.

**Best for:** breadth, and non-office sectors.

### Glassdoor

Listings plus company reviews, salary data, and interview question archives - useful preparation before an interview and before a negotiation. See [Canada salary negotiation](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/canada-salary-negotiation-guide).

### Workopolis and Eluta

Canadian-focused boards. Eluta indexes employer career pages directly, which surfaces roles that never reach the aggregators.

## Provincial and Sector Platforms

Easy to miss, and they carry roles the general boards do not.

**Provincial job banks.** Most provinces run their own, often connected to their Provincial Nominee Program streams - relevant if immigration is part of your plan, as covered in [Express Entry and job offers](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/canada-express-entry-job-offer).

**Government.** Federal roles go through the Government of Canada jobs portal with its own application format. Provincial and municipal governments run separate systems.

**Healthcare.** Provincial health authorities post through their own portals, and licensing bodies often maintain job listings.

**Academia.** University career pages and academic job boards.

**Technology.** Beyond LinkedIn, Canadian startup and tech-specific boards carry early-stage roles - relevant if you are targeting the market covered in [Canada tech interview guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/canada-tech-interview-guide).

**Non-profit and charity.** Dedicated boards, since these roles rarely appear commercially.

## The Channels That Outperform Boards

### Referrals

A large share of Canadian roles are filled through networks. A referred application is read; a cold one on a popular posting frequently is not.

**Informational interviews** are the Canadian-appropriate way in. Ask someone in your field for twenty minutes about the industry, not for a job. This is well understood and generally well received, and it converts into referrals over time. See [networking messages for referrals](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/networking-messages-for-referrals) and [networking for jobs in Canada](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/networking-jobs-canada).

### Immigrant-serving organisations

Provincially funded, free, and consistently underused by the people they exist to help. They offer employment counselling, resume review, interview practice, mentorship matching, bridging program referrals, and direct employer connections.

If you are a newcomer, this is among the highest-return use of a morning available to you.

### Recruitment agencies

More significant in Canada than in the US, particularly for contract and temporary roles. Agencies are also more willing to place candidates with international experience, because the commitment is smaller - and a contract role produces the Canadian reference that unlocks everything else.

### Professional associations

Most fields have one, with events, job boards, and for regulated professions, the licensing pathway. Essential for engineers, accountants, and healthcare workers.

## How Boards Screen You

**Location filters are strict.** Set a Canadian city. A foreign address or phone number quietly removes you from search results and signals you may not be here.

**Standard job titles.** Recruiters search by them, so a creative internal title makes you invisible.

**Named skills, not categories.** Matching is close to exact.

**Clean parsing.** Single column, standard headings, no text inside images, nothing critical in headers or footers - the failure modes are in [ATS resume formatting mistakes](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/ats-resume-formatting-mistakes) and [ATS in Canada](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/ats-in-canada-guide).

**Work authorisation.** State it plainly - "Permanent resident" or "open work permit, no sponsorship required" removes an entire category of doubt.

**Recency.** Several boards weight recently updated profiles, so refresh yours regularly.

## Common Mistakes

**Applying only through boards.** A large share of Canadian roles are filled through referrals, so board-only searching puts you in the hardest channel - and for newcomers, the one where the Canadian experience objection bites hardest.

**Not using immigrant-serving organisations.** They are free, provincially funded, and offer mentorship, employer connections, and bridging referrals - and the newcomers who need them most consistently do not use them.

**Leaving your location set abroad.** Recruiters filter on location, so a foreign city or phone number removes you from results before anyone reads your experience.

**Ignoring Job Bank.** Beyond listings it carries wage and outlook data by occupation and region, which is genuinely useful for deciding where to target and costs nothing.

**Skipping provincial platforms.** Many are connected to Provincial Nominee Program streams, which makes them doubly relevant if immigration is part of your plan.

**Overlooking recruitment agencies.** They place contract roles that convert, and they are more willing to take international experience because the commitment is smaller.

**Not stating work authorisation.** Employers screen on it early, and ambiguity is resolved against you rather than in your favour.

**Uploading a resume that does not parse.** You become invisible to the database searches recruiters actually run, however strong the content is.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best job site in Canada?

LinkedIn for professional roles, Job Bank for newcomers and labour market research, and Indeed for breadth. Add the provincial platform for your province and whichever sector board covers your field.

### Is Job Bank worth using?

Yes, particularly for newcomers. Beyond listings it provides wage and job outlook data by occupation and region, and it carries postings connected to LMIA advertising requirements.

### Do Canadian employers use ATS software?

Most mid-size and large employers do, and recruiters search their databases by keyword regardless. Your resume needs to parse cleanly either way.

### How important are referrals in Canada?

Substantially - a large share of roles are filled through networks. This is why informational interviews and professional associations matter more than application volume, especially for newcomers building a local network from scratch.

### Should I use a recruitment agency?

Yes, particularly for contract roles and if you are a newcomer. Agencies place international candidates more readily than direct employers because the commitment is smaller, and a contract produces the Canadian reference you need.

### Are government jobs on the regular boards?

Federal roles go through the Government of Canada jobs portal with its own format, and provincial and municipal governments run separate systems. They are largely invisible on commercial boards.

### What should my resume say about work authorisation?

State it in one line - permanent resident, open work permit, or requiring sponsorship. Employers screen on it early and being explicit saves a wasted process.

Set your location to a Canadian city and get a Canadian phone number before applying anywhere, because recruiters filter on both and a foreign address removes you from results regardless of how well you fit.

Then pick two platforms rather than six - LinkedIn plus Job Bank for most newcomers, or LinkedIn plus Indeed if you are already established - and add your provincial platform and whichever sector board covers your field. Upload a resume built to Canadian convention with standard job titles and named skills.

Finally, spend at least half your effort outside the boards, since that is where a large share of Canadian roles are actually filled.
