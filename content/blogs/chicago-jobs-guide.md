## Key Takeaways

- Chicago's economy is genuinely diversified, which makes it more resilient than single-industry tech cities.
- Proprietary trading and derivatives are a world-class local specialism paying at the very top of the market.
- Housing costs a fraction of coastal cities for comparable space, and salaries do not fall proportionally.
- Illinois has a flat state income tax, simpler than progressive systems but not zero like Texas or Washington.
- Winter is severe and is a genuine factor in whether people stay.

Chicago rarely appears in "best cities for tech" lists and consistently outperforms them on the thing that actually matters — what you have left after housing. It is a large, diversified economy with a world-class trading sector, real technology employment, and Midwestern costs.

This guide covers what hires, how pay compares, and the trade-offs.

## What hires

**Proprietary trading and derivatives.** Chicago's distinctive strength, rooted in the futures and options exchanges. Low-latency systems, C++, market data infrastructure, and quantitative research. These firms pay at the very top of the US market and hire a genuinely hard bar — comparable to New York's trading sector, in a city that costs far less.

**Finance and banking.** Beyond trading, a substantial commercial and investment banking presence.

**Logistics and transport.** Chicago is the country's rail and freight hub, which creates real demand for supply chain, routing and optimisation work.

**Healthcare.** Major hospital systems, insurers, and pharmaceutical operations. Steady and less cyclical — see [the US healthcare jobs guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/us-healthcare-jobs-guide).

**Manufacturing and industrials.** Several large industrial companies are headquartered in the region, with growing industrial-technology and IoT functions.

**Consulting.** A major hub, both strategy and technology consulting.

**Startups and enterprise software.** A real ecosystem, smaller than the coasts but with a strong enterprise and fintech focus.

## Compensation and cost

**Salaries are below coastal levels but not proportionally.** A technology role might pay meaningfully less than San Francisco while housing costs a fraction — which is why disposable income frequently comes out ahead.

**Trading is the exception.** Proprietary trading firms compete nationally for talent and pay accordingly, without adjusting downward for local costs. This is the single best compensation-to-cost-of-living arbitrage in the US, and it is why quantitative people move here deliberately.

**Illinois has a flat state income tax.** Simpler than a progressive system, though not the zero of Texas or Washington. Property taxes in parts of the metro are high, which matters if you buy.

**Housing is the headline advantage.** Rent and purchase prices for comparable space are dramatically lower than New York, San Francisco or Seattle. A one-bedroom in a good neighbourhood costs a fraction of a coastal equivalent.

**Public transport works**, which means a car is optional in much of the city — a real saving relative to most of the US.

## Breaking in

**Trading firms hire on a distinct process.** Expect quantitative interviews — probability, mental arithmetic, sometimes market-making games — alongside systems and C++ questions. This is a specific preparation track, not a generic technology loop.

**For everything else**, the process resembles standard US technology hiring — see [the US tech interview process guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/us-tech-interview-process-guide) and [the STAR guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/us-behavioral-interview-star-guide).

**Domain knowledge helps**, as in New York. Logistics, healthcare, manufacturing and trading all reward someone who understands the business rather than only the stack.

**The market is well-connected.** Smaller than the coasts, so referral networks and alumni connections work efficiently — see [the US networking and referral guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/us-networking-referral-guide).

**Sponsorship** is available at large employers and trading firms, less commonly at local startups.

## The winter question

Worth addressing directly rather than treating as a joke.

Chicago winters are long, genuinely cold, and windy. For some people this is a minor inconvenience; for others it is the reason they leave after two years. It is the most common reason cited by people who relocate away.

If you are considering a move, visit in January rather than September. The city is at its best in summer and at its most honest in February.

## Common Mistakes

**Overlooking the trading sector.** It pays at the top of the US market while the city costs a fraction of New York — the strongest compensation-to-cost arbitrage available, and most candidates never consider it.

**Comparing gross salary to coastal offers.** Chicago pays less in absolute terms but housing costs far less, so disposable income frequently comes out ahead — compare what remains, not the headline.

**Preparing generically for trading interviews.** Proprietary firms add probability, mental arithmetic and market-making exercises that a standard technology loop does not include.

**Assuming it is a single-industry city.** The diversification across trading, logistics, healthcare, manufacturing and consulting is what makes the market resilient, and it means more entry points than a tech-only city.

**Ignoring property tax when buying.** Rates in parts of the metro are high enough to change the buy-versus-rent calculation.

**Dismissing the winter.** It is the most common reason people leave, and visiting in summer gives you a misleading picture.

**Skipping domain knowledge on your resume.** Logistics, healthcare and manufacturing exposure differentiates here in a way generic technology experience does not.

**Only targeting startups.** The large established employers and consulting firms carry more volume and often better stability.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Chicago good for tech jobs?

Yes, though it is a diversified economy rather than a tech monoculture. Trading, logistics, healthcare, manufacturing and consulting all employ technologists, which makes the market more resilient than single-industry cities.

### What is Chicago known for in tech?

Proprietary trading and derivatives infrastructure, rooted in the futures exchanges. It is a world-class specialism that pays at the very top of the US market.

### Do salaries compare to the coasts?

Below coastal levels in absolute terms, except in trading which competes nationally. After housing, disposable income frequently exceeds coastal equivalents.

### What is the cost of living like?

Far below New York, San Francisco or Seattle for comparable space, with functioning public transport that makes a car optional in much of the city.

### Does Illinois have income tax?

Yes, at a flat rate — simpler than progressive systems but not zero. Property taxes in parts of the metro are high, which matters when buying.

### Are trading interviews different?

Substantially. Expect probability, mental arithmetic and market-making exercises alongside systems questions, which requires its own preparation track.

### Is the winter really that bad?

Long, cold and windy, and it is the most cited reason people relocate away. Visit in January before committing rather than judging from a summer trip.

## Make This Practical

Compare disposable income rather than salary. Chicago pays below coastal levels and costs dramatically less to live in, so the number that matters is what remains after housing — and it frequently favours Chicago by a wide margin.

Then look seriously at trading if you have quantitative or low-latency systems ability. It pays at the top of the US market in a city that costs a fraction of New York, and it requires its own preparation track covering probability and market-making alongside systems questions.

Finally, use the diversification. Logistics, healthcare, manufacturing and consulting all hire technologists here, so surface any domain knowledge prominently and work the referral networks, which are efficient in a market this well-connected.
