The Labour Market Impact Assessment is one of the most misunderstood parts of Canadian immigration, and one of the most exploited. Whole scam industries exist around selling "LMIA job offers" to people who do not realise the employer is supposed to bear the cost and the process.

The mechanics are straightforward once explained. The strategic picture changed significantly in 2025, when arranged employment points were removed from the CRS - which means much of the advice still circulating about chasing an LMIA for points is now simply out of date.

This guide covers what an LMIA is, when you actually need one, the exempt routes worth knowing, and how to search sensibly.

**A note on accuracy:** Canadian immigration policy changed substantially across 2024 and 2025. Verify current rules with IRCC and Employment and Social Development Canada before acting.

## What an LMIA Actually Is

A Labour Market Impact Assessment is a document a Canadian employer obtains from Employment and Social Development Canada, confirming that hiring a foreign worker will not negatively affect the Canadian labour market - essentially, that no suitable Canadian or permanent resident was available.

**Key points:**

**The employer applies, not you.** It is their application, their fee, and their compliance obligation.

**The employer pays.** There is a processing fee, and it is legally the employer's responsibility. Anyone asking you to pay it is doing something improper.

**It requires recruitment effort.** The employer must generally advertise the role and demonstrate they tried to hire locally.

**It takes time.** Weeks to months depending on the stream, which is why many employers avoid it.

**A positive LMIA supports a work permit application**, which you then submit.

That combination - cost, paperwork, delay, and compliance risk - is why most Canadian employers will not do it unless they genuinely cannot fill the role otherwise.

## The 2025 Change

Previously, a qualifying job offer supported by an LMIA added meaningful CRS points under Express Entry, and a substantial amount of effort went into obtaining one for that reason.

**Arranged employment points were removed from the CRS in 2025.** An LMIA no longer directly raises your Express Entry score.

**What this means:**

**Pursuing an LMIA purely for points no longer works.** If that was your strategy, redirect the effort - language test improvement and provincial nomination are now the larger levers, as covered in [Express Entry and job offers](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/canada-express-entry-job-offer).

**The remaining reasons are still real.** An LMIA-supported work permit lets you work in Canada, which builds Canadian experience that does earn CRS points and qualifies you for the Canadian Experience Class. It also solves the problem described in [the Canadian experience problem](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/canadian-experience-problem).

**Provincial streams may still value employment ties**, and those remain a major route.

## LMIA-Exempt Routes

These are frequently easier and consistently overlooked, because they avoid the process entirely.

**Intra-company transfers.** If you work for a multinational with a Canadian office, transferring internally may be exempt. Often the smoothest route available and worth investigating before anything else.

**Trade agreement categories.** Provisions under agreements including CUSMA and CETA allow certain professionals and intra-company transferees from partner countries to work without an LMIA. Whether you qualify depends on nationality and occupation.

**Global Talent Stream.** Part of the Temporary Foreign Worker Program aimed at specific in-demand occupations, particularly technology, with substantially faster processing. Employers use this because it is quicker than a standard LMIA - technology candidates should ask specifically about it.

**Post-Graduation Work Permit.** If you studied in Canada, this gives you an open work permit for a period after graduating - no employer sponsorship needed at all. This is why studying in Canada is such a strong route in, and it directly parallels the OPT logic in the US.

**Spousal open work permits.** If your spouse holds certain permits or is studying, you may be eligible for an open work permit.

**Working holiday and youth mobility.** Age-limited, nationality-dependent, and offering open work rights where available. Worth checking whether you qualify.

**International agreements and specific programs** including certain religious, academic, and charitable work.

**The practical takeaway:** check whether an exempt route applies to you before pursuing an LMIA. It is faster, cheaper, and does not require finding an employer willing to go through a months-long process.

## Finding Employers Who Will Do It

If no exempt route applies, target realistically.

**Occupations with genuine shortages.** Healthcare, skilled trades, agriculture, transport, engineering, and some technology roles. Employers pursue LMIAs where they genuinely cannot hire locally.

**Larger employers** with HR capacity and prior experience of the process. A small business will often decline simply because they have never done it.

**Employers who have done it before.** Some data on approved LMIAs is published, which gives a starting point for who has actually gone through the process.

**The Global Talent Stream** for eligible technology occupations, because faster processing makes employers considerably more willing.

**Regions with labour shortages.** Smaller cities and rural areas frequently have more difficulty hiring locally, and provincial streams often target them. The job boards are covered in [the best job sites in Canada](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/job-sites-canada).

**Be direct in applications.** Say you would require a work permit and note whether an exempt route might apply. Ambiguity wastes everyone's process.

## Avoiding Scams

This is important, and the pattern is consistent.

**Never pay for a job offer or an LMIA.** The employer bears the LMIA cost by law. Anyone asking you for money in exchange for an LMIA-supported offer is engaged in something improper, and participating can jeopardise your immigration status - not just theirs.

**Warning signs:**

- A request for payment in exchange for a job offer or LMIA
- Guarantees of approval, which nobody can offer
- Vagueness about which company is actually employing you
- Pressure to decide immediately
- A job that does not match your background at all
- Communication only through messaging apps and personal email addresses
- Being asked to sign documents you have not read or do not understand

**Verify independently.** Confirm the company exists, check the role against their own careers page, and be sceptical of intermediaries who cannot explain the process accurately.

**Use regulated representatives** if you use anyone. Immigration consultants in Canada must be licensed, and that status is verifiable.

## Common Mistakes

**Pursuing an LMIA for CRS points.** Those points were removed in 2025, so the strategy that dominated online advice for years no longer produces the result it promises.

**Not checking exempt routes first.** Intra-company transfer, trade agreement categories, the Global Talent Stream, and post-graduation work permits all avoid the process entirely and are frequently faster and easier.

**Paying for a job offer.** The employer bears the LMIA cost by law, and participating in a paid arrangement can jeopardise your own status rather than only the employer's.

**Targeting employers with no shortage.** LMIAs require demonstrating no Canadian was available, so applying to roles with a deep local talent pool asks employers to attempt something they will likely fail.

**Approaching small businesses.** Many have never done an LMIA and will decline on unfamiliarity alone, regardless of how well you fit the role.

**Not asking about the Global Talent Stream.** For eligible technology occupations the faster processing makes employers substantially more willing, yet candidates rarely raise it by name.

**Hiding your work permit requirement.** It surfaces at offer stage and wastes weeks of process on both sides, and being upfront reads as organised rather than as a liability.

**Trusting unregulated intermediaries.** Canadian immigration consultants must be licensed and that status is verifiable, so there is no reason to work with someone whose credentials you cannot check.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is an LMIA?

A document a Canadian employer obtains confirming that hiring a foreign worker will not negatively affect the local labour market. The employer applies and pays; a positive result supports your work permit application.

### Do I pay for the LMIA?

No. The fee is legally the employer's responsibility. Anyone asking you to pay for an LMIA or a job offer is engaged in something improper.

### Does an LMIA give me permanent residence?

No. It supports a temporary work permit. Since arranged employment CRS points were removed in 2025, it also no longer directly raises your Express Entry score - though the Canadian work experience it enables does.

### Which jobs are LMIA-exempt?

Intra-company transfers, certain trade agreement categories, Global Talent Stream roles, post-graduation work permits, spousal open work permits, and working holiday programs, among others. Checking these first is usually worthwhile.

### How long does an LMIA take?

Weeks to months depending on the stream. The Global Talent Stream is substantially faster, which is part of why employers prefer it where the occupation qualifies.

### Why won't employers sponsor me?

Cost, paperwork, delay, and compliance obligations, plus the requirement to demonstrate no Canadian was available. Employers pursue it where they genuinely cannot fill a role locally, which is why occupation and region matter so much.

### Is it worth using an immigration consultant?

For complex cases, potentially - but only a licensed one, and their status is verifiable. For straightforward situations, the published criteria are self-assessable.
