## Key Takeaways

- **Mobile gaming is Turkiye's standout technology sector**, with international acquisitions and raises disproportionate to the country's size.
- **Istanbul concentrates almost the entire ecosystem**, with Ankara holding defence technology.
- **English is genuinely used** at gaming studios and international companies, more than in the wider Turkish labour market.
- **Currency instability shapes compensation** and drives many engineers toward foreign remote work.
- **The talent pool is strong**, supported by good technical universities and a large young population.

## The gaming sector

Worth its own treatment because it is what makes this market distinctive.

**Peak Games was acquired by Zynga** in one of the largest acquisitions of a Turkish company, and **Rollic** followed to the same buyer.

**Dream Games** raised at a substantial valuation on the strength of a small number of very successful titles.

**A wider cluster of studios** operates in Istanbul, ranging from established companies to newer teams founded by people from the earlier successes.

**The reasons this happened here are specific** — a large young population that plays mobile games heavily, strong technical education, low development costs relative to Western studios, and early exits that recycled both capital and experienced operators into new ventures.

**The work is genuinely competitive.** Turkish studios ship globally successful titles and the standard is international rather than local.

**Disciplines in demand** include game development in the mainstream engines, live operations, monetisation and product analytics, user acquisition, and art and technical art.

**And the sector recruits internationally**, which makes it one of the more accessible entry points into Turkiye for a foreign professional.

## Beyond gaming

The rest of the ecosystem.

**Getir** became internationally known for rapid grocery delivery and expanded aggressively abroad, with the retrenchment that followed being part of the story too.

**Trendyol** operates at genuine scale in e-commerce and is one of the largest technology employers in the country.

**Fintech and payments**, with a growing sector addressing a large and young population.

**Enterprise software and IT services**, serving domestic and regional clients.

**Defence technology**, concentrated in Ankara, which employs substantial engineering talent and has grown considerably.

**Outsourced development for European clients**, helped by time zone and cost position.

**And a startup ecosystem** that is real, with local funds, accelerators and a track record of exits, while being smaller and more capital-constrained than Poland's or Israel's.

## Compensation

The financial picture, which needs care here.

**Technology salaries are strong relative to the Turkish market** and modest converted into euros or dollars.

**Gaming pays at the top of the domestic market**, and the successful studios pay well by any local standard.

**Denomination matters enormously.** Some employers pay in or index to foreign currency, and this changes the value of an offer more than any difference in the headline figure.

**Review frequency matters almost as much.** Under sustained high inflation, an annual review is a meaningful real-terms cut, and many employers review more often.

**Bonuses and profit sharing** exist particularly in gaming, where performance-linked pay can be substantial at successful studios.

**Equity** exists at startups and warrants the usual scrutiny, complicated further by currency questions.

**Istanbul costs** have risen substantially in local terms, with housing in the desirable districts being the main pressure.

**And the comparison people should actually make** is not against another Turkish offer but against what remote work for a foreign employer would pay, since that is the alternative most Turkish engineers are genuinely weighing.

## Getting hired

The practical mechanics.

**LinkedIn**, which is well used in Turkish technology and by international recruiters targeting the market.

**Kariyer.net** for domestic volume across sectors.

**Studio career pages**, since the gaming companies recruit directly and continuously.

**Games industry job boards** internationally, where Turkish studios post alongside everyone else.

**Meetups, game jams and the community**, which is genuinely active in Istanbul.

**Referrals**, which carry real weight in a relationship-driven professional culture.

**For gaming, a portfolio of shipped titles is decisive** — what you shipped, what your contribution was, and what the numbers looked like.

**For everything else**, deployed work on a [portfolio site](/portfolio) and an [ATS-checked CV](/ats-analysis) are the baseline, since the larger employers screen at volume.

## The interview process

What to expect.

**A recruiter conversation**, in English at gaming studios and international companies, in Turkish elsewhere.

**A technical assessment**, commonly a take-home or a practical exercise.

**Technical interviews** with engineers, practical in orientation.

**A portfolio review** at gaming studios, which is the central part of the process rather than a supplement to it.

**A product or design discussion** where the role touches it, since the successful Turkish studios are product-led rather than purely technical.

**A cultural round.**

**And an offer conversation** where the currency terms should be raised explicitly rather than assumed.

## Living in Istanbul

Daily reality in one of the world's great cities.

**It is enormous and geographically split** across two continents, and which side you live and work on genuinely shapes daily life.

**Levent, Maslak and Sisli** hold much of the corporate and technology employment on the European side.

**Kadikoy and the Asian side** are frequently preferred for living, with a younger and more relaxed character.

**The commute across the Bosphorus** is a real consideration, and people organise their lives around avoiding it.

**Public transport is extensive** — metro, ferries, buses — and the ferry commute is one of the genuine pleasures of the city.

**Costs have risen sharply** in local terms, with housing in the desirable districts under real pressure.

**The cultural and culinary offer is exceptional**, and this is what residents cite first without exception.

**Earthquake risk is a serious consideration** and building quality varies enormously, which makes it a genuine factor in choosing where to live rather than an abstract concern.

**And the city is genuinely welcoming**, with a substantial international community and a hospitality culture that foreigners consistently remark on.

## Career progression

Where an Istanbul technology career leads.

**Within gaming**, where enough successful studios exist to support real movement, and where experience at a company that shipped a global hit carries genuine weight.

**Into founding**, since the gaming ecosystem in particular has a strong pattern of teams leaving successful studios to start new ones with backing from people who have done it before.

**Into international roles**, as Turkish companies operate across a wide region.

**Abroad**, where Turkish game developers and engineers are well regarded and Berlin, London, Amsterdam and increasingly Dubai have substantial Turkish professional communities.

**Into foreign remote work**, which removes the currency exposure and is the most common alternative people weigh.

**And within defence and manufacturing technology**, which offer long-term technical careers of a different character.

**The gaming founding pattern is worth emphasising** — the sector has a genuine flywheel where exits fund and staff the next generation, and being inside it during a successful run is one of the better positions available in this market.

## Comparing Istanbul regionally

Where it sits as a technology destination.

**Against Warsaw and the Central European hubs.** Poland has EU membership, currency stability and a larger outsourcing base; Istanbul has a distinctive gaming cluster that Central Europe does not match.

**Against Tel Aviv.** Israel has far deeper venture capital and a stronger security and enterprise technology sector; Istanbul has lower costs and consumer gaming strength.

**Against Dubai.** The Gulf offers currency stability and tax advantages; Istanbul offers a genuine product and engineering culture rather than primarily a regional headquarters function.

**Against Bucharest and Sofia.** Comparable cost positions with EU membership as the differentiator against them.

**The Istanbul argument** is the gaming cluster, a large young domestic market and a talent pool that is genuinely strong.

**The Istanbul risk** is currency, and it is the honest counterweight to everything else on this list.

## Skills the market rewards

Where demand concentrates.

**Game development** in the mainstream engines, which is the most distinctive local demand.

**Live operations and monetisation**, which the successful mobile studios treat as core rather than peripheral.

**Product analytics and user acquisition**, disciplines that Turkish studios take unusually seriously and where the local expertise is genuinely deep.

**Backend engineering** for the consumer platforms operating at scale.

**Mobile development**, given how mobile-first the domestic market is.

**Data engineering**, growing across e-commerce and fintech.

**And defence and embedded engineering**, concentrated in Ankara and largely separate from the consumer technology market.

## Four honest cautions

What enthusiasm about this market tends to skip.

**Currency risk is the dominant factor** and no amount of interesting work compensates for a salary that loses real value faster than it is reviewed.

**The work permit quota** stops many employers from hiring foreigners regardless of intent, which narrows the accessible market considerably.

**The startup ecosystem is capital-constrained** relative to Western Europe, which affects both equity value and company survival rates.

**And gaming is concentrated.** The sector is genuinely strong and it is a small number of studios, which means both that opportunities are real and that a downturn in mobile gaming would be felt sharply here.

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: You work in mobile games

Istanbul is one of the more interesting places in the world for this outside the traditional centres, with a genuine cluster, international standards and studios that recruit globally.

### Scenario 2: You are a Turkish engineer weighing foreign remote work

The currency situation makes this a materially different calculation than it is elsewhere. Earning in a stable currency removes an exposure that shapes everything about living on a lira salary.

### Scenario 3: You are a foreign engineer considering Istanbul

Gaming and international companies are the realistic entry points. Check the employer's work permit quota position early, because it stops more applications than any question about your qualifications.

> **Tip:** If you work in mobile games, take Istanbul seriously. The cluster is real, the studios ship internationally successful titles, and they recruit globally — which makes it one of the few genuinely accessible routes into the Turkish market for a foreign professional.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Target gaming if your background fits
- Clarify currency denomination and review frequency
- Show shipped titles or deployed work
- Check the employer's quota position early
- Consider Ankara for defence technology
- Research Istanbul living costs realistically

### Don't

- Ignore the currency question
- Assume the ecosystem is small
- Overlook Trendyol and the large employers
- Expect the wider labour market to work in English
- Assume any employer can sponsor a permit
- Dismiss defence technology, which is substantial

## Common Mistakes

- **Underestimating the gaming sector.** It is internationally significant.
- **Ignoring currency terms.** The most consequential part of any Turkish offer.
- **Assuming employers can sponsor.** The quota condition stops many.
- **Overlooking Ankara.** Defence technology employs substantial engineering talent.
- **Expecting English everywhere.** It works in technology and gaming, not beyond.
- **Dismissing the startup scene.** It is real, if capital-constrained.
