The Salesforce ecosystem hires heavily on certifications and hands-on platform experience, so a Salesforce developer [resume](https://www.thetailorcv.com/modify-cv) has to make both instantly visible. Recruiters and the [ATS](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/what-is-an-ats-score-and-why-does-it-decide-your-job-application-before-any-human-reads-it) scan for specific certs, technologies, and the scale of the orgs you've worked in — and if those [keywords](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-match-resume-keywords-to-job-description) don't match the job description, you're filtered before a human looks.

If you work in a related engineering role, the backend developer resume guide and full-stack developer resume guide share the same fundamentals.

## Lead with certifications

In the Salesforce world, certifications are a primary filter — many postings won't consider you without them, and the [ATS](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/ats-score-guide) treats them as high-value [keywords](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-match-resume-keywords-to-job-description). List them clearly near the top: Platform Developer I and II, Administrator, App Builder, and any architect-track credentials. Use the exact official names and format them with [how to list certifications on a resume](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-list-certifications-on-resume).

A tight [summary](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-write-resume-summary) frames your depth:

> "Salesforce developer with 5 years building on the platform. Platform Developer II certified, expert in Apex and Lightning Web Components, and experienced in orgs with 2,000+ users. Automated workflows that cut manual processing 40%."

See [resume objective vs summary](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/resume-objective-vs-summary) for the opening lines.

## Name the technical stack

Include exactly what you build with in your [skills section](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/resume-skills-section-guide-2026): Apex, Lightning Web Components (LWC), SOQL/SOSL, Flows, triggers, REST and SOAP integrations, CI/CD tooling (SFDX, Copado, Gearset), and testing frameworks. These are the [keywords](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-match-resume-keywords-to-job-description) recruiters filter on, so mirror the [job description](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/job-description-analysis-checklist) and confirm coverage with the ATS score checker. Distinguishing admin, developer, and architect skills matters because roles weight them very differently.

## Quantify org scale and impact

Use the [bullet point formula](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-write-resume-bullet-points) — a strong [action verb](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/best-action-verbs-for-resume), what you built, and a measurable result:

- "Built LWC components used across an org with 2,000+ users."
- "Reduced manual processing 40% by automating workflows with Flow."
- "Integrated Salesforce with three external systems via REST APIs."
- "Cut deployment time 50% by introducing a CI/CD pipeline with SFDX."

Numbers show you deliver, not just code — the [quantify achievements guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-quantify-achievements-on-resume) shows how. Skill lists without outcomes are why capable developers' [resumes get no responses](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/why-your-resume-gets-no-responses).

## Show delivery, not just skills

Frame your experience around outcomes and users served, not a raw technology list. A hiring manager wants to know what you shipped, how many people used it, and what problem it solved. Tie each project to business impact, and keep a [portfolio](https://www.thetailorcv.com/portfolio) or GitHub link if you have shareable work — see [GitHub portfolio optimization](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/github-portfolio-optimization).

## Structure a Salesforce developer resume

1. **Header** — name, title, phone, email, GitHub/[portfolio](https://www.thetailorcv.com/portfolio).
2. **Certifications** — Platform Developer I/II, Admin, etc.
3. **Summary** — three lines, cert- and stack-forward.
4. **Skills** — Apex, LWC, integrations, CI/CD.
5. **Experience** — role, org scale, [quantified](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-quantify-achievements-on-resume) bullets.
6. **Education**.

This mirrors the layout in the [resume builder](https://www.thetailorcv.com/modify-cv). For ordering, see the [resume section order guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/resume-section-order-guide).

## Keep it ATS-safe

Use a single-column, [ATS-friendly template](https://www.thetailorcv.com/templates), a [readable font](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/resume-fonts-guide), and standard headings — no graphics or tables that break parsing, per [how to make your resume ATS-friendly](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-make-resume-ats-friendly). Confirm parsing with the ATS score checker, and if it's low, why is my ATS score low explains why. Keep it to the right length.

**Are certifications required?**
Often yes — many roles won't consider you without Platform Developer I/II. Format them with [how to list certifications on a resume](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-list-certifications-on-resume).

**I'm transitioning in via Trailhead — what do I lead with?**
Certs, Trailhead projects, and hands-on org work, per the [career change to tech guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/career-change-to-tech-guide) and [resume with no experience guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-write-resume-with-no-experience-2026).

**Admin vs developer vs architect — one resume?**
No — these weight skills very differently. [Tailor](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-tailor-resume-in-5-minutes) each application.
