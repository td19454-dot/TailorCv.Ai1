## Key Takeaways

- The **Third Level Graduate Scheme** lets non-EEA graduates of Irish institutions stay in Ireland and work **without an employer permit**, on immigration permission known as **Stamp 1G**.
- The **duration depends on your award level** — a longer period for postgraduate awards than for a bachelor's degree.
- **Stamp 1G permits full-time employment with any employer**, which removes the sponsorship obstacle entirely for its duration.
- The intended destination is an **employment permit** — usually a Critical Skills Employment Permit, which is substantially more favourable than the general route.
- **The scheme is not renewable indefinitely**, and there is a cap on total time you can hold this permission.

## What the scheme actually gives you

The Third Level [Graduate](/blog/first-resume-for-gen-z-2026) Scheme is Ireland's [post-study work route](/blog/best-countries-international-graduates-2026). If you completed an eligible award at an Irish higher education institution, you can apply to remain in Ireland to look for and take up employment.

The permission is recorded as **Stamp 1G**, and its defining feature is that it is **not tied to an [employer](/blog/how-recruiters-spot-generic-resumes)**. You do not need a [job offer](/blog/how-to-negotiate-a-job-offer-email) to get it, you do not need anyone to sponsor you, and you can change jobs during it without any immigration process.

For an employer, that means hiring you carries no permit cost, no delay and no risk — exactly the objection that stops most European employers hiring an international graduate.

**Duration is tiered by award.** Holders of a bachelor's-level award get a shorter period; holders of postgraduate awards at master's level and above get longer. The current durations are published by the Department of Justice and by Irish Immigration Service Delivery, and they are the figures to rely on rather than anything secondhand.

## Eligibility, in plain terms

**An eligible award from a recognised Irish institution.** The award must be at a qualifying level on the National Framework of Qualifications, and the institution must be recognised for this purpose.

**You must have held student permission** (Stamp 2) immediately before applying.

**You must apply within a defined period after your results are issued** — not after your graduation ceremony, which is a separate and later event. People miss this, and the distinction is worth reading twice.

**Your passport and immigration registration must be in order**, with sufficient validity remaining.

## What to do with the time

The scheme is a window, and the window closes. Use it in phases.

**Early months — get employed.** Any relevant role beats holding out for the perfect one, because Irish work [experience](/blog/resume-matching-experienced-professionals) and an Irish [reference](/blog/resume-references-guide) change how every subsequent [application](/blog/how-many-jobs-should-you-apply-to-per-day) reads. And a role that is not Critical [Skills](/blog/resume-skills-match-job-description) eligible today can become the experience that qualifies you tomorrow.

**Middle months — target Critical Skills occupations.** The Critical Skills Employment Permit is the favourable route, and it is available for occupations on a published list. Check that list against your own field early, because it shapes which jobs are worth pursuing.

**Later months — convert.** An employment permit application takes [processing time](/blog/how-long-to-hear-back-after-applying) on top of the employer's internal decision. Leaving it to the last weeks of your Stamp 1G is how people end up leaving the country over a [paperwork](/blog/campus-placement-document-checklist) timeline.

**Say it out loud in applications.** "I hold Stamp 1G permission and can work for any employer without a permit until [date]" is one line that removes an entire category of employer hesitation.

## Critical Skills versus General Employment Permit

The difference between these two is significant enough to shape your job search.

| | Critical Skills Employment Permit | General Employment Permit |
|---|---|---|
| **Eligibility** | Occupations on the critical skills list | Broader, but with an ineligible occupations list |
| **Labour market test** | Not required | Generally required — the role must be advertised first |
| **Salary threshold** | Higher, but with a lower band for listed occupations | Lower entry threshold, more restrictions |
| **Family reunification** | More favourable and available sooner | More restrictive |
| **Path to long-term residence** | Faster route to a Stamp 4 | Longer |

**The practical instruction:** find out whether your occupation is on the critical skills list before you decide which roles to chase. It is published by the Department of Enterprise, Trade and Employment and it is revised.

## Getting the employer on side

The scheme removes the immigration obstacle, and the remaining obstacle is that many employers do not know that.

**Explain it in one sentence, unprompted.** "I hold Stamp 1G, which means I can work full time for any employer with no permit and no application on your side, until [date]." Put it on the [CV](/blog/resume-optimization-guide), put it in the [cover letter](/blog/cover-letter-generator-by-country), and say it in the [recruiter screen](/blog/phone-screen-interview-tips).

**Anticipate the follow-up.** The next question is always what happens afterwards. Have the answer ready: an employment permit, ideally Critical Skills if your occupation is listed, applied for by the employer with your support, well before the permission ends.

**Know what the employer actually has to do.** For Stamp 1G, nothing. For the later permit, an application and a fee, which for a Critical Skills role does not require advertising the job first. Being able to describe that accurately makes you look like a low-risk hire rather than a complication.

**Smaller employers need more reassurance.** A multinational's HR function does this weekly. A twenty-person company may never have done it, and the difference between them saying yes and no is often just whether someone explained the process clearly.

## Beyond the permit: longer-term status

Worth understanding early, because it affects which jobs are worth taking.

**Critical Skills holders reach Stamp 4 faster.** After a qualifying period of employment on a Critical Skills Employment Permit, you can apply for Stamp 4 permission, which is not tied to an employer and allows you to work without a permit.

**Stamp 4 is the practical goal** for most people on this path — it decouples your immigration status from your job, which changes your [negotiating](/blog/how-to-negotiate-salary-offer) position entirely.

**Reckonable residence** counts toward eventual citizenship, but not all permissions count equally. Time spent on student permission generally does not count in the same way as time on an employment permit, which is a detail people discover far too late.

**Check the current rules with Irish Immigration Service Delivery** rather than with anyone's recollection. The qualifying periods and the treatment of different stamps are specific, and they have changed.

## Practical setup while on Stamp 1G

The permission is only half of it; the administration around it decides how smoothly the year runs.

**Register with immigration on time.** Non-EEA residents must complete immigration registration and hold a valid Irish Residence Permit card. Appointments are booked in advance and they are not always available at short notice.

**Keep your PPS number and Revenue registration current.** Changing employer means registering the new employment with Revenue, or you land on emergency tax at exactly the moment you were expecting a first payslip.

**Keep documentation of every job.** Contracts, payslips and reference letters. An employment permit application later will want evidence of your employment history, and reconstructing it from memory is unpleasant.

**Watch your passport expiry.** Immigration permission cannot extend beyond your passport's validity, and renewing a passport from abroad takes longer than people plan for.

**Do not let the permission lapse while an application is pending.** Timelines for permits and for permission renewal do not automatically align, and being between valid permissions is a position worth some effort to avoid.

## Comparing Stamp 1G to other post-study routes

Useful context for anyone choosing between countries or explaining the scheme to an employer.

**Against the UK Graduate Route.** Both are open and unsponsored. The significant difference is downstream: time on the UK Graduate Route generally does not count toward settlement, whereas Irish residence on Stamp 1G and a subsequent permit does count toward long-term status.

**Against the Dutch orientation year.** Both are open with no employer sponsorship. The Netherlands additionally offers its route to graduates of certain highly-ranked foreign universities; Ireland's is for graduates of Irish institutions only.

**Against Hong Kong's [IANG](/blog/hong-kong-iang-visa-explained).** IANG has no occupation restriction at any stage; Ireland's follow-on permits are occupation-sensitive through the Critical Skills list.

**Against Australia's 485.** Both give a defined open period. Australia then runs a competitive [points test](/blog/australia-skilled-visa-points-graduates); Ireland runs a criteria-based permit, which is more predictable.

**The Irish advantage** is the combination: English-speaking, EU member state, open post-study permission, and an unusual density of multinational employers already experienced at hiring internationally. For a graduate who wants EU access without learning a new language, it is one of the strongest positions available.

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: You just received your results

Apply now, inside the window. It runs from your results being issued, not from your graduation ceremony, and the two can be months apart.

### Scenario 2: Your occupation is not on the critical skills list

The General Employment Permit route still exists, with a labour market test and tighter conditions. Also check whether a related job title in your field is listed — occupation classification is more specific than people assume.

### Scenario 3: You are past the halfway point with no permanent offer

Prioritise employers who have sponsored before and roles on the critical skills list. Both dramatically shorten the path, and the permit process itself takes time your permission does not have.

> **Tip:** Apply for Stamp 1G from the date your results are issued, not from your graduation ceremony. The two are frequently months apart, the window runs from the earlier one, and missing it is one of the most common and most avoidable ways to lose the scheme.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Apply within the window after results issue
- Take relevant work early rather than holding out
- Check the critical skills list against your occupation
- State your Stamp 1G status plainly on applications
- Start the permit conversation by the halfway point
- Confirm the employer has permit experience

### Don't

- Wait for your graduation ceremony to apply
- Assume the scheme can be extended indefinitely
- Chase only roles outside the critical skills list
- Leave the permit application to the final weeks
- Assume every employer knows what Stamp 1G means
- Rely on durations quoted anywhere but the official source

## Common Mistakes

- **Timing the application from the ceremony.** The window runs from when results are issued.
- **Waiting for a perfect first job.** Irish experience and an Irish reference improve every later application.
- **Ignoring the critical skills list.** It determines which permit route is realistically open.
- **Leaving conversion late.** Permit processing takes time your Stamp 1G does not have.
- **Assuming employers understand the stamp.** One explanatory line does a great deal of work.
- **Treating the scheme as extendable.** There is a cap on total time on this permission.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is Stamp 1G?

The immigration permission granted under the Third Level Graduate Scheme. It allows full-time employment with any employer, without a work permit, for a defined period after graduating.

### How long do I get?

It depends on your award level — longer for postgraduate awards than for a bachelor's. Check the current durations with Irish Immigration Service Delivery.

### When exactly should I apply?

Within the defined window after your results are issued, not after your graduation ceremony. The two events can be months apart and the window runs from the earlier one.

### Do I need a job offer to get Stamp 1G?

No. That is the point of the scheme — you get the permission first and then look for work, with no employer sponsorship required.

### What happens when it expires?

You need an employment permit, most favourably a Critical Skills Employment Permit if your occupation is on the published list. Start that process well before the permission ends.

### Can I change jobs on Stamp 1G?

Yes, freely. The permission is not tied to an employer, so moving between jobs during it involves no immigration process at all.
### How do I explain Stamp 1G to an employer?

In one sentence, unprompted: you can work full time for any employer, with no permit and no application on their side, until a stated date. Then have the answer ready for what happens afterwards.

### What is Stamp 4 and why does it matter?

Permission that is not tied to an employer and allows work without a permit. Critical Skills permit holders can apply after a qualifying period, and it changes your negotiating position entirely.

### Does time on a student visa count toward citizenship?

Generally not in the same way as time on an employment permit. The treatment of different permissions is specific — check with Irish Immigration Service Delivery rather than relying on recollection.
### What do I need to keep track of while on Stamp 1G?

Immigration registration and a valid IRP card, Revenue registration for each new employer, documentation of every job for a later permit application, and your passport expiry date.
### How does Stamp 1G compare to the UK Graduate Route?

Both are open and unsponsored, but Irish residence counts toward long-term status whereas time on the UK Graduate Route generally does not count toward settlement.
