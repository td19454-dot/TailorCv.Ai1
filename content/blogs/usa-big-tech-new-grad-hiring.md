## Key Takeaways

- Passing the interview loop and getting an offer are separate events at several large employers — team matching sits between them.
- Hiring decisions are frequently made by a committee reading written feedback, not by the people who interviewed you.
- New grad roles are levelled, and the level is usually fixed regardless of how well you interviewed.
- Applications reopen more often than in banking or consulting, so missing one window is less final.
- Referrals matter mainly for getting read, not for getting hired — the loop is the same either way.

## The process, end to end

A typical large-employer pipeline:

1. **Application**, usually via the company careers site. Campus and general applications sometimes go into different pools.
2. **Online assessment** — timed coding problems, automatically graded.
3. **Phone or virtual screen** — one or two coding problems with an engineer.
4. **Virtual onsite loop** — three to five rounds, coding-heavy, with at least one behavioral.
5. **Hiring committee or equivalent review** of the written feedback.
6. **Team matching**, at companies that do it.
7. **Offer.**

Steps five and six are where the process differs most from other industries, and where candidates are most often confused about what is happening.

## Hiring committees

At several large employers, the people who interviewed you do not make the decision. They write detailed feedback — what was asked, what you said, code you wrote, a recommendation — and a committee that never met you reads it.

Two consequences worth internalising:

**Your interviewer is writing a document about you while you talk.** This is why thinking aloud matters so much. If you solve a problem silently, there is little to write down beyond the outcome. If you narrate your reasoning, they can quote it — and quotable reasoning is what persuades a committee. [Communicating while you solve](/blog/usa-technical-interview-newgrad-prep) is not a soft skill here, it is the input to the decision.

**Charm does not transfer.** Being likeable in the room helps the interviewer's impression, but the committee reads text. Substance survives the translation; rapport does not.

## Team matching, and why offers stall

At some companies, passing the loop means you are approved to be hired — not that you have a job. You then need a team with headcount that wants you.

This is why a candidate can "pass" and then wait weeks. It is also why people occasionally pass and never receive an offer: no team took them before the approval expired.

If you are in team matching:

- **Respond quickly and be flexible on domain.** A team that has to wait a week for your reply moves on.
- **Ask what the team actually works on**, not just the product name. Team quality varies enormously within the same company and matters more to your first two years than the company name does.
- **Ask about headcount timing.** A manager who is "hoping to open a req next quarter" is not a match yet.
- **Location flexibility helps a lot.** Restricting yourself to one office narrows the pool sharply.

Not every large employer works this way — some assign you after you accept, some hire directly into a team from the start. Ask the recruiter which model applies, because it changes how much control you have.

## Levelling

New grad roles are levelled, and at most large employers the entry level for a bachelor's graduate is fixed. Interviewing exceptionally well usually gets you an offer, not a higher level.

Where variation exists it is normally tied to credentials — a master's or PhD sometimes maps to a different starting level, and prior full-time industry experience can too. Internships generally do not change the level.

This matters for [negotiation](/blog/usa-offer-negotiation-new-grad): if base pay is tied to a level and the level is fixed, base is the least movable part of the package. Signing bonus, start date and location are where flexibility lives.

## Timing and reopening

Big tech runs autumn campus recruiting like everyone else, but with an important difference: **postings reopen.** Large employers hire in volume, candidates decline, headcount shifts, and roles come back.

So while the [autumn window](/blog/usa-campus-recruiting-timeline) is still the highest-volume period, missing it is less final than in banking or consulting. Watching careers pages through winter and spring is genuinely worthwhile here in a way it is not for a summer analyst programme.

Practical habits: check target companies' pages weekly rather than relying on aggregators, and apply the day something opens. Some new grad postings fill their application cap within days.

## Referrals: what they actually do

A referral gets your application read by a human. That is the whole benefit, and it is real — particularly if your resume would otherwise be filtered on school, GPA or keywords.

What a referral does not do is change the loop. You will face the same problems and the same bar. Anyone promising otherwise is mistaken.

The practical approach is [asking for a conversation rather than a referral](/blog/usa-cold-email-networking-students), and letting the referral follow if the conversation goes well.

## What the resume screen is doing

Large employers receive volumes that make individual reading impossible at the first pass. Your resume is parsed and matched before anyone reads it.

Two failure modes account for most rejections:

**The document does not parse.** Columns, tables, text boxes and header-based contact details all break extraction. Running an [ATS check](/ats-analysis) shows what the parser actually got, which is different from how the page looks.

**The language does not match.** The posting names technologies and the resume does not use those words. [Matching keywords to the posting](/blog/how-to-match-resume-keywords-to-job-description) is not gaming the system; it is describing your work in the vocabulary the role uses.

For new grads specifically, projects usually carry more weight than part-time jobs. Something that exists, runs, and can be discussed in depth is the strongest item on an entry-level resume — see [projects that get interviews](/blog/fresher-resume-projects-that-get-interviews).

## Common Mistakes

- **Solving silently in interviews.** The feedback document is what decides; silence produces a thin one.
- **Assuming passing the loop means an offer.** At companies with team matching, it does not.
- **Being rigid in team matching.** Narrow domain or location preferences can leave you unmatched.
- **Applying once and stopping.** Postings reopen through the year at large employers.
- **Over-valuing referrals.** They get you read, not hired.
- **Choosing the company and ignoring the team.** Your first two years are shaped by the team far more than the logo.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I pass the interviews and still not get an offer?

Yes, at companies that use team matching. Passing means approved to hire; you still need a team with headcount. Staying responsive and flexible is what shortens that phase.

### Does interviewing well get me a higher level?

Rarely for new grads. Entry level is usually fixed by credential. Strong performance affects whether you get an offer, not what level it is.

### Do internships convert automatically?

At most large tech employers a strong internship converts, and this is the highest-probability route in. That is why the [internship application](/blog/usa-internship-return-offer-conversion) is often the more important one.

### How long does the process take?

Weeks to a few months, and team matching can add several more. Long silences are common and usually reflect internal process rather than a decision about you.

### Should I apply to multiple roles at the same company?

Usually one or two closely related ones. Applying to fifteen postings looks unfocused and some systems flag it.

### Is a master's degree worth it for big tech?

For the role itself, usually not required. For international students it can matter for other reasons — it may map to a different starting level and it affects [H-1B lottery odds](/blog/usa-h1b-lottery-new-grads), which is a separate calculation from the job itself.
