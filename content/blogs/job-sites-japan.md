Knowing *where* to look is half the battle when job-hunting in Japan. The right channels — foreigner-focused job boards, LinkedIn, and recruitment agencies that specialise in placing foreigners — connect you to the English-friendly and international employers most likely to hire and sponsor you. Search in the wrong places, and you'll drown in Japanese-only listings for roles that aren't open to you. This guide maps the best job sites and channels for finding work in Japan in 2026, with practical tips for using each as a foreign job seeker.

## Key Takeaways

- **Foreigner-focused job sites** ([bilingual](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/bilingual-language-skills-on-resume)/international roles) are the best starting point for most foreigners.
- **LinkedIn** is heavily used by global companies and recruiters hiring foreigners in Japan.
- **Recruitment agencies that place foreigners** are invaluable — they know which employers sponsor and guide you through the process.
- For **English teaching**, the JET Programme, language schools, and international schools are dedicated routes.
- Use **multiple channels**, tailor your application to each role, and prioritise Tokyo for the most opportunities.

## Category 1: Foreigner-Focused Job Sites

The most useful starting point for most foreigners is a job site that specialises in **bilingual and international roles in Japan**. These platforms curate positions open to non-Japanese speakers or requiring English, across tech, finance, teaching, and business — filtering out the noise of Japanese-only listings you can't apply to.

**How to use them well:**

- Filter by **language requirement** (English-friendly / bilingual) and your field.
- Set up **alerts** for your target roles.
- Read requirements carefully — note the Japanese level and visa/sponsorship expectations.

These sites are where many foreigners find their first role in Japan. Combine them with the strategies in [how to get a job in Japan as a foreigner](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-get-a-job-in-japan-foreigner).

## Category 2: LinkedIn

LinkedIn is essential for professional and global roles in Japan:

- **Global companies and their recruiters** actively source candidates on LinkedIn, especially in Tokyo.
- Recruiters can **approach you** about roles you never see advertised.
- It's how you **build a network** in Japan and get referrals.

Optimise your profile with your target keywords and language credentials (JLPT/TOEIC), set your location to your target city, and turn on "Open to Work." Use the keyword approach from [best resume keywords for Japan](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/best-resume-keywords-japan). Reach out to people at your target companies with a short, polite note.

## Category 3: Recruitment Agencies

Agencies that specialise in placing foreigners in Japan are one of your most valuable resources:

- They know **which employers sponsor** work visas and hire foreigners.
- They **match you to suitable roles** and often advocate for you.
- They **guide you** through Japan's hiring process, documents, and expectations.
- Many handle **bilingual and professional roles** in tech, finance, and international business.

Register with a few reputable agencies, be clear about your skills, language level, and visa situation, and stay responsive. A good recruiter can dramatically shorten your search.

## Category 4: Company Career Pages

Global firms and English-friendly employers post roles directly on their own sites:

- Target **multinationals, tech companies, and startups** with a presence in Japan.
- Check the **careers pages** of companies you admire.
- Applying directly often means facing an **ATS**, so make your resume [ATS-friendly](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/ats-friendly-resume-builder) — see [ATS and English resumes for Japan](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/ats-english-resume-japan).

## Category 5: English Teaching Routes

If you're targeting English teaching, there are dedicated channels:

- **The JET Programme** — a well-known government-affiliated route placing assistant language teachers.
- **Language school (eikaiwa) chains and independent schools.**
- **International schools and universities** for more qualified roles.

Teaching typically requires a degree and native/fluent English, but not Japanese — a reliable way to start a career in Japan.

## Category 6: Networking and Communities

Not every opportunity is advertised. In Japan, connections and referrals matter:

- **Professional meetups and industry events** (especially in Tokyo's tech and startup scene).
- **Expat and professional communities.**
- **Alumni networks and informational interviews.**

A warm introduction can carry real weight — see the networking tips woven through [how to find a job in Tokyo](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-find-a-job-in-tokyo).

## The Visa Reality

Whatever channel you use, you'll generally need an employer-sponsored work visa matched to your role and qualifications. Job sites and agencies focused on foreigners will indicate which roles offer sponsorship. Always confirm current rules with official Japanese authorities.

> **Note:** Visa rules change and depend on your situation — verify with the Japanese Immigration Services Agency, an embassy/consulate, or your employer.

## Common Mistakes When Searching for Jobs in Japan

- **Using only Japanese-only job boards.** As a foreigner, start with foreigner-focused sites and English-friendly channels.
- **Relying on a single channel.** Combine job sites, LinkedIn, and agencies.
- **Ignoring recruitment agencies.** They know who sponsors and can advocate for you.
- **Not tailoring applications.** Generic applications rank poorly and get ignored.
- **Overlooking Tokyo.** It has by far the most foreigner-friendly roles.
