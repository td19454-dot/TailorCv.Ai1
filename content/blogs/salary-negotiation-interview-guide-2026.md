## When to Discuss Salary

### During the Interview Process

- **Early HR Screens:** If pressed, provide a salary range but redirect the conversation to learning about the role first.
- **Never Lie About Current Salary:** Many regions now prohibit salary history questions, and honesty is crucial.
- **Focus on Value First:** Prove you are worth hiring by preparing strong answers to questions like [why should we hire you](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-answer-why-should-we-hire-you).

### After the Offer

This is the **best time to negotiate**. They have decided they want you, and you have maximum leverage. 

Do not negotiate before you have a written offer unless you are clarifying a range for a [remote role](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/remote-job-interview-preparation-2026).

## How to Research Your Market Value

Before negotiating, know your number:

1. **Check Salary Data:** Use platforms like Glassdoor, Levels.fyi, and Payscale for your role and location.
2. **Talk to Peers:** Engage with colleagues or professionals in similar roles to gauge current market conditions.
3. **Factor in Total Compensation:** Consider base salary, bonuses, equity, and benefits, including remote stipends.
4. **Consider Company Stage:** Understand the differences between startup equity versus enterprise stability.

If you are changing careers, read the [career change to tech guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/career-change-to-tech-guide) for realistic range expectations.

### Example of Market Research

For instance, if you are applying for a software engineering position in San Francisco, you might find that the average base salary is $120,000, with total compensation often exceeding $150,000 when factoring in bonuses and stock options. This information empowers you to negotiate effectively.

## Salary Negotiation Scripts That Work

### When the Offer is Lower Than Expected

"Thank you - I'm excited about this opportunity and the team. Based on my research for [role] in [location] and my experience with [specific achievement], I was expecting something closer to [target range]. Is there flexibility in the base salary?"

### When They Say the Budget is Fixed

"I understand budget constraints. Could we explore other components - signing bonus, additional PTO, remote work flexibility, professional development budget, or an earlier performance review?"

### When You Have Competing Offers

"I have another offer at [range], but I prefer this role because [specific reason about company/team]. Is there room to close the gap?"

### Practicing Scripts

Practice these scripts aloud with [AI mock interviews](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-practice-interviews-at-home) - compensation conversations feel awkward until you rehearse them. 

### Checklist for Salary Negotiation Prep

- Research market salary data.
- Prepare your negotiation scripts.
- Identify your non-negotiables and flexible areas.
- Practice with a friend or through an AI tool.

## What Else to Negotiate Beyond Base Salary

- **Signing Bonus:** A one-time payment that can help bridge the gap.
- **Equity / Stock Options:** Especially relevant in startups.
- **Performance Bonus Structure:** Ensure you understand how bonuses are awarded.
- **Start Date Flexibility:** Sometimes a later start can be beneficial.
- **Remote / Hybrid Arrangement:** Flexibility can often be negotiated.
- **Title:** Especially for [career growth](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-get-a-promotion) opportunities.
- **Learning and Conference Budget:** Investing in your skills can be a win-win.
- **Relocation Assistance:** If moving for the job, this can be a critical factor.

## Mistakes to Avoid

- **Negotiating Before an Offer:** This puts you in a weak position.
- **Giving a Number First Without Research:** You risk anchoring too low.
- **Accepting Immediately:** Always ask for time to review (24-48 hours).
- **Being Adversarial:** Approach negotiations collaboratively; it's a conversation, not a confrontation.
- **Ignoring Benefits:** Total compensation matters more than just salary.

### Common Mistakes Summary

1. **Not Doing Your Research:** Entering negotiations unprepared can hurt your position.
2. **Failing to Communicate Clearly:** Be direct yet polite in your negotiations.
3. **Being Too Timid:** Employers expect negotiation; a lack of confidence can undermine your request.

## Step-by-Step Salary Negotiation Checklist

1. **Know Your Worth**: Start with thorough market research. Use websites like Glassdoor, PayScale, and LinkedIn Salary Insights to gather data on your role and industry. 

2. **Define Your Salary Range**: Based on your research, establish a realistic salary range. Aim for a range where the lower end is still above your minimum acceptable salary.

3. **Prepare Your Value Proposition**: List your skills, achievements, and any metrics that demonstrate your impact in previous roles. This will help you articulate your value clearly during the negotiation.

4. **Practice Your Pitch**: Role-play your negotiation with a friend or mentor. Use scripts from our [Salary Negotiation Scripts That Work](#) section to guide your practice.

5. **Plan for Counteroffers**: Think about potential counteroffers the employer might present and decide in advance how you will respond.

6. **Stay Professional and Positive**: Approach the negotiation with a collaborative mindset. Keep the tone positive and professional, focusing on mutual benefits.

7. **Follow Up**: After the discussion, send a thank-you email summarizing key points discussed, including your enthusiasm for the role.

## Common Salary Negotiation Mistakes to Avoid

- **Focusing Solely on Salary**: Remember to consider the entire compensation package, including benefits, bonuses, and work-life balance. 

- **Not Doing Enough Research**: Failing to research industry standards can leave you underprepared. Use resources like the [Remote Job Search Guide 2026](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/remote-job-search-guide) for insights on remote roles.

- **Accepting the First Offer**: Employers often expect negotiation. Accepting the first offer can suggest a lack of confidence or knowledge about your worth.

- **Neglecting to Practice**: Going into negotiations without practicing can lead to nervousness. Mock interviews available at [Mock Interview](https://www.thetailorcv.com/mock-interview) can help you prepare.

- **Being Unprofessional**: Avoid using ultimatums or aggressive tactics. Maintain a respectful tone throughout the process.

## Do's and Don'ts of Salary Negotiation

### Do's:
- **Do Prepare Thoroughly**: Equip yourself with data and examples of your past successes.
- **Do Keep Emotions in Check**: Approach the negotiation as a business discussion, not a personal one.
- **Do Be Open to Compromise**: Flexibility can lead to creative solutions that satisfy both parties.

### Don'ts:
- **Don't Discuss Personal Financial Needs**: Focus on your value and market data, not personal circumstances.
- **Don't Rush the Process**: Take your time to consider the offer and discuss it thoughtfully.
- **Don't Burn Bridges**: Even if negotiations don’t go as planned, remain professional. You may cross paths with the employer again.

## Worked Example: Calculating Your Salary Range

1. **Identify the Average Salary**: Research indicates that the average salary for your role is $70,000.
2. **Assess Your Experience**: If you have five years of experience versus the average of three, you may add 10-20% to the average salary.
3. **Account for Skills**: If you possess in-demand skills relevant to the job, factor in an additional 5-10%.
4. **Final Range Calculation**:
   - Base Salary: $70,000
   - Experience Adjustment: +$10,500 (15%)
   - Skills Adjustment: +$3,500 (5%)
   - Final Range: $84,000 to $90,000

This calculated approach not only prepares you for negotiation but also provides a solid foundation for your value proposition during discussions.
