## Key Takeaways

- **ARRT certification with your registry number** goes at the top. It is verified, so include it.
- Modalities are the keywords that matter: R, CT, MR, M, VI, CI. Write the ones you hold as ARRT writes them.
- Name the equipment vendor — Siemens, GE, Philips, Canon. Postings do, and generic "imaging equipment" matches nothing.
- Exam volume per shift is the number that separates rad tech resumes.
- Cross-modality experience is the strongest thing on the page. Say it explicitly.

Radiologic technology hiring is credential-driven and equipment-specific. Departments need to know exactly which modalities you are registered in and which systems you have operated, because the answer determines whether you can work unsupervised on day one.

## Best Radiologic Technologist Resume Format for 2026

Reverse-chronological, one to two pages, single column.

One page is fine early in your career. Move to two once you hold multiple modality registries and equipment experience worth listing — see our [resume length guide](/blog/ats-resume-length-guide).

Single column, no tables: imaging departments frequently run older ATS installations. [ATS formatting mistakes](/blog/ats-resume-formatting-mistakes) covers what breaks parsing.

Section order:

1. Header
2. Certification and licensure
3. Summary
4. Modalities and equipment
5. Experience
6. Education

## Certification and Licensure Section

Directly under the header. This gets verified against the ARRT registry, so present it cleanly.

```
CERTIFICATION & LICENSURE
ARRT Registered Technologist — R.T.(R)(CT), Registry #1234567, current through 09/2027
State Radiologic Technologist License, Ohio, #RT-88213, active through 07/2028
BLS for Healthcare Providers, AHA — current through 03/2027
Venipuncture certified — 2023
```

**Write your credentials as ARRT writes them.** The postnominal format is `R.T.(R)` for radiography, with additional modalities appended: `R.T.(R)(CT)`, `R.T.(R)(MR)(CT)`. Postings use this notation and so should you.

The modality codes:

- **(R)** Radiography
- **(CT)** Computed Tomography
- **(MR)** Magnetic Resonance
- **(M)** Mammography
- **(VI)** Vascular-Interventional
- **(CI)** Cardiac-Interventional
- **(N)** Nuclear Medicine
- **(S)** Sonography
- **(BD)** Bone Densitometry

Some states licence separately from ARRT certification — include both where they apply.

## Professional Summary

> ARRT-registered technologist R.T.(R)(CT) with 6 years in a 400-bed acute care hospital, averaging 45-60 exams per shift across radiography and CT. Experienced on Siemens SOMATOM and GE Revolution systems, with trauma and stroke-protocol CT coverage on nights.

Specific in four ways most summaries are not: registry, volume, vendor, and coverage type.

## Modalities and Equipment

This section decides fit, and vague versions of it fail.

> **Modalities:** Radiography (6 yrs) · CT (4 yrs) · Fluoroscopy (3 yrs) · Portable/mobile radiography · OR C-arm
> **Equipment:** Siemens SOMATOM Definition AS · GE Revolution CT · Philips DigitalDiagnost · Canon Radrex · OEC 9900 C-arm
> **Systems:** Epic Radiant · PACS (Sectra, Agfa) · RIS · Dose tracking (DoseWatch)

Vendor names matter more than people expect. A department running Siemens wants someone who has run Siemens — the workflow differences are real, and the posting will often say so. This is straightforward [keyword matching](/blog/how-to-match-resume-keywords-to-job-description): use the posting's own terms where they are true of you.

## Best ATS Keywords for a Rad Tech Resume

**Clinical:** patient positioning, radiation safety, ALARA, image acquisition, contrast administration, IV access, venipuncture, exam protocols, trauma imaging, portable radiography, fluoroscopy, OR imaging, quality assurance

**Credentials:** ARRT, R.T.(R), registered technologist, state licensure, BLS, venipuncture certified

**Technical:** CT, MRI, mammography, C-arm, PACS, RIS, DICOM, kVp/mAs technique, dose optimisation, image post-processing, 3D reconstruction

**Setting:** Level I/II trauma, outpatient imaging, acute care, mobile imaging, stroke protocol, on-call

## Writing Experience Bullets

**Weak:**
> Performed x-rays on patients and maintained equipment.

**Strong:**
> Perform 45-60 diagnostic exams per shift across radiography and CT in a 400-bed acute care hospital, including trauma, stroke-protocol and post-operative imaging.

**Weak:**
> Followed radiation safety procedures.

**Strong:**
> Reduced average CT dose index by 18% across routine abdominal protocols by re-baselining technique charts with the lead physicist, with no change in diagnostic image quality.

Numbers most rad techs have: exams per shift, repeat rate, dose reduction, modalities covered, students precepted, on-call frequency, turnaround time on stat exams. [How to quantify achievements](/blog/how-to-quantify-achievements-on-resume) covers finding them.

## Cross-Modality Experience Is Your Strongest Card

A technologist who can cover radiography *and* CT — or CT and MRI — is worth more to a department than a single-modality tech, because it solves staffing problems. Say it explicitly rather than leaving it implied across two job entries:

> Cross-trained across radiography, CT and fluoroscopy; regularly cover CT independently on nights and weekends, including stroke and trauma protocols.

If you are working toward another registry, state it: *"Currently completing clinical hours toward ARRT (MR), exam scheduled 04/2027."*

## New Graduate Radiologic Technologists

Lead with clinical hours by modality:

> **CLINICAL EDUCATION — 1,850 hours, 2025-2026**
> **Diagnostic Radiography**, Mercy Regional (1,200 hrs). Performed 1,000+ supervised exams: chest, extremity, spine, abdomen, portable and trauma.
> **Computed Tomography**, Mercy Regional (400 hrs). Assisted and performed routine head, chest and abdomen/pelvis studies; contrast administration under supervision.
> **Fluoroscopy / OR** (250 hrs). C-arm operation for orthopaedic and GI cases.

Include competency counts — programmes track these, and hiring managers read them as evidence of readiness. Note your ARRT exam status clearly if results are pending. Our [no-experience resume guide](/blog/how-to-write-resume-with-no-experience-2026) covers the wider framing.

## Common Rad Tech Resume Mistakes

**Writing "ARRT certified" without the modalities.** `R.T.(R)(CT)` is the information; "ARRT certified" is not.

**Omitting the registry number.** It gets verified regardless.

**Saying "imaging equipment" instead of vendors.** Postings name Siemens, GE, Philips. Match where true.

**No exam volume.** Throughput is the core operational fact about your work — [weak bullets](/blog/weak-resume-bullets-fix) covers why duty lists fail.

**Hiding cross-modality coverage.** It is your best differentiator.

**Leaving out on-call and shift coverage.** Departments hiring for nights specifically want to see you have done it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I write my ARRT credentials?**
As ARRT does: `R.T.(R)`, `R.T.(R)(CT)`, `R.T.(R)(MR)(CT)`. Include the registry number and expiry.

**Do I need a state licence as well as ARRT?**
It depends on the state. Where one exists, list both.

**Should I list every piece of equipment?**
List systems you have operated independently, by vendor and model where you know it. Do not pad.

**I am registered in R only but want CT roles. What do I do?**
Say where you are in the process, list any CT clinical hours, and apply. Departments often train candidates who show the pathway is underway.

**How long should the resume be?**
One page early on; two once multiple registries and equipment lists justify it.
