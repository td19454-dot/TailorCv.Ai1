## Key Takeaways

- Foreign work permits in China are categorised, broadly reflecting an applicant's qualifications, experience and role, with different categories carrying different requirements and treatment.
- Some categories use a points-based assessment considering factors including salary, education, age and language ability, among others.
- Higher categories generally carry more favourable treatment, sometimes including exemption from certain standard requirements that apply to lower categories.
- Understanding which category a specific offer would place you in matters more for planning your career than simply knowing that a work permit exists.
- Requirements and point thresholds are set by national policy and reviewed periodically, verify current specific criteria directly rather than relying on general summaries.

## The categorisation system

Work permits for foreign nationals in China are sorted into three named categories under the unified Foreigner's Work Permit system administered nationally (built on the earlier "foreign expert" and standard work permit systems merged in 2017). This categorisation matters beyond simple eligibility, since different categories carry meaningfully different requirements and treatment:

| Category | Who it's for | Typical requirement | Graduate-relevant? |
|---|---|---|---|
| A — "foreign high-end talent" | The most favourable tier | A high points threshold (commonly referenced around 85) or a recognised talent programme | Rare for new grads, but often exempts the standard prior-experience rule where it applies |
| B — "foreign professional talent" | Where most graduate hires land | Bachelor's degree plus a defined period of prior relevant work experience | Yes — and the experience requirement is exactly the obstacle that trips up recent graduates, [covered in the work visa guide](/blog/china-work-visa-z-visa-for-graduates) |
| C — "foreign ordinary personnel" | Lower-skill, seasonal roles | Quota-controlled, considerably more restricted | Not the relevant category for most graduate professional hiring |

Some cities pilot exceptions to Category B's experience requirement for outstanding recent graduates without a full work history — worth checking directly rather than assuming the standard rule is absolute everywhere.

**Higher categories are generally associated with more established**, senior or specifically valued qualifications, potentially including exemption from certain standard requirements, such as the general prior work experience threshold that presents a real obstacle for many recent graduates, covered in the [work visa guide](/blog/china-work-visa-z-visa-for-graduates).

**Lower categories generally apply to roles requiring less specialised qualification**, and may carry more restrictions or a more standard, less flexible process.

**Which category applies to a specific person and role depends on a combination of factors**, rather than a single simple threshold, understanding roughly where you would fall helps you interpret what a specific job offer's sponsorship process actually involves.

## Points-based assessment factors

Some categories are assessed using a points-based framework, broadly analogous in spirit to points-based immigration systems used in some other countries, though the specific mechanics, weightings and thresholds are distinct to this system and worth understanding on their own terms rather than assuming direct equivalence.

Salary level relative to local benchmarks is commonly a significant factor, a higher offered salary generally contributes positively.

Educational qualification level matters, generally with higher degrees contributing more favourably, though the specific weighting depends on the framework in force.

Age is a factor in some assessments, generally with certain age ranges scoring more favourably than others.

Chinese language ability, where relevant, can contribute to the overall assessment in some frameworks, another reason the [language ability question](/blog/china-mandarin-requirements-for-jobs) is worth taking seriously even where a specific role does not strictly require fluency for the work itself.

Specific in-demand skills or fields, and in some cases the reputation tier of your educational institution, may also factor into certain assessments.

The specific points thresholds and exact weightings are set by policy and reviewed periodically, this is genuinely not the kind of detail to rely on a general article for, verify current, specific criteria directly with the relevant authority or a qualified immigration advisor before making any decision based on where you expect to fall.

Applications run through a national online system — the Foreigner's Work Permit Service System (外国人来华工作管理服务系统) — where your employer submits the sponsoring application and you're assessed against the current published points table. Knowing this system's existence by name is useful mainly so you can ask your employer directly "have you submitted through the national work permit system before," a concrete question that tests real experience rather than a general assurance.

**A higher offered salary**, a higher degree level, and other factors within the points framework can meaningfully improve your position, worth understanding this if you are weighing between two offers that differ in these respects, beyond simply comparing take-home pay.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between the work permit categories?

Category A (high-end talent) carries the most favourable treatment and can exempt applicants from requirements like the standard prior-experience threshold; Category B (professional talent) is where most graduate hires land, typically requiring a bachelor's degree plus relevant experience; Category C (ordinary personnel) is for lower-skill, quota-controlled roles. Verify current specific distinctions directly, since exact thresholds are reviewed periodically.

### Does a points-based system apply to every applicant?

Certain categories use a points-based assessment considering factors like salary, education, age and language ability, worth checking which specific category and process would apply to your own situation.

### Can a higher salary improve my chances?

In frameworks that weight salary as a points factor, yes, generally, though this is one factor among several rather than the sole determinant.

### Does my degree from abroad count the same as a Chinese degree?

Educational qualification level is generally a relevant factor, the specific treatment of foreign versus domestic qualifications is worth verifying directly with the relevant authority for your situation.

### How do I find out which category I would fall into?

Ask a prospective employer with genuine experience sponsoring foreign staff, and consult a qualified immigration advisor for a specific, informed assessment of your own circumstances.

### Where do I find current accurate information on point thresholds?

The relevant national and local labour and immigration authorities publish current official guidance, and a qualified immigration advisor can assess your specific situation directly, both are more reliable than a general summary.
