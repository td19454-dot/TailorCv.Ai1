## Key Takeaways

- **English is an official language** and the working language of business, which makes Malta one of only two EU states fully accessible to English speakers alongside Ireland.
- **Online gaming (iGaming) is the standout industry**, employing a very large share of the island's international workforce.
- **Financial services, maritime registration, aviation and technology** are the other pillars.
- Malta is **tiny** — around half a million people — so the market is concentrated and networks form immediately.
- **Costs and housing have risen substantially** with the influx of international workers, which is the main constraint.

## Why Malta has this economy

A country this small should not host industries of this scale, and the reasons are deliberate policy.

**English as an official language**, a legacy of British administration, combined with EU membership.

**Regulatory positioning.** Malta was among the first EU states to build a comprehensive regulatory framework for online gaming, and the Malta Gaming Authority licence became a European standard. Operators clustered accordingly.

**Financial services regulation** similarly attracted funds, insurance and payments businesses.

**Ship and aircraft registration.** Malta operates one of the world's largest ship registries and a substantial aircraft register, with the professional services around them.

**Favourable corporate arrangements** which attracted international structures.

**And a small, educated, multilingual workforce**, supplemented heavily by international arrivals.

**The result:** a country of half a million with industries employing tens of thousands of international professionals, which is genuinely unusual.

## The sectors

| **iGaming** | Online casino and sportsbook operators, platform providers, affiliates — the largest international employer |
| **Financial services** | Funds, insurance, payments, banking and the professional services around them |
| **Maritime** | One of the world's largest ship registries, plus yachting and superyacht services |

**iGaming dominates the international employment picture.** Operators, platform suppliers, payment providers, affiliates and the regulatory and compliance ecosystem around them employ a very large number of people, overwhelmingly international.

**The roles are varied** — customer support in many languages, compliance and AML, technology, [marketing](/blog/resume-matching-marketing) and CRM, data and analytics, [finance](/blog/resume-matching-insurance-jobs), and operations.

**A note on the sector:** online gaming is regulated and reputationally mixed, and operators vary enormously in conduct and quality. Research a specific [employer](/blog/how-recruiters-spot-generic-resumes) rather than the industry, and be clear-eyed about which end of it you are joining.

## Language and accessibility

**English and Maltese are both official**, and English is the working language of business, government and higher education.

**Which makes Malta genuinely accessible.** For an English-speaking [graduate](/blog/first-resume-for-gen-z-2026) wanting EU access, Malta sits alongside Ireland as one of only two fully open EU labour markets.

**Italian is widely understood** given proximity and television.

**Other European languages are valuable** in iGaming, where customer-facing roles serve markets across Europe and multilingual staff are actively recruited.

**Maltese is not required** for professional work, though it matters socially over the long term.

## Money, tax and cost of living

The financial picture.

**[Salaries](/blog/how-to-negotiate-salary-offer) are moderate** by EU standards — above Southern European levels, below Northern European ones, with iGaming and financial services paying above the national average.

**Tax is progressive**, and Malta operates a remittance basis for individuals resident but not domiciled there, meaning foreign income not remitted to Malta is generally outside the Maltese net. For someone with foreign-source income this is significant.

**Highly qualified persons rules** offer a flat rate on employment income for qualifying senior roles in defined sectors including gaming, financial services and aviation, subject to a minimum income threshold.

**Social security contributions** apply on both sides at moderate rates.

**[Housing](/blog/remote-job-search-guide) is the constraint.** Rents in Sliema, St Julian's and the surrounding areas rose substantially with the international influx, and this is now the main pressure on the Maltese proposition.

**Living outside the main corridor** — Gozo, the south, the inland towns — is considerably cheaper and the island is small enough to commute across.

**Everyday costs are moderate**, with imported goods more expensive as on any island.

**And [healthcare](/blog/first-job-abroad-practical-checklist) is good**, publicly funded and free at the point of use for residents.

## Working culture and daily life

Worth calibrating.

**Workplaces are international.** In iGaming particularly, a team of fifteen may hold twelve nationalities, and English is the working language throughout.

**[Hierarchies](/blog/hr-round-interview-questions-answers) are moderate** and the register is informal in the international sector.

**Hours are standard** in most professional roles.

**The island is genuinely small.** You will encounter the same people repeatedly, professional and social circles overlap heavily, and reputation travels quickly.

**Summer is very hot** and the island is crowded with visitors.

**Transport is limited.** Buses exist and most people drive, with traffic being a persistent complaint.

**Gozo** is the quieter sister island, reachable by ferry, and increasingly popular with remote workers.

**And the international community is large and transient** — easy to join and constantly turning over, which is the standard small-market pattern.

## Where roles are advertised

Malta's channels.

**[LinkedIn](/blog/linkedin-profile-optimization-guide) is dominant** for the international sector, iGaming and financial services.

**Keepmeposted and Jobsplus** carry domestic listings, the latter being the public employment service.

**Company [career](/blog/career-change-to-tech-guide) pages** for the gaming operators and financial services firms, which recruit continuously.

**Specialist iGaming [recruiters](/blog/how-recruiters-read-resumes)** are very active and worth engaging with directly — the sector uses agencies heavily.

**SiGMA and the industry events** bring the gaming sector together and function as genuine [networking](/blog/networking-tips-for-job-search) venues.

**Malta Gaming Authority's licensee register** is a useful list of who actually operates there, and it is public.

**And [referrals](/blog/networking-messages-for-referrals)**, which on an island this size move [applications](/blog/how-many-jobs-should-you-apply-to-per-day) materially.

## Building a career from Malta

Where it leads.

**iGaming [experience](/blog/resume-matching-experienced-professionals) is portable** within the industry — Malta, Gibraltar, the Isle of Man, Cyprus, Curaçao and increasingly regulated markets across Europe all use the same [skills](/blog/resume-skills-match-job-description).

**Compliance and AML experience transfers broadly** across European financial services, since the regulatory framework is EU-derived.

**Technology roles** in gaming build high-availability, payments and real-time systems experience that transfers to fintech generally.

**Financial services and fund administration** transfers to Luxembourg, Ireland and the other EU centres.

**EU membership matters** — long-term residence eventually brings mobility across the union.

**And many people treat Malta as a defined period** — a few years building sector expertise and savings, then moving on with a portable specialism. That is a well-worn and sensible pattern rather than a failure to settle.

## Practical setup

The administrative sequence.

**Single permit** application covering both residence and work for third-country nationals, submitted through Identita with your employer.

**EU and EEA nationals** register rather than applying for permission, which is a fundamentally simpler position.

**Social security registration** and a social security number.

**Tax registration** and a tax number.

**A local bank account**, which is known for being slower and more document-heavy in Malta than in most of Europe.

**Health entitlement** through the public system where you qualify, with private cover being common.

**Housing**, where costs have risen substantially and the market moves quickly.

**And an ID card**, which underpins most administrative interaction on the island.

## Living on a small island

Considerations that genuinely affect whether people stay.

**Malta is very small**, and this is the single factor that decides whether people stay past their first contract.

**Everything is close** and the convenience is real.

**The social scene is international** and heavily weighted toward the gaming and finance sectors.

**Housing costs have risen sharply** and the rental market is competitive.

**Traffic is a genuine daily problem** given the population density.

**English is an official language**, which removes the barrier that most European destinations present.

**The climate and the sea** are consistently what people cite as the compensation.

**And island fatigue is real** — a widely discussed phenomenon where the smallness that felt charming becomes constraining, usually somewhere in the second year.

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: You speak English and want EU access

Malta belongs on your list with Ireland. English is official and the working language of business, the island is an EU member, and the international sector is disproportionately large.

### Scenario 2: You speak English plus another European language

iGaming operators recruit specifically for language combinations to serve European markets, in customer support, CRM, marketing and account management. It is a direct and accessible route in.

### Scenario 3: You have compliance or AML experience

Both iGaming and financial services are heavily regulated and compliance capability is in continuous demand. It is one of the most reliable professional entries into the Maltese market.

> **Tip:** If you speak English plus another European language, Malta's iGaming sector will hire you quickly. It recruits continuously for market-facing roles across Europe, and the language combination frequently matters more than the specific background.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Consider Malta alongside Ireland for EU access
- Use a second European language in iGaming
- Research specific employers and their regulatory standing
- Look at compliance and AML roles
- Research housing before accepting
- Understand the island's scale honestly

### Don't

- Assume a small island means a small international market
- Treat all gaming operators as equivalent
- Underestimate how much housing costs have risen
- Expect a broad market outside the main sectors
- Assume Maltese is required for professional work
- Ignore the maritime and aviation registers

- **Dismissing Malta as too small.** Its international sectors employ tens of thousands.
- **Treating iGaming as one thing.** Operators vary enormously in conduct and quality.
- **Underestimating housing.** Costs rose substantially with the international influx.
- **Ignoring compliance roles.** The most reliable professional entry into both main sectors.
- **Overlooking maritime and aviation.** Malta runs major international registries.
- **Expecting breadth.** Outside the main sectors the market is genuinely thin.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is English enough to work in Malta?

Yes. English is an official language and the working language of business, government and higher education, making Malta one of only two fully English-accessible EU labour markets alongside Ireland.

### What is iGaming?

Online gaming — casino and sportsbook operators, platform providers, payment processors and affiliates. Malta hosts one of Europe's largest concentrations and it is the main international employer.

### Is the gaming sector reputable?

It is regulated by the Malta Gaming Authority and operators vary substantially in conduct and quality. Research the specific employer rather than judging the sector as a whole.

### What else does Malta do?

Financial services including funds and payments, one of the world's largest ship registries, aircraft registration and leasing, technology, tourism and pharmaceutical manufacturing.

### Is Malta expensive?

Costs and particularly housing have risen substantially with the influx of international workers, and this is now the main constraint on the island's proposition.

### Do I need Maltese?

Not for professional work. English covers business, government and higher education. Maltese matters socially over a longer period.
### How does tax work in Malta?

Progressive, with a remittance basis for residents who are not domiciled there — foreign income not remitted is generally outside the Maltese net. Highly qualified persons rules offer a flat rate for qualifying senior roles in defined sectors.

### Is Malta expensive?

Housing in the Sliema and St Julian's corridor rose substantially with the international influx and is the main constraint. Gozo, the south and inland towns are considerably cheaper.
### Where should I look for jobs in Malta?

LinkedIn for the international sector, Keepmeposted and Jobsplus for domestic listings, company career pages for gaming operators, and specialist iGaming recruiters who are very active in this market.

### Does Maltese experience travel?

Yes. iGaming skills transfer across regulated gaming jurisdictions, compliance and AML transfer across EU financial services, and gaming technology experience transfers to fintech generally.
### What does setting up in Malta involve?

A single permit covering residence and work for third-country nationals through Identita, social security and tax registration, a bank account which is notably slow locally, and an ID card.

### What is living in Malta actually like?

Very small, extremely convenient, with an international social scene weighted toward gaming and finance, rising housing costs, real traffic problems, and English as an official language.
