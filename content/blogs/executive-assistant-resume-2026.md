## Key Takeaways

- Say exactly who you supported - "CEO and 3 VPs of a 400-person SaaS company" - because the level of the executive sets your level.
- Executive assistant resumes stand out with judgement: decisions you made, problems you handled before they reached your executive, and projects you owned.
- Count the complex work: international travel, board meetings, events, budgets.
- Confidentiality and discretion matter - show them through what you handled, not with adjectives.

An executive assistant resume is different from an admin resume: it has to show you act as a force multiplier for a senior leader. Hiring managers look for who you supported, how complex their world was, and where you used your own judgement. "Managed the CEO's calendar" is expected; "Protected 8 hours a week of the CEO's focus time by redesigning the meeting cadence with her leadership team" gets you the interview.

## What the job ad is really asking for

| Job ad says | What it means for your resume |
|---|---|
| "Support the CEO / C-suite" | Name the role(s) and company size in your first bullet. |
| "Complex calendar and travel management" | Time zones, countries and trips per month. |
| "Board meetings and executive events" | Board packs, offsites or events you ran, with size. |
| "Exercise discretion and sound judgement" | A decision you made on your own or a sensitive matter you handled. |

## Bullet Rewrites

**Before:** Managed the CEO's calendar.
**After:** Managed the CEO's calendar across 5 time zones, protecting 8 hours a week of focus time by redesigning the leadership meeting cadence.

**Before:** Booked travel.
**After:** Planned 120+ domestic and international trips a year for 3 executives, saving $40K through advance booking and a preferred-airline agreement.

**Before:** Helped with board meetings.
**After:** Coordinated 4 board meetings a year, preparing board packs with the CFO and managing logistics for 12 directors.

**Before:** Organised events.
**After:** Ran 3 company offsites for up to 150 people, delivering each under a $250K budget.

**Before:** Handled confidential information.
**After:** Coordinated confidential documents and interviews during an acquisition, working with legal and HR on a need-to-know basis.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Not naming the executive's level.** "Supported leadership" undersells you.
2. **Duty lists.** Show judgement and outcomes, not tasks.
3. **Adjectives for discretion.** Show it through what you were trusted with.
4. **Ignoring tools.** Many EA ads filter for Notion, Asana, Concur or similar.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How is an executive assistant resume different from an administrative assistant resume?
An EA resume shows support for senior leaders and the judgement that comes with it - board work, complex travel, confidential projects. An admin resume focuses on office and team support.

### Should I name the executive I supported?
Name the role and company ("CEO of a 400-person SaaS company") rather than the person, unless they are publicly known and you have their permission.

### How long should an executive assistant resume be?
One page for under 8 years of experience, two pages for senior EAs with a long record of C-suite support.
