`yourname.com` reads very differently on a resume than a long, generic subdomain. A custom domain is one of the cheapest, highest-leverage upgrades you can make to a [portfolio](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-build-professional-portfolio), and it takes under an hour to set up. Pair this with [How to Build a Professional Portfolio](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-build-professional-portfolio) and [Portfolio Domain Name Guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/portfolio-domain-name-guide).

## Key Takeaways

- A custom domain enhances credibility, memorability, and longevity for your portfolio.
- Choose a domain that is your name, short, easy to spell, and preferably a `.com`.
- Use reputable registrars for domain purchase and expect to pay around $10-15/year.
- Follow a straightforward process to connect your custom domain to your portfolio, including DNS settings and SSL confirmation.
- Avoid common mistakes like not linking your domain or forgetting to update your resume and online profiles.

## Why It Matters
| Reason | What it gets you |
|---|---|
| **Credibility** | a custom domain reads as more established than a free subdomain |
| **Memorability** | `yourname.com` is easier for a recruiter to type from memory or a printed resume than a long generated URL |
| **Longevity** | your domain stays yours even if you switch which platform or builder hosts the site behind it |
| **Email matching** | you can pair it with a matching professional email address later if you choose |

## Choosing Your Domain
1. **Use your name if it is available** - `firstlastname.com` is the safest, most professional choice
2. **Keep it short and easy to spell over the phone** - avoid hyphens, numbers, or uncommon spellings
3. **Prefer `.com`** where available; `.dev`, `.me`, or `.io` are acceptable alternatives for tech roles specifically
4. **Buy from a reputable registrar** (Namecheap, Google Domains successor, Cloudflare) - expect to pay roughly $10-15/year for a standard `.com`

## Common Mistakes
- Buying a domain and never connecting it, leaving your resume linking to a placeholder page
- Choosing a cute or clever domain that is hard to spell or remember under pressure
- Forgetting to update the link on your resume, LinkedIn, and email signature after switching domains
- Letting the domain lapse after a year because auto-renewal was not enabled - see [Portfolio Checklist Before Applying](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/portfolio-checklist-before-applying)

## Pro Tips
- Set a calendar reminder a month before your domain renews, even with auto-renewal enabled, in case a payment method expires
- If your exact name is taken, try a middle initial, a hyphen, or adding your profession (`janedoedesign.com`) rather than an unrelated domain
- Keep the free `thetailorcv.com` portfolio link as a backup while your custom domain's DNS is propagating, so you are never without a working link

## Step-by-Step Guide to Setting Up Your Custom Domain

1. **Select Your Domain Name**: Choose a domain name that reflects your personal brand or the nature of your work. Use a name generator if you’re stuck. Aim for a name that is short, memorable, and easy to spell.

2. **Register Your Domain**: Use a reputable registrar like GoDaddy or Namecheap. Follow their prompts to complete the registration process. Make sure to purchase domain privacy protection if you want to keep your personal information private.

3. **Choose a Hosting Provider**: If your portfolio platform doesn’t include hosting, select a hosting service like Bluehost or SiteGround. Ensure they offer easy integration with your portfolio platform.

4. **Link Your Domain to Your Hosting Provider**: 
   - Log into your domain registrar’s account.
   - Find the DNS settings and replace the nameservers with those provided by your hosting provider.
   - Save changes, which may take up to 48 hours to propagate.

5. **Set Up Your Portfolio Platform**: If you're using a platform like WordPress, Wix, or Squarespace, go to the settings and find the option to connect your custom domain. Follow their instructions, which usually involve entering your domain name and verifying ownership.

6. **Test Your Domain**: Once everything is set up, go to your custom domain in a web browser to confirm it displays your portfolio correctly. 

7. **Optimize for SEO**: Utilize SEO best practices by adding relevant keywords to your portfolio content, optimizing images with alt tags, and ensuring your site loads quickly.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Choosing a Complicated Domain Name**: Avoid names that are difficult to spell or remember. Simplicity is key to ensuring visitors can easily find your portfolio.

- **Neglecting Mobile Optimization**: Ensure your portfolio is [mobile-friendly](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/portfolio-website-themes-2026). Use responsive design templates that look great on all devices, as many users will access your site via smartphones.

- **Ignoring Content Updates**: Regularly update your portfolio with new projects or achievements. An outdated portfolio can give potential employers the wrong impression of your skills.

- **Overlooking Security**: Implement an SSL certificate for your custom domain to ensure that the data exchanged between your visitors and your site is secure. This is especially important if you collect any personal information.

## Do's and Don'ts for a Successful Portfolio Domain
