## Round 1: The Price Depends Where You Are

Here is something most comparison articles get wrong. They quote one price, usually in dollars, as though it were universal. It is not.

Opening Novoresume's pricing page from India returns **₹1,299/month, ₹2,499/quarter, ₹8,499/year**. Open it from elsewhere and you will see different figures in a different currency. The plan structure stays the same; the numbers do not.

**TailorCV is region-split too**, and we would rather state it than hide it: **₹1,999/year in India** (₹299/month, ₹149/week), **$24.99/year** on lower-income-country pricing, and **$59.99/year** globally.

So if you are reading this in India, the comparison is ₹8,499/year against ₹1,999/year — and that is a real gap. If you are reading it elsewhere, check both pricing pages from your own connection rather than trusting any article's numbers, including this one. That is the honest advice.

One thing in Novoresume's favour: **it is pay-once**. Their pricing page says it explicitly — no recurring billing, the period ends and you are not charged again. Ours is a subscription you cancel. For someone who needs a tool for one application round, pay-once is the friendlier model and we will not pretend otherwise.

## Round 2: The One-Page Cap

Novoresume's Basic plan is free and unlimited in duration, which sounds generous until you read the limits:

- **Single version** — one resume, not several
- **Max 1-page resume** — a hard cap
- Predefined layouts only
- No cover letter
- 3 fonts, 30 colour themes

Premium lifts these to multiple versions, up to 10 pages, custom layouts, a matching cover letter, 12 fonts and 74 themes.

The one-page cap is fine for a fresher. It is a problem the moment you have five years of experience, and our [resume length guide](/blog/ats-resume-length-guide) explains why forcing a senior résumé onto one page usually means cutting the evidence that would have got the interview.

The **single-version** limit is the bigger one for this comparison. If you are applying to more than one kind of role, you need more than one resume — that is the entire premise of tailoring. On the free plan, you cannot do it.

## Round 3: A Builder, Not a Matcher

This is the honest structural difference. Novoresume is a document builder: it helps you produce a well-designed resume, and its templates and layout controls are good at that.

It is not a job-match tool. It does not take a job description, score your resume against it, and tell you which of the posting's requirements you have failed to evidence. That is not a criticism — it is not trying to do that.

TailorCV starts there. You paste the posting, upload your resume, and get an [ATS match score](/ats-analysis) that names the gaps. Then it rewrites your bullets to carry the missing language *only where your experience supports it* — the rule is that it never claims a skill your resume shows no evidence for. Our [guide to matching a resume to a job description](/blog/how-to-match-resume-keywords-to-job-description) covers the method if you want to do it by hand instead.

If your resume is not getting callbacks, the question is usually not "does this look good" but "does this match what they asked for." Those need different tools.

## Round 4: One Resume vs a Version Per Job

Novoresume's model assumes one resume you maintain. TailorCV's assumes the opposite — that each posting deserves its own version, because postings differ more than people expect.

We keep every version in [My Resumes](/my-resumes), and the [Chrome extension](/extension) generates one from the job page directly, so you are not copying descriptions between tabs. The [tailored vs generic comparison](/blog/tailored-vs-generic-resume) has the reasoning behind why this matters more than most people assume.

If you send three applications a month, one good resume is genuinely enough and Novoresume serves you well. At twenty applications, version management becomes the job.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does Novoresume have an ATS score checker?**
Not a job-match scorer of the kind we are describing. It builds ATS-friendly documents, but it does not score your resume against a specific job description and list what is missing.

**Is Novoresume really free?**
Yes, permanently — but capped at one page and one version, with no cover letter. For a fresher's first resume that is often enough. For anything more, you will hit the ceiling quickly.

**Why do I see a different price than this article quotes?**
Because Novoresume prices by region. The figures here (₹1,299/₹2,499/₹8,499) are what an Indian connection returns. Check from your own country — this is exactly why we flagged it rather than quoting one number.

**Is pay-once better than a subscription?**
For a short job hunt, often yes. If your search runs several months, compare total cost over the period you will actually be applying, not the sticker price.

**Can I use both?**
Yes. Build in Novoresume, then run the result through our [free ATS scan](/ats-analysis) against a real posting to see how it matches. That costs nothing and tells you whether the document is the problem or the match is.

## Putting It All Together

Novoresume builds a good-looking resume and charges you once for it. That is a clean proposition, and for a student with a single page of experience and no interest in subscriptions, it is a sensible choice.

The limits that decide it are the one-page cap and the single version. Both are fine for a first resume and both become obstacles the moment you have real experience or are applying to genuinely different roles.

If you want to know which side of that line you are on, the quickest test is to [scan your current resume against a posting you care about](/ats-analysis) and look at what comes back missing.
