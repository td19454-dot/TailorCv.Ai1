## Key Takeaways

- NHS applications use a structured form, not a CV — the supporting statement is what shortlisting actually scores.
- Shortlisting is scored against the person specification, so the statement must address each criterion explicitly.
- Agenda for Change bands 1-9 set pay nationally, with London weighting on top; the band is fixed, not negotiated.
- A large share of NHS roles are non-clinical — IT, finance, administration, analytics, estates.
- Roles are posted on NHS Jobs and trust websites, largely invisible on commercial job boards.

The NHS employs well over a million people and hires continuously, yet applications from strong candidates fail routinely — almost always for the same reason. People submit a CV-style application to a process that scores against a published checklist, and never address the checklist.

The good news is that the checklist is public. Every NHS vacancy publishes a person specification, and shortlisting scores you against it. Once you know that, the application becomes mechanical.

This guide covers the form, the bands, and the statement.

## The person specification is the marking scheme

Every NHS vacancy publishes one, split into criteria marked **Essential** or **Desirable**, usually grouped under headings like Qualifications, Experience, Skills and Knowledge.

**Shortlisting scores your application against those criteria, one by one.** A panel member works down the list deciding whether you have evidenced each.

Which produces the single most important rule: **fail to evidence an essential criterion and you are rejected, regardless of how strong you are otherwise.** Not because the panel doubts you — because they cannot score what you did not write.

**How to use it:** copy the person specification into a document. Under each criterion, write the evidence that proves you meet it. That document becomes your supporting statement. The approach mirrors [how to tailor your resume for every job](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-tailor-resume-for-every-job), applied to a scored form.

## The supporting statement

The free-text section, and the part that decides shortlisting. Typically several hundred to a couple of thousand words.

**Structure it against the person specification.** Use its own headings. A panel scoring you should be able to find each criterion without hunting.

> **Experience of managing a caseload**
> In my current Band 5 role at [Trust] I hold a caseload of approximately 25 patients across two wards, prioritising daily against acuity and discharge planning...

**Mirror their language.** If the specification says "evidence of quality improvement activity", use that phrase rather than a synonym. This is a literal scoring exercise, not a search algorithm.

**Cover desirable criteria too**, briefly. Where several applicants meet all essentials, desirables separate them.

**Use real examples with numbers.** Caseload sizes, team sizes, budgets, waiting time reductions, audit results — see [how to quantify achievements on your resume](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-quantify-achievements-on-resume).

**Do not write a covering letter.** A flowing narrative about your passion for the NHS scores nothing. The structured version feels mechanical and scores well, which is the only thing that matters at this stage.

## Agenda for Change bands

Pay is set nationally by band, from Band 1 up to Band 9, with incremental points within each band and additional weighting for London and the surrounding area.

Roughly:

- **Bands 2-4** — healthcare assistants, administrative and support roles
- **Band 5** — newly qualified nurses, allied health professionals, many analyst roles
- **Band 6** — experienced practitioners, specialist roles, junior management
- **Band 7** — senior specialists, team leaders, advanced practitioners
- **Bands 8a-8d** — senior management and consultant-level non-medical roles
- **Band 9** — director level

Medical and dental staff sit on separate contracts entirely.

**The band is not negotiable.** Unlike private-sector hiring, you cannot negotiate salary — it is nationally set. What is occasionally negotiable is your **starting increment point** within the band if you bring relevant prior experience. That is the conversation to have, not a salary one — a genuine difference from [UK salary negotiation](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/uk-salary-negotiation-guide) in the private sector.

**The pension is a substantial part of the package** and is frequently undervalued by applicants comparing NHS pay to private offers.

## Non-clinical roles

Consistently overlooked, and a large share of NHS employment.

**Digital and IT.** The NHS runs enormous systems — electronic patient records, imaging, data platforms. Real scale, and a chronic skills shortage.

**Data and analytics.** Performance reporting, population health, informatics. Growing quickly.

**Finance.** Management accounting, costing, procurement.

**Administration and management.** Service management, patient pathway coordination, business support.

**Estates and facilities.** Engineering, maintenance, capital projects.

**HR, communications, governance.** As in any large organisation.

For technologists in particular, NHS roles offer scale, stability, a strong pension, and better hours than most private-sector equivalents — at lower headline pay.

## The process

1. **Find the vacancy** on NHS Jobs or a trust's own website. Commercial boards carry very little of it — see [the best job sites in the UK](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/job-sites-uk).
2. **Complete the application form** — employment history with no gaps, qualifications, references.
3. **Write the supporting statement** against the person specification.
4. **Shortlisting** against that specification.
5. **Interview**, usually panel-based and structured, frequently with values-based questions — see [UK interview tips](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/uk-interview-tips).
6. **Conditional offer**, then pre-employment checks — references, occupational health, DBS check, and professional registration verification.

**Pre-employment checks take weeks.** Budget for it; the gap between offer and start is longer than in the private sector.

**Account for every gap** in your employment history. The form asks, and unexplained gaps are queried — see [how to explain an employment gap](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-explain-employment-gap-on-resume-2026).

## Common Mistakes

**Submitting a CV-style personal statement.** Shortlisting scores against published criteria, and a flowing narrative about your passion for the NHS scores nothing against them.

**Missing an essential criterion.** The panel cannot score what you did not write, so an unaddressed essential means rejection regardless of your actual suitability.

**Paraphrasing the person specification.** Scoring is literal — use their wording where it is genuinely true of you rather than a synonym the panel is not looking for.

**Skipping the desirable criteria.** When several candidates meet every essential, desirables are what separate them.

**Trying to negotiate the salary.** Bands are set nationally and are not negotiable; the starting increment point sometimes is.

**Undervaluing the pension.** It is a substantial part of NHS total reward and is routinely omitted when comparing against private-sector offers.

**Searching commercial job boards.** NHS Jobs and trust websites carry the vacancies; general boards carry very little of it.

**Not planning for pre-employment checks.** References, occupational health, DBS and registration verification take weeks between offer and start.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a CV for NHS jobs?

Generally not — NHS applications use a structured form with a supporting statement. Keep a CV for other applications, but the form is what is scored here.

### What is a supporting statement?

The free-text section where you evidence that you meet each criterion in the person specification. It is what shortlisting scores, so structure it under their headings.

### What are Agenda for Change bands?

The national NHS pay structure, Bands 1 to 9, with increments within each band and additional weighting for London. Medical and dental staff are on separate contracts.

### Can I negotiate NHS pay?

Not the band itself, which is nationally set. Your starting increment point within the band is sometimes negotiable if you bring relevant experience.

### Are there non-clinical NHS jobs?

Many — digital, data, finance, administration, estates, HR. The NHS is a very large organisation and employs the same professional functions as any other.

### How long does NHS recruitment take?

Weeks to months, with pre-employment checks — references, occupational health, DBS, registration — adding significant time between offer and start.

### Where are NHS jobs advertised?

Primarily NHS Jobs and individual trust websites. Commercial job boards carry a small fraction of the vacancies.

## Make This Practical

Copy the person specification into a document before writing anything. Put each criterion as a heading, then write your evidence underneath it — that document *is* your supporting statement, and building it this way makes missing an essential criterion almost impossible.

Then use their language rather than your own. Scoring is literal, so mirror the phrasing of each criterion where it genuinely applies to you, cover the desirable criteria briefly as well, and put real numbers against caseloads, team sizes, budgets and improvements.

Finally, plan the timeline and look wider than clinical roles. Budget weeks for pre-employment checks between offer and start, account for every gap in your employment history on the form, weigh the pension when comparing against private offers, and search NHS Jobs and trust websites directly rather than commercial boards.
