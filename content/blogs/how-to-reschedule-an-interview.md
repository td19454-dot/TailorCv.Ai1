- The single most important rule is to reach out the moment you know there is a conflict.
- You do not owe a detailed explanation - a brief, honest, professional reason is enough.
- Propose two or three specific alternative slots rather than a vague "sometime next week".

- Rescheduling buys you extra preparation time, so use it rather than just deferring.

Sometimes life gets in the way of an [interview](https://www.thetailorcv.com/interview-prep) — an illness, a family emergency, an unavoidable work conflict, or a scheduling clash between two opportunities. The good news is that rescheduling is usually fine as long as you handle it professionally and promptly. Done poorly, though, it can read as unreliable and cost you the opportunity. This guide shows you how to reschedule gracefully and keep the employer's confidence.

Rescheduling well is really about communication and respect for the interviewer's time — the same qualities the interview itself is testing. Handle it right and you can even reinforce a good impression. ## Do it as early as possible

The single most important rule: the moment you know there's a conflict, reach out. A last-minute cancellation reads as careless and disrespectful of the interviewer's schedule; early notice reads as considerate and organized. If it's more than a day away, email is ideal. If it's within a few hours, call if you have a number, then follow up in writing. Speed signals that you take the opportunity seriously.

## Keep the reason brief and honest

You don't owe a detailed explanation. A simple, professional reason is enough — "a personal emergency," "an unavoidable work conflict," or "a family matter." Don't over-share or invent an elaborate story; brevity is more professional and more believable. This is the same honesty principle that runs through a good [resume](https://www.thetailorcv.com/modify-cv) and [interview answers](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/behavioral-interview-questions-and-answers) — keep it truthful and move forward.

## Offer specific alternatives

Make rescheduling easy for them by proposing two or three specific new time slots rather than a vague "sometime next week." This shows you're still eager and respects their time by minimizing back-and-forth. Offer a range across a couple of days so they can pick what works. The goal is to make saying "yes, let's do Thursday at 2" as effortless as possible.

## Reschedule email template

> **Subject:** Interview reschedule request — [Your Name], [Role]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Thank you again for the opportunity to interview for [Role] — I'm genuinely looking forward to it. Unfortunately, an unavoidable [conflict/personal matter] has come up for our scheduled time on [date/time], and I'd like to request a reschedule.
>
> I'm available [option 1], [option 2], or [option 3], and I'm happy to work around your calendar. I apologize for any inconvenience, and I appreciate your flexibility.
>
> Best regards,
> [Your Name]

Keep the tone warm, appreciative, and confident — not apologetic to the point of sounding like you expect to be dropped.


## Special case: rescheduling for a competing offer

If you're rescheduling because you're juggling another opportunity, never say so — simply cite a scheduling conflict and offer alternatives. Managing multiple processes is normal; the [negotiating multiple job offers guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/negotiating-multiple-job-offers) covers how to handle the bigger picture without burning bridges. Stay professional with every employer, because timelines shift and you want all options open.

## After you reschedule: prepare and reconfirm

Rescheduling gives you extra time — use it well. [Research the company](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-research-company-before-interview) more deeply, refine your [STAR stories](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/interview-story-bank-star-method), run a [free AI mock interview](https://www.thetailorcv.com/mock-interview), and plan what to bring and what to wear. Send a brief confirmation once the new time is set, and treat the rescheduled interview with extra care — arrive early (or test your tech for a video interview) and bring your best. Afterward, a prompt thank-you email reinforces your professionalism.

## What not to do

- **Don't go silent** and simply miss the interview — it's the fastest way to be dropped.
- **Don't reschedule more than once** unless truly unavoidable; repeated changes signal unreliability.
- **Don't over-apologize** or grovel — one clean, professional note is enough.
- **Don't invent a dramatic excuse** — brief and honest is stronger.

## Reschedule checklist

To reschedule without losing ground:

- **Reach out as early as possible**, in writing (and by call if it's imminent).
- Give a **brief, honest reason**.
- **Offer two or three specific alternatives**.
- **Reconfirm** the new time and prepare thoroughly with a [mock interview](https://www.thetailorcv.com/mock-interview).
