## Key Takeaways

- Ashby is a newer ATS, popular particularly with startups, with generally solid PDF handling but a strict requirement for exact standard section headings.
- A non-standard section heading — "Career History" instead of "Work Experience" — can cause that entire section to be misclassified and effectively lost from the structured view.
- Ashby surfaces skills as structured tags extracted from your resume, and recruiters commonly search by these tags rather than full-text scanning.
- Ashby includes an AI-assisted review feature that evaluates your resume against specific criteria and cites the exact source text as evidence — a human still makes the final decision.
- Image-based PDFs remain unreadable here as on every platform — always confirm your file's text is genuinely selectable.

Ashby is a newer applicant tracking system that has gained particular traction among startups and growth-stage companies. It behaves differently from older, more established platforms in some specific, useful-to-know ways.

## PDF Generally Works Well Here

Unlike some other platforms with specific file-format quirks, Ashby's parser is generally reported to handle PDF at least as reliably as .docx — meaning you can use PDF with more confidence here than on a platform like [Taleo](/blog/taleo-legacy-ats-what-still-breaks) or [Workday](/blog/workday-application-resume-tips), where .docx is sometimes the safer specific choice.

**The one real PDF risk that still applies:** an image-based PDF — one where the text is not genuinely selectable, often the result of certain design-tool exports — remains completely unreadable regardless of platform. Confirm your file passes the selection test: open it and try to select a paragraph of text with your cursor. If your cursor instead selects a graphic or image boundary rather than actual text, the file needs to be regenerated from a genuine text-based source like Word or Google Docs, not a design tool that flattens text into images.

## Section Headings Must Be Exact

This is the most important specific thing to know about Ashby. Its parser is reported to be strict about recognising standard section headings — and a creative or non-standard heading can cause an entire section to be misclassified, effectively dropping that content from the structured, recruiter-visible view even though the text is technically present in the file.

**Use conventional headings exactly:** "Work Experience" (not "Career History" or "Professional Journey"), "Education," "Skills." This platform's specific strictness on this point makes it worth double-checking even if you have used a creative heading successfully elsewhere.

## Skills Are Extracted as Structured Tags

A distinctive feature worth understanding: Ashby is reported to surface your skills as structured tags extracted from your resume content, and recruiters — particularly at the startups where Ashby is common — commonly search and filter candidates by these specific skill tags rather than through broader full-text keyword scanning.

This means messy or unclear skills-section extraction can mean missing tags entirely, which affects your discoverability in recruiter searches beyond just the initial application review. **Exact, clearly stated wording in your skills section matters more here than visual presentation** — a skill conveyed only through design emphasis rather than clear text is more likely to be missed by this specific tag-extraction mechanism.

## The AI-Assisted Review Feature

Ashby includes a feature sometimes described as AI-assisted application review, which is worth understanding because it works differently from a simple pass/fail scoring system. Rather than auto-rejecting or assigning a numeric score, this feature is generally reported to return a verdict — something like "meets" or "does not meet" — against specific criteria a recruiter has defined, along with cited source text from your resume as the evidence behind that verdict.

**A human reviewer is still reported to make the final advance-or-reject decision** — this feature functions as a structured summarisation and evidence-citation tool for the recruiter's benefit, rather than as an autonomous rejection mechanism. This is a meaningfully different design from a simple numeric ATS score, and it reinforces something covered generally in [improving your score without lying](/blog/improve-score-without-lying): since specific text from your resume can be directly cited as evidence for or against a criterion, having genuine, specific, defensible content matters more than ever — vague claims have nothing concrete to be cited as evidence for.

## Standard Formatting Fundamentals Still Apply

Beyond the specific points above, the general ATS-safe formatting baseline remains the safe approach: single column, no tables or text boxes, consistent date formatting (see [dates and gaps parsing errors](/blog/dates-and-gaps-parsing-errors)), and contact information in the document body rather than a header or footer (see [contact section parsing failures](/blog/contact-section-parsing-failures)).

## A Practical Ashby Checklist

1. **Use PDF with confidence**, but confirm the text is genuinely selectable, not an image.
2. **Use exact, standard section headings** — this platform is specifically strict here.
3. **Make sure your skills section clearly and explicitly states each skill as text**, since these are extracted as structured tags that drive recruiter search.
4. **Write specific, evidence-backed claims throughout your resume**, since the platform's AI-review feature can cite exact source text as evidence for a given criterion.
5. **Keep the general formatting fundamentals** — single column, standard structure, clean dates.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Ashby's AI review feature the same as an ATS auto-reject?**
No — it is generally reported to produce a verdict with cited evidence for a human recruiter to review and act on, rather than autonomously rejecting candidates itself.

**Does a non-standard section heading really cause that much of a problem on Ashby?**
Based on how the platform's parsing is generally described, yes — it can cause that entire section to be misclassified and effectively dropped from the structured view, even though a human opening your actual file would still see it.

**Should I avoid PDF for Ashby?**
No — PDF is generally fine here, unlike on some other platforms. The specific risk to watch for is an image-based PDF where the text is not genuinely selectable.

**How does the skill-tag extraction affect my application?**
It affects your discoverability in recruiter searches specifically — clear, explicit text in your skills section matters more than visual presentation for this particular mechanism.

**Is Ashby more common at certain types of companies?**
It has gained particular traction at startups and growth-stage companies, based on general reporting about its adoption patterns.
