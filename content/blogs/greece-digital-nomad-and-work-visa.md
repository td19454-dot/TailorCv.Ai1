## Key Takeaways

- **EU, EEA and Swiss citizens** need no permit — registration is the only formality.
- Greece introduced a **digital nomad visa** for remote workers employed by or contracting with companies outside Greece.
- A **tax incentive for newcomers** offers a substantial exemption on foreign-sourced or relocated employment income for a defined period.
- The **Golden Visa** grants residency through property investment and has been among Europe's most heavily marketed, with thresholds raised in high-demand areas.
- **Standard work permits** for third-country nationals involve a labour market test and are considerably harder than the nomad and investment routes.

## The routes

Greece has several, and they serve different people.

**Standard employment visa and residence permit.** Requires a Greek [employer](/blog/how-recruiters-spot-generic-resumes), a [job offer](/blog/how-to-negotiate-a-job-offer-email), and generally a labour market test confirming no suitable EU candidate is available. This is the conventional route and the hardest.

**Digital nomad visa.** For people working remotely for employers or clients outside Greece, whether employed or self-employed. An income requirement applies, assessed over a period.

**[Golden Visa](/blog/uae-golden-visa-explained).** Residency through qualifying investment, most commonly real estate. Thresholds have been raised in high-demand areas including parts of Athens, Thessaloniki and the popular islands, precisely because the scheme was contributing to [housing](/blog/remote-job-search-guide) pressure.

**Financially independent person visa**, for those with sufficient passive income.

**[EU Blue Card](/blog/germany-eu-blue-card-graduates)**, available with its own [salary](/blog/how-to-negotiate-salary-offer) threshold.

**And for [graduates](/blog/first-resume-for-gen-z-2026) of Greek institutions**, provisions exist to remain and seek work — check the current terms with the Greek authorities.

## The tax incentive

The provision that materially changes the arithmetic.

**Greece introduced a regime aimed at attracting people to [relocate](/blog/first-job-abroad-practical-checklist)**, offering a substantial exemption on a portion of employment or business income for individuals who transfer their tax residence to Greece and commit to staying for a defined period.

**Conditions apply**, including not having been Greek tax resident for a defined number of prior years, and taking up employment or self-employment in Greece.

**Separate provisions exist** for pensioners and for high-net-worth individuals with their own structures.

**The exemption runs for a defined number of years**, after which normal rates apply.

**This is genuinely significant** for someone relocating on a reasonable salary, and it is the single most useful thing to check before accepting a Greek offer.

**Verify current terms** with the Greek tax authority or a professional, because these schemes are revised and the detail matters.

## The Golden Visa, honestly

Worth a direct account because it is heavily marketed and widely misunderstood.

**It grants residency through investment**, most commonly property purchase above a threshold.

**It does not require you to live in Greece**, which is precisely why it attracted so much international interest — it is a residency-by-investment product rather than a route for people intending to work there.

**Thresholds have been raised** in Athens, Thessaloniki, Mykonos, Santorini and other high-demand areas, in response to the scheme's contribution to local housing costs.

**It does not grant work rights automatically** in the way an employment permit does, and the conditions on economic activity should be checked carefully rather than assumed.

**It leads toward long-term residence and eventually citizenship**, subject to the usual conditions including physical presence for naturalisation, which the visa itself does not require.

**For someone actually planning to work in Greece**, the employment, Blue Card or digital nomad routes are the relevant ones, and the Golden Visa is a different product for a different purpose.

## Practical application steps

The mechanics.

**[Applications](/blog/how-many-jobs-should-you-apply-to-per-day) are generally made at a Greek consulate** in your country of residence for the visa, then converted to a residence permit after arrival.

**[Documents](/blog/campus-placement-document-checklist) typically required:** passport, proof of income at the applicable level, health insurance, criminal record certificate with [apostille](/blog/international-resume-tailoring-guide), accommodation evidence, and proof of the employment or contractual relationship for the nomad route.

**Apostilles and certified translations** are the slowest element, as everywhere.

**An AFM tax number** is needed early and can be obtained through a representative if you are not yet in Greece.

**After arrival**, the residence permit application goes through the relevant migration authority with a biometrics appointment.

**[Processing times](/blog/how-long-to-hear-back-after-applying) vary** and Greek administration is not fast, so plan generously.

**Renewals** are applied for before expiry with the conditions continuing to be met.

## Toward long-term status

**Permanent residence** follows a qualifying period of legal residence.

**EU long-term residence** is available with its own conditions and mobility rights across the union.

**Citizenship** requires a longer residence period plus a Greek language and civics examination, and the [language requirement](/blog/bilingual-language-skills-on-resume) is the binding constraint for most people.

**Physical presence matters** for naturalisation in a way it does not for investment-based residency, which is why Golden Visa holders who never live in Greece do not progress toward citizenship.

**And continuity counts**, so track absences if long-term status is the objective.

## Comparing Greece to its Mediterranean peers

If you are weighing the southern European options.

**Against Portugal.** Portugal's international community is larger and longer established, and its citizenship timeline shorter. Greece has the stronger specific industry in shipping and comparable costs.

**Against Spain.** Spain's economy is far larger with more actual jobs; Greece is cheaper outside Athens and has the maritime specialism.

**Against Italy.** Italy has stronger industrial niches and a harder immigration system with its annual quota. Greece's routes are more accessible.

**Against Cyprus and Malta.** Both are more English-friendly than Greece and smaller. Cyprus in particular overlaps with Greece on shipping while being considerably easier to work in without the local language.

**On tax**, all of these have newcomer regimes of some form, all have been revised, and all reward professional advice before arrival rather than after.

**And on the sea and the light**, which is not a trivial consideration for people choosing between them, they are broadly comparable and all considerably better than Northern Europe.

## What can go wrong

Five things that delay Greek applications.

**Documents not apostilled or translated.** Certified translation is required and the process takes weeks.

**No AFM.** The tax number gates a lease, a bank account and much of the process, and obtaining it takes coordination if you are not yet in the country.

**Consular appointment availability**, which varies by country and can be a genuine bottleneck.

**Income evidence not meeting the format required** for the nomad route — bank statements, contracts and employer letters all need to demonstrate the position clearly.

**Assuming the tax incentive applies automatically.** It has conditions, including prior non-residence and a commitment period, and it must be claimed properly.

**And administrative pace generally.** Greek bureaucracy has improved substantially through gov.gr digitisation and it is still slower than Northern European equivalents.

## Who each route actually suits

Matching the route to the person.

**Digital nomad visa.** Remote workers with foreign employers or clients who want Mediterranean living against a foreign salary. The strongest financial arrangement available in Greece.

**Employment permit.** People with a specific Greek job offer, typically in shipping or the international sector, where an employer is willing to run the process.

**EU Blue Card.** Qualified professionals with a [degree](/blog/how-to-list-education-on-resume)-level offer above the threshold — better than the standard route where it applies.

**Golden Visa.** Investors seeking residency without an intention to work there. Not a work route.

**Financially independent person visa.** People with sufficient passive income who intend to live in Greece without working.

**And EU citizens** need none of it, which is worth restating because much online discussion of Greek visas is aimed at third-country nationals and confuses people who already have free movement.

## Costs and documents

What to budget and what takes longest.

**Consular visa fee** plus the residence permit fee after arrival.

**Criminal record certificate with apostille** from every country of recent residence — the slowest single item.

**Certified translations** into Greek, by a recognised translator.

**Health insurance** covering you until public cover applies.

**Proof of income** over a period rather than as a snapshot, in the format the route requires.

**Accommodation evidence**, which creates the familiar problem of needing a lease before you have residence.

**And an AFM**, obtainable through a representative if you are not yet in the country.

**Budget several months** for the full sequence, and more if consular appointments are scarce where you are.

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: You work remotely for a foreign employer

The digital nomad visa is built for this, and combined with Greece's cost base and the tax provisions it is a genuinely strong arrangement. Verify the income requirement against current figures.

### Scenario 2: You have a Greek job offer

Check the newcomer tax incentive immediately. For someone relocating on a reasonable salary it changes take-home substantially, and the conditions must be met from the start rather than claimed retrospectively.

### Scenario 3: You are considering the Golden Visa

Understand what it is. It is residency by investment, it does not require you to live there, thresholds have risen in popular areas, and it is not a work permit. For working in Greece, look at the other routes.

> **Tip:** Check Greece's newcomer tax regime before you accept an offer, not after. It offers a substantial exemption for a defined period, the conditions have to be satisfied from the outset, and it is the single biggest financial variable in a Greek relocation.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Check the tax incentive before accepting an offer
- Verify nomad income thresholds against current figures
- Get an AFM early
- Understand the Golden Visa as an investment product
- Check the graduate route if you studied in Greece
- Take professional advice on tax residency

### Don't

- Confuse the Golden Visa with a work permit
- Assume the tax incentive applies automatically
- Rely on thresholds quoted secondhand
- Expect the standard employment route to be easy
- Underestimate Greek administrative paperwork
- Ignore the labour market test on the employment route

## Common Mistakes

- **Confusing the Golden Visa with work residency.** It is an investment product with different conditions.
- **Missing the tax incentive.** Substantial, time-limited, and conditions must be met from the start.
- **Using stale thresholds.** Both the nomad income requirement and Golden Visa levels have changed.
- **Underestimating the employment route.** A labour market test makes it genuinely harder than the alternatives.
- **Leaving the AFM late.** Almost everything practical depends on it.
- **Assuming residency equals work rights.** They are separate questions in the investment routes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does Greece have a digital nomad visa?

Yes, for people working remotely for employers or clients outside Greece, employed or self-employed, subject to an income requirement assessed over a period.

### What is the Greek tax incentive for newcomers?

A regime offering a substantial exemption on a portion of employment or business income for people transferring their tax residence to Greece, subject to conditions and running for a defined period.

### Is the Golden Visa a work permit?

No. It is residency through investment, most commonly property, and it does not require you to live in Greece. Work rights are a separate question and should be checked rather than assumed.

### Why were Golden Visa thresholds raised?

Because the scheme contributed to housing pressure in popular areas. Thresholds are now higher in Athens, Thessaloniki and several islands than elsewhere.

### Is the standard employment route difficult?

Comparatively, yes. It generally involves a labour market test confirming no suitable EU candidate is available, which makes it harder than the nomad, Blue Card or investment routes.

### Can I stay after studying in Greece?

Provisions exist for graduates of Greek institutions to remain and seek work. Check current terms with the Greek authorities, as these have been adjusted.
### How do I apply for a Greek visa?

Generally at a Greek consulate in your country of residence, converting to a residence permit after arrival, with apostilled documents and an AFM tax number obtained early.

### Does Greek residency lead to citizenship?

After a longer qualifying period plus a Greek language and civics examination. Physical presence matters for naturalisation, which is why investment-visa holders who do not live there do not progress.
### How does Greece compare to Portugal or Spain for relocating?

Portugal has a larger international community and shorter citizenship path; Spain has a far larger economy. Greece's distinctive advantage is shipping, and its costs outside Athens are low.
### Which Greek visa route suits me?

The digital nomad visa for remote workers with foreign income, an employment permit or Blue Card for those with a Greek job offer, and the Golden Visa only for investors not intending to work there.
### What takes longest in a Greek visa application?

Apostilled criminal record certificates from every country of recent residence, certified Greek translations, and obtaining accommodation evidence before you have residence.
