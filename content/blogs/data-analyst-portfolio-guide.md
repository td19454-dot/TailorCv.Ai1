A data analyst portfolio proves one thing above all: you can turn raw data into a decision. Hiring managers do not want another rainbow dashboard — they want the **insight** and the **business action** it supports. Pair this with [How to Build a Professional Portfolio](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-build-professional-portfolio) and [Data Scientist Portfolio Guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/data-scientist-portfolio-guide).

## Projects to Include
- A polished **dashboard** (Tableau Public or Power BI Web) on a real dataset
- A **SQL case study** with non-trivial joins, window functions, and CTEs
- An **end-to-end analysis**: question → data → method → insight → recommendation
- An **A/B test, cohort, or churn** analysis

## Always Frame the Business Question
For every project, state: the question, the data source, your method, the finding, and the **decision it would drive**. "Which acquisition channel has the best ROI and where should we move budget?" beats "here is a bar chart."

## Where to Host (Free)
- **GitHub** — notebooks, SQL, and documentation ([GitHub Portfolio Optimization](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/github-portfolio-optimization))
- **Tableau Public** — free dashboard hosting
- **Power BI Web** — shareable reports
- **Kaggle** — notebooks, datasets, and competitions

## Make It Skimmable
Recruiters skim. For each project: a one-line takeaway, a clean visual, and a short write-up. Lead with the insight, not the methodology.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many projects for a data analyst portfolio?

Three to five: at least one dashboard, one SQL case study, and one full analysis with a recommendation.

### Tableau or Power BI?

Whichever your target jobs list most. Showing one well is enough; mention the other if you know it.
