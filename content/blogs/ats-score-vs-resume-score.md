You may have seen tools report an "[ATS score](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/ats-score-guide)" and others report a "resume score." Are they the same thing? Not quite. This guide explains the **difference between an [ATS score](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/ats-score-guide) and a resume score**, how they overlap, and which to prioritize.

You can get both perspectives by scanning your resume with the [free ATS score checker](/solutions).

## Quick Definitions

| Term | What It Measures |
|---|---|
| **[ATS score](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/does-my-resume-pass-ats)** | How well your resume matches a **specific job description** and parses through Applicant Tracking Software |
| **Resume score** | The **general quality** of your resume - structure, clarity, achievements, readability - often independent of any one job |

In short: [ATS score](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/ats-score-guide) is about **match and machine-readability**; resume score is about **overall quality**.

---

## What an ATS Score Measures

An [ATS score](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/ats-score-guide) is always relative to a job description. It evaluates:

- Keyword and skills match with the posting
- Formatting and parseability
- Job title relevance
- Measurable experience
- Standard sections and contact info

Because it is job-specific, the same resume can earn different ATS scores for different roles. Learn more in [what is an ATS score](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/what-is-an-ats-score-and-why-does-it-decide-your-job-application-before-any-human-reads-it).

---

## What a Resume Score Measures

A resume score rates the general strength of your resume, often without a specific job in mind. It typically looks at:

- Clear structure and standard sections
- Strong, quantified [bullet points](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-write-resume-bullet-points)
- [action verbs](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/best-action-verbs-for-resume) and concise writing
- Consistent formatting
- Appropriate length

A high resume score means your resume is well-built - but it does not guarantee it matches the job you are applying to.

---

## How They Overlap

The two scores share a lot of best practices:

- Clean, single-column formatting helps **both**
- Quantified achievements help **both**
- Standard headings help **both**
- Clear, concise writing helps **both**

The key difference is **keyword matching to a specific job**, which only the [ATS score](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/ats-score-guide) captures.

---

## Which Score Should You Focus On?

**Focus on your [ATS score](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/ats-score-guide) for each job you apply to**, because that is what determines whether you pass the automated filter for that role. But build on a strong resume foundation first:

1. Start with a high-quality, well-written resume (good resume score).
2. Then tailor and check the **[ATS score](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/ats-score-guide)** for each specific job.

Think of resume quality as the foundation and [ATS score](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/ats-score-guide) as the per-job tuning.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid When Optimizing for ATS and Resume Scores

When optimizing your resume for both ATS and resume scores, avoiding common pitfalls can significantly enhance your chances of getting noticed. Here are some frequent mistakes job seekers make:

1. **Using a Generic Template**: Many candidates rely on generic templates, which often lead to low scores. Instead, tailor your resume for each job application. [How ATS Detects a Generic Resume - And Why It Filters You Out](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-ats-detects-generic-resume).

2. **Ignoring Keywords**: Failing to include relevant keywords from the job description can hurt both scores. Always analyze the job posting and incorporate specific terms and phrases that relate to the skills and qualifications required.

3. **Overstuffing Keywords**: While it's crucial to include keywords, stuffing them unnaturally can lead to a poor user experience. Use keywords in context and ensure they flow naturally within your resume.

4. **Neglecting Formatting**: Complex formatting can confuse ATS systems, leading to misinterpretation of your information. Stick to standard fonts, bullet points, and a clear structure to enhance readability.

5. **Not Tailoring the Summary Statement**: Your summary should reflect the specific role you're applying for. A generic summary may fail to resonate with hiring managers and ATS alike.

6. **Failing to Update Your Resume Regularly**: Keeping your resume static can lead to outdated information, which may not align with current job market demands or keyword trends.

## Real-World Scenario: Tailoring Your Resume for a Specific Job

Imagine you’re applying for a marketing manager position that emphasizes digital marketing skills. Here’s how you would tailor your resume:

1. **Review the Job Description**: Identify keywords such as "digital marketing," "SEO," "content strategy," and "social media management."

2. **Update Your Summary**: Rewrite your summary to highlight your experience in digital marketing, using the identified keywords.

3. **Revise Work Experience**: In your work experience section, focus on roles where you utilized these skills. Include specific achievements, such as “increased website traffic by 40% through targeted SEO strategies.”

4. **Adjust Skills Section**: Ensure that your skills section lists the relevant skills mentioned in the job posting. This may include tools like Google Analytics or specific social media platforms.

5. **Proofread and Score**: After making adjustments, proofread your resume and check your ATS score again to ensure it meets the desired benchmarks.

By following this scenario, you align your resume closely with the job requirements, increasing your chances of passing both ATS and human reviews.

## Do's and Don'ts for ATS and Resume Score Success

### Do's

- **Do Research**: Understand the industry standards and required skills for your target job.
- **Do Use Standard Section Headings**: Stick to headings like "Work Experience," "Education," and "Skills" to ensure ATS can easily categorize your information.
- **Do Keep Formatting Simple**: Use standard fonts and bullet points to maintain clarity.
- **Do Update Regularly**: Keep your resume current with your latest achievements and skills.

### Don'ts

- **Don't Use Graphics or Images**: These can confuse ATS systems and lead to misinterpretation of your resume.
- **Don't Lie**: Be honest about your skills and experiences; misrepresentation can lead to disqualification.
- **Don't Ignore Feedback**: Use feedback from ATS checkers to improve your resume continuously.
- **Don't Submit Without Testing**: Always check your ATS score before submitting your application to ensure it meets employer expectations.

By following these practical tips and strategies, you can effectively optimize your resume for both ATS and human readers, enhancing your chances of landing that coveted interview.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is ATS score more important than resume score?

For getting past automated screening, yes - the [ATS score](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/ats-score-guide) decides whether a human sees your resume. But a strong resume score ensures you impress that human afterward.

### Can I have a high resume score but a low ATS score?

Absolutely. A beautifully written resume that doesn't match the job's keywords - or uses a complex layout - can still score low on [ATS](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/what-is-an-ats-score-and-why-does-it-decide-your-job-application-before-any-human-reads-it).
