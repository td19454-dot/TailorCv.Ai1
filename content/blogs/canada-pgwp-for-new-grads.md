## Key Takeaways

- The PGWP is generally an open work permit, so you are not tied to one employer — a major advantage over most graduate visas.
- Its length depends on the length of your study programme, and it is normally issued only once in a lifetime.
- Eligibility rules have changed in recent years, including field-of-study requirements for some programmes. Verify current rules with IRCC.
- The permit's real value is the Canadian work experience it lets you accumulate, which feeds most permanent residence pathways.
- The category your job falls under can matter as much as the salary, because PR programmes weight certain categories.

## What the PGWP is

An open work permit for graduates of eligible Canadian institutions and programmes. Open means it is not tied to a specific employer — you can work for anyone, change jobs, and are not dependent on a single company sponsoring you.

That is a genuinely different position from most graduate work routes internationally. Compare it with the [US H-1B](/blog/usa-h1b-lottery-new-grads), which is employer-sponsored and lottery-dependent, or [Singapore's Employment Pass](/blog/singapore-employment-pass-graduates), which is applied for by the employer and tied to that job. The PGWP gives the graduate the mobility rather than the employer the control.

**It is normally a once-in-a-lifetime permit.** You cannot ordinarily do a second Canadian programme and get a second PGWP. That makes the timing of when you use it a real decision rather than an administrative step.

## Length and eligibility

Length is tied to the length of your programme, up to a maximum. Shorter programmes yield shorter permits; longer ones yield the maximum.

Eligibility depends on the institution, the programme, and — following changes in recent years — in some cases the field of study. Requirements have been revised more than once recently, and rules that applied to a student who started three years before you may not apply to you.

Because of that, this article deliberately gives no specific durations or lists. **Check IRCC's current published requirements, and confirm with your institution's international student advisor.** Advice from a graduate who went through it two years ago is not reliable on this point.

Two things that are stable enough to plan around:

- **Apply within the permitted window after completing your programme.** Miss it and you may lose eligibility entirely.
- **You must have maintained full-time status** through your programme, with limited exceptions.

## Why the first job matters beyond the salary

Most permanent residence pathways for graduates run on Canadian work experience. Express Entry's Canadian Experience Class and many provincial programmes weight it heavily.

What matters is not only that you worked, but **what category the work falls under**. Occupational classification determines whether experience counts toward specific programmes, and some categories are weighted more heavily in particular draws.

The practical implication for a graduating student: when comparing two offers, the classification of the role and whether it accumulates qualifying experience can matter more over three years than a difference in starting salary.

This is worth understanding *before* accepting your first job, not after. Whether a [job offer itself affects Express Entry](/blog/canada-express-entry-job-offer) is a separate question, and the answer is more limited than most applicants assume — the experience usually does more work than the offer.

Provincial programmes are worth reviewing too, since several have streams aimed at graduates of institutions in that province. The [provincial nominee routes](/blog/canada-provincial-nominee-guide) differ considerably and a role in one province may be worth more than the same role in another.

## Timing your job search against the permit

The permit is finite and starts running whether or not you have found work. That produces the same structural pressure international graduates face everywhere: the search should start well before graduation.

Practical sequence:

**Penultimate year.** Apply for internships and co-op terms. Canadian work experience obtained during study is the strongest possible preparation, and the [co-op route](/blog/canada-co-op-student-guide) is a genuine hiring channel.

**Final year, autumn.** Structured graduate programmes open. This is the main window and it closes early — the [campus recruiting timeline](/blog/canada-campus-recruiting-timeline) runs earlier than most students expect.

**After graduation.** Apply for the permit within the window. Keep applying for roles while the application is processing; you may be able to work while a decision is pending, subject to current rules.

**Once working.** Track your accumulated experience against whichever PR pathway you are targeting, and understand what the clock requires.

## What employers understand about it

Most Canadian employers know what a PGWP is. Because it is an open permit, hiring a holder involves no sponsorship, no application and no cost to them.

That means you can and should say so plainly:

> "I'll be on a post-graduation work permit, which is an open permit — no sponsorship needed on your side."

That single sentence removes the main objection an employer might have. Many candidates fail to say it and let the employer assume a complication that does not exist.

Where it becomes relevant is later: when the permit approaches expiry and you need a longer-term status. Raising that at the right point, rather than at the offer, is the same judgement as the [visa sponsorship conversation anywhere](/blog/usa-visa-sponsorship-question-interview).

## Common Mistakes

- **Missing the application window after graduating.** It can cost eligibility entirely.
- **Assuming rules are unchanged.** Requirements have been revised repeatedly in recent years.
- **Not saying it is an open permit.** Employers assume sponsorship burden unless told otherwise.
- **Choosing a first job without checking its classification.** It can matter more than salary for PR pathways.
- **Starting the job search after graduating.** The permit clock does not wait, and structured hiring closed months earlier.
- **Relying on peer advice for immigration rules.** IRCC and your institution's advisor are the authorities.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is the PGWP tied to one employer?

No. It is generally an open work permit, so you can work for any employer and change jobs freely — one of its main advantages.

### How long is it valid for?

It depends on the length of your programme, up to a maximum. Confirm current rules with IRCC, as both lengths and eligibility criteria have changed.

### Can I get a second one?

Ordinarily no. It is normally issued once, which is why the decision about when to use it matters.

### Can I work while my application is being processed?

There have been provisions allowing this in certain circumstances, but the details have changed. Confirm the current position rather than assuming.

### Does the PGWP lead directly to permanent residence?

Not directly. It gives you the ability to accumulate Canadian work experience, which is what most PR pathways are built on. The permit is the bridge, not the destination.

### Does it matter which province I work in?

It can. Several provincial nominee streams target graduates of local institutions or workers in specific regions, so the same role can be worth more in one province than another.
