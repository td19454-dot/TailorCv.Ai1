## Key Takeaways

- The sector employs hundreds of thousands of people, and most roles have nothing to do with fundraising or frontline delivery.
- Pay is generally below private sector, though large national charities and housing associations can be competitive.
- Roles are posted on sector-specific boards and charity websites rather than mainstream job sites.
- Grant and contract funding means some posts are fixed-term, which is normal but worth asking about.
- Charity accounts are public, so you can research an organisation's finances and senior pay before applying.

The UK charity sector is far larger and more professionalised than its public image suggests. Major national charities, housing associations, universities' fundraising arms, hospices and research foundations all employ finance directors, data engineers, HR managers and policy specialists on ordinary professional terms.

The pay is generally lower. Almost everything else about the work is comparable, and several things are better.

This guide covers what the sector looks like, how to find roles, and how to move in.

## What the sector actually contains

**Large national charities.** Household names with thousands of staff, running services, campaigns and retail operations.

**Housing associations.** Frequently overlooked and among the largest employers in the sector, with substantial finance, development, asset management and digital functions. Pay is often closer to private sector than elsewhere in the sector.

**Health and medical research charities.** Funding research, running services, employing scientists and grant managers.

**Hospices and care providers.** Clinical and non-clinical roles, often working closely with the NHS — see [the NHS jobs guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/uk-nhs-jobs-guide).

**International development NGOs.** Programme, policy and operational roles, frequently funded by institutional grants.

**Policy and advocacy organisations.** Research, public affairs, campaigns.

**Arts, heritage and culture.** Museums, galleries, theatres, trusts.

**Foundations and grant-makers.** Small teams, competitive roles, often well paid.

## The roles, not just the causes

The point most career changers miss: the sector needs the same functions as any organisation.

**Finance** — management accounting, charity SORP reporting, treasury. Charity finance has its own technical requirements, which makes experienced charity finance staff genuinely scarce.

**Digital and data** — websites, CRM systems, data analysis, digital fundraising. Chronically short of skilled people, which makes it one of the easiest entry points for a private-sector technologist.

**Fundraising** — trusts and foundations, major donors, corporate partnerships, legacies. A professional discipline with clear progression, and not the same thing as street collection.

**Policy and public affairs.**

**HR, governance, legal, operations, facilities.**

**Retail** — the large charities run substantial retail chains with area and regional management structures.

## Pay, honestly

**Generally below private sector**, with the widest gaps in technology, finance and legal roles where private alternatives pay a lot.

**But the spread within the sector is very wide.** A large housing association or national charity pays very differently from a small local organisation. Treating "charity pay" as one number is the mistake.

**What partially compensates:**

- **Pension** contributions are frequently decent, and some organisations participate in generous schemes
- **Annual leave** is often above statutory minimum
- **Flexible working** was normal in much of the sector before it was normal elsewhere
- **Autonomy** — smaller teams mean broader roles and more visible impact

**Research before applying.** Charity accounts are filed publicly with the Charity Commission, including senior staff pay bands and overall financial position. That is more transparency than most private-sector research offers — use it when benchmarking, alongside [UK salary benchmarking](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/uk-salary-benchmarking-guide).

## Finding roles

**Sector-specific job boards** carry the overwhelming majority of charity vacancies. Mainstream boards carry a small fraction.

**Charity websites directly**, particularly for larger organisations.

## Applications

**Expect a supporting statement** against a person specification, similar to the public sector rather than a private-sector CV-and-cover-letter approach. Structure it against their criteria explicitly — the method in [the NHS jobs guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/uk-nhs-jobs-guide) transfers directly.

**Address the mission genuinely.** Hiring managers are wary of private-sector applicants treating the sector as a break, or expecting private pay. A specific, credible answer about why this cause matters, and explicit acknowledgement that you understand the pay reality, both help.

**Translate your achievements into their terms.** "Grew revenue 30%" becomes "grew unrestricted income 30%, funding two additional support workers." Not spin — outcomes genuinely are measured differently.

**Volunteering or trustee experience** is real currency and demonstrates commitment beyond an application form.

## Fixed-term and funding

Ask directly, because it affects your security.

Many roles are funded by a specific grant or contract with a defined end date. This is normal in the sector and not a warning sign, but you should know:

- **Is this post grant-funded, and until when?**
- **Has the funding been renewed before?**
- **What is the plan if it is not renewed?**

Organisations answer these openly, and the answer should shape how you weigh the offer — the same discipline as [the UK probation period guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/uk-probation-period-guide) applies to understanding what you are actually signing.

## Common Mistakes

**Assuming the sector means fundraising.** Finance, digital, data, policy, HR and operations make up most of the professional roles.

**Overlooking housing associations.** Among the largest employers in the sector, with pay frequently closer to private-sector levels.

**Treating charity pay as one number.** The spread between a large national charity and a small local organisation is enormous.

**Not reading the public accounts.** Charity finances and senior pay bands are filed publicly, which is more research transparency than private-sector applications allow.

**Searching mainstream job boards.** Sector-specific boards and organisation websites carry the overwhelming majority of vacancies.

**Submitting a private-sector application.** Most charity roles use a supporting statement against a person specification, and a CV-and-cover-letter approach scores poorly.

**Giving a generic answer on motivation.** Hiring managers are alert to private-sector candidates treating the sector as a rest, and vagueness confirms it.

**Not asking whether a post is grant-funded.** Fixed-term posts are normal, but you should know the end date and renewal history before accepting.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does the charity sector pay badly?

Generally below private sector, but the range is wide. Large national charities and housing associations can be competitive, and pension and leave are frequently better than the salary suggests.

### What jobs are there besides fundraising?

Finance, digital, data, policy, HR, governance, operations, retail management and programme delivery. The sector needs the same functions as any organisation.

### Where are charity jobs advertised?

Sector-specific job boards and organisations' own websites. Mainstream job boards carry only a small fraction.

### Can I research charity salaries?

Partly — charities file public accounts including senior pay bands and financial position, which gives you more research material than most private-sector applications.

### Do I need charity experience to move in?

Not necessarily, particularly in digital, data and finance where skills are scarce. Volunteering or trustee experience helps demonstrate commitment.

### Are charity jobs less secure?

Some posts are grant or contract funded with defined end dates. Ask whether the role is funded that way, until when, and whether it has been renewed before.

### How should I write a charity application?

Against the person specification, as a structured supporting statement rather than a private-sector cover letter, with a genuine and specific answer on why the cause matters to you.

## Make This Practical

Look past the causes to the functions. Finance, digital and data roles in the charity sector are chronically short of skilled people, which makes them the most realistic entry point for anyone moving from the private sector — and housing associations in particular pay closer to commercial rates than the sector's reputation suggests.

Then research properly before applying. Charity accounts are public, including senior pay bands and financial position, so you can benchmark and assess stability in a way private-sector applications rarely allow. Ask directly whether a post is grant-funded and until when.

Finally, write the application the way this sector reads it. Use a structured supporting statement against the person specification rather than a cover letter, translate your achievements into mission outcomes, and give a specific answer on why this cause — hiring managers are alert to candidates treating the sector as a break, and a generic answer confirms the suspicion.
