## Why accuracy is the whole audition

In legal support, a typo or a formatting slip is a red flag — it suggests you'll make the same mistake on a filing. Your [resume](https://www.thetailorcv.com/modify-cv) is a live demonstration of the precision the job requires, so [proofread meticulously](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/resume-proofreading-checklist) and use the [resume review checklist](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/resume-review-checklist-before-applying) before you send it. Precision plus exact-match keywords is what clears both the ATS and the hiring attorney.

## Lead with certifications and practice area

Note any paralegal certification (CP, ACP), your education, and your practice area — litigation, corporate, real estate, immigration, or IP — near the top. Specificity beats a generic "paralegal," and your practice area is a strong [keyword](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-match-resume-keywords-to-job-description) the [ATS](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/ats-score-guide) scans for. Format credentials with [how to list certifications on a resume](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-list-certifications-on-resume).

A tight [summary](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-write-resume-summary) frames your fit:

> "Certified paralegal (CP) with 6 years supporting litigation teams. Managed documents for 50+ active matters, prepared discovery and trial binders, and drafted pleadings. Expert in Relativity, Clio, and Westlaw."

See [resume objective vs summary](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/resume-objective-vs-summary) for the opening lines.

## Name your tools

List the platforms you know — Relativity, Clio, iManage, Westlaw, LexisNexis, and advanced Microsoft Office. Legal software proficiency is a genuine differentiator and a [keyword](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-match-resume-keywords-to-job-description) the [ATS](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/ats-score-guide) rewards.

## Prove reliability with detail

Use the [bullet point formula](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-write-resume-bullet-points) — a strong [action verb](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/best-action-verbs-for-resume), the work, and a measurable result:

- "Managed documents for 50+ active matters simultaneously."
- "Prepared discovery and exhibits for cases that settled favorably."
- "Cut document-review time 25% by building a tagging protocol in Relativity."
- "Drafted and cite-checked pleadings with zero filing errors over two years."

Numbers and specifics prove reliability — the [quantify achievements guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-quantify-achievements-on-resume) shows how. Duty-only bullets are a top reason a capable paralegal's [resume gets no responses](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/why-your-resume-gets-no-responses).

## Structure a paralegal resume

1. **Header** — name, certification, phone, email, city.
2. **Summary** — three lines, practice-area-forward.
3. **Certifications & practice area**.
4. **Skills** — research, drafting, e-filing, tools.
5. **Experience** — firm, role, [quantified](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-quantify-achievements-on-resume) bullets.
6. **Education**.

This mirrors the layout in the [resume builder](https://www.thetailorcv.com/modify-cv). For ordering, see the [resume section order guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/resume-section-order-guide).

## Paralegal resume summary examples

Match your [summary](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-write-resume-summary) to your level:

- **Entry-level:** "Certified paralegal (CP) with a litigation internship, skilled in legal research, drafting, and e-filing, proficient in Westlaw and Clio."
- **Mid-level:** "Certified paralegal with 6 years supporting litigation teams, managing 50+ matters and preparing discovery and trial binders."
- **Senior:** "Senior paralegal with 10 years in corporate transactions, leading due diligence, closing checklists, and mentoring junior staff."
