## Key Takeaways

- Everything companies screen on — CGPA, projects, internships, aptitude ability — is built before final year.
- Second year is when CGPA is still recoverable and when a genuine project can actually mature.
- Thirty minutes of aptitude practice daily from now puts you far ahead of people starting in semester seven.
- The summer after second year is the highest-value internship window you will get.
- Almost nobody starts this early, which is precisely why it works.

If you are in second year and thinking about placements, you are already doing better than most of your batch. Not because you are ahead on any skill, but because you are thinking about it while the things that matter are still changeable.

By semester seven, your CGPA is largely fixed, your projects are whatever you managed to build, and your aptitude ability is whatever you developed. Final year is for executing; second year is for building.

This guide covers what to actually do with the time.

## Protect the things that become unchangeable

**CGPA is cumulative and it is the hardest filter to work around.** Many companies screen at 6.5, 7.0 or 7.5, and those filters are automatic with no appeal.

**The arithmetic matters here.** A student averaging 6.0 through first and second year needs sustained 8.0+ across the remaining semesters to reach 7.5. That is difficult. The same student acting in second year rather than fourth has a far easier path.

**Clear backlogs immediately.** Many companies filter on current backlogs and some on backlog history. A paper carried forward is a filter you may not clear later — see [the campus placement backlog guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/campus-placement-backlog-guide) and [the low CGPA guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/campus-placement-low-cgpa-guide).

## Start aptitude now, at thirty minutes a day

This is the highest-return habit available to you, and the reasoning is specific.

**Aptitude eliminates more candidates than any other round**, and it is the most improvable through consistent practice because the question types genuinely repeat.

**But it improves through spaced repetition, not cramming.** Thirty minutes daily from second year builds pattern recognition that eight hours a day in the final month cannot replicate.

**Start with the highest-frequency topics** — percentages, profit and loss, ratio, averages, time and work, time-speed-distance. Accuracy first, speed second.

See [the campus placement aptitude test guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/campus-placement-aptitude-test-guide) for the full plan.

## Build programming depth, not breadth

**One language, properly.** C++, Java or Python. Depth in one transfers; shallow familiarity with four does not.

**Start DSA properly rather than casually.** Arrays, strings, hashing, recursion — the foundations that carry the largest share of campus questions. Two or three problems a week consistently beats forty in a weekend before placements, because pattern recognition builds through repetition.

**Log patterns, not problem counts** — see [the campus placement DSA roadmap](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/campus-placement-dsa-roadmap).

**Use version control from now.** Put everything on GitHub as you build it. By final year you will have a genuine commit history rather than an account created in a panic with three commits — see [how to optimise your GitHub profile](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/github-profile-for-job-search-2026).

## Build one real project, slowly

**Second year is when a project can actually mature.** Started now, it has eighteen months to acquire real users, hit real constraints, and give you something to discuss for twenty minutes.

**Choose a problem you actually have.** Your department's attendance system is painful, your hostel wastes food, your club cannot track equipment. Unglamorous problems interview far better than generic clones, because your motivation is genuine and specific.

**Deploy it.** A live link is the cheapest credibility available.

**Let it break.** The bugs and constraints you hit over eighteen months become your "hardest part" answer, which is the single most valuable thing you can bring to a technical interview — see [choosing a final year project for placements](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/final-year-project-for-placement).

## Target the summer after second year

**This is the highest-value window you will get**, and most students waste it.

**Why it matters:** an internship after second year gives you external validation on your resume, real experience to discuss in every subsequent interview, and — frequently — a route into a pre-placement offer that means entering placement season with an offer already secured.

**Applications open months ahead**, so this is a second-year task, not a third-year one.

**A small company or startup counts.** Unpaid counts if the work is real. The validation matters more than the brand.

See [converting an internship into a PPO](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/internship-to-ppo-conversion-guide).

## Join something and take a role

Technical clubs, coding chapters, fest organising committees. Not for the line on your resume, but because HR rounds ask for examples of teamwork, conflict, leadership and initiative, and students who spent four years only attending classes genuinely struggle to answer.

**Take a role with responsibility** rather than membership. "Coordinated a 400-participant technical fest managing a team of 12" is evidence; "member of technical club" is not.

The behavioural questions this feeds are in [campus placement HR interview questions](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/campus-placement-hr-interview-questions).

## Decide your direction by end of second year

**Service-based or product-based?** They require different preparation — aptitude and communication weight for one, DSA depth and project ownership for the other. Deciding early lets you specialise; deciding in semester seven leaves you mediocre at both — see [service-based vs product-based companies](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/service-based-vs-product-based-companies).

## Common Mistakes

**Assuming second year is too early.** Everything companies screen on is built before final year, which makes this the only time it is genuinely changeable.

**Letting CGPA drift.** It is cumulative, and recovering from a weak start requires sustained high performance that gets harder each semester.

**Carrying backlogs forward.** They become eligibility filters you cannot clear once the season begins.

**Cramming aptitude later instead of daily practice now.** Pattern recognition builds through spaced repetition and cannot be replicated by intensity in the final month.

**Collecting tutorial projects instead of building one real thing.** Six clones interview worse than one deployed project with real users.

**Wasting the summer after second year.** It is the highest-value internship window and applications open months ahead.

**Attending clubs without taking a role.** HR rounds ask for leadership and conflict examples, and membership provides neither.

**Learning four languages shallowly.** Depth in one is what campus rounds actually reward.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is second year too early to prepare for placements?

No — it is the ideal time. CGPA is still recoverable, projects have time to mature, and aptitude ability builds through months of practice rather than weeks.

### What is the single most useful habit?

Thirty minutes of aptitude practice daily. It eliminates more candidates than any other round and improves through spaced repetition rather than cramming.

### Should I do an internship after second year?

Yes — it is the highest-value window available. It provides external validation, interview material, and frequently a route to a pre-placement offer.

### How many projects should I build?

One real one, built slowly over eighteen months, beats several tutorial clones. Depth is what interviewers probe.

### Which language should I learn?

One, properly — C++, Java or Python. Depth transfers between problems; shallow familiarity with several does not.

### Do clubs and societies matter?

Yes, if you take a role with responsibility. HR rounds ask for teamwork, conflict and leadership examples, and students without them struggle.

### When should I decide between service-based and product-based companies?

By the end of second year, because the preparation differs and specialising early is what makes you competitive in either.

## Make This Practical

Start thirty minutes of daily aptitude practice this week and protect your CGPA deliberately. Those two things are the highest-leverage actions available to you, and both become substantially harder or impossible to fix later — aptitude because it needs months of spaced repetition, CGPA because it is cumulative.

Then start one real project on a problem you actually have, deploy it, and let it run for eighteen months. The bugs and constraints you hit over that period become the "hardest part" answer that carries technical interviews, and a project started in final year cannot produce them.

Finally, apply for a summer internship after second year, take a role with real responsibility in a club, and decide by year end whether you are targeting service-based or product-based companies — because that decision determines where your next two years of preparation should go.
