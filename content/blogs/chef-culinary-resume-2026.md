Kitchens move fast, and so do the people who hire for them — but a sharp [resume](https://www.thetailorcv.com/modify-cv) still gets you the interview over a walk-in. And because hotel groups and restaurant chains increasingly screen with an [ATS](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/what-is-an-ats-score-and-why-does-it-decide-your-job-application-before-any-human-reads-it), your keywords have to match the job description before a chef ever reads it.

## What restaurants and hotels hire for

Hiring chefs and F&B managers care about three things: can you cook the cuisine they serve, can you run a station or a team under pressure, and are you reliable. Your [resume](https://www.thetailorcv.com/modify-cv) should answer all three in seconds. For high-volume and hotel roles, the [hospitality resume matching guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/resume-matching-hospitality-hotel) covers the language these employers scan for.

## Lead with cuisine and level

State your specialty and level clearly: "Sous Chef — French & Mediterranean, high-volume fine dining." Restaurants hire for fit with their concept, and cuisine is a strong [keyword](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-match-resume-keywords-to-job-description) the [ATS](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/ats-score-guide) scans for.

A short [summary](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-write-resume-summary) sets the tone:

> "Sous chef with 6 years in fine dining and high-volume hotel kitchens. Ran a line serving 250+ covers nightly, cut food cost 6%, and trained a brigade of 10. French and Italian specialty."

See [resume objective vs summary](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/resume-objective-vs-summary) for the opening lines.

## Show kitchen leadership and scale

Volume, team size, and cost control matter in hospitality. Use the [bullet point formula](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-write-resume-bullet-points) with a strong [action verb](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/best-action-verbs-for-resume) and a measurable result:

- "Ran a line serving 250+ covers a night with a team of 8."
- "Cut food cost 6% by tightening ordering and reducing waste."
- "Trained 10 line cooks and standardized 30+ recipes."
- "Maintained a 5-star health inspection score across 3 years."

Numbers turn a duty list into proof — the [quantify achievements guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-quantify-achievements-on-resume) shows how. Duty-only bullets are a top reason a strong cook's [resume gets no responses](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/why-your-resume-gets-no-responses).

## Structure a chef resume

1. **Header** — name, level (e.g., Sous Chef), phone, email, city.
2. **Summary** — three lines, cuisine- and level-forward.
3. **Skills & certifications** — stations, ServSafe, cost control.
4. **Experience** — restaurant, role, [quantified](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-quantify-achievements-on-resume) bullets.
5. **Education & training** — culinary school, apprenticeships.

This mirrors the layout in the [resume builder](https://www.thetailorcv.com/modify-cv). For ordering, see the [resume section order guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/resume-section-order-guide).

## Sample chef resume bullets

Strong culinary bullets pair a [action verb](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/best-action-verbs-for-resume), the work, and a [quantified](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-quantify-achievements-on-resume) result:

- "Ran the sauté station on a line serving 250+ covers nightly with consistent ticket times."
- "Developed a seasonal menu that lifted average check 12% and cut food cost to 28%."
- "Trained and scheduled a brigade of 10, reducing turnover 20% over a year."
- "Maintained a perfect health-inspection record across three years and two openings."

Each bullet shows scale, skill, and impact — the difference between a [resume](https://www.thetailorcv.com/modify-cv) that lists duties and one that proves you run a tight kitchen, as explained in [how to write resume bullet points](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-write-resume-bullet-points). ## Where chefs find the best roles

Beyond job boards, kitchens hire heavily through word of mouth, stages, and direct outreach. A [cold email](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-write-cold-email-to-recruiter) to a chef you admire — with your [resume](https://www.thetailorcv.com/modify-cv) attached and a genuine note about their food — can land a trail shift faster than any application. Hospitality [recruitment agencies](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-work-with-recruitment-agencies) place chefs into hotels and groups, and the best job search websites guide covers where to look. Networking matters enormously in this industry, so keep your resume current and ATS-ready.

## Common chef resume mistakes

The errors that keep good cooks out of good kitchens:

- **No numbers.** Covers per night, food cost, team size — [quantify](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-quantify-achievements-on-resume) everything.
- **Cuisine left vague.** Name your specialties; they're [keywords](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-match-resume-keywords-to-job-description) the concept hires for.
- **Missing certifications.** List ServSafe and food-safety credentials — see [how to list certifications](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-list-certifications-on-resume).
- **Two cluttered pages.** Keep it one clean page.
- **Same resume everywhere.** Tailor to each restaurant.

For a broader list, read [why your resume gets no responses](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/why-your-resume-gets-no-responses) and [generic resume mistakes that cost interviews](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/generic-resume-mistakes-that-cost-interviews).

## Chef resume FAQ

**How long should a chef resume be?**
One page for most cooks and sous chefs; two only for executive chefs with extensive history. See the [ideal resume length guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/ideal-resume-length-guide).

**I've only done stages and part-time cooking — what do I put?**
Lead with stages, apprenticeships, and any station experience — the [resume with no experience guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-write-resume-with-no-experience-2026) shows how to frame it.

**Do I list every restaurant?**
Focus on relevant, recent roles that show cuisine fit and growth; group older or brief roles. [Quantify](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-quantify-achievements-on-resume) covers and cost wherever you can.

**How do I pass the ATS for hotel groups?**
Mirror the [job description](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/job-description-analysis-checklist), list ServSafe and stations, keep it [clean](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-make-resume-ats-friendly), and check the [ATS score](https://www.thetailorcv.com/solutions).

**Should I include a cover letter?**
For a concept you love, yes — a tailored [cover letter](https://www.thetailorcv.com/cover-letter) shows genuine fit with the restaurant.
