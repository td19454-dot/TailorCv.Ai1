## Key Takeaways

- Accountancy firms hire graduates from any degree discipline and fund the professional qualification.
- The main qualifications differ in focus — practice and audit, general finance, or management accounting in industry.
- The Big Four are the most visible employers and mid-tier and regional firms hire in volume with less competition.
- Training contracts combine paid work with study leave and examinations over roughly three years.
- Qualification is portable and opens routes well beyond accountancy itself.

## The qualifications

**Practice-focused chartered qualification.** Traditionally associated with audit and professional practice, typically completed through a training contract with a firm. Well regarded internationally and the standard route into audit and assurance.

**General chartered certified qualification.** Broader in setting, completable in practice or in industry, with more flexibility in how and where you study.

**Management accounting qualification.** Focused on management accounting, business partnering and finance within organisations rather than professional practice. The natural route if industry rather than audit is your target.

**Which to choose depends on where you want to work.** Practice and audit points one way; a finance function inside a business points another. Firms will generally tell you which they sponsor, and the choice is frequently made for you by the employer you join.

**All of them are funded by the employer** in a typical graduate scheme, alongside a salary, which is the central attraction — you are paid to gain a professional qualification rather than paying for one.

## The employers

**The largest professional services firms.** The most visible, with structured graduate schemes across audit, tax, consulting and advisory. Large intakes, strong training, and high application volumes.

**Mid-tier firms.** Substantial national and international firms with comparable qualifications and training, materially less competition, and frequently earlier client responsibility.

**Regional and independent practices.** Smaller intakes, broader early exposure across clients, and a genuinely different working pattern. Consistently overlooked and consistently viable — the [regional argument](/blog/uk-regional-jobs-outside-london) applies directly.

**Industry finance schemes.** Graduate programmes inside businesses, frequently sponsoring the management accounting route.

**The public sector.** Government, the NHS and local authorities run finance graduate schemes with sponsored qualification.

**The practical instruction:** apply across tiers rather than only to the best-known names. The qualification is the same and the ratio of applicants to places is dramatically better outside the largest firms.

## What the training contract involves

**Roughly three years**, combining paid work with study leave and a sequence of examinations.

**You work while you study**, which is demanding and is why employers structure study leave and provide tuition.

**Examinations are the constraint.** Progression through the contract depends on passing, and firms have policies about resits.

**Rotation is common**, particularly in larger firms, across clients or service lines.

**On qualification, options widen considerably.** Staying in practice, moving into industry finance, or moving into adjacent commercial roles are all common, which is why the qualification is valued as a portable credential rather than a narrow one.

## Selection

**Application, tests, then assessment centre** at most employers.

**Psychometric and situational judgement tests** are standard — the [preparation](/blog/uk-psychometric-tests-guide) applies, and practising under time pressure is what actually moves scores.

**Video interviews** at many firms, following the [standard format](/blog/uk-video-interview-hirevue-guide).

**Assessment centres** with case work, group exercises and interviews — the [mechanics](/blog/uk-assessment-centre-guide) transfer directly.

**Competency and strengths questions** throughout, drawing on the same [evidence bank](/blog/uk-competency-questions-guide) that serves any UK graduate application.

**Academic requirements** vary by firm and frequently include degree classification and sometimes prior results — see the [classification guide](/blog/uk-degree-classification-and-employers).

**Commercial awareness** is assessed, though generally less heavily than in [law](/blog/uk-law-training-contract-guide) or investment banking.

## Common Mistakes

- **Assuming you need an accounting degree.** Firms recruit across disciplines deliberately.
- **Applying only to the largest firms.** Mid-tier and regional practices offer the same qualification with far less competition.
- **Not understanding which qualification suits your goal.** Practice and industry routes differ.
- **Underestimating the examination load.** Working and studying simultaneously is genuinely demanding.
- **Neglecting the online tests.** An early filter, and trainable.
- **Applying late.** Autumn deadlines with rolling review at many firms.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a finance or accounting degree?

No. Firms recruit graduates from any discipline and provide the technical training, and numerate degrees are common without being required.

### Which qualification should I choose?

It depends on whether you want professional practice or a finance role inside a business, and frequently the employer you join determines it.

### Does the firm pay for it?

In a typical graduate scheme, yes — tuition, examination fees and study leave alongside a salary.

### How hard are the exams?

Demanding, particularly alongside full-time work. Pass rates vary by paper and firms provide structured support.

### Is it worth going to a smaller firm?

Frequently. The qualification is the same, competition is lower, and early client exposure is often broader.

### What can I do after qualifying?

Stay in practice, move into industry finance, or move into commercial and advisory roles. The qualification is portable and widely recognised.
