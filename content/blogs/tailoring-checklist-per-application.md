## Key Takeaways

- Nine checks, run in order, cover everything worth doing per application.
- The first three take two minutes and catch the errors that cost you the most.
- Structural checks (parsing, file name, contact details) only need running once per resume version, not per application.
- Stop at the end of the list - the instinct to do one more pass rarely pays.
- Print it or keep it open; the value is in not having to remember the order.

Checklists work because they remove the decision about what to do next. Here is the whole thing, in order, with a time budget.

## Before You Start: Once Per Resume Version

These are structural and do not change between applications. Run them when you build or edit a resume version, then forget them.

**☐ Copy-paste test passes.** Select all in your PDF, paste into a plain text editor. Every section present, columns not interleaved, contact details visible, dates attached to their roles.

**☐ Contact details are in the document body**, not the page header.

**☐ File is real text, not an image.** If nothing selects, re-export it.

**☐ File name is professional.** `Priya-Sharma-Resume.pdf`, not `resume_final_v3_USE THIS.pdf`.

**☐ Every bullet under your two most recent roles states an outcome,** not a duty.

If those five are clean, everything below takes ten minutes per application.

## The Nine Per-Application Checks

### 1. Read the posting for three things (2 min)

☐ The two or three requirements that repeat in the title, opening paragraph and bullets
☐ The employer's vocabulary for them
☐ Whether this is a doing role or an owning role

### 2. Does your summary name this role? (2 min)

☐ First line names the role or its closest accurate equivalent
☐ It includes the two capabilities the posting repeats most
☐ It contains one specific proof point, not adjectives
☐ It is three lines or fewer

### 3. Is the most relevant bullet first? (1 min)

☐ Under your most recent role, the first bullet speaks to the posting's top requirement
☐ If your best evidence sits in an older role, the capability is named in your summary

### 4. Does your skills line lead with theirs? (1 min)

☐ The posting's central terms appear first
☐ Irrelevant items removed for this application
☐ Nothing listed you could not discuss for two minutes

### 5. Have you renamed two or three things? (3 min)

☐ Work you did under a different name now uses the employer's term
☐ Each rename is accurate — you could defend it if asked
☐ You have not attempted the whole missing-keyword list

### 6. Does your title line register? (30 sec)

☐ If your internal title is unclear, the market equivalent appears in brackets
☐ Nothing inflated — the real title is still present

### 7. Are the obvious knockouts handled? (30 sec)

☐ Work authorisation, location and any hard credential are addressed if the posting names them
☐ Application-form questions answered consistently with your resume

### 8. Sanity read the top third (1 min)

☐ Read only the top third of page one, as a stranger would
☐ Ask: would this person plausibly do this job? If not, the problem is up there, not further down

### 9. Stop

☐ Send it

That last one is a real check. The temptation to do another pass is strong and almost never changes the outcome.

## What Is Deliberately Not on the List

**Clearing the whole missing-keyword list.** Two or three genuine renames capture most of the value; the rest is padding.

**Rewriting older roles.** Skimmed at best on a first pass.

**Reaching a target score.** Get to a workable range once, then stop measuring.

**A second cover letter draft.** If you write one, one focused draft beats two hedged ones.

**Changing your design.** If it parses and reads cleanly, it is done.

## Adapting It

**Applying at volume?** Group postings by role type, build two or three base versions, and per-application work drops to checks 2, 3 and 5 — about five minutes.

**A role you really want?** Run the full list, then spend extra time only on check 8 — the top third is where a deeper rework pays.

**A stretch role?** Weight check 5 heavily. Your relevance is less obvious and needs surfacing in the employer's language.

**A career change?** This checklist is not enough on its own; the framing needs deeper work before the per-application layer helps.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I really need to check parsing every time?**
No — only when you change the layout or export from a different tool. It is structural, not per application.

**What if I do not have time for all nine?**
Do 1, 2 and 3. Reading the posting properly, fixing the summary and reordering the top bullet is most of the benefit in five minutes.

**Should I track which version I sent where?**
Yes, if you are applying at volume. A one-line note per application prevents the awkward interview moment where you cannot remember what you sent.

**How do I know when my base resume is good enough to stop fixing it?**
When every bullet under your two most recent roles states an outcome and the copy-paste test is clean. After that, work is per application.

**Is ten minutes really enough?**
For a well-matched posting with a solid base, yes. The checklist exists to stop the work expanding past the point of return.

The reading step and the renaming step are where the time goes, and both get quicker when you can see which terms actually matter.
