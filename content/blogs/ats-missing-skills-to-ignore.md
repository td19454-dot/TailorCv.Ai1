## Key Takeaways

- Scanners tokenise the entire posting, including culture text, benefits and legal boilerplate - none of which are requirements.
- Seven recognisable categories account for most false flags.
- Clustered alternatives ("Tableau, Power BI, Looker") usually mean "one of these," not all three.
- Acting on false flags is how resumes end up padded and unconvincing.
- The remaining genuine gaps are usually a short, manageable list.

A scan does not know which parts of a job posting are requirements. It reads the whole page - the role description, the culture paragraph, the benefits, the equal-opportunity statement - and reports words that do not appear on your resume.

That is why "collaborative," "PTO" and the company's own name turn up as missing keywords. Learning to discard these quickly is most of what makes a report usable.

## The Seven Categories

**1. Culture and personality language.** Fast-paced, dynamic, self-starter, team player, passionate, detail-oriented, go-getter.

These describe a desired temperament, not a checkable skill. Adding "self-starter" to your resume satisfies nothing and costs you a line. If you want to convey the trait, do it through evidence - a bullet showing you started something without being asked.

**2. Company nouns.** The employer's name, product names, internal team names, their office locations.

You cannot have prior experience with a company you have not worked at, and nobody expects you to. Ignore entirely.

**3. Benefits and administrative text.** Health insurance, 401(k), PTO, hybrid, equity, visa sponsorship, equal opportunity employer.

This is contract and compliance language that happened to sit on the same page. It is never a requirement.

**4. Clustered alternatives.** The posting lists Tableau, Power BI and Looker. Or AWS, Azure and GCP. Or Jira, Asana and Monday.

Several similar tools listed together almost always means "one of these is fine." If you have one, that cluster is satisfied. Listing all three because the scan flagged them is transparent padding, and it is the kind that gets probed in an interview.

**5. Generic verbs and business nouns.** Manage, collaborate, deliver, support, drive, execute, synergy, ownership, impact.

These appear in every posting and most resumes. If your resume describes real work, these concepts are already present in substance. Inserting the literal words adds nothing.

**6. Seniority and framing words.** Senior, junior, lead, principal, experienced, proven track record.

These describe the level of the role, not a skill to list. Your seniority is communicated by your titles, scope and dates.

**7. Degree and certification names you do not hold.** Flagged because the posting names them; not actionable because you either have the credential or you do not.

If you hold it, it should be on the resume already. If you do not, adding the name is a false claim about a verifiable fact - the easiest kind to get caught on.

## The Test That Sorts Them

For any flagged term, ask: **could a specific person's resume genuinely evidence this, and could they be asked about it in an interview?**

- "Kubernetes" - yes. Real skill, real follow-up question. Act on it if you have it.
- "Fast-paced environment" - no. Nobody has ever been asked to describe their fast-paced-environment experience. Ignore.
- "Stakeholder management" - yes. Real practice, real follow-up. Act on it if you have done it.
- "401(k)" - no. Ignore.

The question filters almost everything correctly, and it takes a second per term.

## What a Real Sort Looks Like

A posting for a mid-level data analyst produced nineteen flagged terms:

> SQL, Python, Tableau, Power BI, Looker, dbt, Snowflake, stakeholder management, A/B testing, fast-paced, self-starter, cross-functional, Acme Analytics, hybrid working, equity, detail-oriented, communication, Agile, bachelor's degree

Applying the categories:

- **Ignore as culture:** fast-paced, self-starter, detail-oriented, communication, cross-functional
- **Ignore as company or benefits:** Acme Analytics, hybrid working, equity
- **Clustered - one is enough:** Tableau / Power BI / Looker
- **Credential - have it or not:** bachelor's degree
- **Probably secondary:** Agile

That leaves **SQL, Python, dbt, Snowflake, stakeholder management, A/B testing** - six genuine items, of which you likely have several already and simply need to name correctly.

Nineteen became six, and the six are specific enough to act on.

## The Cost of Acting on False Flags

Three things go wrong when you clear the whole list.

Your skills section fills with terms you cannot discuss, and interviewers select from that section when choosing what to probe.

Your bullets acquire parenthetical keyword clusters that read as obviously machine-fitted, which recruiters recognise immediately.

And your genuinely strong evidence gets diluted - the two things you are excellent at now sit among fifteen things you have heard of.

The resume scores better and performs worse.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**If a term is flagged, doesn't that mean the employer wants it?**
Not necessarily. The scan flags words present in the posting and absent from your resume, regardless of whether that word was a requirement, a benefit, or a description of office culture.

**How do I know whether clustered tools mean "one of these"?**
Position and phrasing. Several similar tools listed adjacently without separate requirements attached almost always means flexibility. If the posting says "must have Tableau" specifically, that is different.

**Should I add soft-skill keywords anywhere on my resume?**
Better to evidence them than to list them. A bullet showing you resolved a conflict conveys more than the word "collaborative" in a skills line.

**What if ignoring these leaves my score lower?**
It usually does, slightly. A moderately scoring honest resume outperforms a high-scoring padded one at the stage that decides your outcome, which is the human read.

**Is there any case for adding a term I barely know?**
Only if you can genuinely discuss it for two minutes. If not, you are trading a marginal scoring gain for an interview risk.
