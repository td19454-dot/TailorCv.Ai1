## Key Takeaways

- New Zealand is a **small market** — around five million people — which changes search tactics fundamentally: networks and direct approaches matter more than volume.
- **Auckland holds roughly a third of the population** and the majority of corporate employment; Wellington is government and technology; Christchurch is engineering and rebuild-driven construction.
- The **Green List** identifies occupations in genuine shortage and carries streamlined residence pathways.
- **Post-study work rights** are available to graduates of New Zealand institutions, with duration and conditions depending on the qualification.
- **Cultural fit is weighted heavily**, and New Zealand employers are explicit about wanting people who will work well in a small, informal team.

## What a small market changes

This is the single most useful reframing for anyone arriving from a large economy.

**There are fewer roles, and fewer applicants per role.** Both halves matter. A specialist position in Auckland might attract a fraction of the applications the equivalent role in London would.

**Everyone knows everyone.** Professional communities are small enough that a reference from the right person carries disproportionate weight, and a bad reputation travels fast.

**Direct approaches work.** Emailing a hiring manager at a mid-sized company is a normal thing to do in New Zealand and it is frequently answered. The formality that would make this awkward in Switzerland or Japan is largely absent.

**Fewer structured graduate programmes exist**, simply because there are fewer very large employers. Direct-entry roles, internships and small-company hiring account for most graduate employment.

**The consequence for your strategy:** twenty well-researched, personally addressed approaches beat two hundred portal submissions. This is genuinely a different game from the UK or US graduate market.

| **Wellington** | Central government, policy, and a substantial technology and film-effects sector |

**Wellington's public sector is a genuine graduate employer** with structured intakes, and it is under-considered by international candidates who assume government roles are closed to them. Many are open to residents and some to work-visa holders.

**Christchurch's engineering base is real**, built partly around post-earthquake reconstruction and increasingly around aerospace and agricultural technology.

## The Green List and why it matters

New Zealand publishes a **Green List** of occupations in genuine shortage, and it is the most useful single document for anyone planning a career here.

**Two tiers.** Some occupations offer a **straight to residence** pathway; others offer a **work to residence** pathway after a qualifying period of employment.

**It shapes which qualification is worth having.** Healthcare, engineering, construction trades, technology and teaching feature heavily. If you are choosing between fields or between specialisms, checking this list first is a rational thing to do.

**It is revised.** Occupations are added and removed as shortages change, so consult Immigration New Zealand directly rather than a summary.

**Employer accreditation matters too.** Employers hiring migrants on the Accredited Employer Work Visa must be accredited, which — as in the Netherlands — gives you a way to identify employers already committed to international hiring.

## What New Zealand employers look for

Beyond competence, three things come up consistently.

**Cultural fit, stated openly.** New Zealand workplaces are informal, flat and collaborative, and employers say plainly that they are hiring for how someone will fit a small team. This is not code for anything — it means they want someone easy to work with.

**A demonstrated commitment to staying.** Employers have been burned by people who treat New Zealand as a working holiday. Explaining why you want to be there specifically, and for how long, addresses a real concern.

**Practical, hands-on capability.** In a small market people wear several hats, and a candidate who can only do one narrow thing is less useful than one who will pick up adjacent work.

**A note on Te Ao Māori.** Awareness of Māori culture and Te Reo is increasingly valued, particularly in the public sector and in organisations with a Treaty of Waitangi obligation. You are not expected to be fluent, but ignorance of why it matters reads poorly.

## Writing a CV for New Zealand

The convention sits close to the Australian and British one, with a few local specifics.

**Two to three pages is acceptable**, which is more generous than the UK. Content over compression.

**No photo, no date of birth, no marital status.** New Zealand takes anti-discrimination seriously and these are conventionally omitted.

**State your work rights explicitly.** "New Zealand citizen", "resident", "post-study work visa valid until [date]" or "requires Accredited Employer Work Visa" — employers screen on this and ambiguity costs you.

**Referees are expected**, named with contact details, and they are genuinely called. This is a small market and references carry weight beyond the formality they represent elsewhere.

**Include community and volunteer involvement.** New Zealand employers read it as a signal about how you will fit a team, and it is not padding here.

**Outcomes, not duties**, with numbers wherever they exist. The standard is the same as anywhere; the market simply gives you space to say more.

**Run it through an [ATS check](/ats-analysis)** — larger employers screen automatically even in a small market.

## Salaries and cost of living

The honest picture, because it is easy to be surprised in either direction.

**Salaries are lower than Australia's** for equivalent roles, in most sectors noticeably so. This is the main reason New Zealanders move across the Tasman, and it is a real consideration.

**Auckland housing is expensive** relative to income, comparable in seriousness to Sydney's problem though on smaller absolute numbers.

**KiwiSaver** is the retirement savings scheme — you contribute a percentage of pay, your employer contributes as well, and the government adds an annual contribution subject to conditions. Opting out is possible and usually unwise.

**ACC** is the Accident Compensation Corporation, a universal no-fault injury scheme funded by levies including one on your earnings. It replaces the ability to sue for personal injury, and it means injury cover is genuinely universal.

**Tax is straightforward.** PAYE is deducted at source, there are no state or regional income taxes, and most employees never file a return.

**Quality of life is the trade.** Lower salaries, high housing costs in Auckland, and an outdoor environment and pace of life that people move continents for. Whether that trade is worth it is a personal question, not a financial one.

## Where roles are advertised, and how to go around them

A small number of channels carry the advertised market, and the unadvertised market is proportionally larger than in bigger economies.

**Seek NZ** is the dominant board, as it is in Australia.

**Trade Me Jobs** is genuinely significant here in a way that has no international equivalent — the country's main classifieds platform carries a large share of listings.

**LinkedIn** works well in Auckland and Wellington and less so elsewhere.

**Government jobs portals** carry the substantial Wellington public-sector market, which runs its own recruitment separately.

**University careers services** connect to local employers directly and are worth using if you studied in New Zealand.

**And then go around all of it.** Identify companies you want to work for, find the person who would manage you, and write to them with something specific about what they do and what you would bring. In a country of five million this is normal behaviour and it produces interviews that no job board would have given you.

## Interviewing in New Zealand

Informal in register, serious in substance.

**First names from the outset.** Titles and formality are minimal, and over-formality reads as stiff rather than respectful.

**Behavioural questions dominate.** Prepare specific examples with concrete outcomes, and expect follow-up questions that probe whether the story is real.

**Modesty is expected but so is clarity.** New Zealand culture is uncomfortable with self-promotion, and the way through is factual specificity — describe what happened and let the numbers carry the claim.

**Be ready for the fit question.** "How would you get on with a small team?" is a real question here, not a filler one.

**Ask about the team and the work**, not only about progression. In a small organisation, curiosity about what people actually do lands well.

**Reference checks happen and they are thorough.** Brief your referees properly, because in this market the person calling may well know them.

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: You are applying from overseas

Lead with why New Zealand specifically and for how long. Employers have a genuine concern about people treating the country as a stopover, and addressing it directly puts you ahead of applicants who do not.

### Scenario 2: Your occupation is on the Green List

Say so explicitly in your application, and check whether yours is straight-to-residence or work-to-residence. It changes both your pitch and your planning.

### Scenario 3: You are getting no response from job boards

Switch tactics entirely. Identify twenty target employers, find the actual hiring manager, and write to them individually. In a market this size that works in a way it does not in London.

> **Tip:** Send twenty personally researched approaches instead of two hundred portal applications. New Zealand's market is small enough that a direct, specific email to a hiring manager is normal, welcome and frequently answered.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Check the Green List first
- Approach employers directly
- Explain why New Zealand, specifically
- Target accredited employers if you need a visa
- Consider Wellington's public sector
- Show you will pick up adjacent work

### Don't

- Apply by volume through portals alone
- Assume Auckland is the only option
- Treat cultural fit language as meaningless
- Ignore the Green List when choosing a specialism
- Present New Zealand as a stopover
- Dismiss Te Ao Māori awareness as irrelevant

## Common Mistakes

- **Applying by volume.** In a market this small, personal approaches outperform portals decisively.
- **Ignoring the Green List.** It determines both job prospects and residence pathways.
- **Not addressing commitment.** Employers genuinely worry about candidates treating the country as a stopover.
- **Overlooking Wellington.** The public sector is a substantial and under-considered graduate employer.
- **Over-specialising.** Small teams value people who pick up adjacent work.
- **Underestimating references.** The professional community is small and reputation travels.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is the New Zealand job market too small to bother with?

Small, but with proportionally fewer applicants per role and a genuine skills shortage in listed occupations. The tactics differ; the opportunity is real.

### What is the Green List?

A published list of occupations in genuine shortage, with straight-to-residence and work-to-residence pathways attached. It is the most useful single document for planning a career in New Zealand.

### Do New Zealand employers run graduate programmes?

Some, particularly in government, banking and professional services — but fewer than in the UK or Australia, because there are fewer very large employers. Direct-entry roles dominate.

### Does direct outreach to employers actually work?

Yes, considerably better than in larger markets. Emailing a hiring manager at a mid-sized company is normal here and is frequently answered.

### Why do employers keep asking why I chose New Zealand?

Because many international candidates treat it as a stopover. A clear, specific answer about why here and for how long addresses a genuine concern.

### What is an accredited employer?

An employer approved to hire migrants under the Accredited Employer Work Visa. Like the Dutch sponsor register, it identifies companies already committed to international hiring.
### How long should a New Zealand CV be?

Two to three pages, which is more generous than the UK convention. Omit photo, date of birth and marital status.

### What is KiwiSaver?

New Zealand's retirement savings scheme. You contribute a percentage of pay, your employer contributes too, and the government adds an annual amount subject to conditions.

### Are New Zealand salaries lower than Australian ones?

Generally yes, noticeably so in most sectors. It is the main reason New Zealanders move to Australia, and it is a real factor to weigh.
### Where should I look for New Zealand jobs?

Seek NZ and Trade Me Jobs carry most of the advertised market, with LinkedIn strong in Auckland and Wellington. The government portals cover the substantial public sector separately.

### What is the interview style like?

Informal in register and serious in substance — first names, behavioural questions with follow-ups, and a genuine focus on how you would fit a small team.
