## Key Takeaways

- A large share of UK graduate employers and roles sit outside London, despite London dominating student attention and recruiting visibility.
- The real income comparison between London and regional cities is frequently closer than the salary comparison suggests, once housing is properly counted.
- Several UK cities have genuine, deep sector clusters, not a scaled-down version of London, but a real market in their own right.
- Competition for regional roles is consistently lower relative to quality, because most students apply to London by default.
- Employers with multiple UK offices sometimes allow you to express a location preference, and asking early matters.

## The real distribution of jobs

**London is the largest single market and it is not the whole market.** A substantial share of UK graduate employment sits outside it, across finance, technology, law, accountancy, engineering, healthcare and the public sector.

**Several regional cities have genuine sector depth.** Manchester and Leeds for finance and technology. Edinburgh and Glasgow for finance and life sciences. Bristol for technology, aerospace and finance. Birmingham for law, professional services and engineering. Cambridge for technology and life sciences specifically. These are not smaller copies of London, they are distinct, substantial markets with their own employer bases.

**Public sector and NHS roles are genuinely national**, distributed everywhere rather than concentrated, the [NHS careers guide](/blog/uk-nhs-graduate-scheme-guide) covers how widely non-clinical roles are spread.

**Why students still concentrate on London anyway.** Visibility, not availability. London recruiting events, London-based societies and the sheer volume of London postings create the impression that is where the jobs are, when the accurate statement is that is where the applications are.

## The real cost comparison

This is the arithmetic most graduates never do. A London salary that looks higher on the offer letter frequently produces a lower real income than a regional offer, once housing is properly counted.

Housing is the dominant variable, exactly as in [any city comparison](/blog/usa-relocation-and-cost-of-living-first-job). London rent for a comparable standard of living is substantially higher than in most regional cities, often by a larger margin than the salary premium itself.

Build the real comparison properly. Take the net salary for each offer, subtract twelve months of realistic rent for somewhere you would actually live in each city, and compare what remains, the same method that applies to any location decision.

The result surprises most people. A regional offer with a lower headline salary frequently leaves more disposable income at the end of the month than the London equivalent.

## Why competition is lower

**The mechanism is simple.** Most students default to applying in London because that is where the visible recruiting activity concentrates. Regional offices of the same employers, and genuinely regional employers, receive a fraction of the applications for comparable roles.

**Which means the ratio of applicants to seats is consistently more favourable outside London.** This is not a statement about quality, the roles are frequently identical in substance, sometimes with earlier responsibility because regional offices are smaller.

**Structured graduate schemes with multiple UK locations** sometimes let you indicate a preference. Ask early, before placement decisions are made, rather than assuming you have no influence.

## Building a regional-inclusive search

Add regional offices of your target employers to your list, not only their London office, many large firms recruit into both.

Search specifically for genuinely regional and city-specific employers, which never appear if you search only for nationally recognisable names.

Check whether a milkround or careers fair visits your target regional cities, many do, in addition to the London-centric events, and they are consistently less crowded.

Ask alumni working outside London what the market is actually like in their city, a fifteen-minute conversation tells you more than any general guide.

Do not assume career progression requires London eventually. Many careers, including senior ones, are built entirely in regional markets, and internal transfer to London later remains available if you want it.

## Researching a specific regional city properly before committing

**Spend real time researching the specific character of a regional city's employer base**, rather than relying on a general national reputation for that city's economy, a city with genuine strength in one sector can be a weak choice for a different one, matching your specific target sector to the specific city's actual strength matters more than the city's general prestige.

**Visit if genuinely possible before making a final decision**, or at minimum speak with people currently working there in your target field, a first-hand account of what daily life and the local professional community actually feel like is worth considerably more than research alone.

> **Tip:** Run the real comparison: take each salary, subtract actual rent for the specific city, and compare what remains. Regional roles frequently win that calculation outright.

## Common Mistakes

- **Applying only in London by default.** The most common and least examined choice graduates make.
- **Comparing salary, not cost of living.** Comparing salaries without comparing real cost of living. The headline number misleads more often than it helps.
- **Assuming regional means lesser.** Assuming regional roles are lesser versions of London ones. Frequently the same substance, sometimes with earlier responsibility.
- **Not stating a location preference.** Not asking about location preference at structured schemes. Some genuinely allow input if you ask early.
- **Ignoring genuinely regional employers entirely.** Ignoring genuinely regional employers entirely a market that never appears in a London-centric search.
- **Assuming a regional start traps you.** Assuming a regional start closes off London later. Internal transfer remains a normal route if you want it eventually.

## Adjusting your search strategy for a regional target

Regional employers are less likely to run large, highly visible national recruitment campaigns, worth searching more directly and specifically for regional-specific opportunities, rather than assuming the same channels that surface London-based roles will surface an equivalent regional opportunity.

Local university career services in target regional cities, even ones you did not attend, sometimes maintain useful regional employer relationships and job boards worth checking, alongside your own institution's resources.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do regional cities really have the same quality of graduate jobs?

In several sectors, yes, genuine sector clusters exist with real depth, not simply smaller branches of London firms.

### Is the pay cut moving to a regional city worth it?

Run the real comparison including housing costs. It is frequently smaller than it looks, and sometimes the regional offer produces more disposable income overall.

### How do I find regional employers if most search results default to London?

Search specifically by city, check regional milkround and careers fair schedules, and ask alumni working outside London directly.

### Will starting my career outside London limit me later?

Not typically. Internal transfer and lateral moves to London remain available later if you want them, and many strong careers are built entirely in regional markets.

### Which UK cities have the strongest graduate markets outside London?

Manchester, Leeds, Edinburgh, Glasgow, Bristol, Birmingham and Cambridge each have genuine sector depth, varying by field.

### Should I mention a location preference in my application?

Yes, if the employer has multiple UK offices, ask early, before placement decisions are finalised, rather than assuming you have no say.
