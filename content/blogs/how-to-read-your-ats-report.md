## Key Takeaways

- A missing-terms list is a list of differences, not a list of failures - most items on it require no action.
- Sort every flagged term into one of four buckets: have it (reword), can get it (add honestly), do not have it (ignore), or noise (ignore).
- The terms worth acting on are usually two or three, not twenty.
- Terms you cannot honestly claim are information about fit, not gaps to paper over.
- Fix the rewording bucket first; it is the cheapest and produces most of the movement.

The report says 64% and lists nineteen missing keywords. The instinct is to work down the list until it is empty. That instinct produces padded, unconvincing resumes and takes an hour per application.

A missing-terms list is not a to-do list. It is a comparison of two documents, and most of the differences do not matter.

## The Four Buckets

Read the list once and put every item into one of four buckets. This takes about three minutes and replaces an hour of aimless editing.

**Bucket 1 - You have it, you called it something else.** The posting says "stakeholder management"; you wrote "client liaison." You have done this work. This is the highest-value bucket and usually the largest.

**Bucket 2 - You have it, you left it out.** Genuinely part of your experience, just not on this version of the resume. Cheap to add, entirely honest.

**Bucket 3 - You do not have it.** The posting wants Kubernetes; you have never used Kubernetes. Nothing to do here. Do not add it, do not imply it.

**Bucket 4 - Noise.** Terms like "fast-paced environment," "team player," "self-starter," the company's own name, or a benefit description that got scraped into the requirements. These are artefacts of how the scan tokenises the posting, not requirements.

Bucket 4 is usually a third of any list. Bucket 3 is often another third. That leaves a handful of items that actually deserve your attention.

## Working Through Bucket 1

This is where the score moves, and it moves without you claiming anything untrue.

> **Posting asks for:** cross-functional collaboration, requirements gathering, process documentation
>
> **Your resume currently says:** Worked with sales and ops teams to understand what they needed and wrote up how the new system should work.
>
> **Rewritten:** Led cross-functional collaboration with sales and operations, running requirements gathering sessions and owning process documentation for the new system.

Identical work. Identical truth. The second version is legible to a scanner matching against that posting, and it reads more professionally to a human too - which is the test that matters.

The caution: do this where the term genuinely describes what you did. If "requirements gathering" is a grander name than the two conversations you had, do not use it. You will be asked about it.

## Working Through Bucket 2

Straightforward. If you have used the tool or done the work and it is simply absent from this version, add it where it belongs - the skills section, or inside the bullet that describes the relevant work.

One rule: add it to the place where it is evidenced, not only to the skills list. A term that appears in your skills section and nowhere else in your history is weak evidence to a human reader, even when it satisfies a scanner.

## Leaving Bucket 3 Alone

This is the part people find hardest. The report flags a requirement you do not meet, in a colour that implies you should fix it.

You cannot fix it, and the attempts available to you are all bad:

- Adding the term to your skills list - defensible to a scanner, indefensible in an interview.
- Writing "familiar with X" for something you read about once - transparent to anyone who asks a follow-up.
- Burying it in a bullet where it did not happen - the worst option, because it is a straightforward false claim.

The honest read is that this posting wants something you do not have. If it is one item among many, apply anyway. If it is the central requirement repeated throughout the posting, that is a signal to spend your time on a better-matched role.

Either way, the correct action on Bucket 3 is none.

## What the Score Is Doing Underneath

Understanding this stops the list feeling like a verdict.

The scan tokenises the posting, tokenises your resume, and compares. It has no model of your career. It cannot tell that "led the migration" and "migration experience" are the same thing unless the matching is generous, and it cannot tell that you would learn Kubernetes in a fortnight.

That is why the report needs a human pass. You are supplying the judgment the tool does not have: which differences are real and which are vocabulary.

## A Three-Minute Routine

**1. Skim the list and delete the noise.** Company names, culture phrases, benefits language. Gone.

**2. Mark the ones you can honestly claim.** These are Buckets 1 and 2 - your actual worklist.

**3. Rewrite two or three lines** to use the posting's term for work you already did.

**4. Add anything from Bucket 2** to the place it is evidenced.

**5. Ignore the rest and apply.**

If your worklist is empty and the score is still low, you have learned something useful: the gap is real, not cosmetic.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Should I try to clear the entire missing-keywords list?**
No. A cleared list usually means a resume that has absorbed the posting's language wholesale, which reads badly to humans and is hard to defend in interview.

**Why does the report flag words like "team player"?**
Because it tokenises the whole posting, including culture and benefits text. Those tokens are not requirements and can be ignored.

**Is it dishonest to use the posting's exact wording?**
Not when it describes work you genuinely did. Using the employer's vocabulary for your real experience is translation, not deception. Using it for work you did not do is the line.

**What if every missing keyword is something I do not have?**
Then the scan has told you the match is weak, which is useful. Apply if you want, but weight your time toward better-fitting postings.

**How many terms should I actually act on?**
Usually two or three. If your worklist is longer than five, you are probably including items from Bucket 3.

## Run One and Sort It

The sorting method above works on any report, from any tool. It is worth doing by hand once so the four buckets become automatic.
