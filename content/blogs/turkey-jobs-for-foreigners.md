## Key Takeaways

- Turkiye has a **large and diversified economy** with a genuine technology sector, particularly strong in gaming.
- **Turkish is required** for most roles, with international companies and technology being the main exceptions.
- **Istanbul dominates** professional employment, with Ankara, Izmir and Bursa holding real sectors.
- **Currency instability is the defining economic feature** and it shapes compensation decisions profoundly.
- **Work permits are employer-sponsored** and tied to specific quota and salary conditions.

## The economy

Scale and structure.

**Manufacturing** is substantial — automotive, textiles, white goods, machinery and increasingly defence, with Turkish companies exporting across Europe, the Middle East and Central Asia.

**Construction and contracting**, where Turkish firms are internationally significant and operate across a wide region.

**Tourism**, which is enormous and one of the largest foreign currency earners.

**Agriculture and food processing**, which is large and export-oriented.

**Financial services**, with a substantial banking sector.

**Technology**, which is the growth story and treated separately below.

**Defence and aerospace**, which has expanded considerably and now includes genuinely significant indigenous programmes.

**And logistics**, given a geographic position between Europe, Asia and the Middle East that is genuinely strategic rather than merely rhetorical.

## Technology and gaming

Turkiye's most internationally visible sector.

**Mobile gaming is a genuine Turkish strength.** Peak Games and Rollic were acquired by international buyers at significant valuations, Dream Games raised substantially, and the country has produced a cluster of successful mobile game studios that is disproportionate to its size.

**The reasons are specific** — a large young population, strong technical education, a domestic market that provided early testing, and successful exits that recycled talent and capital into new studios.

**Fintech and e-commerce**, with Getir having become internationally known and Trendyol operating at genuine scale in e-commerce.

**Enterprise software and IT services**, serving domestic and regional clients.

**Defence technology**, which employs substantial engineering talent and has become a significant sector.

**And outsourced development** for European clients, helped by time zone proximity and cost position.

**For an engineer**, gaming is the clearest international opportunity, and the studios recruit with genuine ambition.

## Where the work is

| City | What concentrates there |
|---|---|
| **Istanbul** | Finance, technology, gaming, media, corporate headquarters, the overwhelming majority of professional employment |
| **Ankara** | Government, defence, aerospace, universities, a growing technology sector |
| **Izmir** | Trade, manufacturing, tourism, a more relaxed coastal alternative |
| **Bursa** | Automotive manufacturing and components, a genuine industrial centre |
| **Kocaeli and Gebze** | Heavy industry, chemicals, logistics near Istanbul |
| **Antalya** | Tourism, seasonal and enormous |

**Istanbul dominates** to a degree that surprises people, holding the great majority of professional employment across almost every white-collar sector, and it is a city of genuine scale.

**Ankara is the second professional centre**, driven by government, defence and the universities, with a considerably more ordered daily life.

**Izmir has become a real alternative**, particularly for people prioritising quality of life over career depth.

**And Bursa and Kocaeli** are serious industrial centres that are frequently overlooked by anyone thinking about Turkiye as a technology destination.

## The currency question

The factor that shapes every decision in this market, and one that no honest guide can treat lightly.

**Turkish inflation has run high for an extended period**, and the lira has depreciated substantially against major currencies over several years.

**What this means practically** is that a salary agreed in lira loses purchasing power over the course of a year unless it is reviewed, and annual review is not always sufficient.

**Many employers review more frequently**, and this is a legitimate and important thing to ask about at offer stage.

**Some international employers pay in foreign currency** or index to it, which changes the picture entirely and is worth seeking out.

**Savings behave differently.** People hold foreign currency, gold or hard assets in ways that would be unusual in a stable-currency economy, and this is rational rather than exotic.

**Costs have risen substantially** in local terms while remaining low when converted from a strong currency, which is why Turkiye simultaneously feels expensive to residents and cheap to visitors.

**For a foreign professional**, the practical conclusion is to negotiate the denomination and review frequency as carefully as the amount, because those terms determine what the offer is actually worth.

## Language and daily life

The practical picture.

**Turkish is required** for most professional employment outside the international and technology sectors.

**English is genuinely used** at international companies, gaming studios, technology firms and in tourism.

**Turkish is a harder language** for speakers of Indo-European languages than Spanish or Portuguese, being agglutinative and structurally unfamiliar, and this should be factored into any plan honestly.

**Daily life in Istanbul** is manageable with limited Turkish in the international districts and considerably harder elsewhere.

**Hospitality is genuine** and Turkish social warmth toward foreigners is real and frequently remarked on.

**And the cultural offer is exceptional** — the food, the history, the geography and the sheer interest of Istanbul as a city are consistently what residents cite first.

## Finding work

The practical channels.

**LinkedIn is well used** in Turkiye, particularly in technology and by international employers.

**Kariyer.net** is the largest domestic job board by volume.

**Secretcv.com** and **Yenibiris** for broader coverage.

**Company career pages** for the gaming studios and larger technology employers, most of which recruit directly.

**Recruitment agencies**, active particularly in manufacturing and executive search.

**Referrals matter considerably**, and Turkish professional networks are relationship-driven.

**For gaming specifically**, the studios recruit internationally and through specialist channels, and a portfolio of shipped work is what matters.

**Apply in Turkish** unless the posting is in English, and run the document through an [ATS check](/ats-analysis) since Kariyer.net and the larger employers screen at volume.

**And publish your work** — a [portfolio](/portfolio) with deployed projects or shipped games does more than any description.

## Working culture

Calibrating expectations.

**Relationships come first** and business is built on personal trust to a degree that surprises people from more transactional cultures.

**Hospitality is genuine and important**, extending well into professional contexts — tea, meals and time spent together are part of the working relationship rather than a distraction from it.

**Hierarchy is respected** and seniority carries real weight, though technology companies are considerably flatter.

**Communication is indirect** in traditional settings and more direct in technology.

**Hours can be long**, particularly in the private sector and at startups.

**Personal warmth is real** and the social dimension of Turkish workplaces is genuine rather than performative.

**And bureaucracy is a recognised feature** of dealing with institutions, though digitalisation through the e-Devlet system has genuinely improved many processes.

## Building a career

Where a Turkish career leads.

**Within the gaming sector**, which now has enough successful studios to support real movement and genuine progression.

**Into international roles**, since Turkish companies operate across a wide region and regional responsibility is a common path.

**Abroad**, where Turkish engineers and particularly game developers are genuinely well regarded and Berlin, London and Amsterdam have substantial Turkish professional communities.

**Into founding**, supported by an ecosystem that has produced real exits and now has experienced operators and returning capital.

**Within manufacturing and defence**, both of which offer long-term technical careers.

**And into remote work for foreign employers**, which is attractive precisely because it removes the currency exposure that shapes everything else here.

## Setting up

The administrative sequence for foreigners.

**Work permit** from the Ministry of Labour and Social Security, applied for by your employer.

**The permit functions as a residence permit** for the period it covers, which simplifies matters compared with several other countries.

**Foreigner identification number**, which underpins banking, tax and most administrative interaction.

**Tax registration**, which follows.

**Social security registration** through SGK, handled by your employer.

**Bank account**, requiring your identification number and permit.

**Address registration**, which is an obligation rather than a formality.

**And e-Devlet**, the government digital services portal, which handles a genuinely wide range of administration once you have the credentials for it.

## Four common misreadings

Beliefs that mislead people about this market.

**"It is a cheap place to live."** It is cheap converted from a strong currency and it is not cheap on a Turkish salary, and confusing the two leads to bad decisions.

**"The technology sector is small."** Turkish mobile gaming has produced international acquisitions and raises at a scale disproportionate to the country's size, and the defence technology sector employs substantial engineering talent.

**"English is enough."** In gaming and international companies, frequently yes. Across the wider labour market, no.

**"Istanbul is the whole country."** It holds most professional employment and Ankara, Izmir, Bursa and Kocaeli have genuine sectors that are consistently overlooked.

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: You work in mobile gaming

Istanbul is a genuinely significant place to do it, with a cluster of successful studios, real capital and a track record of international exits. It is the strongest single argument for this market.

### Scenario 2: You are weighing a Turkish offer against another market

Model the currency exposure carefully. Turkish inflation has been high for an extended period and a salary negotiated in lira behaves very differently over a year than one in a stable currency.

### Scenario 3: You speak no Turkish

Your market is international companies, technology and gaming studios, where English is genuinely used. It exists and it is narrower than the wider labour market.

> **Tip:** Whatever else you negotiate, understand the currency position. Turkish inflation has run high for an extended period, and how your salary is denominated and how frequently it is reviewed matter more here than almost anywhere else on this list.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Consider the gaming sector specifically
- Negotiate with inflation and review frequency in mind
- Learn Turkish for anything outside the international sector
- Understand the work permit quota conditions
- Consider Ankara and Izmir alongside Istanbul
- Check diploma equivalence if regulated

### Don't

- Ignore currency exposure
- Assume English suffices outside technology
- Accept a salary without asking about review frequency
- Overlook the defence and manufacturing sectors
- Skip document apostille
- Assume the permit transfers between employers

## Common Mistakes

- **Ignoring the currency.** The most consequential factor in any Turkish offer.
- **Assuming English works everywhere.** The international sector is real and narrow.
- **Overlooking gaming.** The most internationally significant Turkish technology sector.
- **Not asking about salary review.** Frequency matters enormously under high inflation.
- **Underestimating manufacturing.** It is large and technically serious.
- **Assuming permit portability.** Work permits are employer-specific.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need Turkish to work in Turkiye?

For most roles, yes. International companies, technology firms and gaming studios use English genuinely, and outside that the working language is Turkish.

### Why is Turkish gaming significant?

A cluster of successful mobile game studios has produced major international acquisitions and raises, supported by a large young population, strong technical education and capital recycled from earlier exits.

### How does currency instability affect me?

Profoundly. High inflation over an extended period means how your salary is denominated and how often it is reviewed matter more than the headline figure.

### Which cities should I consider?

Istanbul for the great majority of professional employment, Ankara for government, defence and technology, Izmir for a more relaxed coastal option, and Bursa for automotive manufacturing.

### Is the work permit process difficult?

It is employer-sponsored and subject to quota and salary conditions, which means small employers frequently cannot sponsor even when they want to.

### What sectors are strongest?

Manufacturing across automotive and white goods, construction and contracting, tourism, defence and aerospace, and a technology sector led by mobile gaming.

Istanbul holds the great majority of professional employment, Ankara covers government, defence and universities, Izmir offers a coastal alternative, and Bursa and Kocaeli are serious industrial centres.

Negotiate the denomination and the review frequency as carefully as the amount. Some international employers pay in or index to foreign currency, which changes the picture entirely.
### How do I find work in Turkiye?

LinkedIn is well used in technology, Kariyer.net carries the largest domestic volume, and the gaming studios recruit directly and internationally where a portfolio of shipped work matters most.
