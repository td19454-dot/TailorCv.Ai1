## Key Takeaways

- Nearshoring is the **relocation of production and services from Asia to Mexico** to serve the North American market.
- **Manufacturing engineering, supply chain and quality** are the roles growing fastest.
- **The Bajio and the northern states** absorbed most of the expansion, with Monterrey and Queretaro leading.
- **Services nearshoring followed manufacturing** — engineering, software, finance and customer operations.
- **The advantages are structural**, meaning demand is durable rather than a temporary reaction to one policy.

## What nearshoring actually is

Being precise about a term that gets used loosely.

**It describes moving production closer to the end market** rather than to the lowest-cost location, and for the North American market that means Mexico.

**The drivers are concrete** — supply chain disruption exposed the fragility of very long routes, shipping costs and lead times became strategic rather than operational concerns, tariffs changed the arithmetic, and companies decided resilience was worth paying for.

**USMCA provides the framework**, replacing NAFTA and maintaining the rules of origin that make North American production advantageous.

**It is not only manufacturing.** Services followed, and engineering, software development, finance operations and customer support serving North America have all expanded in Mexico for the same time zone and proximity reasons.

**And it is not a temporary shift.** The advantages — geography, time zone, trade framework and cost position — do not depend on any particular administration, which is why companies have committed capital rather than merely relocating orders.

## The roles this creates

Where the actual jobs are.

**Manufacturing and industrial engineering** — process design, automation, lean and continuous improvement, which is the largest single category.

**Quality engineering**, particularly in automotive, aerospace and medical devices where certification requirements are demanding.

**Supply chain and logistics** — planning, procurement, customs, trade compliance and distribution, which nearshoring has made considerably more complex and therefore more valuable.

**Automation and controls**, as new plants are built with modern equipment.

**Design and product engineering**, increasingly moving alongside production rather than staying at headquarters.

**Software and IT services** serving North American clients.

**Finance and shared services**, running North American operations from Mexico.

**And bilingual management**, since these operations report to North American parents and need people who can operate in both directions.

## Where it is happening

| Region | What concentrates there |
|---|---|
| **Monterrey and Nuevo Leon** | Industry, heavy manufacturing, business services, corporate |
| **Queretaro and the Bajio** | Aerospace, automotive, IT services, fast growth |
| **Saltillo and Coahuila** | Automotive assembly and components |
| **Tijuana and Baja California** | Medical devices, electronics, cross-border services |
| **Ciudad Juarez and Chihuahua** | Electronics, automotive, established maquiladora base |
| **Guadalajara** | Electronics, technology, software |

**The Bajio has grown fastest**, combining Queretaro, Guanajuato, Aguascalientes and San Luis Potosi into a genuine industrial corridor with reasonable living conditions.

**Monterrey remains the industrial capital** with the deepest corporate infrastructure.

**The border cities** have the longest maquiladora history and the most direct cross-border integration.

**And infrastructure is the constraint** — water, electricity, housing and transport in the fastest-growing areas are under real pressure, which is the most cited practical limit on how fast this can continue.

## The maquiladora question

Worth addressing because the term carries dated associations.

**Maquiladoras are manufacturing plants operating under a specific customs regime**, importing components duty-free for assembly and re-export.

**The historical association is with low-skill, low-wage assembly**, and for a long period that was accurate for much of the sector.

**It is substantially less accurate now.** Modern operations in automotive, aerospace, medical devices and electronics involve sophisticated processes, demanding quality systems and genuine engineering.

**Aerospace in Queretaro** requires certification regimes that are among the most demanding in any industry.

**Medical devices in Tijuana** operate under regulatory requirements that leave no room for casual process control.

**Which means** dismissing the sector on the basis of its reputation costs engineers real opportunities, and the technical content of the work is frequently higher than the label suggests.

**Wages remain well below North American levels**, which is the point of the arrangement and should be understood honestly rather than resented or ignored.

## Skills and certifications

What the market actually rewards.

**Lean manufacturing and Six Sigma**, which are genuinely valued and frequently expected in process roles.

**Quality systems** — IATF 16949 for automotive, AS9100 for aerospace, ISO 13485 for medical devices — where certification familiarity is a direct hiring criterion.

**Automation and controls** including PLC programming and robotics, where the shortage is acute.

**Supply chain qualifications**, including APICS certifications which carry weight.

**Customs and trade compliance knowledge**, which USMCA rules of origin have made considerably more valuable.

**Project management**, particularly for plant expansion and new product introduction.

**And English**, which for nearshoring specifically is not optional at any level of responsibility, since the operations report to North American parents.

## Getting hired

Practical channels for this sector.

**LinkedIn**, where nearshoring recruiters are extremely active.

**OCCMundial** and **Computrabajo** for domestic volume.

**Specialist recruitment agencies** for engineering and manufacturing, which handle a large share of these roles and are worth engaging directly.

**Company career pages** for the major manufacturers, several of which recruit continuously in the growth regions.

**Industrial park and cluster organisations**, which frequently list openings and run events.

**University career services**, since the manufacturers recruit directly from Tec de Monterrey, the ITESM network and the regional technical universities.

**Apply in Spanish** with your English level stated clearly, and run the document through an [ATS check](/ats-analysis) since the large manufacturers screen at volume.

## Living in the nearshoring regions

Practical considerations that decide whether a role works.

**Queretaro** is consistently well regarded — manageable size, good infrastructure, reasonable costs and generally good safety outcomes.

**Monterrey** has the strongest corporate infrastructure, hot summers, higher costs and a distinctly business-oriented culture.

**Saltillo, Aguascalientes and San Luis Potosi** are smaller, cheaper and quieter, with less international community.

**Guadalajara** combines technology with a genuinely appealing city.

**Tijuana** offers direct San Diego proximity, with the border crossing being a real daily factor for those who cross.

**Ciudad Juarez** has the deepest maquiladora history and the most serious security reputation, which needs honest research rather than assumption in either direction.

**Housing pressure** in the fastest-growing areas is genuine, and arriving without a plan is harder than it was a few years ago.

**And international schooling** is available in the larger centres and thin in the smaller ones, which matters for anyone relocating a family.

## Career progression

Where a nearshoring career leads.

**Within a plant**, from engineer to supervisor to plant management, which is a genuine ladder at growing operations.

**Across the corporate group**, since these are subsidiaries of multinationals with internal mobility to North America, Europe and Asia.

**Into regional or corporate roles**, covering multiple plants or the Latin American operation.

**Into consulting**, where operational improvement expertise transfers well.

**Into the United States**, via internal transfer or the TN visa for Mexican nationals in listed professions including engineering.

**And into the supplier ecosystem**, which grows alongside the assemblers and frequently offers faster progression than the large plants.

**The internal mobility point is worth emphasising** — being inside a multinational in Mexico is one of the more reliable routes to an international career, and it is underused relative to how well it works.

## Four honest cautions

What the enthusiasm tends to skip.

**Infrastructure is straining.** Water availability in the Bajio, electricity capacity and housing supply in the fastest-growing areas are genuine constraints, and companies discuss them openly.

**Wages are the reason the work moved**, and expecting North American compensation misreads the entire arrangement.

**Policy risk exists.** Trade arrangements are reviewed and renegotiated, and while the underlying advantages are durable, specific rules are not permanent.

**And security varies by location** in ways that matter for daily life, particularly in some of the border manufacturing centres, and this deserves honest research rather than either dismissal or panic.

**None of this argues against the opportunity.** It argues for choosing the region and employer deliberately rather than following the headline.

## Comparing Mexico with the alternatives

Where nearshoring competes.

**Against China and Southeast Asia.** Lower unit cost and much longer lead times, greater supply chain exposure and no time zone overlap with North America.

**Against Eastern Europe.** A comparable nearshoring proposition for the European market rather than the North American one, and the two rarely compete for the same work.

**Against Central America and the Caribbean.** Smaller scale, less developed industrial infrastructure and a shallower engineering talent pool.

**Against reshoring to the United States.** Substantially higher labour costs, and a domestic manufacturing workforce that is genuinely constrained in several trades.

**Which leaves Mexico** as the practical option for most North American manufacturers wanting shorter, more resilient supply chains without American cost structures — and that structural position is why the investment has been sustained rather than tentative.

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: You are a manufacturing or industrial engineer

This is the clearest opportunity in the Mexican market. Monterrey, Queretaro and the Bajio have genuine expansion and the roles are real rather than speculative.

### Scenario 2: You work in supply chain

Nearshoring made North American supply chains more complex, not less, and trade compliance, customs and planning expertise has become correspondingly more valuable.

### Scenario 3: You are bilingual

You are in the most reliably valued category in this market. Operations reporting to North American parents need people who can operate in both languages and both business cultures, and the shortage is genuine.

> **Tip:** If you are bilingual and technically qualified, you are in the scarcest category in this entire market. Nearshoring operations report to North American parents and consistently struggle to find people who can lead a technical team in Spanish and represent it in English.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Target manufacturing engineering and quality
- Consider supply chain and trade compliance
- Research the Bajio specifically
- Pursue relevant certifications
- State your English level accurately
- Weigh living conditions alongside opportunity

### Don't

- Assume this means American salaries
- Ignore the secondary cities
- Overlook services nearshoring
- Dismiss the maquiladora sector as low-skill
- Skip safety research at city level
- Underestimate infrastructure constraints

## Common Mistakes

- **Assuming American pay.** The cost gap is the reason the work moved.
- **Overlooking services.** Software, finance and engineering services followed manufacturing.
- **Dismissing maquiladoras.** Modern operations are technically sophisticated.
- **Ignoring the Bajio.** The fastest-growing region and frequently the best living conditions.
- **Understating certifications.** They matter considerably in regulated manufacturing.
- **Ignoring infrastructure limits.** Water and housing pressure is real in the boom areas.
