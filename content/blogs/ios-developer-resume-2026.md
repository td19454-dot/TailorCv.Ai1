## How to Write iOS Resume Bullet Points

Formula:

Built / Shipped / Improved + [feature or app component] + [Swift/framework used] + [downloads, rating, crash rate, or performance result]

### Weak Bullet Points

- Built iOS apps using Swift
- Worked on SwiftUI screens
- Integrated APIs in the app
- Fixed crashes

### Strong Bullet Points

- Shipped a personal finance app in SwiftUI and MVVM with Core Data sync, reaching 180K App Store downloads and a 4.7 average rating within 8 months.
- Migrated the main feed from UIKit/UITableView to SwiftUI LazyVStack, reducing rendering time by 55% and eliminating a persistent scroll performance regression that had affected 20K daily users.
- Implemented a real-time chat feature using WebSockets (URLSessionWebSocketTask) and Combine, supporting 5K concurrent connections with automatic reconnection and message delivery receipts.
- Integrated StoreKit 2 for subscription management, reducing in-app purchase failure rate from 3.8% to 0.7% and improving subscription conversion by 12%.
- Reduced app crash rate from 2.4% to 0.3% by resolving 12 critical memory management issues identified through Instruments memory profiler and Crashlytics symbolication.

## Common iOS Resume Mistakes

### Mistake 1: No App Store evidence

If you have apps on the App Store, include download counts, ratings, or crash rates. This is concrete proof of ship-quality code.

### Mistake 2: UIKit only

In 2026, SwiftUI is increasingly expected alongside UIKit. If you only show UIKit, add SwiftUI [projects](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-add-projects-in-resume-for-freshers) before applying to modern iOS roles.

### Mistake 3: No architecture pattern

Production iOS code should have an architecture pattern. MVVM, VIPER, or Clean Architecture should appear on your [resume](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/resume-keyword-map-template).

### Mistake 4: No testing mentioned

XCTest unit tests and XCUITest UI tests should be on any senior iOS [resume](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-choose-resume-template).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should I include in my iOS developer resume header?
Your iOS developer resume header should prominently feature your name, contact information, and links to your professional profiles, such as your [GitHub](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/github-portfolio-optimization) and App Store. This helps potential employers easily find your work and assess your coding skills and projects.

### How can I optimize my resume for ATS in 2026?
To optimize your resume for ATS in 2026, incorporate relevant keywords from the job description and use an [ATS-friendly resume template](https://www.thetailorcv.com/templates). Additionally, ensure your technical skills, such as Swift and SwiftUI, are clearly listed to increase your chances of passing through the ATS filters.

### What format is best for an iOS developer resume?
The best format for an iOS developer resume includes a clear structure: start with a header, followed by a summary, technical skills, work experience, projects (with App Store links), and education. This format allows you to showcase your strengths effectively while maintaining clarity for hiring managers.

### How can I demonstrate my app development experience on my resume?
To demonstrate your app development experience, include specific metrics in your resume summary, such as the number of apps shipped, download counts, and average ratings. Highlight your experience with frameworks and architectures, such as MVVM or VIPER, to show your technical depth and ability to deliver high-quality applications.
