## Key Takeaways

- With limited time, three edits produce most of the available improvement: summary, skills order, top bullets.
- Skip anything that does not touch those three areas — a full rewrite is not the priority tonight.
- A rushed application with the core edits done beats a polished one submitted after the deadline.
- Check parsing before anything else — a five-minute check that a broken file cannot outrun.
- Ten minutes is enough to make a real difference if spent in the right order.

A posting closes tomorrow and you have an hour, not an evening. Here is what to actually do with that hour, in the order that produces the most improvement per minute.

## First: Confirm the File Actually Works (2 minutes)

Before touching content, [scan your resume](/ats-analysis) and glance at what was extracted. If your job titles, dates or skills are missing from the extraction, every other edit tonight is wasted effort — fix that first, and it usually takes less time than people expect. See [ATS formatting mistakes](/blog/ats-resume-formatting-mistakes) if something looks wrong.

If the extraction looks clean, move on immediately.

## Second: Read the Missing List (3 minutes)

Run the scan against this specific posting and read what comes back as missing. Do not skim — this list is your entire priority order for the next ten minutes.

Mentally sort it fast:

- **Things you have but did not say** — these are your edits tonight.
- **Things you genuinely lack** — ignore them. You cannot manufacture experience in an hour, and you should not try.

See [which missing skills actually matter](/blog/which-missing-skills-actually-matter) for the fuller version of this triage when you have more time; tonight, the fast version is enough.

## Third: Rewrite the Summary (5 minutes)

This is the single highest-return edit available in a time crunch. Two or three lines, naming the target role and the posting's two or three central requirements, using language close to the posting's own.

> Data analyst with 4 years in retail, focused on SQL reporting and inventory forecasting for merchandising teams.

Do not overthink the prose. A slightly rough but accurate summary beats a polished generic one every time under a deadline.

## Fourth: Reorder the Skills Section (3 minutes)

Move the posting's required tools to the top. Add anything true that is missing and that you have but had not listed. Do not add anything you cannot defend — see [improving your score without lying](/blog/improve-score-without-lying), because a rushed decision to stretch a claim is exactly the kind of mistake deadline pressure produces.

## Fifth: Touch the Top Three Bullets of Your Most Recent Role (5-8 minutes)

You do not have time to rewrite the whole resume, and you do not need to. The top of your most recent role gets read first and carries the most weight. If any of the "things you have but did not say" items belong there, insert the posting's language into the bullet that already proves it:

> Before: Built the reporting process for the merchandising team.
> After: Built weekly SQL-based reporting and inventory forecasting for the merchandising team.

Same claim, now matchable, and it took under a minute per bullet.

## If You Have Ten More Minutes

**Check your job title is recognisable.** If it is an internal or branded title, add the standard equivalent — see [job titles that don't match your industry](/blog/job-titles-dont-match-industry). One line, meaningful improvement.

**Pair any acronym the posting uses in the form you did not.** "SEO" versus "Search Engine Optimization" — write both once. See [acronyms vs full terms](/blog/acronyms-vs-full-terms-on-resume).

**Re-scan.** Confirm the specific items you targeted actually cleared — see [what to expect when you re-scan](/blog/rescan-after-editing-what-to-expect). This takes a minute and tells you whether the last thirty were well spent.

## What to Skip Tonight

Under real time pressure, these are not worth the minutes:

- **Redesigning the template.** Formatting changes risk breaking something you do not have time to test.
- **Rewriting your entire work history.** Older roles matter less to matching and to the human reader's first pass.
- **Perfecting the prose.** Grammatically rough but accurate beats polished but late.
- **Adding a cover letter from scratch**, if it costs you the resume edits. A strong resume without a cover letter beats a weak resume with one, in most first-pass filtering.

## A Realistic Hour, Minute by Minute

| Minutes | Task |
|---|---|
| 0-2 | Check extraction |
| 2-5 | Read missing list, sort mentally |
| 5-10 | Rewrite summary |
| 10-13 | Reorder skills |
| 13-21 | Edit top 3 bullets |
| 21-31 | Optional: title, acronyms, re-scan |
| 31-35 | Export, check the exported file, submit |

That leaves buffer. If you genuinely only have twenty minutes, do the summary and skills reorder and stop there — those two alone typically produce most of the available gain.

## Submitted Late Beats Not Submitted

If you are down to the wire, submit what you have rather than losing the deadline chasing a marginal improvement. A resume with the summary and skills fixed, submitted on time, outperforms a fully polished one submitted after the posting closes — which is not an application at all.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a rushed application worse than not applying?**
No, provided the core edits are done. A ten-minute tailored application beats both a generic one and a missed deadline.

**Should I skip the scan to save time?**
No — it is the step that tells you where to spend the rest of your time. Skipping it means guessing, which usually costs more time than it saves.

**What if I only have five minutes total?**
Rewrite the summary. It is the single highest-return edit and takes under five minutes.

**Can I fix the rest after submitting?**
No, but you can apply what you learned to the next application, and keep this version as a base — see [My Resumes](/my-resumes).

**Is it better to submit early or right before the deadline?**
Earlier is generally better where possible — many roles are effectively assessed before the posting closes.
