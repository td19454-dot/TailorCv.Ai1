## Key Takeaways

- Czechia has **very low unemployment** and a genuine labour shortage, which makes the market unusually open.
- **Automotive and manufacturing** dominate the economy — Škoda, and an enormous supplier base serving German industry.
- **Prague and Brno have real technology sectors**, including shared service centres, product companies and a strong startup scene in Brno.
- **Salaries are low by Western European standards** but rising, and costs remain moderate.
- The **Employee Card** is the main work and residence permit, with a fast-track programme for qualifying employers.

## The economy

Czechia is an industrial economy of genuine sophistication, tightly integrated with Germany.

**Automotive is the backbone.** Škoda Auto is a major manufacturer within the Volkswagen Group, and around it sits an extensive supplier ecosystem. Hyundai and Toyota also manufacture in the country. For automotive and mechanical engineers this is a serious market.

**Manufacturing and engineering** more broadly — machinery, electronics, precision components, aerospace parts.

**Shared service centres.** Prague and Brno host a very large number of multinational business service operations — [finance](/blog/resume-matching-insurance-jobs), IT, HR and customer support for European operations — and they recruit for language combinations continuously. This is the single most accessible entry for an international [graduate](/blog/first-resume-for-gen-z-2026).

**Technology and software.** Prague has product companies and international offices; Brno has a genuine technology and startup ecosystem anchored by strong technical [universities](/blog/how-to-list-education-on-resume) and a long-standing security software presence.

**Nanotechnology and materials research**, with real academic and applied strength.

**And extremely low unemployment.** Czechia has consistently had among the lowest unemployment rates in the EU, which means [employers](/blog/how-recruiters-spot-generic-resumes) compete for people rather than the reverse — a genuinely different dynamic from Spain or Italy.

## Prague and Brno

| | Prague | Brno |
|---|---|---|
| **Character** | Capital, corporate, international | University city, technical, startup-oriented |
| **Sectors** | Shared services, finance, technology, corporate HQs | Software, security, hardware, research, startups |
| **Cost of living** | Highest in Czechia | Meaningfully lower |
| **International community** | Large | Smaller but real |
| **English at work** | Widespread in the international sector | Widespread in technology |

**Brno is genuinely under-known internationally.** Two strong technical universities, a long history in security software, hardware and embedded work, and a startup community that punches well above the city's size.

**Ostrava and Plzeň** hold industrial employment with much lower costs again.

## Language and the international sector

**Czech is required for most of the domestic market** and it is a genuinely difficult Slavic language for most learners.

**Where English works:** shared service centres, technology companies, international corporates, research institutions and startups. This is a substantial slice.

**Shared service centres recruit for language combinations specifically.** English plus German, French, Dutch, Nordic or Italian makes you directly employable in Prague or Brno, frequently with no Czech at all. This is the most accessible route into the country and it is genuinely worth targeting.

**German is particularly valuable** given the integration with German industry.

**Czech opens the wider market** and social life, and even modest ability is appreciated disproportionately.

## Money and daily life

The financial picture.

**[Salaries](/blog/how-to-negotiate-salary-offer) are low by Western European standards and rising**, with technology and the international sector paying well above the national average.

**Costs are moderate.** Prague is the most expensive Czech city and remains considerably cheaper than Vienna or Munich; Brno, Ostrava and Plzeň are cheaper again.

**Income tax is relatively simple** with a progressive structure, and social and [health insurance](/blog/first-job-abroad-practical-checklist) contributions apply on both sides.

**Meal vouchers or a meal allowance** are a standard and tax-advantaged benefit widely offered.

**Public healthcare** is good and funded through contributions, with private options available.

**And Czechia's location matters.** Prague is within a few hours of Vienna, Berlin, Munich, Bratislava and Kraków by train, which makes the rest of Central Europe genuinely accessible.

## Getting set up

The administrative sequence.

**Residence registration**, which for EU citizens is straightforward and for others follows the Employee Card process.

**Birth number (rodné číslo)**, which functions as your administrative identifier.

**Health insurance registration**, public through employment or commercial beforehand.

**Bank account** and the usual practical setup.

**Tax registration**, handled mostly through payroll for employees.

**And a note on Czech administration:** it is more paper-oriented and appointment-dependent than Estonia's or Poland's digitised systems, and building in time for it is sensible.

## Sector deep dive

Where the actual roles are, beyond the summary.

**Automotive.** Škoda at Mladá Boleslav plus an extensive Tier 1 and Tier 2 supplier base across the country. Roles span design, manufacturing engineering, quality, supply chain and increasingly software as vehicles become more electronic.

**Business services.** Finance and accounting, IT support and infrastructure, HR operations, procurement, and increasingly analytics, treasury and advisory work. The centres have moved up the value chain considerably.

**Software and IT.** Product companies, international development centres, and a security software heritage particularly around Brno.

**Manufacturing and engineering.** Machinery, precision components, electronics and aerospace parts, integrated tightly with German supply chains.

**Pharmaceuticals and life sciences**, with real research capability.

**Nanotechnology and materials**, an area of genuine academic and applied strength.

**Energy**, including the nuclear sector and the transition programmes.

**And research institutions** including the Czech Academy of Sciences and the technical universities, which employ researchers internationally.

## Interviewing with Czech employers

What to expect once your [application](/blog/how-many-jobs-should-you-apply-to-per-day) lands.

**International sector [interviews](/blog/how-to-prepare-for-a-job-interview-in-2026-complete-guide-for-every-industry) run on international norms** — competency questions, structured assessment, English throughout.

**Traditional Czech employers are more formal**, with surnames and titles until invited otherwise.

**Expect questions about staying.** Czechia has a transient international population and employers ask whether you intend to be there in three years.

**Language expectations will be clarified.** If the role requires any Czech, it will be discussed honestly rather than assumed.

**[Technical interviews](/blog/coding-interview-preparation-guide) are rigorous** in IT and engineering, and Czech technical education produces a high standard that sets the bar.

**Salary is discussed relatively openly**, frequently with [reference](/blog/resume-references-guide) to the market rather than to a formal collective agreement.

**Ask about the permit process** if you need one — an employer who answers precisely is a good sign, and one who is vague is worth probing.

## Building a career from Czechia

Where the market takes you.

**Business services [experience](/blog/resume-matching-experienced-professionals) is highly portable.** Finance operations, procurement, analytics and process [skills](/blog/resume-skills-match-job-description) map onto centres across Europe, and the largest employers move people internally between countries.

**Automotive engineering transfers directly** to Germany, Slovakia, Poland and the wider European supply chain.

**Technology experience travels**, and Brno's security software heritage in particular produces expertise that is recognised internationally.

**Internal moves within multinationals** are the most common progression path, and joining a Czech operation of a large company frequently opens Western European roles later.

**Salaries continue to rise**, which means staying is increasingly rational rather than a compromise.

**And Czechia's central location** means Vienna, Munich, Berlin and Warsaw are all close, which makes it a genuinely good base for a European career rather than a corner of one.

## Common misreadings

Four beliefs that mislead people about Czechia.

**"It is a low-cost outsourcing destination."** That was true two decades ago. The business services sector has moved substantially up the value chain and now houses analytics, treasury and engineering functions.

**"Prague is the only option."** Brno has a genuine technology sector, two strong technical universities and much lower costs.

**"The [job market](/blog/choosing-country-to-study-abroad-for-jobs) is difficult."** Czechia has among the lowest unemployment in the EU and employers compete for candidates.

**"I need Czech."** For business services, technology and international companies, frequently not. For the domestic market, yes.
