## Key Takeaways

- The Z visa is only the entry document - the work permit and residence permit are the parts that let you stay and work legally.
- The employer drives the process; you cannot apply for a China work permit independently.
- Applicants are scored into tiers on degree, experience, salary, age, and language, which decides eligibility before anything else.
- Degree and criminal record authentication is the slowest step and the one people start too late.
- Working on a tourist or business visa is a serious legal risk, not an administrative shortcut.

The China work visa process has a reputation for being complicated. It is more accurately described as sequential - a set of steps that each depend on the one before, where the cost of getting the order wrong is measured in months.

The single most useful thing to understand up front is that the Z visa is not the work authorisation. It is the entry visa that lets you arrive in order to complete the process. The documents that actually matter are the work permit and the residence permit.

This guide covers the sequence, the documents, and where applicants lose time.

## The Tier System Decides Eligibility First

Foreign workers are classified into categories, commonly described as:

- **Category A** - high-level talent. Senior specialists, high earners, recognised expertise. The most straightforward route, with the fewest restrictions.
- **Category B** - professionals. Where most applicants sit. Requires relevant qualifications and experience for the role.
- **Category C** - other workers, subject to quotas and restrictions.

Placement is scored across factors including education, salary, years of relevant experience, age, Chinese language ability, and the location of the role. The scoring is set by the authorities, not the employer, which is why a willing employer is not by itself sufficient.

The common baseline for a Category B professional role is a bachelor's degree plus roughly two years of relevant post-graduation experience, with variation by city and job category. Requirements do change, so confirm the current position for your specific city and role rather than relying on a general summary.

Two of the scored factors are worth planning around because you can influence them. ## The Sequence, Start to Finish

### 1. Get a job offer

Nothing starts without this. Your employer must be licensed to hire foreign nationals and willing to sponsor you. Confirm both explicitly before you resign from anything.

### 2. Employer applies for the Work Permit Notification

The employer submits your documents to the relevant labour authority. If approved, they receive a **Notification Letter of Foreigner's Work Permit**. This is the document that unlocks the visa application.

### 3. You apply for the Z visa

With the notification letter, you apply at a Chinese embassy, consulate, or visa centre in your country of residence. The Z visa is typically single-entry and valid for a limited window - generally intended to get you into the country within about 90 days of issue.

### 4. You enter China

You must arrive within the validity window. The Z visa alone does not entitle you to work indefinitely.

### 5. Medical examination

Completed in China, at an approved facility, unless you have a valid certificate from an accredited centre abroad that is accepted locally.

### 6. Work permit card

Applied for within a set period after arrival - commonly around 15 days, though local practice varies. Your employer normally handles the submission.

### 7. Residence permit

Applied for at the local exit-entry administration, typically within 30 days of entry. This replaces the Z visa and is what allows you to live and re-enter the country for the duration of your employment.

Miss the deadlines in steps 6 and 7 and you can face fines and complications, so treat the arrival dates as hard.

## Documents You Will Need

Exact requirements vary by city, category, and nationality. Expect to provide:

- **Passport** valid well beyond the intended stay, with blank pages
- **Degree certificate**, authenticated
- **Criminal record check** from your home country and, in some cases, countries where you have recently lived, authenticated
- **Reference letters** documenting the relevant work experience you are claiming
- **Employment contract** with the sponsoring employer
- **Medical examination report**
- **Passport photographs** to specification
- **CV or resume**, which the labour authority does read as part of assessing your experience claim

That last item catches people out. The resume submitted with a work permit application is an official document supporting your eligibility claim, so it must be consistent with your reference letters and contract. ## Authentication Is the Slow Part

This is where applicants lose the most time, almost always because they started late.

Your degree certificate and criminal record check generally need to be legalised so they are recognised in China. Depending on your country, this runs through either the Hague Apostille process or consular legalisation - China joined the Apostille Convention in 2023, which simplified this considerably for many countries, but the applicable route still depends on your nationality and the issuing authority.

Practical advice:

**Start before you have an offer** if you are seriously pursuing China. Authenticated documents do not expire quickly, and having them ready turns a two-month delay into a two-week one.

**Check the validity window on the criminal record check.** Many authorities will only accept one issued within a recent period - often six months - so timing matters.

**Order multiple certified copies** of anything you can. It is far cheaper than repeating the process.

## Common Mistakes

**Assuming the Z visa is the work permit.** It is the entry document. The work permit and residence permit are what make you legal to work and stay.

**Starting document authentication after the offer.** This is the single most common cause of delay.

**Working on a tourist or business visa.** An M or L visa does not permit employment. This carries real consequences - fines, detention, deportation, and bans on re-entry - and the risk sits with you as much as the employer. An employer suggesting it is telling you something important about the employer.

**Inconsistent employment dates.** Your resume, reference letters, and contract are cross-read. Discrepancies trigger queries.

**Missing the post-arrival deadlines.** The work permit and residence permit steps have tight windows after entry.

**Not confirming the employer is licensed to sponsor.** Not every company is. Ask directly and early.

**Assuming the permit transfers with you.** The work permit is tied to your employer. Changing jobs requires a formal transfer process - do not resign on the assumption it moves automatically.

## Bringing Family

Spouses and dependent children can generally apply for accompanying residence permits, usually through an S visa route. Note that a dependent permit does not by itself grant the right to work - a spouse who wants a job typically needs their own work permit, which means their own sponsoring employer.

Factor this into the decision if you are relocating as a couple, because it is a frequent and unwelcome surprise. If your spouse intends to work, treat their search as a parallel job hunt starting now rather than something to sort out after arrival - the sectors realistically open to foreign nationals are covered in [how to get a job in China as a foreigner](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-get-a-job-in-china-foreigner), and [teaching English in China](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/teaching-english-in-china-resume) is often the most accessible route for an accompanying partner.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does the China work visa process take?

Plan for several months end to end. Document authentication alone can take weeks to months depending on your country, the work permit notification typically takes a few weeks, and the post-arrival steps add several more. Starting authentication early is the main lever you control.

### Can I apply for a China work permit without a job offer?

No. The process is employer-driven. Your sponsoring company applies on your behalf, which is why the job search comes first.

### Do I need a degree?

For most Category B professional roles, yes, and it must be authenticated. Some categories allow exceptions based on exceptional experience or specific skills, but planning around an exception is risky.

### What happens if I change jobs?

The work permit is tied to your employer, so you need a formal transfer to the new one. Do not resign until the new employer has confirmed they will handle it - a gap can jeopardise your residence permit.

### Is there an age limit?

Age is one of the scored factors, and some categories apply upper limits, with variation by city and role. Older applicants generally need stronger credentials or salary to offset it.

### Can my spouse work on a dependent visa?

Not on the dependent permit alone. They would need their own sponsoring employer and their own work permit.
