More job postings in 2026 are drafted, expanded, or entirely written by AI. Hiring teams use AI to turn a rough bullet list into a polished, keyword-rich [job description](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/job-description-keyword-extraction-guide) in seconds, and while that produces more consistent postings, it also produces a specific pattern: longer, more generic, and sometimes internally inconsistent job descriptions that are harder to match accurately.

## How to Spot an AI-Generated or AI-Expanded Job Description

- **Unusually uniform structure** across every posting from the same company, with near-identical phrasing patterns repeated section to section
- **Long, padded qualification lists** that mix genuinely important requirements with generic filler ("strong communication skills," "passion for excellence") in roughly equal weight
- **Repetitive or slightly redundant phrasing**, where the same requirement is restated in two or three different ways
- **Inconsistent seniority signals**, such as a posting that asks for "5+ years of experience" in one section and describes entry-level responsibilities in another
- **Buzzword density** without concrete specifics, like "leverage cutting-edge technology to drive innovative solutions" without naming any actual tools or outcomes

None of this means the job itself is not real or not worth applying to. It means the posting requires more interpretation before you can extract what genuinely matters.

---

## Why This Matters for Resume Matching

A padded, AI-expanded job description makes it harder to tell required qualifications apart from generic filler. If you treat every line as equally important, following [required vs preferred qualifications](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/matching-resume-required-vs-preferred-qualifications) becomes more difficult, and you risk either underselling a strong match or wasting effort chasing keywords that carry little real weight.

The goal is to filter signal from noise before you start [matching your resume](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/resume-matching-with-job-description-complete-guide) to the posting.

---

## How to Extract What Actually Matters From an AI-Generated Posting

### Step 1: Identify the Concrete, Specific Requirements First

Look for anything with a specific number, tool name, credential, or measurable outcome. These are almost always genuine requirements, regardless of how the rest of the posting reads.

### Step 2: Deprioritize Generic Buzzword Phrases

Phrases like "self-starter," "passion for excellence," or "thrives in a dynamic environment" carry little matchable signal on their own. Do not spend significant resume real estate trying to directly mirror them; instead, let your results imply them, following [matching soft skills in a job description](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/matching-resume-to-soft-skills-in-job-description).

### Step 3: Cross-Reference With the Company's Other Postings, If Available

If a company has multiple open roles, comparing their postings can reveal which phrases are boilerplate (repeated across every listing) versus role-specific (unique to this posting). Role-specific language is more likely to reflect genuine priorities.

### Step 4: When in Doubt, Match to the Job Title and Core Responsibilities

If the qualifications section is heavily padded or unclear, fall back to matching your resume against the job title and the core responsibilities section, which tend to stay more concrete even in AI-expanded postings.

## Should You Still Fully Tailor Your Resume for These Postings?

Yes, but focus your tailoring effort on the concrete signals rather than trying to mirror every buzzword. A resume that reflects the genuine, specific requirements clearly will still outperform a generic resume, even against an AI-written posting. | Required skills with tool names | Usually genuine | Match precisely |
| Vague culture-fit adjectives | Often filler | Deprioritize, imply through results |
| Years of experience range | Usually genuine, but watch for inconsistency | Match if consistent across the posting |
| Repeated restatements of the same requirement | AI padding | Treat as one requirement, not several |
| Specific responsibilities section | Usually most reliable | Anchor your matching here if qualifications are unclear |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does it matter if a job description was written by AI?

Not for the legitimacy of the role, but it does change how carefully you need to read the posting to separate genuine requirements from generic, padded language.

### How can I tell if a requirement is genuine or just AI filler?

Specific, concrete requirements (tools, numbers, credentials) are almost always genuine. Vague adjectives and buzzwords without specifics are more likely to be filler.

### Should I mirror an AI-generated posting's buzzword language in my resume?

Sparingly. Focus your resume on demonstrating the underlying skill through real results rather than repeating vague buzzwords directly.

### Are AI-generated job descriptions less reliable overall?

Not necessarily less reliable, just often less precise. The role and core responsibilities are usually accurate; the qualifications list may just be more padded than a manually written posting.
