## Key Takeaways

- **Several distinct routes exist**, and which one is open depends mostly on your occupation.
- **The Green List is the fastest**, with some occupations qualifying for residence immediately.
- **The Skilled Migrant Category** is the general points-based route for everyone else.
- **Residence removes the employer tie** entirely, which is the practical thing it buys you.
- **Plan the route before accepting a role**, because the job you take determines what becomes available.

## The main routes

What exists and who each one serves.

**Straight to Residence** applies to occupations on the highest tier of the Green List, allowing a residence application without first completing a qualifying period of work.

**Work to Residence** applies to other Green List occupations, requiring a defined period of employment in that role before applying.

**The Skilled Migrant Category** is the general route, using a points system built on skills, qualifications, experience and income.

**Sector agreements** cover defined industries with their own conditions, generally in areas of persistent shortage.

**Partner and family categories** run entirely separately and depend on a relationship with a citizen or resident rather than on employment.

**And the Care Workforce and transport pathways** exist for specific occupations with their own criteria.

**The important point is that these are not interchangeable.** Your occupation, not your ambition, determines which of them you can use.

## Why the occupation matters most

The variable that decides everything else.

| Your situation | Route available | Relative speed |
|---|---|---|
| **Green List tier one occupation** | Straight to Residence | Fastest |
| **Green List tier two occupation** | Work to Residence | Fast, after a qualifying period |
| **Sector agreement occupation** | Sector pathway | Varies by sector |
| **Skilled role, not on any list** | Skilled Migrant Category | Slower, points dependent |
| **Role below the skill threshold** | Generally none directly | Requires a change of role |

**Check the Green List before accepting any offer**, because [two roles](/blog/resume-for-two-jobs-same-company) with identical salaries can sit on completely different sides of this divide.

**Salary matters, but it is not the whole story.** A well-paid role in a non-listed occupation can leave you with a slower route than a moderately paid one on the Green List.

**Occupational registration is frequently the gate.** Regulated professions require registration with the relevant New Zealand body before you can work, and that process runs separately from immigration and takes its own time.

**And the lists change.** Occupations are added and removed as shortages shift, so a route that existed when you started planning may not be there when you apply, and the reverse is equally true.

## What the application involves

The practical requirements common to most routes.

**Identity and passport documentation** for you and every included family member.

**Evidence of your employment** — the offer, the contract, payslips and confirmation the role matches what was assessed.

**Qualification documentation**, frequently requiring an assessment of overseas qualifications against New Zealand equivalents.

**Evidence of work experience**, in the form of [references](/blog/reference-check-process-guide) and employment records covering the claimed period.

**English language evidence**, either through a recognised test or an exemption based on nationality, qualifications or work history.

**Health requirements**, including medical examinations and chest x-rays depending on your circumstances and intended stay.

**Character requirements**, including police [certificates](/blog/how-to-prove-skills-without-certificates) from countries where you have lived for a defined period.

**And occupational registration** where your profession is regulated, which must generally be in place rather than pending.

**Gather these early.** Overseas qualification assessments and police certificates from multiple countries both take considerably longer than people plan for, and they are the usual cause of delay.

## After residence

What changes and what comes next.

**The employer tie disappears.** You can change jobs, work for anyone, or work for yourself, which is the single largest practical difference.

**Travel conditions apply initially**, and resident visas typically carry conditions on re-entry that need attention before long absences.

**Permanent residence follows** after meeting commitment requirements, removing the travel conditions and making the status indefinite.

**Citizenship becomes available** after a qualifying period of residence, subject to presence requirements and the other criteria.

**Citizenship carries the Australia right**, allowing you to live and work in Australia essentially freely, which is a substantial and frequently underweighted benefit of the whole process.

**Family sponsorship options open**, though these categories have their own criteria and queues.

**And access to public services broadens**, with entitlements that differ from those held on a temporary work visa.

## Common planning errors

Where people lose time.

**Accepting a role without checking the pathway**, which is the most expensive mistake available and the easiest to avoid.

**Assuming the Green List is static.** Occupations move on and off it, and a decision made on last year's list may not hold.

**Leaving occupational registration late**, when it gates both the work and the visa and runs on its own timetable.

**Changing employers mid-qualifying-period** without confirming the effect, which can reset progress you had already made.

**Underestimating documentation time**, particularly overseas qualification assessments and police certificates.

**Relying on remembered thresholds** rather than current published figures, since wage and points criteria are revised regularly.

**And planning around forum advice** rather than the official source, which is where most of the confusion in this area originates.

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Your occupation is on the Green List

You are in the strongest position available. Confirm which tier applies, since the difference between immediate residence and a qualifying period is substantial for planning.

### Scenario 2: You are in a skilled role that is not listed

The Skilled Migrant Category is your route. Assess your points honestly against the current threshold, since the qualifying score is the whole question and it has moved over time.

### Scenario 3: You are offered a role below the skill threshold

Take it only with clear eyes. It may not lead anywhere directly, and the realistic plan is to use it as an entry point while working toward a qualifying role.

> **Tip:** Ask the immigration question before you [negotiate](/blog/how-to-negotiate-salary-offer) salary, not after. An offer that pays slightly less in a Green List occupation is frequently worth considerably more than a better-paid role that leaves you on the slowest available route.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Check the Green List before accepting anything
- Confirm which tier your occupation sits in
- Start occupational registration early
- Verify current thresholds rather than remembered ones
- Plan the route from the first job offer
- Keep evidence of employment and income throughout

### Don't

- Assume any skilled role leads to residence
- Rely on a list you checked a year ago
- Leave occupational registration until later
- Treat salary as the only variable
- Change roles without checking the effect on your pathway
- Overlook the English language requirement

## Common Mistakes

- **Not checking the Green List.** It is the single largest variable.
- **Using outdated criteria.** Lists and thresholds both move.
- **Delaying registration.** It gates the work, not just the visa.
- **Optimising only for salary.** The occupation matters more.
- **Changing jobs carelessly.** It can reset a qualifying period.
- **Assuming a pathway exists.** Some roles genuinely lead nowhere directly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the main residence pathways in New Zealand?

Straight to Residence and Work to Residence for Green List occupations, the points-based Skilled Migrant Category for other skilled roles, and separate sector, partner and family categories.

### What is the Green List?

A list of occupations in genuine shortage, split into tiers. The highest tier allows a residence application immediately, while the second requires a defined period of employment in the role first.

### Does my salary determine my pathway?

Only partly. Your occupation matters more, since a well-paid role in a non-listed occupation can leave you on a slower route than a moderately paid Green List position.

### How does the Skilled Migrant Category work?

Through a points system covering skills, qualifications, experience and income. It is the general route for skilled workers whose occupations do not appear on the Green List.

### What does residence actually give me?

Indefinite stay with work rights and no tie to a specific employer, which is the practical change. Citizenship follows after a qualifying period and carries the right to live and work in Australia.

### Do the requirements change?

Yes, regularly. Occupation lists, wage thresholds and points criteria are all revised, so check the current position with Immigration New Zealand rather than relying on remembered figures.
### What documents does a residence application need?

Passport and identity documents, employment evidence, qualification assessments, work experience references, English language evidence, medical examinations, police certificates, and occupational registration where required.

### What happens after I get residence?

The employer tie disappears entirely. Travel conditions apply initially, permanent residence follows after commitment requirements, and citizenship becomes available later — carrying the right to live and work in Australia.

### What is the most common planning mistake?

Accepting a role without first checking which pathway it opens. Two similarly paid jobs can sit on completely different sides of the Green List divide.
