## Key Takeaways

- The **AEWV is New Zealand's principal temporary work visa**, and it runs through accredited employers only.
- **Three checks apply** — employer accreditation, a job check on the role, and then your own application.
- **The median wage threshold** is central to how the system works, affecting duration and onward options.
- **The visa is employer-specific**, and changing jobs requires a variation or a new application.
- **It is a stepping stone**, with residence pathways following for those who qualify.

## The three-stage system

How the AEWV actually works.

**Stage one is employer accreditation.** The employer applies to Immigration New Zealand to become accredited, demonstrating they are a genuine, compliant business meeting employment standards.

**Stage two is the job check.** The specific role is assessed — its terms, its pay against the median wage, and in many cases whether the labour market was tested by advertising it locally first.

**Stage three is your application.** You apply for the visa itself, evidencing your identity, health, character and that you meet the role's skill and experience requirements.

**All three must succeed**, and the first two are the employer's responsibility rather than yours.

**Which means the practical question** for any candidate is whether the prospective employer is already accredited and has a current job check for the role — because if not, your timeline extends considerably.

**Labour market testing** is waived for roles on the Green List and for those paid above a defined threshold, which is a substantial simplification where it applies.

**And accreditation has tiers**, with standard accreditation covering a limited number of migrant workers and higher tiers available for employers hiring more.

## The median wage and why it matters

The number that governs the system.

**New Zealand's median wage is the reference point** used throughout the immigration system, updated periodically.

**Roles paid at or above it** generally receive better treatment on visa duration and onward pathways.

**Roles paid below it** face more constraints, and policy in this area has changed repeatedly in recent years.

**Green List roles** — occupations in genuine shortage — carry their own arrangements including faster residence routes.

**Sector agreements** exist for defined industries with their own wage and condition settings.

**Because the threshold moves**, an offer that qualified last year may not this year, and checking the current figure rather than a remembered one is genuinely necessary.

**And the interaction with residence** is the key point — pay level affects not just this visa but what you can apply for afterwards.

## Applying in practice

The mechanics from your side.

**Your application is online** through Immigration New Zealand, once the employer's job check is approved.

**You will need** your passport, the job check reference, evidence of your qualifications and work experience, and proof you meet any occupational registration requirements.

**Health requirements** include a medical examination and chest x-ray in many cases, depending on your country and intended stay length.

**Character requirements** include police certificates from countries where you have lived for a defined period.

**Occupational registration** is mandatory before you can work in regulated professions — nursing, teaching, engineering, medicine and others — and it is a separate process from the visa that takes its own time.

**Processing times are published** and vary considerably with application volume.

**Fees and the International Visitor Levy** apply.

**And your partner and dependent children** can generally be included or apply alongside, with partner work rights depending on your role's pay level and occupation.

## After the AEWV

Where the route goes next.

**The Straight to Residence pathway** applies to some Green List occupations, allowing residence application immediately rather than after a qualifying period.

**The Work to Residence pathway** applies to other Green List occupations after a defined period of employment in that role.

**The Skilled Migrant Category** uses a points system based on skills, qualifications, experience and income, and is the general route for those outside the Green List.

**Sector agreement pathways** exist for defined industries with their own conditions.

**Partner and family categories** run separately.

**Residence removes the employer tie** entirely and grants indefinite stay with work rights.

**And citizenship follows** after a qualifying period of residence, bringing with it the right to live and work in Australia — which is a substantial and frequently underweighted benefit.

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: The employer is already accredited with a current job check

You are in the fastest case. Your own application is the only remaining stage and processing is considerably quicker.

### Scenario 2: Your occupation is on the Green List

You are in the strongest position available. Labour market testing is generally waived and a defined residence pathway follows, which is why checking the Green List first is worthwhile.

### Scenario 3: The employer has never hired a migrant worker

Expect a longer timeline. Accreditation and the job check both take time, and some employers abandon the process once they see what it involves — so confirm their commitment early.

> **Tip:** Before investing in an application, ask the employer two direct questions: are you accredited, and do you have a current job check for this role? Those two answers determine whether your visa takes weeks or months, and most candidates never think to ask.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Confirm employer accreditation before anything
- Check the Green List for your occupation
- Verify the current median wage figure
- Evidence your skills and experience thoroughly
- Plan the residence pathway from the start
- Check family accompanying rights

### Don't

- Assume any employer can sponsor you
- Rely on a remembered wage threshold
- Change employers without a variation
- Overlook health and character requirements
- Ignore sector agreements if relevant
- Treat this visa as an end point

## Common Mistakes

- **Not checking accreditation.** It determines your entire timeline.
- **Using an outdated wage threshold.** It moves and it governs everything.
- **Missing the Green List.** It offers the fastest route where it applies.
- **Changing jobs informally.** The visa is employer-specific.
- **Weak experience evidence.** Skill requirements are assessed properly.
- **No residence plan.** The pay level you accept affects later options.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the AEWV?

New Zealand's principal temporary work visa, available only through employers accredited by Immigration New Zealand and requiring a job check on the specific role.

### What are the three stages?

Employer accreditation, a job check on the role, and your own visa application. The first two are the employer's responsibility and determine your timeline.

### Why does the median wage matter?

It is the reference point governing visa duration, conditions and onward residence pathways. Roles at or above it generally receive better treatment.

### What is the Green List?

A list of occupations in genuine shortage, carrying waived labour market testing and defined residence pathways — the strongest position available under this system.

### Can I change employers?

Not freely. The visa is employer-specific and a change requires a variation of conditions or a new application through another accredited employer.

### Does this lead to residence?

It can. Residence pathways follow for those meeting the criteria, and the pay level and occupation you accept materially affect which ones are open.
### What documents does an AEWV application need?

Passport, the approved job check reference, qualification and experience evidence, a medical examination in many cases, police certificates, and occupational registration where your profession is regulated.

### What comes after the AEWV?

Straight to Residence or Work to Residence for Green List occupations, the points-based Skilled Migrant Category otherwise, and eventually citizenship which carries the right to live and work in Australia.
