## Key Takeaways

- Ten minutes is enough if you work in a fixed order and resist rewriting.
- Two minutes reading, three on the summary, one reordering bullets, one on skills, three on vocabulary.
- Set a timer - the work expands to fill whatever time you give it, with diminishing returns after the first ten minutes.
- The routine only works on top of a strong base resume; fix the base once rather than per application.
- Applying to ten jobs with ten-minute tailoring beats applying to three with an hour each.

The reason most people stop tailoring is that they have no routine, so each application becomes an open-ended editing session. Give it a fixed order and a clock and it becomes something you can do ten times in an afternoon.

## Before You Start: The Base Resume

This routine assumes a base resume that is already good — parses cleanly, bullets that state outcomes, no formatting traps.

If your base is weak, tailoring it repeatedly is wasted effort. Spend two hours fixing the base once. Then every application afterwards costs ten minutes.

## Minute 0–2: Read for Three Things

Read the posting once. You are looking for exactly three things, not absorbing the whole document.

**The real requirements.** Which two or three items appear in the title, the opening paragraph, and again in the bullets? Repetition marks what matters. Ignore anything mentioned once at the bottom.

**The vocabulary.** What does this employer call things? "Stakeholder management" or "client relations"? "Analytics engineering" or "reporting"? Note their words.

**The level.** Is this a doing role or an owning role? That determines whether your bullets should foreground hands-on specifics or scope and decisions.

Two minutes. Do not take notes beyond three terms.

## Minute 2–5: Rewrite the Summary

Your summary is three lines and it is the highest-leverage thing you can change. Structure:

> [Your role] with [X years] in [the two capabilities this posting repeats]. [One specific proof point relevant to this job.]

Applied:

> Operations coordinator with four years in process improvement and stakeholder management. Delivered a 120-person relocation across four months on a £180k budget, 4% under.

The first sentence matches the posting's language. The second gives one concrete reason to keep reading. That is the whole job.

## Minute 5–6: Reorder Your Top Bullets

Under your most recent role, move the most relevant bullet to first position.

No rewriting. Just reordering. A recruiter reads the first two bullets under your current job, and this ensures the relevant one is among them.

If your most relevant achievement sits under an older role, either move that bullet up within its role, or name the capability in your summary so it appears early regardless.

## Minute 6–7: Adjust the Skills Line

Reorder so the posting's central terms come first. Drop items that are irrelevant to this application.

> **Base:** Excel, PowerPoint, SQL, Tableau, dbt, Snowflake, Jira, Confluence
>
> **For a data engineering posting:** SQL, dbt, Snowflake, Python, Airflow, Git

Shorter and led by what they asked for. You are not adding anything you lack; you are surfacing what fits.

## Minute 7–10: Rename Two or Three Things

Find the two or three places where you did the work but used different words, and switch to theirs.

> **Before:** Looked after relationships with our agency partners and kept projects on schedule.
>
> **After:** Owned vendor management across three agency partners, holding delivery to contracted timelines.

Three of these is plenty. This is the step where people overrun, because the missing-keyword list is long and tempting. Two or three genuine renames capture most of the value.

## Then Stop

At ten minutes, send it.

The instinct to do one more pass is strong and almost never pays. A second pass on the same posting typically produces cosmetic changes, and the time is worth more spent on the next application.

If you have an hour, apply to six jobs at ten minutes each rather than one job at sixty.

## When to Spend Longer

Three cases justify more:

**A role you genuinely want**, where a deeper rework of bullets and section order is worth an hour.

**A career change**, where the whole framing needs to shift and ten minutes cannot do it.

**A stretch role**, where your relevance is not obvious and needs deliberate surfacing.

Everything else gets ten minutes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is ten-minute tailoring actually enough?**
For a well-matched role with a strong base resume, yes. The summary, bullet order, skills emphasis and two or three renames cover most of what tailoring achieves.

**Should I tailor my cover letter too?**
If you are writing one, it needs more adaptation than the resume - it is the document where role-specific reasoning belongs. Budget separately for it.

**What if I am applying to 20 jobs a week?**
Group by role type. Build two or three base versions, then the per-application work drops to the summary and bullet order - about four minutes.

**Does this work for senior roles?**
Yes, with more weight on the summary and scope framing. Senior readers are assessing level fit, which the summary establishes fastest.

**How do I stop myself over-editing?**
Set an actual timer. It sounds trivial and it is the single most effective part of the routine.
