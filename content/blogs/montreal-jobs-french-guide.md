## Key Takeaways

- Montreal has the lowest housing costs of any major Canadian city, and by a substantial margin.
- AI research, gaming and aerospace are genuine world-class clusters, not local specialisms.
- French is the working language for most employers and the legal framework reinforces it, though the tech sector is more anglophone than the wider market.
- Quebec runs its own immigration system separate from federal Express Entry.
- Quebec income tax is higher than most provinces, which offsets part of the housing advantage.

Montreal is the most distinctive job market in Canada — a major AI research centre, the largest video game production hub in the country, a serious aerospace cluster, and housing costs that make the rest of Canada look expensive.

The trade-offs are French and taxes, both of which are real and both of which people underestimate in opposite directions.

This guide covers the sectors, the language question honestly, and how Quebec immigration differs.

## What hires

**Artificial intelligence and [machine learning](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/machine-learning-interview-questions).** Montreal is one of the world's genuine AI research centres, anchored by internationally recognised research institutes and university groups, with corporate research labs and a commercial layer around them. For anyone in machine learning research or applied AI, this is a first-tier global location.

**Video games.** The largest games production cluster in Canada and one of the largest anywhere, supported by provincial tax credits. Studios of every size, covering engineering, art, design and production.

**Aerospace.** A long-established and substantial cluster — aircraft, simulation, components and maintenance.

**Visual effects and animation.** Closely linked to the games sector, similarly supported by tax incentives.

**Software and enterprise technology.** A broad market, with satellite offices of larger companies.

**Life sciences and pharmaceuticals.** A real sector with research and manufacturing.

**Financial services.** Smaller than Toronto but genuine.

## The French question, honestly

This is where people get either over- or under-confident.

**The legal framework matters.** Quebec's language legislation establishes French as the language of work, with obligations on employers around francisation, internal communications and documentation. The requirements have been strengthened in recent years.

**In practice, it varies sharply by sector:**

**Technology, AI and games** are the most anglophone parts of the market. Many studios and labs operate substantially in English, particularly those with international teams. English-only roles genuinely exist here.

**Most other employers** operate in French. Customer-facing roles, healthcare, public sector, retail and the majority of local businesses expect functional French.

**What you actually need:**

- **For an English-operating tech or AI role:** you can start with little French, though daily life is easier with some
- **For most other professional roles:** functional French is expected
- **For public sector and healthcare:** French is effectively required
- **For long-term progression anywhere:** French matters more the more senior you get, exactly as in [working in China without Mandarin](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/working-in-china-without-mandarin) — the entry point is not the ceiling

**Learning French here is easier than elsewhere** because you are immersed, and provincial francisation programs are available, often free.

## Quebec immigration is different

Important and frequently missed.

**Quebec administers its own economic immigration selection**, separate from federal Express Entry. Candidates are selected under Quebec's own programs, receive a Quebec Selection Certificate, and then apply to the [federal government](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/federal-government-resume-guide) for permanent residence.

**Which means:**

- **Express Entry does not apply the same way** if you intend to settle in Quebec
- **Quebec's criteria weight French heavily**, more than the federal system does
- **Provincial nominee streams do not cover Quebec** — see [the Canada provincial nominee guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/canada-provincial-nominee-guide) for the rest of the country
- **Programs and criteria change**, so verify current requirements with Quebec's immigration ministry directly

**If you are flexible on province**, compare the Quebec route against federal Express Entry properly rather than assuming one is easier.

## Cost, tax, and the real comparison

**Housing is the cheapest of any major Canadian city**, by a wide margin. Rent and purchase prices are dramatically below Toronto and Vancouver, and meaningfully below Calgary and Ottawa.

**Quebec income tax is higher than most provinces.** This is the offset people forget. On the same gross salary, take-home in Quebec is lower than in Ontario or Alberta.

**Salaries also run below Toronto** for equivalent roles in many sectors.

**So the comparison needs all three:**

> (Montreal salary − Quebec tax − Montreal housing) versus (other city salary − that province's tax − housing)

Montreal frequently still wins on disposable income because the housing gap is so large, but it is closer than the rent figures alone suggest. Benchmark using [the Canada salary benchmarking guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/canada-salary-benchmarking-guide).

**Other factors:** excellent public transport, strong cultural life, subsidised childcare that is genuinely significant for families, and cold winters.

## Common Mistakes

**Assuming you need fluent French for any job.** Technology, AI and games are substantially anglophone, and English-operating roles genuinely exist.

**Assuming you need no French at all.** Most of the market outside tech operates in French, and the legal framework has been strengthened.

**Forgetting Quebec has its own immigration system.** Express Entry works differently here and provincial nominee streams do not apply.

**Comparing housing costs without comparing tax.** Quebec income tax is higher than most provinces, which offsets part of the housing advantage.

**Underestimating French for progression.** The entry point is not the ceiling — seniority and management roles weight it more heavily.

**Not using free francisation programs.** They are available provincially and are the cheapest way to convert a limited-French position into an unlimited one.

**Overlooking the games and VFX clusters.** They are among the largest anywhere and supported by stable tax credit structures.

**Writing "basic French" on a resume.** State a concrete level, since employers here screen on it specifically.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need French to work in Montreal?

For technology, AI and games roles, frequently not — many operate in English. For most other professional roles, healthcare and public sector, functional French is expected.

### Is Montreal really an AI centre?

Yes, genuinely world-class, anchored by internationally recognised research institutes and university groups with corporate labs and a commercial ecosystem around them.

### How does Quebec immigration differ?

Quebec selects its own economic immigrants separately from federal Express Entry, issues a Quebec Selection Certificate, and weights French more heavily. Provincial nominee streams do not apply.

### Is Montreal cheap?

Housing is the cheapest of any major Canadian city by a wide margin, though higher Quebec income tax and generally lower salaries offset part of the advantage.

### What about the games industry?

One of the largest production clusters anywhere, supported by provincial tax credits, employing across engineering, art, design and production.

### Can I learn French there?

Yes, and more easily than elsewhere given the immersion. Provincial francisation programs are available and frequently free.

### Is Quebec tax really higher?

Yes, generally higher than Ontario or Alberta, which is the offset most people omit when comparing on rent alone.

## Make This Practical

Be precise about which half of the market you are targeting. Technology, AI and games roles frequently operate in English and are genuinely open with limited French; almost everything else expects functional French, and the legal framework has been reinforced rather than relaxed.

Then run the three-way comparison rather than the rent one. Montreal housing is the cheapest of any major Canadian city, but Quebec income tax is higher and salaries generally lower — compare what remains after all three, not just the rent difference.

Finally, if Quebec is your target, plan the immigration route separately. Quebec selects its own economic immigrants outside Express Entry and weights French heavily, so check its current programs directly — and start free provincial francisation early, because French determines your ceiling here even where it does not determine your entry.
