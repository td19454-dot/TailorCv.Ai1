## Key Takeaways

- **OTR/L is the credential line** — NBCOT certification plus state licence. Put it at the top with numbers.
- Setting is the single strongest matching keyword: acute care, SNF, inpatient rehab, outpatient, school-based, home health. These are different jobs.
- Productivity percentage and caseload are the numbers hiring managers look for, and most OT resumes omit both.
- Name your assessments — FIM, COPM, MMT, Berg, MoCA. Generic "evaluations" matches nothing.
- Fieldwork carries a new-grad resume. Present Level II placements with hours and settings.

Occupational therapy hiring is setting-driven. A school-based OT and an acute-care OT do different work with different populations and different documentation, and postings screen accordingly. A resume that does not name its setting clearly is hard to place.

## Best Occupational Therapist Resume Format for 2026

Reverse-chronological, one to two pages, single column. Single column matters — rehab departments often run older ATS installations, and [multi-column layouts parse badly](/blog/ats-resume-formatting-mistakes). On length, see our [resume length guide](/blog/ats-resume-length-guide).

Section order:

1. Header with credentials
2. Licensure and certification
3. Summary
4. Settings and populations
5. Assessments and interventions
6. Experience
7. Education and fieldwork

## Header and Licensure

```
Anita Rao, MS, OTR/L
Seattle, WA · (206) 555-0164 · a.rao@email.com
```

**OTR/L** = Occupational Therapist, Registered (NBCOT) and Licensed (state). Both halves matter and both get verified.

```
LICENSURE & CERTIFICATION
Occupational Therapist, Washington, License #OT-60219, active through 04/2028
NBCOT Certified (OTR), certification #556231, current
Certified Hand Therapist (CHT) — 2024
LSVT BIG Certified — 2023
BLS, AHA — current through 02/2027
```

Specialty certifications are strong differentiators: **CHT** (hand therapy), **SCEM**, **ATP** (assistive technology), **CBIS** (brain injury), **LSVT BIG**, **SIPT**. If a posting names one, it is usually a genuine requirement.

## Setting Is the Matching Keyword

This is the most common OT resume failure. Name your settings explicitly and repeatedly:

Acute care · Inpatient rehabilitation (IRF) · Skilled nursing facility (SNF) · Outpatient orthopaedic · Hand therapy · Pediatrics / school-based · Early intervention · Home health · Mental health / behavioural · Assisted living

A posting for a school-based OT is not matching against acute-care language like "discharge planning" and "bedside evaluation." Use the setting's own vocabulary — this is ordinary [keyword matching](/blog/how-to-match-resume-keywords-to-job-description), and it moves scores more than most candidates expect.

## Professional Summary

> OTR/L with 5 years in inpatient rehabilitation, carrying a caseload of 10-12 patients daily at 90% productivity. Specialise in neurological rehabilitation following CVA and TBI, with LSVT BIG certification and experience leading a weekly interdisciplinary team conference.

Four specifics most summaries lack: credential, setting, caseload, productivity.

## Assessments and Interventions

Name the tools. This section is scanned for exact matches:

> **Assessments:** FIM · COPM · MMT and ROM · Berg Balance Scale · MoCA · MMSE · Nine-Hole Peg Test · Jebsen-Taylor · Sensory Profile · Beery VMI · Barthel Index
> **Interventions:** ADL and IADL retraining · Neuromuscular re-education · Splinting and orthotic fabrication · Adaptive equipment training · Energy conservation · Cognitive remediation · Sensory integration · Home modification assessment · Wheelchair seating and positioning
> **Documentation:** Epic Rehab · WebPT · Casamba · Net Health · Medicare Part A/B documentation · G-codes and functional reporting

## Best ATS Keywords for an OT Resume

**Clinical:** activities of daily living, ADL, IADL, functional mobility, fine motor, gross motor, upper extremity rehabilitation, cognitive assessment, adaptive equipment, durable medical equipment, splinting, home safety evaluation, discharge planning, patient education

**Practice:** plan of care, goal setting, treatment planning, evidence-based practice, interdisciplinary team, caseload management, productivity standards, reimbursement documentation, Medicare compliance

**Credentials:** OTR/L, NBCOT, state licensure, CHT, ATP, CBIS, LSVT BIG

## Writing Experience Bullets

**Weak:**
> Provided occupational therapy services to patients in a rehab setting.

**Strong:**
> Carry a daily caseload of 10-12 patients in a 30-bed inpatient rehabilitation unit at 90% productivity, specialising in post-CVA upper extremity and ADL retraining.

**Weak:**
> Worked with the team on discharge planning.

**Strong:**
> Lead weekly interdisciplinary discharge conferences for a 30-bed unit; reduced average home-modification delay from 6 days to 2 by pre-ordering equipment at admission.

Numbers most OTs have: daily caseload, productivity percentage, average length of stay, FIM gain, evaluations per week, home assessments completed, students supervised, group sessions run. Our [guide to quantifying achievements](/blog/how-to-quantify-achievements-on-resume) covers finding them.

## Productivity Is a Real Metric — Use It

Rehabilitation employers manage to productivity targets, and a candidate who states theirs is speaking the hiring manager's language. "Maintained 90% productivity while meeting documentation deadlines" is a genuinely differentiating line, and almost nobody writes it.

Similarly for caseload: *"10-12 patients daily"* tells a manager immediately whether you can handle their volume.

## New Graduate Occupational Therapists

Fieldwork carries the resume.

> **FIELDWORK — Level II, 24 weeks total**
> **Inpatient Rehabilitation**, Harborview (12 weeks). Carried an independent caseload of 6-8 patients by week 8; post-CVA and TBI populations; FIM scoring and interdisciplinary rounds.
> **School-Based Pediatrics**, Seattle Public Schools (12 weeks). Served 25 students on IEP caseload; sensory integration, fine motor and handwriting intervention; attended IEP meetings.
> **Level I:** Acute care (Swedish Medical), Outpatient hand therapy, Community mental health

State NBCOT status: *"NBCOT exam scheduled 08/2026; WA licence application pending exam result."* Employers hire on this basis routinely. Our [no-experience resume guide](/blog/how-to-write-resume-with-no-experience-2026) covers the general approach.

## Common OT Resume Mistakes

**Writing "OT" without OTR/L.** The credential is the filter.

**Not naming the setting.** School-based and acute care are different jobs; postings screen on the difference.

**Generic "performed evaluations."** Name the assessments — FIM, COPM, Berg.

**Omitting productivity and caseload.** These are the operational facts managers hire on.

**Leaving out documentation systems.** Epic, WebPT and Casamba are frequently named in postings.

**Duty lists instead of outcomes.** See [weak resume bullets](/blog/weak-resume-bullets-fix).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Should I write OTR/L or OT?**
OTR/L. It shows both NBCOT certification and state licensure.

**How do I switch settings — say SNF to pediatrics?**
Name the target setting in the summary, foreground any relevant fieldwork or continuing education, and connect transferable skills honestly rather than restyling your history.

**Do I include productivity percentage?**
Yes, if it is good. It is a metric rehab managers actively hire on.

**How long should an OT resume be?**
One page for new graduates, two once you have multiple settings, certifications and outcomes worth listing.

**Are specialty certifications worth listing?**
Yes — CHT, ATP, CBIS and LSVT are frequently listed as required or preferred.
