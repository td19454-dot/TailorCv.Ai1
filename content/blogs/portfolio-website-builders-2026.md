## For Developers
- **GitHub Pages** — free, simple static hosting straight from a repo ([GitHub Portfolio Optimization](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/github-portfolio-optimization))
- **Vercel** — perfect for Next.js, generous free tier
- **Netlify** — easy deploys for static sites
- **Cloudflare Pages** — fast global CDN, free

## For Designers & Creatives
- **Framer** — beautiful, animation-friendly, fast
- **Webflow** — powerful and flexible (steeper learning curve)
- **Adobe Portfolio** — free with Creative Cloud
- **Behance / Dribbble** — for reach (see [Graphic Designer Portfolio Guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/graphic-designer-portfolio-guide) and [UX Designer Portfolio Guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/ux-designer-portfolio-guide))

## No-Code, Any Field
- **Squarespace / Wix** — polished templates, easy
- **Notion** — fast, free, surprisingly clean (great for PMs — [Product Manager Portfolio Guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/product-manager-portfolio-guide))
- **Carrd** — single-page sites in minutes

## Keep Your Resume and Portfolio in Sync

Your resume, your LinkedIn, and your portfolio should tell the **same** story — same name, same headline, same top projects — just at different levels of depth. A recruiter who sees a 'Full Stack Developer' resume and a portfolio headlined 'Aspiring Designer' gets confused, and confusion loses interviews. Lock the resume down first with the [ATS score checker](https://www.thetailorcv.com/solutions) and an [ATS-friendly template](https://www.thetailorcv.com/templates), then mirror that exact positioning in your portfolio. When they reinforce each other, every recruiter touchpoint pushes you forward. See [How to Add Your Portfolio Link to Your Resume](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/add-portfolio-link-to-resume) for placing the link correctly.
