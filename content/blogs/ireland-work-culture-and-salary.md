## Key Takeaways

- **Informality is genuine but hierarchy persists** underneath it, more than in the Netherlands or Australia.
- **The multinational-domestic divide** is the single biggest factor in Irish pay — the same role can differ substantially between the two.
- **Dublin rent is the dominant financial reality**, and it reshapes what any salary is actually worth.
- **Relationships and conversation matter enormously**, and the social dimension of Irish work is not optional.
- **Indirectness is common** — Irish colleagues frequently avoid flat refusal, and reading around it takes practice.

## The multinational-domestic divide

The structural fact behind most Irish career decisions.

**Ireland hosts an unusual concentration of multinational headquarters** — technology, pharmaceuticals, medical devices and financial services — drawn by tax treatment, EU access and an English-speaking workforce.

**These employers pay to international benchmarks** rather than Irish domestic ones, which creates a genuine two-tier market.

**Domestic Irish employers** — SMEs, indigenous firms, much of the public and community sector — pay considerably less for equivalent roles.

**The gap is largest in technology, finance and pharma** and narrower in professions with regulated pay scales.

**Multinational roles concentrate in Dublin, Cork, Galway and Limerick**, with pharma and medtech notably strong outside the capital.

**Which means** the first strategic question for anyone building a career here is which side of that divide you want to be on, because it affects pay, progression and the kind of work far more than the job title does.

**And the trade-off is real.** Multinationals pay better and frequently offer narrower roles within a large global structure; domestic firms pay less and often give broader responsibility earlier.

## Work culture in practice

How Irish workplaces actually run.

**Informality is immediate.** First names throughout, easy conversation, and a low tolerance for anyone who takes themselves too seriously.

**Hierarchy still exists** underneath that, more than the friendliness suggests. Decisions frequently route upward in ways that a flat-seeming culture obscures.

**Conversation is the medium.** Small talk before meetings is not a delay, it is the relationship maintenance that makes the rest work.

**Humour is constant** and self-deprecation is the default register, much as in Australia. Taking yourself seriously in public is the reliable way to lose people.

**Slagging** — affectionate mockery — is a bonding mechanism and being included in it is a good sign.

**Directness is moderate.** Irish colleagues often soften disagreement heavily, and "I'll have a look at that" can mean anything from genuine interest to a polite no.

**The pub remains socially significant** in many workplaces, though considerably less universally than the stereotype suggests, and non-drinkers are entirely unremarkable now.

## Understanding your payslip

The Irish deductions that confuse newcomers.

**PAYE** is income tax deducted at source, applied through standard and higher rate bands.

**Tax credits** reduce your liability directly and are personal to your circumstances — getting them applied correctly is the single most common source of newcomer overpayment.

**USC**, the Universal Social Charge, is a separate levy applied in bands on top of income tax.

**PRSI** funds social insurance and builds entitlement to benefits and the state pension.

**Emergency tax** applies if your employer does not have your details registered correctly, and it is punitive — register with Revenue and get your Revenue Payroll Notification sorted in your first week.

**Pension contributions** vary enormously between employers, and multinational schemes are frequently far more generous than domestic ones.

**Benefit in kind** applies to company cars, health insurance and similar benefits.

**And the practical action** is to set up your Revenue online account immediately on arrival, because refunds of overpaid emergency tax are straightforward but only once you are registered properly.

## Where the jobs actually are

Geography matters more than the size of the country suggests.

| Location | What concentrates there | Cost pressure |
|---|---|---|
| **Dublin** | Technology, finance, professional services, headquarters | Severe housing shortage |
| **Cork** | Pharmaceuticals, medical devices, technology | High but below Dublin |
| **Galway** | Medical devices, medtech, technology | Moderate, tight rental market |
| **Limerick** | Manufacturing, technology, aviation services | Lower |
| **Waterford and the south-east** | Pharma, manufacturing | Low |
| **Belfast (Northern Ireland)** | Technology, financial services, cyber | Considerably lower |

**Dublin holds most professional employment** and most of the housing problem.

**Cork is the genuine second centre** with a very strong pharmaceutical and medical device cluster and a considerably more manageable housing market.

**Galway is a medtech hub** of real international significance relative to its size.

**And Northern Ireland is a separate jurisdiction** with a different currency, tax system and immigration position — worth knowing if you are searching for roles on the island generally, because the conditions differ substantially.

## Leave and working conditions

The statutory framework.

**Annual leave** is four working weeks for full-time employees, plus public holidays.

**Public holidays** number nine per year following the addition of a February holiday.

**Sick pay** became a statutory entitlement through a phased scheme, on top of any employer arrangement.

**Parental and maternity leave** are provided by statute with employer top-ups varying widely.

**Remote and hybrid working** are well established, and employees have a legal right to request remote work with a defined process.

**Notice periods** are set by statute based on service length and frequently extended by contract.

**And working time is regulated** with limits on average weekly hours and mandatory rest breaks.

## Negotiating in Ireland

How the conversation runs here.

**Salary comes up early**, often in the first recruiter call, and giving a specific figure is expected.

**Benchmark to the right segment.** Multinational and domestic pay differ substantially, and quoting the wrong one damages you in both directions.

**Recruitment agencies are heavily used** in Ireland, more than in many European markets, and a good agency recruiter will tell you the real band for a role.

**Total package matters** — pension contribution, health insurance, bonus and share schemes at multinationals can be worth a large fraction of base.

**Counter politely and specifically.** Irish negotiation is relationship-preserving rather than adversarial, and a reasoned counter with market evidence lands well.

**Get it in writing** including pension percentage, bonus structure and any share entitlement.

**And review annually**, since structured yearly cycles are standard at larger employers.

## Four honest cautions

Things the enthusiasm about Ireland tends to skip.

**Housing is the single biggest problem.** Supply in Dublin is genuinely inadequate, competition for rentals is intense, and this affects quality of life more than any other factor.

**The multinational concentration is a risk as well as a benefit.** A sector-wide downturn in technology or pharma is felt disproportionately here.

**Costs generally are high** — childcare, health insurance, motor insurance and energy all sit above EU averages.

**And the weather and daylight** are a genuine adjustment for people from sunnier climates, more than they expect before arriving.

**None of that outweighs the opportunity**, which is real. It just deserves to be seen clearly.

## Building a career here

Where an Irish role leads over time.

**Within a multinational**, internal mobility to other European sites or to headquarters is a genuine and underused route.

**Across the cluster**, since the density of pharma in Cork or medtech in Galway means moving employer rarely means moving city.

**Into contracting**, which is well established in Irish technology and pharma and pays a substantial premium with the usual security trade-off.

**Into the domestic sector** at a senior level, where multinational experience is valued and the broader remit can be appealing.

**Into Europe**, since Ireland is an EU member and Irish experience travels well across the Union.

**And into founding**, supported by Enterprise Ireland and a startup ecosystem that is small but genuinely supported.

## A note on remote and hybrid work

Ireland moved further on this than most of Europe.

**Hybrid is the default** at most multinational and professional employers rather than an exception.

**There is a statutory right to request remote working**, with a defined process an employer must follow in considering it.

**Remote-first roles are common** in technology, and some employers hire anywhere in Ireland rather than within commuting distance of an office.

**Which genuinely changes the housing calculation**, since a Dublin salary earned from Galway, Sligo or Kerry is a materially different financial proposition.

**And the regional development effect is real**, with towns outside the main cities gaining professional residents in a way that did not happen before.

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: You have offers from a multinational and an Irish SME

The multinational will almost certainly pay more and give you a narrower role in a bigger machine. The SME will pay less and hand you broader responsibility sooner. Both are legitimate; decide which you actually want.

### Scenario 2: You are moving to Dublin

Model rent before you model anything else. Housing is the binding constraint in Dublin and it changes the value of a salary more than tax does.

### Scenario 3: A colleague says they will look into your proposal

Follow up specifically — ask who owns it and by when. Irish professional communication softens refusal heavily, and a specific question surfaces the real position without anyone being blunt.

> **Tip:** Benchmark your salary against the right half of the market. Irish multinational and domestic pay for the same job title can differ substantially, and quoting a domestic benchmark in a multinational process is one of the more expensive mistakes people make here.

- **Benchmarking against the wrong segment.** The two-tier market is real and wide.
- **Underestimating Dublin rent.** It dominates the financial picture.
- **Reading informality as flat hierarchy.** Decisions still route upward.
- **Missing soft refusals.** Vague positives frequently mean no.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why do Irish salaries vary so much for the same job?

Because of the multinational-domestic divide. Multinationals benchmark to international pay scales while domestic employers pay Irish market rates, and the gap is widest in technology, finance and pharma.

### Is Dublin expensive?

Housing is the dominant cost and the binding constraint. Model rent before anything else, because it changes the value of a salary more than tax does.

### Are Irish workplaces hierarchical?

More than the informality suggests. First names and easy conversation sit on top of structures where decisions still route upward.

### What does it mean when an Irish colleague is vague?

Frequently a polite no. Direct refusal is often avoided, so a specific follow-up about ownership and timing surfaces the real answer.

### Should I consider working outside Dublin?

Yes. Cork, Galway and Limerick host substantial multinational employment, particularly in pharma and medical devices, at considerably lower housing cost.

### Is the pub culture still important?

Socially significant in many workplaces but far less universal than the stereotype, and not drinking is entirely unremarkable now.
### What are PRSI and USC?

PRSI is the social insurance contribution building entitlement to benefits and the state pension. USC is the Universal Social Charge, a separate levy applied in bands on top of income tax.

### Why am I being taxed so heavily in my first month?

Probably emergency tax, applied when Revenue does not have your details registered. Set up your Revenue account and payroll notification immediately, and overpayments are refunded.

### Which Irish cities have real professional employment?

Dublin holds most of it, Cork is a strong second with pharma and medical devices, Galway is a genuine medtech hub, and Limerick has manufacturing, technology and aviation services.
### How do I negotiate salary in Ireland?

Directly and early, with a specific figure benchmarked to the right market segment. Recruitment agencies are heavily used and a good one will tell you the real band for a role.

### What are the main downsides of working in Ireland?

Dublin housing supply is genuinely inadequate, the multinational concentration creates sector risk, and childcare, insurance and energy costs sit above EU averages.
### Where does an Irish career lead?

Internal mobility within a multinational, moves across the local pharma or medtech cluster without relocating, contracting at a premium, senior domestic roles, or elsewhere in the EU.
