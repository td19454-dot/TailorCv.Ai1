A resume review checklist helps you catch mistakes before you apply. Many candidates submit resumes with [missing keywords](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/hidden-keywords-in-job-description), weak bullets, broken links, or formatting problems that could have been fixed in five minutes. The cost of skipping this step is invisible: you never find out that a great application was filtered out before a human ever read it.

The good news is that a structured final pass catches almost all of these issues. This checklist walks through the seven checks that matter most, the mistakes that quietly sink applications, and how to run the whole review in under 15 minutes. Use it alongside the [ATS score checker](https://www.thetailorcv.com/solutions) and the [resume proofreading checklist](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/resume-proofreading-checklist) so nothing slips through.

## Why a Review Step Matters

Most rejections are not about talent. They are about small, fixable gaps: a title that does not match the job, three missing keywords, a bullet that lists a duty instead of a result, or a PDF that an [applicant tracking system](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/does-my-resume-pass-ats) could not read cleanly. Reviewers spend only seconds on a first pass, so a single avoidable error can end your chances. A five-minute review is the highest-return activity in the entire job search.

## 1. Check Contact Details

Make sure your:

- Email is professional (name-based, not a nickname)
- Phone number is correct and includes a country code if you apply abroad
- [LinkedIn](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-use-linkedin-for-job-search) link works and is public
- [portfolio](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-build-professional-portfolio) or GitHub link works
- City/country is appropriate for the role

Broken contact information can cost interviews. A recruiter who is interested but cannot reach you simply moves to the next candidate. Click every link yourself, on both desktop and mobile, before you submit. For a deeper pass, use the [Resume Contact Section](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/resume-contact-section) guide.

## 2. Match the Job Description

Compare your resume with the role. Check whether your resume includes the main skills, tools, and responsibilities from the [job description](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/job-description-keyword-extraction-guide). Recruiters and ATS software both scan for the exact language used in the posting, so a resume that says "customer support" when the job says "customer success" can score lower even when the experience is identical.

Do not add false skills. Add truthful experience using the employer's language. If you genuinely used a tool but described it differently, rename it to match. The [resume matching guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/resume-matching-with-job-description-complete-guide) explains how to align without exaggerating.

## 3. Review the Summary

Your summary should answer:

- What role are you targeting?
- What skills are most relevant?
- What proof do you bring?

Avoid vague lines like "hardworking and passionate professional." A strong summary names the role, two or three core skills, and one measurable achievement. Compare weak and strong examples in the [resume summary guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-write-resume-summary), and if you are early in your career, see [resume summary with no experience](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/resume-summary-no-experience).

## 4. Improve Bullet Points

Each bullet should show:

- Action (a strong verb)
- Tool or context
- Result
- Scope or metric

If a bullet only describes a duty, rewrite it. "Responsible for reports" becomes "Built 12 weekly dashboards that cut manual reporting time by 40%." Numbers create trust because they are specific and hard to fake. Pull stronger openers from the [best action verbs for a resume](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/best-action-verbs-for-resume) and learn the pattern in [how to write resume bullet points](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-write-resume-bullet-points).

## 5. Check ATS Formatting

Avoid:

- Text boxes
- Complex tables
- Icons for important links
- Scanned PDFs
- Unusual section headings
- Tiny fonts

Parsers read top to bottom, left to right. Anything that breaks that flow — columns, graphics, headers inside image boxes — can scramble your experience or drop it entirely. Use an [ATS-friendly template](https://www.thetailorcv.com/templates) if needed, and review the full list of traps in [ATS resume formatting mistakes](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/ats-resume-formatting-mistakes).

## 6. Remove Irrelevant Content

Delete old, unrelated, or weak content. Your resume should support the target job, not document your entire life. A ten-year-old part-time role, an unrelated hobby, or a skill you no longer use only dilutes the keywords that matter. If you are unsure what to cut, [what not to put on a resume](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/what-not-to-put-on-resume) draws a clear line.

## 7. Proofread Carefully

Check spelling, dates, tense, punctuation, and consistency. Read the resume out loud once. Small errors can create doubt — if a candidate is careless with their own resume, a hiring manager wonders how careful they will be on the job. After the basic review, look for issues that make recruiters hesitate. [Resume Red Flags](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/resume-red-flags) explains the warning signs that hurt trust.

## Common Resume Review Mistakes

Even careful candidates repeat the same errors:

- **Reviewing once and reusing forever.** A resume that fit one job rarely fits the next without small edits.
- **Editing only the words, not the targeting.** Fixing typos does nothing if the resume aims at the wrong role.
- **Trusting spellcheck alone.** It misses "manager" vs "manger" and wrong-but-real words.
- **Ignoring the file name.** `Resume_Final_v7.pdf` looks unprofessional; use `Firstname-Lastname-Resume.pdf`.

Run the [resume customization checklist](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/resume-customization-checklist) for each serious role so targeting stays sharp.

## How Often Should You Review?

Do a light review — keywords, title, top three bullets — for every application. Do a deep review whenever you change target roles, add a new job, or notice your response rate dropping. If applications are getting no replies at all, the problem is usually upstream; [why your resume gets no responses](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/why-your-resume-gets-no-responses) covers the most common causes.

## FAQ

### How long should a resume review take?

A quick review takes about 10 minutes: scan keywords, confirm the [job title matches](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/resume-headline), and tighten your top three bullets. A serious role-specific review may take 30-45 minutes, especially if you also run the [ATS score checker](https://www.thetailorcv.com/solutions) and rewrite your [summary](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-write-resume-summary).

### Should I review every application?

Yes. At minimum, check keywords, role title, and top bullets for each job. Even a two-minute pass to align your resume with the [job description](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/job-description-keyword-extraction-guide) meaningfully improves your response rate compared with sending the same generic file everywhere.

### Can AI review my resume?

Yes. AI can catch gaps quickly and score your match, but you should still verify truthfulness and tone. Use the [free ATS checker](https://www.thetailorcv.com/solutions) for an instant read, then apply human judgment for voice — the two together beat either one alone, as explained in [how AI resume matching works](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-ai-resume-matching-works).

### What is the single most important check?

Keyword and title alignment with the [job description](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/resume-matching-with-job-description-complete-guide). Formatting and grammar matter, but if the resume targets the wrong role or misses the core skills, a clean layout will not save it.
