## Key Takeaways

- UK technology employers span far more than the well-known graduate schemes, and most of the market hires year-round.
- Assessment is skills-based — coding tests and technical interviews rather than competency frameworks alone.
- A project that exists and runs is the strongest credential and the most commonly missing one.
- Technology roles are the most geographically distributed of any UK graduate sector, which matters for cost of living.
- For international graduates, checking the [sponsor register](/blog/uk-skilled-worker-visa-for-graduates) before applying is the highest-value filter.

## Where the jobs actually are

**Large technology companies' UK offices.** Structured graduate schemes, autumn applications, standardised technical assessment. Highly contested and the most visible.

**Financial services technology.** Banks, insurers and asset managers employ very large engineering organisations building trading, payments, risk and customer systems. Consistently overlooked by students focused on product companies, and consistently substantial — the [same pattern seen in Singapore](/blog/singapore-banking-graduate-programmes).

**Mid-sized software and product companies.** The largest segment by volume, hiring year-round on need rather than on a calendar, and dramatically less contested than the named schemes.

**Startups and scale-ups.** Concentrated in a few cities, with the [usual trade-offs](/blog/usa-startup-vs-big-tech-new-grad) around structure, learning and risk.

**Public sector and government digital.** Substantial digital organisations building services used at national scale, with structured entry routes and — relevant for international candidates — reliable sponsor licences.

**Consultancies and IT services.** Large graduate intakes, client-facing work, and broad early exposure.

**Universities and research institutions.** Research software, infrastructure and data roles, frequently overlooked and generally holding sponsor licences.

## What is assessed

Coding tests. Online assessments early in the process, typically algorithmic and timed. Speed under pressure is the binding constraint rather than difficulty — practise with a clock running.

Technical interviews. Data structures, algorithms, and reasoning aloud while solving. The [preparation](/blog/usa-technical-interview-newgrad-prep) transfers almost entirely, since question banks are effectively international.

Project discussion. Frequently the most important round for a graduate. What you built, why you made the choices you did, what broke, what you would change. This is where a real project pays off and where coursework alone cannot.

Behavioural and competency questions, particularly at larger and more traditional employers, drawing on the [same evidence bank](/blog/uk-competency-questions-guide) as any UK application.

System design, lightly. Rarely a serious round for graduates, and frequently a conversation about how your own project would handle more load.

## What makes a graduate competitive

**One substantial project that runs.** Deployed, reachable, with a README that leads with what it does and what was hard. This outweighs a longer list of modules by a wide margin — the [project standard](/blog/fresher-resume-projects-that-get-interviews) applies directly.

**Evidence someone else has used it.** Even ten users. Adoption is what turns a project into a credential.

**Open-source contributions**, which demonstrate working to someone else's standard.

**An internship or placement year.** The [placement route](/blog/uk-internship-placement-year-guide) is a genuine advantage and converts at high rates.

**A [CV that parses](/ats-analysis) and [matches the posting](/blog/how-to-tailor-resume-for-every-job).** Every employer of size screens with software first.

## Geography

Technology is the most distributed UK graduate sector. London dominates by volume, and Manchester, Edinburgh, Bristol, Cambridge, Leeds, Glasgow, Birmingham and Newcastle all have genuine technology employment.

Which matters financially. A salary difference between London and a regional city is frequently smaller in real terms than it appears once housing is counted — the [regional argument](/blog/uk-regional-jobs-outside-london) covers the arithmetic, and the [general cost-of-living principle](/blog/usa-relocation-and-cost-of-living-first-job) applies identically.

Remote and hybrid roles widen this further, though fully remote graduate roles are less common than mid-career ones because of the mentoring involved.

## For international graduates

**Check the sponsor register first.** Public, searchable, and it determines whether an employer could keep you after the [Graduate route](/blog/uk-graduate-visa-route-explained) expires. Filtering on it before applying is the single highest-value step.

**Larger employers, financial services, government digital and universities** are more likely to hold licences than small startups.

**Ask directly** whether they sponsor for this role, since holding a licence and using it are different.

**Target roles that clear the skill and salary thresholds**, since a role below them will not convert regardless of performance.

## Preparing your own portfolio properly

Deploy your strongest project somewhere reachable with a live URL, and write documentation that leads with what it does and why, rather than opening with installation instructions, exactly [the standard that separates a convincing project from a forgotten repository](/blog/fresher-resume-projects-that-get-interviews) anywhere technical hiring happens.

Be ready to discuss specific trade-offs and decisions within your own project in real depth, this is what the technical interview genuinely probes, a project you can only describe superficially, however impressive it looks on the surface, performs worse under real questioning than a simpler project you genuinely understand end to end.

> **Tip:** Look beyond London to Manchester, Edinburgh, Bristol and Cambridge. Each has a genuine tech cluster with materially lower competition and cost of living than the capital.

## Common Mistakes

- **Applying only to named graduate schemes.** Applying only to named graduate schemes the largest segment hires year-round and is far less contested.
- **No project evidence.** No project evidence the strongest credential and the most commonly missing.
- **Practising coding untimed.** Speed under pressure is what is actually tested.
- **Ignoring financial services and government technology.** Substantial, technically real, and overlooked.
- **Not checking the sponsor register.** Not checking the sponsor register for international candidates, the filter that determines everything else.
- **Assuming London is the only market.** Technology is the most distributed sector in UK graduate hiring.

## Weighing a placement year against direct graduate recruitment

**A [placement year](/blog/uk-internship-placement-year-guide) taken during your degree, where available, is one of the strongest possible credentials for graduate technology hiring**, worth genuinely considering this route if your degree structure permits it, since it converts at meaningfully higher rates than direct graduate applications with no prior industry experience.

**If a placement year is not available or you have already completed your degree without one**, a strong self-built project and any shorter internship experience can substitute effectively, the underlying evidence an employer is looking for, can you actually build things and reason about trade-offs, matters more than the specific credential that demonstrates it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a computer science degree?

It helps and it is not required. Converts from mathematics, physics and engineering are common, and the project evidence carries proportionally more weight without the degree.

### When do applications open?

Named graduate schemes follow the [autumn milkround](/blog/uk-milkround-and-careers-fairs). Most of the market hires year-round, which makes missing the autumn far less costly than in law or banking.

### How do UK technical interviews compare internationally?

Broadly similar in format and content, so preparation transfers. The behavioural rounds follow UK competency conventions.

### Is a placement year worth it?

Yes — it converts at high rates and is one of the strongest credentials available to a UK student.

### What about salary?

London pays more and costs more. Compare real income rather than headline figures — the [salary guide](/blog/uk-graduate-salary-expectations) covers the picture.

### Do startups sponsor visas?

Frequently not, since obtaining a licence takes effort many small companies have not undertaken. Check the register before investing time.
