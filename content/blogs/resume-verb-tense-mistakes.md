## Key Takeaways

- The rule is simple: **present tense for your current role, past tense for everything before it.**
- Mixing tenses within the same job entry reads as carelessness, even when the content is strong.
- Bullets under different roles should not share identical verb forms copy-pasted between jobs.
- This does not affect ATS matching directly, but it affects the human read that follows.
- A two-minute audit catches nearly every instance.

Verb tense errors do not break ATS matching, but they are one of the fastest ways to signal carelessness to a human reader — and the human reader is the one who decides whether to call you.

## The Rule

**Current role: present tense.** You are still doing this work.
> Manage a team of 6 analysts. Build weekly reporting dashboards. Lead the quarterly planning process.

**Every previous role: past tense.** You are no longer doing this work.
> Managed a team of 4 analysts. Built weekly reporting dashboards. Led the quarterly planning process.

That is the entire rule. Almost every tense error on a resume is a violation of one half of it.

## The Two Common Mistakes

**Mistake one: present tense on a past role.**
> Marketing Coordinator, Acme Inc, 2021-2023
> - Manage the social media calendar
> - Coordinate with the design team

This reads as though you are still there, or as a copy-paste error from a template. Fix by converting to past tense throughout that entry.

**Mistake two: past tense on your current role.**
> Marketing Manager, Current Company, 2023-Present
> - Managed the rebrand launch
> - Led the Q3 campaign

This one is subtler and just as common — it undersells ongoing work as finished, and it is inconsistent with the "Present" end date sitting right next to it.

## Mixed Tense Within One Entry

This is the version that reads worst, because it happens within a single job and looks like the resume was assembled from different drafts:

> Marketing Manager, Current Company, 2023-Present
> - Manage a team of 4
> - Led the Q3 campaign
> - Building the new attribution model

Three different tenses in three bullets under the same still-current job. Fix by making the whole entry consistent — all present tense, since this is the current role.

## Why This Matters More Than It Seems

A recruiter reading dozens of resumes an hour is not analysing your grammar deliberately. They are pattern-matching for competence and attention to detail, and a tense inconsistency registers as a small negative signal even when the reader could not articulate why. It sits in the same category as a typo — individually minor, collectively costly when several appear together.

It is also one of the easiest things to get right, which makes getting it wrong stand out more, not less. Unlike a genuinely hard writing problem, this is purely a proofreading pass.

## The Two-Minute Audit

**1. Find your current role.** Confirm every bullet under it uses present tense: manage, build, lead, own, drive, coordinate, oversee.

**2. Find every previous role.** Confirm every bullet uses past tense: managed, built, led, owned, drove, coordinated, oversaw.

**3. Check within each entry for mixing.** Read the bullets under one job consecutively. If the tense shifts mid-list, fix it.

**4. Check your summary separately.** Summaries are conventionally present tense regardless of role — "Data analyst with 4 years..." — since they describe you generally, not a specific past action.

## Copy-Paste Between Jobs Is the Usual Cause

Most tense errors come from copying a bullet from one job entry to another while updating the role — the tense from the original context survives the copy. If you build your resume by duplicating an existing bullet and editing it, re-check the tense every time, because it is the detail most likely to be missed in an edit pass focused on content.

## A Quick Reference Table

| Situation | Tense |
|---|---|
| Current job bullets | Present |
| Past job bullets | Past |
| Resume summary | Present |
| Skills section | No verb needed — nouns |
| Education | Past (unless currently enrolled) |
| Currently enrolled degree | Present ("Pursuing", "Currently completing") |

## Does This Affect ATS Matching?

Not directly. Matching systems generally recognise both "manage" and "managed" as the same underlying skill. This is a human-readability issue, not a keyword-matching one — which is exactly why it is easy to overlook when you are focused on getting your keywords right, and why it deserves its own separate pass. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Should my resume summary be past or present tense?**
Present. It describes who you are now, not a completed action.

**What about a role I am still in but on leave from?**
Present tense — you are still employed there even if not actively working day to day.

**Is it wrong to use present tense for a very recent past role?**
Yes, if the role has genuinely ended. Use past tense regardless of how recently it ended.

**What tense should freelance or ongoing project work use?**
Present, if the work is ongoing. Past, if a specific project has concluded even if you continue freelancing generally.

**Does this matter for a resume submitted through an ATS?**
It matters for the human read that follows the automated filter, which is why it is worth two minutes even though it will not change your match score.
