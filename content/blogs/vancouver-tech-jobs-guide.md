## Key Takeaways

- Vancouver hosts a large cluster of US technology satellite offices, drawn by proximity to Seattle and easier immigration.
- Gaming, visual effects and animation are world-class local sectors supported by provincial tax credits.
- Housing is the least affordable in North America relative to local incomes, and it dominates every decision.
- Salaries sit below Seattle and Toronto while costs sit above both, which is the market's central tension.
- British Columbia runs provincial nominee streams that have targeted technology occupations.

Vancouver has the strongest quality-of-life pitch of any Canadian city and the worst affordability arithmetic. Both are true simultaneously, and which one dominates depends entirely on your salary, your stage, and whether you already own property.

The technology market itself is genuine — not a satellite of Seattle so much as a beneficiary of it.

This guide covers what hires, what it pays, and how to weigh the housing problem honestly.

## What hires

**US satellite offices.** The defining feature. Major US technology companies run substantial Vancouver engineering offices, and a significant reason is immigration — Canadian permits are easier and faster than US ones, so companies place internationally hired engineers here. That makes Vancouver unusually accessible for skilled foreign workers.

**Gaming.** A world-class cluster with major studios, supported by provincial tax credits. One of the largest games production centres anywhere.

**Visual effects and animation.** Similarly world-class, serving the film industry that shoots extensively in the region. Also tax-credit supported.

**Film and television production** itself, one of North America's largest production centres.

**Enterprise software and SaaS.** A broad market with established local companies.

**Clean technology and life sciences.** Real sectors with provincial support.

**Natural resources and shipping**, reflecting the port and the province's resource economy.

## The housing problem

This has to come before compensation, because it dominates everything.

**Vancouver housing is the least affordable in North America** relative to local incomes. Purchase prices in particular are far out of line with what professional salaries support, and rents are correspondingly high.

**Practical consequences:**

- **Buying is out of reach** for most people on a single technology salary
- **Renting consumes a large share** of take-home pay
- **Many people live in the suburbs** — Burnaby, Surrey, Coquitlam, New Westminster — and commute
- **The disposable income comparison** against Calgary, Ottawa or Montreal is stark, and Vancouver loses badly

**The honest framing:** Vancouver is a lifestyle decision that costs money, not a financial optimisation. People choose it for mountains, ocean, mild winters and the city itself. Those are legitimate reasons. They are just not financial ones.

## Compensation

**Below Seattle substantially**, which is the comparison that stings given the two-hour drive. US technology compensation at satellite-office parent companies is materially higher than what their Vancouver offices pay.

**Broadly comparable with Toronto**, sometimes slightly below.

**Above the Canadian average**, but not by enough to offset housing.

**British Columbia income tax** is moderate by Canadian standards, which helps marginally.

**Gaming and VFX** pay below general software for equivalent experience, which is a known feature of those industries and worth factoring in.

## Common Mistakes

**Comparing Vancouver salary to Seattle salary.** The gap is substantial and Vancouver costs more to live in, which is the market's central and unavoidable tension.

**Treating it as a financial optimisation.** It is a lifestyle decision that costs money — legitimate, but it should be made knowingly.

**Not comparing disposable income against other Canadian cities.** Against Calgary, Ottawa or Montreal the comparison is stark and Vancouver loses clearly.

**Overlooking the satellite office advantage for immigration.** These offices exist partly because Canadian permits are easier, which makes them unusually receptive to international candidates.

**Ignoring BC provincial nominee tech streams.** They have targeted technology occupations at thresholds below the federal cutoff.

**Expecting general software pay in gaming or VFX.** Both pay below equivalent software roles and are project-cyclical.

**Only searching the city proper.** Burnaby, Surrey and Coquitlam host employers and cost substantially less to live in.

**Underestimating contract cycles in film and VFX.** Work surges with productions and contracts frequently end with them.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Vancouver good for tech jobs?

Yes — a large cluster of US satellite offices plus world-class gaming, VFX and animation sectors. The market is genuine; the affordability is the problem.

### How do salaries compare to Seattle?

Substantially lower, which is a difficult comparison given the proximity. Vancouver also costs more to live in than Seattle in housing terms.

### Why do US companies have offices there?

Partly talent, and substantially immigration — Canadian work permits are faster and easier than US ones, so companies place internationally hired engineers in Vancouver.

### Is housing really that expensive?

It is the least affordable in North America relative to local incomes. Buying is out of reach for most people on a single technology salary.

### Is gaming a good career there?

The cluster is world-class and among the largest anywhere, though pay sits below general software and work is project-cyclical.

### Does BC have tech immigration streams?

Yes, provincial nominee streams have targeted technology occupations, frequently at thresholds below the federal Express Entry cutoff.

### Should I live outside the city?

Many people do. Burnaby, Surrey and Coquitlam cost considerably less, with a commute in exchange.

## Make This Practical

Decide honestly whether you are optimising for money or for life, because Vancouver cannot do both. The disposable income comparison against Calgary, Ottawa or Montreal is stark and Vancouver loses clearly — choosing it anyway is legitimate, but it should be a decision rather than a surprise.

Then use the immigration advantage if you need a permit. Satellite offices exist here partly because Canadian permits are faster than US ones, which makes this one of the most receptive markets in North America for international candidates — and BC's provincial nominee tech streams have run below the federal cutoff.

Finally, match your preparation to the sector. Satellite office loops mirror their US parent companies, while gaming and VFX hire on portfolios and demo reels with project-cyclical contracts and pay below general software. Search Burnaby, Surrey and Coquitlam alongside the city, where the same employers cost far less to live near.
