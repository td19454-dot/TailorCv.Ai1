## Key Takeaways

- CPT is for work during your programme and is tied to your curriculum; OPT is for work during or after it and is not tied to a specific employer.
- OPT gives 12 months. A qualifying STEM degree can extend that by a further 24 months, which is the single biggest factor in an international student's US job odds.
- On post-completion OPT you may not accumulate more than 90 days of unemployment. This is a hard clock, and it starts whether or not you are ready.
- Twelve months of full-time CPT eliminates your OPT eligibility. Part-time CPT does not.
- Your Designated School Official is the authority on your specific case. This article explains the shape of the rules, not your individual situation.

## The distinction in one paragraph

**CPT** is work authorisation attached to your academic programme. It exists because the work is part of your curriculum — an internship that earns credit or is required by your degree. Your school authorises it, and it is employer-specific.

**OPT** is work authorisation attached to your degree field rather than to a course. It is granted by USCIS, not your school, and it is not tied to one employer. You can use part of it before graduating (pre-completion) or, far more commonly, after (post-completion).

The practical difference students care about: CPT is for the internship during your degree, OPT is for the job after it.

## CPT, in detail

CPT must be an integral part of your established curriculum. In practice that means either the work earns academic credit, or your programme requires it for the degree.

Key mechanics:

- **Employer-specific.** Your authorisation names one employer. Change jobs, and you need a new authorisation.
- **Authorised by your school**, through your DSO, not by USCIS. It is faster than OPT for that reason.
- **You must have completed one academic year** before becoming eligible, with narrow exceptions for programmes requiring immediate work.
- **Part-time is 20 hours or fewer per week**; anything above is full-time.

### The 12-month trap

This one catches people every year. **Twelve months or more of full-time CPT makes you ineligible for OPT.** Not reduced — eliminated.

Part-time CPT does not carry this penalty regardless of duration. So a student who works part-time CPT for two years keeps full OPT eligibility, while a student who works full-time CPT for exactly twelve months has lost it entirely.

If you are approaching heavy CPT use, count the months carefully before accepting another term. Trading OPT for a slightly better internship is almost never worth it, because OPT is what carries you through the job search after graduation.

## OPT, in detail

Post-completion OPT gives **12 months** of work authorisation in your field of study.

What matters practically:

- **Not employer-specific.** You can change jobs without new paperwork, provided the work relates to your field.
- **You apply to USCIS**, and processing takes months. This is why the application window matters so much.
- **You can apply up to 90 days before your programme end date**, and up to 60 days after. Apply early — processing delays are common and your start date is not flexible.
- **Work must relate to your degree field.** This is checked loosely in practice but is a real requirement.

### The 90-day unemployment clock

On post-completion OPT you may not accumulate more than **90 days of unemployment**. Exceed it and you fall out of status.

Things students misunderstand about this:

- The clock starts on your OPT start date, not on graduation, and not when you begin looking.
- Days accumulate whether or not you have applied for anything. There is no pause for effort.
- Part-time work counts as employed for this purpose if it is more than 20 hours per week and related to your field.
- Unpaid work can count, if it is genuinely related and meets the hour threshold — but document it.

Ninety days sounds generous until you map it against the [US campus recruiting calendar](/blog/usa-campus-recruiting-timeline). A May graduate whose OPT starts in July is out of days by early October — which is precisely when autumn recruiting for the *following* year is happening, not when employers are filling immediate seats. This mismatch is the core structural difficulty for international students, and it is why applying during your penultimate year matters so much more for you than for domestic classmates.

## STEM OPT: the 24-month extension

If your degree is on the official STEM designated degree programs list, you can apply for a **24-month extension**, giving 36 months total.

Why this is decisive:

- Three years of authorisation means you can be entered into the [H-1B lottery](/blog/usa-h1b-lottery-new-grads) multiple times rather than once. Given the odds, multiple attempts change the outcome materially.
- Employers know this. A candidate with 36 months of runway is a substantially easier hire than one with 12, because the company has time before the sponsorship decision becomes urgent.

Requirements that differ from standard OPT:

- Your employer must be enrolled in **E-Verify**. Many small companies are not, which quietly rules them out.
- A formal training plan (Form I-983) must be completed with the employer. Some smaller employers will not do the paperwork.
- The unemployment allowance increases — an additional 60 days across the extension period.

### Check the list, do not assume

The STEM list is specific and based on the CIP code of your programme, not on whether the subject sounds technical. Some business analytics and economics programmes qualify; some computer-adjacent programmes do not. Ask your DSO for your programme's CIP code and check it against the current list rather than assuming.

## How this shapes your job search

The rules are not just paperwork — they change what strategy works.

**Apply earlier than everyone else.** Sponsorship makes you a slower hire. Being in the pipeline early is worth more to you than to a domestic student.

**Filter employers by whether they sponsor.** Applying to companies that never sponsor is wasted effort. Many state this on careers pages. For STEM OPT specifically, E-Verify enrolment is a hard filter.

**Have your dates ready.** When an employer asks about authorisation, a precise answer — how many months you have, what happens after, whether you need sponsorship immediately — reads as competence. Vagueness reads as risk. How to handle the [visa sponsorship question in interviews](/blog/usa-visa-sponsorship-question-interview) is worth rehearsing before it comes up.

**Do not hide your status.** It surfaces during the offer process regardless, and discovering it late costs you goodwill you cannot rebuild.

Your resume should not mention visa status — it is not relevant to the screen and takes space from your work. Focus that space on evidence, and make sure the document parses cleanly, since [ATS rejection](/blog/how-to-check-ats-score-free) removes you before any human considers sponsorship at all.

## Common Mistakes

- **Accumulating 12 months of full-time CPT.** It destroys OPT eligibility, and students often discover this after the fact.
- **Applying for OPT late.** Processing takes months. Applying at the last legal moment means unpaid gaps you cannot control.
- **Assuming the 90-day clock starts when you begin job hunting.** It starts on your OPT start date.
- **Assuming your degree is STEM.** Verify the CIP code rather than guessing from the subject name.
- **Not confirming E-Verify enrolment before relying on a STEM extension.** A job offer from a non-enrolled employer cannot support the extension.
- **Taking immigration advice from other students.** Rules change and individual cases vary. Your DSO is the authority; forums are not.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I work on CPT and OPT for the same employer?

Yes. Many students intern on CPT and then continue with the same employer on OPT after graduating. The authorisations are separate and you must have OPT approved before the CPT work ends.

### Does part-time CPT affect my OPT?

No. Only full-time CPT counts toward the 12-month threshold that eliminates OPT eligibility. Part-time CPT of any duration leaves OPT intact.

### What counts as employment for the 90-day rule?

Paid or unpaid work related to your field, generally at more than 20 hours per week. Volunteer and unpaid roles can count if genuinely related, but keep documentation — the burden of proof is yours.

### Can I be self-employed or freelance on OPT?

It is possible on standard post-completion OPT if the work relates to your field and you can document it properly. STEM OPT is much more restrictive because it requires an E-Verify employer and a formal training plan.

### When exactly should I apply for OPT?

As early as the rules allow — up to 90 days before your programme end date. Processing times vary and there is no benefit to waiting. An approved authorisation you have not started using is far better than a pending one on your intended start date.

### Does travelling abroad affect OPT?

It can, and the rules differ depending on whether OPT is pending or approved and whether you have a job. This is exactly the kind of question to take to your DSO before booking anything, not after.
