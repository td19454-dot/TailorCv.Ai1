## Key Takeaways

- Place your portfolio link in the header/contact line of your resume for visibility during quick skims.
- Use a clean, readable URL and hyperlink the text while displaying the URL to ensure accessibility for both ATS and humans.
- Enhance clickability by adding a one-line hook that describes your portfolio's content and value.
- Ensure consistency across your resume, LinkedIn, and portfolio regarding name, headline, and featured work to avoid confusion.
- Regularly test and update your portfolio link, ensuring it is mobile-friendly and optimized for SEO.


A portfolio only helps if recruiters can find and click it - and surprisingly many candidates either hide the link, format it badly, or let it 404. ## Where to Put It
In the **header/contact line**, alongside your email and LinkedIn. Keep it near the top so it survives the seven-second skim. See [Resume Contact Section](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/resume-contact-section).

## Format It for ATS and Humans
- Use a **clean, readable URL**: yourname.com or [GitHub](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/github-portfolio-optimization).com/you
- **Hyperlink the text and also show the URL** - some [ATS](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/what-is-an-ats-score-and-why-does-it-decide-your-job-application-before-any-human-reads-it) strip hyperlinks, so the visible text must still be usable
- Avoid long tracking URLs and link shorteners (they look spammy and can break)
- Make sure it is **https** and live

## Make People Want to Click
A bare URL is easy to ignore. Add a one-line hook: "Portfolio: live projects + case studies -> yourname.com." Context earns clicks. If you are a developer, link both your site and your GitHub ([GitHub Portfolio Optimization](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/github-portfolio-optimization)).

## Common Mistakes to Avoid
When adding your portfolio link to your resume, many candidates make easily avoidable mistakes. Here are some of the most common pitfalls:

1. **Using a Non-Professional URL**: Avoid using URLs that are unprofessional or contain unnecessary characters. Instead, stick with a straightforward format like yourname.com or yournameportfolio.com.

2. **Forgetting to Update Links**: Ensure that your portfolio link is always current. An outdated link can lead to missed opportunities.

3. **Neglecting Mobile Optimization**: Many recruiters review resumes on mobile devices. Make sure your portfolio is mobile-friendly. Test how it looks on various devices.

4. **Overloading with Content**: While showcasing your work is essential, too much information can overwhelm recruiters. Curate your portfolio to highlight your best projects.

5. **Ignoring SEO Best Practices**: If your portfolio is online, apply SEO techniques to improve its visibility. Use relevant keywords, meta descriptions, and alt tags for images.

## How to Choose the Right Portfolio Format
The format of your portfolio can significantly impact how recruiters perceive your work. Here are some formats to consider based on your profession:

- **Website Portfolio**: Ideal for creatives like designers and photographers. A personal website allows for complete customization and branding. Use [How to Put Your Portfolio on a Custom Domain](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/custom-domain-portfolio-guide) to learn how to set this up.

- **GitHub Portfolio**: Perfect for developers. A GitHub account can showcase your coding skills and projects effectively. Ensure your repositories are well-documented to enhance recruiter understanding.

- **PDF Portfolio**: Useful for professionals in fields like business analysis. A well-designed PDF can be easily shared and printed. Refer to the [Business Analyst Portfolio Guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/business-analyst-portfolio-guide) for tips.

- **Video Portfolio**: If you're in a field like marketing or broadcasting, a video portfolio can be a dynamic way to showcase your work. Keep it concise and engaging.

## Creating a Compelling Portfolio
If you don’t have a portfolio yet or want to improve your existing one, consider these steps:

1. **Select Your Best Work**: Choose 4-6 projects that showcase your skills and align with the jobs you are applying for.
2. **Write Case Studies**: For each project, include a brief description of the problem, your solution, and the impact it had. This contextualizes your work for recruiters.
3. **Use Visuals**: Include images or videos of your work. Visuals can make your portfolio more engaging and memorable.
4. **Keep It Updated**: Regularly update your portfolio with new projects and achievements to keep it fresh and relevant.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Will ATS read my portfolio link?

Most parse the visible URL text fine; some strip the clickable hyperlink. Always show the URL as readable text, not hidden behind a word like 'here'.

### Should I link GitHub or my website?

Both if relevant. For developers, [GitHub](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/github-portfolio-optimization) plus a site is ideal. For designers, the site (or [Behance](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/portfolio-website-vs-resume)) comes first.

### Where exactly on the resume?

The header contact line. Keep it with email and [LinkedIn](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-use-linkedin-for-job-search) so it is seen immediately. You can also consider using a [portfolio builder](https://www.thetailorcv.com/portfolio) to create a professional online presence.
