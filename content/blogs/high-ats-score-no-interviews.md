## Key Takeaways

- A high score confirms legibility and keyword relevance - it does not confirm that your resume is persuasive.
- The five usual causes are weak evidence, a buried lead, a seniority mismatch, targeting, and volume.
- Duties-instead-of-results is the most common and the most fixable.
- Scanners reward coverage; humans reward specificity and outcomes. Optimising only for the first produces exactly this situation.
- If your applications are well-matched and the resume is strong, the remaining variable is often competition, not you.

You scan at 85%. The missing-keyword list is nearly empty. And nobody calls.

This is a genuinely different problem from a low score, and continuing to optimise keywords will not touch it. The scanner stage is solved. What follows is a human reading your resume for about seven seconds and deciding whether to spend ten minutes more.

## 1. Your Bullets List Duties, Not Results

This is the most common cause by a wide margin, and a scanner cannot detect it. "Responsible for managing the reporting process" contains good keywords and tells a hiring manager nothing about whether you are any good.

> **Scores well, persuades nobody:** Responsible for managing weekly sales reporting and stakeholder communication.
>
> **Scores the same, persuades:** Rebuilt weekly sales reporting from manual spreadsheets to an automated dashboard, cutting turnaround from two days to under an hour and removing a recurring source of month-end errors.

The second version contains the same keywords. It also contains a before, an after, and a consequence - which is what the reader is actually looking for.

**The test:** read each bullet and ask "so what?" If the answer is not in the bullet, it is a duty, not an achievement.

## 2. Your Best Work Is on Page Two

A recruiter's first pass covers the top third of page one: your summary, your most recent title, and the first two or three bullets under it.

If your strongest, most relevant achievement is the fourth bullet of your second job, it is effectively invisible during the decision that matters.

**The fix is ordering, not writing.** For each application, make sure the single most relevant thing you have done appears in the top third. That may mean reordering bullets within a role, or naming the achievement in your summary.

## 3. You Are Reading as the Wrong Level

Scanners match terms. Humans read seniority signals - scope, autonomy, who you influenced, what you owned.

Two failure modes:

**Reading junior for a senior role.** Your bullets describe tasks you completed rather than outcomes you owned. Nothing indicates you set direction, handled ambiguity, or worked across teams.

**Reading senior for a hands-on role.** Everything is strategy and oversight, and a hiring manager who needs someone to do the work cannot see evidence you still do it.

**The fix:** match the scope language to the level. For a step up, foreground ownership, cross-team work and decisions you made. For a hands-on role, keep the specifics of what you personally built or ran.

## 4. You Are Applying to the Wrong Jobs Well

A high score is achievable on a posting you are not competitive for. Keyword overlap is not the same as candidacy.

If you are consistently three or more years short of the stated experience, missing the central credential, or applying across an industry boundary with no bridging evidence, the resume is doing its job and the targeting is the problem.

**The fix:** look at your last twenty applications. If they were mostly a stretch, the answer is not another resume edit - it is a more realistic band of roles, plus a few stretches rather than only stretches.

## 5. Volume and Timing

Some of this is not about you. A posting open for three weeks with six hundred applicants gives even strong resumes poor odds. Roles filled internally get posted anyway. Hiring freezes arrive mid-process.

**The fix:** apply earlier where you can, prioritise postings under a week old, and treat referral routes as worth more than additional cold applications - a referred application is read differently regardless of score.

## Diagnosing Which One You Have

The pattern of responses tells you a lot:

**No responses at all, across many applications.** Look at evidence quality and targeting - causes 1 and 4.

**Occasional recruiter screens, no manager rounds.** Usually seniority or evidence depth - causes 1 and 3.

**Manager rounds but no offers.** The resume is working. The problem has moved to interviews, and no resume edit will help.

**Responses only from smaller companies.** Often a brand or scope signal - your evidence may not read at the scale larger employers screen for.

## The Honest Part

A resume cannot make a weak match strong. If you are applying for roles a level above your experience in a competitive market, an excellent resume improves your odds without making them good.

What an excellent resume does is ensure you are not losing applications you should have won - and that is worth securing before concluding the market is the problem.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Should I keep optimising keywords if my score is already high?**
No. Past a good range, further keyword work adds nothing and usually costs readability. Move to evidence quality.

**How do I know if my bullets are strong enough?**
Ask whether each one contains a result a stranger would find impressive without knowing your company. If it needs context you have not given, rewrite it.

**Is my resume design the problem?**
Rarely, if it is clean and parses. Recruiters read content. Design matters mainly when it obstructs - dense text, tiny type, or a layout that hides the top third.

**Could my resume be too tailored?**
Yes. A resume that mirrors the posting too closely reads as generic and reveals nothing distinctive. Tailoring should surface your relevant evidence, not replace your voice.

**How many applications before I conclude something is wrong?**
Twenty to thirty well-matched applications with no responses is a real signal. Fewer than ten tells you very little.

## Check the Part a Score Cannot See

The scanner stage is solved; the next reader is human. That means looking at whether each bullet states an outcome, and whether your strongest evidence sits high enough to be seen.

If you want a second read on the document itself, [scan it against a posting](/ats-analysis) and then ignore the score - look instead at which of your bullets carry a measurable result, and which are duties in disguise.
