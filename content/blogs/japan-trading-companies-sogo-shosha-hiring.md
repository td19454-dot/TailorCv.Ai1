## Key Takeaways

- General trading companies operate across an unusually wide range of industries, combining trade, investment and business development.
- They are consistently among the most contested graduate employers in Japan, with high compensation and strong prestige.
- Overseas assignment is a core part of the career, which is a genuine differentiator for anyone wanting international work.
- Recruiting follows the conventional graduate system closely, with high language and communication expectations.
- Foreign graduates are hired, and the bar is high and the language requirement is generally substantial.

## What they actually do

**A business model with few international equivalents.** General trading companies began in trade intermediation and have evolved into diversified conglomerates that trade goods, invest in and operate businesses, develop infrastructure and natural resource projects, and build supply chains across sectors.

**The range is genuinely wide.** A single company may be involved in energy, metals, chemicals, machinery, food, textiles, logistics, real estate, healthcare and technology simultaneously.

**Which makes the work varied and hard to describe.** A graduate might work on commodity trading, on structuring an investment in an overseas business, on developing a supply relationship, or on managing an operating subsidiary. The common thread is connecting parties, capital and capability across borders.

**Why this matters for a graduate.** The breadth means genuine variety and it means you frequently do not choose your area — you are assigned, which is characteristic of the [conventional graduate system](/blog/japan-new-grad-shinsotsu-hiring-explained) and particularly pronounced here.

## Why they are so contested

**Prestige.** Consistently among the most desired employers for Japanese graduates.

**Compensation.** Among the highest graduate packages in Japan, with the [bonus structure](/blog/japan-salary-expectations-new-grad) contributing substantially.

**Global scope.** Overseas assignment is a standard part of the career path rather than an occasional possibility, which is a genuine attraction for anyone wanting international work built into the job.

**Breadth of experience.** Exposure across industries and functions that few employers can match.

**The consequence:** application volumes are very high, selection is long and rigorous, and the bar on communication and character assessment is set accordingly.

## The recruiting process

**It follows the conventional system closely.** [The shūkatsu timeline](/blog/japan-job-hunting-shukatsu-timeline) applies, with summer internships in the penultimate year functioning as an important early stage.

**Written applications matter.** Entry sheets with substantial free-response sections, read carefully, and where generic answers fail visibly.

**Aptitude testing** is standard — the [test formats](/blog/japan-group-interview-and-spi-tests) apply.

**Group discussions** feature prominently, and given that the job is fundamentally about working with and between people, collaborative behaviour is weighted heavily.

**Multiple [interview](/blog/japan-mensetsu-interview-questions-guide) rounds**, progressing to senior management, assessing character, motivation and fit at length. The [standard question set](/blog/japan-mensetsu-interview-questions-guide) applies, with particular attention to why this industry and why this company.

**Commercial curiosity is assessed.** Being able to discuss business, trade and international developments with genuine interest separates candidates.

## For foreign graduates

**They do hire foreign graduates**, particularly those educated in Japan, and the bar is high.

**Language expectations are generally substantial.** The work is relationship-driven and conducted largely in Japanese, which means business-level ability is typically expected — see the [language requirements guide](/blog/japan-japanese-language-requirements-jobs).

**Your international background can be a genuine asset**, particularly language ability in a market the company operates in, cultural knowledge of a region, or experience that maps to their business.

**Be specific about which business area interests you** and why, drawing on their actual operations rather than the general idea of trading.

**Expect overseas assignment to be discussed**, and be honest about your willingness. Being posted abroad is part of the deal rather than a perk you opt into.

**Visa sponsorship is routine** at this scale — the [work visa](/blog/japan-work-visa-for-graduates) is applied for by the employer.

## Common Mistakes

- **Applying without understanding the business model.** It is genuinely unusual and a vague answer is immediately visible.
- **Not having a specific area of interest.** "Anything" reads as no research.
- **Underestimating the language requirement.** The work is relationship-driven and conducted in Japanese.
- **Treating overseas assignment as optional.** It is a core part of the career path.
- **Missing the internship stage.** It functions as early relationship-building in this system.
- **No commercial awareness.** Assessed directly and frequently thin in candidates.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does a general trading company actually do?

Trades goods, invests in and operates businesses, develops projects and builds supply chains across a very wide range of industries. The model has few direct international equivalents.

### Do they hire foreign graduates?

Yes, particularly graduates of Japanese universities, with a high bar and generally substantial [Japanese language](/blog/japan-japanese-language-requirements-jobs) expectations.

### Do I choose my business area?

Usually not initially. Assignment after joining is characteristic of the conventional system and particularly pronounced at these companies.

### Is overseas assignment guaranteed?

It is a standard part of the career path at most of these companies rather than an occasional possibility, though timing and location are the company's decision.

### How does compensation compare?

Among the highest graduate packages in Japan, with the bonus structure contributing substantially — see the [salary structure guide](/blog/japan-salary-expectations-new-grad).

### What if my Japanese is not business level?

These are generally not the right target at that level. [Technology](/blog/japan-tech-jobs-for-foreign-graduates) and [English-conducted employers](/blog/japan-english-speaking-jobs-guide) are the more realistic routes while you build the language.
