## Key Takeaways

- Selection is by lottery when registrations exceed the cap, so a strategy that depends on being chosen is fragile by design.
- Public disclosure data lets you identify which employers have actually sponsored, in what roles, and at what salaries.
- Cap-exempt employers - universities, affiliated non-profits, and some research bodies - avoid the lottery entirely and are consistently overlooked.
- The cycle is annual and rigid: registration in spring, employment starting in autumn, so missing it costs a year.
- Build a plan with alternatives - L-1 transfer, cap-exempt roles, or working abroad for a US company - rather than one that only works if the lottery goes your way.

The H-1B is the main route into US employment for skilled foreign workers, and it has a feature that makes it unlike almost any other visa process: at the final step, it is partly random.

When registrations exceed the annual cap - which they consistently do, by a wide margin - selection is made by lottery. That means a strong candidate with a willing employer can still be unsuccessful, through no fault of anyone involved.

Understanding that changes how you should search. The goal is not only to find a sponsor, but to build a plan that survives not being selected.

**A note on accuracy:** immigration rules, caps, fees, and registration procedures change, and the details matter. Verify current requirements with USCIS or an immigration attorney rather than relying on any summary, including this one.

## How the Process Actually Works

The sequence, in outline:

**1. You get a job offer** from a US employer willing to sponsor. This comes first; you cannot obtain an H-1B independently.

**2. The employer registers you** in the electronic registration system during the annual registration window, typically in March.

**3. A lottery is run** if registrations exceed the cap. The regular cap plus a separate allocation for US master's degree holders means advanced-degree candidates effectively get additional consideration.

**4. If selected**, the employer files the full petition, including a Labor Condition Application establishing that you will be paid at least the prevailing wage for the role and location.

**5. If approved**, employment can typically begin at the start of the government fiscal year in October.

The two features that shape everything: **it is annual**, so timing is rigid, and **it is capped**, so selection is uncertain.

## Finding Employers Who Sponsor

This is where most candidates waste months, and where a few hours of research changes the search entirely.

### Use public disclosure data

Employers filing H-1B petitions and permanent labour certifications submit data that becomes public record. Several free websites aggregate this into searchable databases.

What you can look up:

- **Which employers** have sponsored, by name
- **How many** petitions they filed, by year
- **Which job titles** they sponsored for
- **What salaries** were reported
- **Which locations**

This is enormously more useful than guessing. An employer who filed dozens of petitions last year in your job category has a working process, in-house familiarity, and demonstrated willingness. An employer who has never filed one may not know how, and may not want to find out.

**Practical approach:** build a target list of thirty to fifty employers who have sponsored in your field and location, and treat that as your primary application set.

### Read postings literally

Many US job postings state their position explicitly - either that sponsorship is unavailable, or that it is offered. Take both at face value. Applying to a posting that says sponsorship is not available is not persistence; it is wasted effort.

### Understand who sponsors and who does not

**More likely:** large technology companies, IT consulting and staffing firms, engineering firms, healthcare systems, universities, research institutions, financial services, and management consulting.

**Less likely:** small businesses, retail, hospitality, most government roles, anything requiring security clearance, and companies with no prior filing history.

## Cap-Exempt Employers: The Overlooked Route

This is the most underused piece of information in H-1B job searching.

Certain employers are exempt from the annual cap, meaning **no lottery**. Broadly, these include:

- **Institutions of higher education**
- **Non-profit organisations affiliated with or related to a university**
- **Non-profit research organisations and governmental research organisations**

If you can obtain a role with a cap-exempt employer, you may be able to file at any time of year without lottery uncertainty. That is a fundamentally different proposition from the regular route.

**Why candidates miss this:** they assume it means academic research positions only. In practice, universities and affiliated medical centres, hospitals, and research institutes employ people across a wide range of functions - IT, data, engineering, administration, finance.

**Worth knowing:** there are also arrangements involving concurrent employment with cap-exempt and cap-subject employers, but the rules are specific and this is exactly the kind of area where you should consult an immigration attorney rather than internet summaries.

If a lottery-free route exists in your field, it deserves serious investigation before you place all your hopes on a spring registration.

## Timing the Annual Cycle

The calendar is rigid, and international candidates routinely misjudge it.

**Autumn to winter** - job searching. You need an offer in hand before registration.

**Spring (typically March)** - the registration window. Your employer registers you. If you do not have an offer by then, you have missed the cycle.

**Spring** - lottery results announced.

**Spring to summer** - if selected, the employer files the full petition.

**October** - the earliest employment start date for that cycle.

**The practical consequence:** if you graduate in May and start looking then, you have already missed that year's registration. This is why students on OPT matter so much - OPT bridges the gap, giving you work authorisation while you wait for a lottery cycle, and STEM OPT extends that across multiple cycles.

## Making Your Application Work

**State your situation clearly.** Application forms ask whether you require sponsorship, and you must answer honestly. Add your timeline - "F-1, STEM OPT eligible through 2029" tells an employer they have years before any filing is needed, which changes the risk calculation completely.

**Target roles that meet the criteria.** H-1B is for specialty occupations normally requiring at least a bachelor's degree in a specific field. Your role and your degree need to align, and the employer's petition has to establish that.

**Understand the wage requirement.** The employer must pay at least the prevailing wage for that occupation and location. This is a genuine floor - it prevents lowball offers, and it means an employer cannot sponsor you cheaply. It also means an unusually low offer should raise questions.

**Be someone worth the effort.** Sponsorship costs money and time. Your resume needs to make the value obvious - specific skills, quantified results, clear relevance. ## If You Are Not Selected

This happens to many well-qualified people. Plan for it in advance rather than reacting to it.

**Re-register next cycle.** If you still hold valid status - OPT, particularly STEM OPT - you can be registered again the following year. Multiple attempts across several years is a common pattern.

**Target cap-exempt employers.** No lottery. This is the most direct alternative.

**Consider the L-1 route.** Join a multinational's office in your home country, work there for the qualifying period, then transfer to the US. Slower, but it avoids the lottery entirely and many people plan for it deliberately.

**Look at other visa categories.** O-1 for those with substantial documented achievement, TN for Canadian and Mexican citizens, and country-specific arrangements where they exist.

**Work for a US company from abroad.** Remote and offshore roles with US employers build relevant experience and a relationship, both of which help later. See [remote jobs in the USA](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/remote-jobs-in-usa-guide).

**Consider other markets.** Canada, the UK, and elsewhere have routes that do not involve a lottery - see [how to get a job in Canada](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-get-a-job-in-canada) and [the UK Skilled Worker visa guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/uk-skilled-worker-visa-guide).

## Common Mistakes

**Applying without checking sponsorship history.** Public data exists; use it.

**Ignoring cap-exempt employers.** The only route that removes lottery uncertainty.

**Missing the registration window.** Annual and rigid - no offer by spring means no cycle that year.

**Hiding your sponsorship requirement.** Forms ask directly and honesty is required.

**Assuming a big company must sponsor.** Many large employers do not, in some categories.

**Planning only around the lottery.** Build alternatives before you need them.

**Accepting a below-prevailing-wage offer.** It cannot support a petition and signals a problem.

**Relying on internet summaries for legal decisions.** Consult an immigration attorney for anything consequential.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I find companies that sponsor H-1B visas?

Public disclosure data from H-1B and labour certification filings is aggregated by several free websites, searchable by employer, job title, year, and location. This shows who has actually sponsored rather than who might.

### What does cap-exempt mean?

Certain employers - higher education institutions, affiliated non-profits, and non-profit or governmental research organisations - are not subject to the annual cap, so petitions can be filed without going through the lottery.

### When is the H-1B registration period?

Typically in March, for employment starting in October of the same year. Dates and procedures are set annually by USCIS, so confirm the current cycle rather than assuming.

### Does a master's degree improve my chances?

A US master's degree qualifies you for an additional allocation beyond the regular cap, which effectively provides a second chance in the selection process. Degrees from outside the US do not confer this.

### What happens if I am not selected in the lottery?

You can be registered again in a future cycle if you maintain valid status. OPT and particularly STEM OPT are what make multiple attempts possible, which is why they matter so much.

### Can I change employers on an H-1B?

Transfers are possible, and a transfer petition for someone already counted against the cap generally does not go through the lottery again. The specifics matter, so get advice before resigning anything.

### Should I use an immigration attorney?

For anything consequential, yes. The employer usually engages counsel for the petition itself, but for your own planning - particularly around cap-exempt routes, status changes, and timing - professional advice is worth the cost given what is at stake.
