## Key Takeaways

- **Mining and resources pay substantially above** most other Australian sectors for equivalent experience, particularly for engineers, geologists and skilled trades.
- **FIFO — fly-in fly-out — is the dominant model** for remote operations, and the roster shapes your entire life rather than just your working week.
- **Western Australia and Queensland dominate**, with the Pilbara, the Goldfields and the Bowen Basin being the major centres.
- **The sector is cyclical.** Commodity prices drive hiring, and downturns are severe when they come.
- **Critical minerals and decarbonisation** have created genuine new demand alongside traditional iron ore, coal and gas.

## What the sector actually contains

Broader than most people assume.

**Iron ore** in the Pilbara, which is the largest single export earner and the heart of Western Australian mining.

**Coal**, both metallurgical and thermal, concentrated in Queensland's Bowen Basin and New South Wales' Hunter Valley.

**Gold**, particularly in the Western Australian Goldfields around Kalgoorlie.

**LNG and gas**, offshore and onshore, with major processing facilities and a large engineering workforce.

**Critical minerals** — lithium, rare earths, nickel, cobalt — which have grown rapidly with battery and defence demand.

**Bauxite, alumina and aluminium**, copper, zinc and mineral sands.

**And the services ecosystem around all of it** — METS, mining equipment technology and services, which employs more people than the mines themselves and includes engineering consultancies, drilling contractors, automation specialists and maintenance providers.

## The roles

Who actually gets hired.

**Mining engineers, geologists and metallurgists** — the core technical disciplines, in persistent demand.

**Mechanical, electrical, civil and process engineers** across operations, maintenance and projects.

**Skilled trades** — diesel fitters, boilermakers, electricians, riggers, heavy vehicle mechanics — which are in acute demand and pay exceptionally well.

**Operators** for haul trucks, drills, loaders and processing plant, frequently with employer-provided training.

**Automation and remote operations specialists**, since Australian mining leads globally in autonomous haulage and remote operations centres.

**Environmental scientists, hydrogeologists and rehabilitation specialists**, driven by regulatory requirements and closure obligations.

**Safety, health and training professionals**, which the sector takes extremely seriously.

**Supply chain, logistics and procurement**, which is enormous given the distances involved.

**And corporate functions in Perth and Brisbane** — finance, legal, community relations, technical services — which are city-based rather than site-based.

## FIFO, honestly

The part that decides whether people stay.

**Rosters vary widely** — two weeks on and one week off, eight days on and six off, and many other patterns. The roster matters more than the salary for long-term sustainability.

**Even-time rosters** (equal days on and off) are increasingly common and are far easier on relationships than heavily skewed patterns.

**Camp accommodation** is generally good at modern sites — private rooms, gyms, catered meals, internet — and varies considerably at older or contractor-run camps.

**The travel is unpaid time in most arrangements**, and a long charter flight plus a bus transfer eats into your break.

**Shifts are twelve hours** at most operations, days and nights, which is a genuine physical adjustment.

**Alcohol is restricted or prohibited** on most sites, and drug and alcohol testing is routine and unannounced.

**Relationships are where the strain shows.** People with strong routines and clear agreements at home do well; people using FIFO to avoid something at home generally do not.

**And the money is genuinely excellent**, which is why the trade-off is worth making explicitly rather than drifting into.

## Getting hired

The practical mechanics.

**Tickets and certifications gate site access** — a construction white card, a standard 11 generic induction, first aid, working at heights, confined space and vehicle licences depending on the role.

**A pre-employment medical** is standard, covering fitness, hearing, vision and drug screening.

**Operators are frequently trained by employers**, and entry-level operator roles are among the more accessible ways in without a degree.

**Contractors hire heavily** — drilling, maintenance, shutdown and construction contractors run much of the workforce and are frequently the fastest route to first site experience.

**Shutdowns and turnarounds** are short intensive projects that hire large numbers temporarily and are a well-known entry point for trades.

**Graduate programmes** at the major operators are competitive, structured and an excellent start for engineers and geologists.

**Seek, LinkedIn and specialist boards** carry the volume, and recruitment agencies specialising in resources are genuinely worth engaging.

**Run your CV through an [ATS check](/ats-analysis)** since the major operators screen at volume, and keep a [tailored version](/tailor-resume) that names the specific commodity, equipment and systems you have worked with.

## Pay and the cycle

The financial picture, with the caveat that defines it.

**Site-based technical and trade roles pay substantially above** their equivalents in general industry, with site allowances, roster loadings and overtime on top of base.

**Superannuation applies to the whole package** in most arrangements, which compounds the advantage considerably over a career.

**Camp costs are covered** — accommodation, meals and flights — which means a high proportion of gross pay is genuinely disposable.

**City-based resources roles** in Perth and Brisbane pay less than site but well above the general professional market.

**The cycle is the caveat.** Commodity prices drive capital spending, capital spending drives hiring, and downturns arrive quickly and cut deeply.

**Contractors feel the cycle first**, since operators reduce contracted services before their own permanent workforce.

**Which means** the sensible pattern is to treat the high-earning years as a window — clear debt, build savings and acquire transferable qualifications — rather than as a permanent baseline that will always be there.

## Where the sector is going

The genuine growth areas.

**Critical minerals** for batteries, magnets and defence supply chains, with lithium, rare earths and nickel driving new projects and new processing capacity.

**Downstream processing** rather than raw export, which is a deliberate policy direction and creates chemical and process engineering demand.

**Automation and remote operations**, where Australian mining leads globally — autonomous haulage, automated drilling and integrated remote operations centres in Perth running sites hundreds of kilometres away.

**Decarbonisation of operations** — electrification of fleets, renewable power at remote sites, hydrogen trials — which has created genuinely new engineering roles.

**Mine closure and rehabilitation**, a growing regulatory obligation and a whole discipline of its own.

**Water and tailings management**, where standards have risen sharply following international failures.

**And METS export**, since Australian mining technology and services companies sell expertise worldwide, which is a route into the sector that never involves a site at all.

## Building a career beyond site

Where mining experience leads.

**Into technical services and consulting**, where site experience is the credential that matters.

**Into operations management**, progressing from supervisor to superintendent to manager.

**Into corporate and technical roles** in Perth and Brisbane, which is the standard move when FIFO stops fitting your life.

**Into METS and equipment suppliers**, which value operational credibility highly.

**Into project development and studies**, working on feasibility rather than production.

**Internationally**, since Australian mining experience is genuinely portable to Canada, Chile, Africa and Central Asia.

**And into adjacent sectors** — infrastructure, energy and heavy industry all recruit from mining, particularly for maintenance, safety and project disciplines.

## Safety culture, and why it is different here

Something newcomers consistently underestimate.

**Australian mining safety standards are among the strictest anywhere**, and the culture around them is genuine rather than performative.

**Stop-work authority is real.** Any person on site can halt a task they consider unsafe, and using it is supported rather than penalised.

**Pre-start meetings, take-fives and JHAs** structure every shift, and treating them as paperwork rather than thinking is the fastest way to lose credibility.

**Reporting near-misses is expected**, and a site with low reported incidents is frequently a site with poor reporting rather than a safe one.

**Fatigue management is regulated**, which is why rosters and shift patterns are structured the way they are.

**And the consequences are personal.** Serious breaches end careers, and the sector has a long institutional memory for people who cut corners.

## A note on Indigenous engagement

A genuine and growing part of the sector.

**Native title and land access agreements** govern much mining activity, and community relations is a serious professional function rather than a public relations one.

**Indigenous employment and procurement targets** are standard in agreements and are actively managed.

**Cultural heritage management** became a far higher priority across the industry following well-publicised failures, and specialists in this area are in genuine demand.

**Cultural awareness training** is standard for site personnel.

**And for anyone building a career here**, understanding this properly is now part of professional competence in Australian resources rather than an optional extra.

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: You are an engineer early in your career

Site experience early is the single most valuable thing you can acquire, and it opens consulting, technical services and management roles later. Two to four years on site is a common and deliberate pattern.

### Scenario 2: You have a trade qualification

This is among the best-paid destinations for your skills anywhere in the world. Confirm your tickets are recognised in the relevant state and be realistic about the roster before committing.

### Scenario 3: You want the pay without the roster

City-based roles in Perth and Brisbane exist across engineering consultancies, technical services and corporate functions. They pay less than site but far above general market, without the FIFO life.

> **Tip:** Before accepting a FIFO role, talk to someone two years into the same roster rather than someone two months in. The money is genuinely excellent and the pattern that decides whether people stay is how the roster interacts with relationships and routine, which nobody can assess from the offer letter.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Get site experience early if you are an engineer
- Confirm your tickets are recognised in that state
- Understand the roster before signing
- Consider METS and services alongside operators
- Look at critical minerals as growth areas
- Plan your exit from site work in advance

### Don't

- Underestimate the lifestyle impact of FIFO
- Assume the boom lasts forever
- Ignore the cyclical nature of the sector
- Overlook city-based resources roles
- Skip the medical and fitness requirements
- Treat safety culture as bureaucracy

## Common Mistakes

- **Underestimating FIFO's personal cost.** The money is real and so is the strain.
- **Ignoring cyclicality.** Downturns in this sector are severe and fast.
- **Overlooking METS.** The services ecosystem employs more people than the mines.
- **Assuming all mining is remote.** Perth and Brisbane hold large corporate workforces.
- **Neglecting tickets.** Certifications are state-specific and gate site access.
- **Dismissing safety culture.** It is genuinely central and non-negotiable here.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is FIFO?

Fly-in fly-out — working a roster at a remote site and flying home between swings. It is the dominant model for remote Australian operations and it shapes your whole life, not just your working week.

### Does mining pay better than other sectors?

Substantially, for engineers, geologists and skilled trades in particular. City-based resources roles pay less than site but still well above the general market.

### Where are the main mining regions?

The Pilbara and Goldfields in Western Australia, the Bowen Basin in Queensland and the Hunter Valley in New South Wales, with Perth and Brisbane as the corporate centres.

### Is the sector stable?

It is cyclical. Commodity prices drive hiring and downturns are severe, which is why diversification of skills and an exit plan from site work both matter.

### What are critical minerals?

Lithium, rare earths, nickel, cobalt and similar minerals central to batteries and defence supply chains. They are the fastest-growing part of the Australian resources sector.

### Can I work in resources without going to site?

Yes. Engineering consultancies, technical services, automation, corporate functions and remote operations centres in Perth and Brisbane are all city-based.
### What is a FIFO roster actually like?

Patterns vary from two weeks on and one off to even-time arrangements, with twelve-hour shifts, camp accommodation, restricted alcohol and routine drug testing. The roster matters more than the salary for sustainability.

### How do I get into mining without experience?

Operator roles with employer-provided training, contractor positions in drilling and maintenance, and shutdown work are the common entry points. Site access tickets and a pre-employment medical are prerequisites.
### How does the commodity cycle affect mining careers?

Prices drive capital spending and hiring, downturns arrive quickly and cut deeply, and contractors feel it first. The sensible pattern is treating high-earning years as a window rather than a permanent baseline.

### What are the growth areas in Australian resources?

Critical minerals, downstream processing, automation and remote operations where Australia leads globally, decarbonisation of operations, mine closure and rehabilitation, and METS technology export.

### Where does mining experience lead?

Technical services and consulting, operations management, city-based corporate roles, equipment suppliers, project development, international mining markets and adjacent heavy industries.
