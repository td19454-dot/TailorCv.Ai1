## Key Takeaways

- **EU, EEA and Swiss citizens** need only registration; the process is straightforward.
- The **Business Facilitation Unit and the headquartering regime** allow qualifying foreign-interest companies to employ third-country nationals with facilitated permits.
- **Spouse work rights** are provided for under that regime, which is more generous than many European systems.
- A **tax exemption for first employment in Cyprus** and the **non-domiciled regime** together make the personal tax position genuinely favourable.
- **Limassol is where most international employment is**, and housing there has become the main cost pressure.

## The two positions

**EU, EEA and Swiss citizens** have free movement. You arrive, register with the Civil Registry and Migration Department, obtain a registration certificate, and work without restriction.

**Third-country nationals** need a permit, and here the route matters enormously.

**The standard route** involves employer application with conditions on the position and the labour market.

**The facilitated route** applies to companies registered under the regime for foreign-interest companies — those with a physical presence in Cyprus, foreign ownership above a threshold and defined minimum investment. These companies can employ third-country nationals in categories including directors, middle management, key personnel and support staff, subject to minimum salary levels rather than a labour market test.

**The difference is substantial.** A third-country professional joining a registered company faces a far more tractable process than one joining an ordinary Cypriot employer.

**Practical implication:** identify whether a prospective employer is registered under the regime. It changes your position more than anything else about the job.

## Family and spouse rights

A genuinely favourable feature.

**Family reunification** is available to permit holders under the regime, for spouses and minor children.

**Spouses of third-country nationals employed under the regime, above a defined salary level, have the right to work** without needing a separate employer-sponsored permit.

**This is more generous than several European systems**, where a spouse's employment depends on their own sponsorship, and it materially changes the proposition for a couple.

**Children's schooling** is available through the public system in Greek and through a substantial private international school sector in English.

**Healthcare** operates through GESY once you are contributing.

## The tax position

The other major advantage and the reason many people relocate.

**Exemption on employment income** is available for individuals taking up first employment in Cyprus who were not previously resident, with the exemption percentage, income threshold and duration set out in legislation and revised over time.

**The non-domiciled regime** exempts qualifying individuals who are Cyprus tax resident but not domiciled there from the special defence contribution on dividends, interest and rental income. For someone with investment income this is significant.

**The 60-day rule** allows individuals to become Cyprus tax resident with a shorter physical presence than the standard 183 days, subject to conditions including not being tax resident elsewhere and having business or employment ties to Cyprus.

**Corporate tax is low** by EU standards, which is why the headquartering strategy works.

**Get advice before arriving.** The sequencing of when you become resident, when income is received and which exemptions you claim is exactly the kind of thing that is optimisable in advance and fixed afterwards.

## Relocating in practice

What to arrange and in what order.

**Confirm your permit route** before anything else, since it determines the timeline.

**Documents:** passport, qualifications with apostille where required, criminal record certificate, employment contract, health insurance and proof of accommodation.

**Housing first if you can.** Limassol's rental market tightened considerably with the corporate influx, and arriving without accommodation is a genuine problem rather than an inconvenience.

**Banking takes time.** Cypriot banks apply substantial compliance checks and account opening can be slower than you expect — start early and bring thorough documentation.

**A car is close to essential** given limited public transport, and note the island drives on the left.

**Schools** should be arranged early if you have children, since the international schools in Limassol and Nicosia have waiting lists.

**And register for GESY** promptly, since healthcare access follows from it.

## Long-term status

Where residence leads.

**Permanent residence** follows a qualifying period of legal residence.

**EU long-term residence** is available with its own conditions and mobility rights across the union — a genuine consideration given Cyprus is an EU member.

**Citizenship** requires a longer qualifying period plus a Greek language and knowledge requirement.

**Note that Cyprus's investor citizenship scheme was terminated**, so anyone reading older material about citizenship by investment there should disregard it entirely.

**And continuity matters** for all of these, so track absences if long-term status is your goal.

## Comparing Cyprus to Malta

The natural comparison, since both are small English-friendly EU islands.

**On language**, Malta is fully English-official; Cyprus uses English widely in business with Greek as the official language. Malta is marginally more accessible.

**On sectors**, Malta leads in iGaming; Cyprus leads in ship management. Both have substantial financial services and forex.

**On size**, both are small, with Cyprus somewhat larger in population and land area.

**On tax**, both are favourable with different mechanisms — Cyprus's non-dom regime and first-employment exemption against Malta's remittance-based system for non-domiciled residents.

**On cost**, both have seen substantial housing increases driven by international arrivals, with Limassol and the Sliema-St Julian's corridor being the pressure points.

**On permits**, Cyprus's foreign-interest company regime and Malta's Key Employee Initiative both facilitate third-country hiring at qualifying employers.

**And on feel**, Cyprus is larger and more spread out; Malta is denser and more compact. Both are Mediterranean, both are EU, and the choice usually comes down to sector rather than country.

## Common questions before moving

**Is the market big enough?** In its specialisms, yes. Outside them, genuinely not — this is the defining consideration.

**Will I be isolated?** The international communities in Limassol and the main towns are large and connected, and the island is small enough that networks form in months.

**Can I bring a partner who works?** Under the foreign-interest regime and above the salary threshold, yes — spouse work rights are provided for.

**How is healthcare?** GESY provides national coverage funded by contributions, with private provision widely used alongside.

**And what about the divided island?** For professionals working in the Republic it is a background reality rather than a daily one, though it is worth understanding rather than being surprised by.

## Timelines and planning

What to expect and how to sequence it.

**EU citizens:** effectively immediate. Arrive, register, work.

**Third-country nationals at a registered foreign-interest company:** substantially faster than the standard route, though still measured in weeks to months depending on documentation.

**Third-country nationals at an ordinary employer:** longer, with more conditions on the position.

**Document preparation is the long pole**, as everywhere — apostilles, translations and criminal record certificates from every country of recent residence.

**Do not resign** until the permit is granted and confirmed.

**Housing should be arranged in parallel**, not afterwards, given how tight the Limassol market has become.

**And schooling**, if relevant, needs to start earliest of all — the international schools have genuine waiting lists.

## Life on the island, practically

The daily realities.

**A car is close to essential.** Public transport is limited and the island drives on the left, which surprises people arriving from continental Europe.

**Summer is very hot**, with daily life shifting toward early mornings and evenings.

**The winters are mild** and the shoulder seasons are excellent.

**Beaches and mountains are both close**, with the Troodos range offering genuine relief from summer heat.

**The international community is large in Limassol**, smaller elsewhere, and organised around sport, nationality groups and workplace circles.

**Flights connect well** to Europe and the Middle East, with Larnaca the main airport.

**And the island is small enough** that everywhere is reachable in a couple of hours, which makes weekends genuinely easy.

## Practical relocation notes

What the move actually involves beyond the permit.

**Registration with the Civil Registry and Migration Department** after arrival, within the required period.

**Social insurance registration**, handled with your employer.

**Tax registration** and obtaining a tax identification number.

**A local bank account**, which requires your documentation and takes longer than people expect.

**Health insurance** through GESY where you qualify, with private cover being common alongside.

**Housing**, where Limassol has become considerably more expensive than the rest of the island.

**A driving licence exchange** where your existing licence qualifies.

**And school registration** if you are relocating a family, with a substantial private and international school sector available.

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: You are an EU citizen

Straightforward. Register, obtain your certificate, and work. Say your EU status at the top of applications so employers know there is nothing to arrange.

### Scenario 2: You are a third-country national

Filter your search to companies registered under the foreign-interest regime. It is the difference between a facilitated process with spouse work rights and a considerably harder standard route.

### Scenario 3: You are relocating with a partner who also works

Cyprus is unusually good for this. Spouse work rights under the regime, above a salary threshold, mean both of you can work without a second sponsorship process.

> **Tip:** Before applying anywhere as a third-country national, check whether the employer is registered as a foreign-interest company. That single fact determines whether you face a facilitated permit with spouse work rights or the considerably harder standard route.

- Confirm spouse work rights if relevant

- Consider non-domiciled status for investment income

- Rely on exemption figures quoted secondhand

## Common Mistakes

- **Not checking employer registration.** It is the single biggest variable for a third-country national.
- **Missing the tax exemption.** Substantial for someone taking first employment in Cyprus.
- **Ignoring non-dom status.** Significant for anyone with dividend or interest income.
- **Late tax planning.** Sequencing matters and cannot be fixed retrospectively.
- **Underestimating Limassol costs.** Housing has risen substantially with the corporate influx.
- **Assuming spouse sponsorship is separate.** Under the regime it frequently is not.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do EU citizens need a Cyprus work permit?

No. Free movement applies; you register with the Civil Registry and Migration Department and obtain a registration certificate.

### What is the foreign-interest company regime?

A framework allowing qualifying companies with a Cypriot presence and foreign ownership to employ third-country nationals in defined categories with facilitated permits and salary thresholds rather than labour market tests.

### Can my spouse work in Cyprus?

Under the regime and above a defined salary level, spouses of third-country employees have the right to work without separate employer sponsorship — more generous than several European systems.

### What is the first-employment tax exemption?

An exemption on a portion of employment income for individuals taking up first employment in Cyprus who were not previously resident, with the percentage, threshold and duration set in legislation.

### What is non-domiciled status?

A regime exempting Cyprus tax residents who are not domiciled there from special defence contribution on dividend, interest and rental income — significant for people with investment income.

### What is the 60-day rule?

A provision allowing tax residency with a shorter physical presence than the standard 183 days, subject to conditions including business or employment ties to Cyprus and no tax residency elsewhere.
### What should I arrange first when relocating to Cyprus?

Confirm your permit route, then housing — Limassol's rental market tightened considerably with the corporate influx. Start banking early, as Cypriot compliance checks make account opening slower than expected.

### Does Cyprus still offer citizenship by investment?

No. The investor citizenship scheme was terminated, so older material describing it should be disregarded entirely. Residence-based naturalisation remains, with a language and knowledge requirement.
### Cyprus or Malta?

Malta is marginally more English-accessible and leads in iGaming; Cyprus leads in ship management. Both have favourable tax regimes, facilitated permits at qualifying employers, and housing pressure from international arrivals. Choose on sector.
### How long does a Cyprus permit take?

EU citizens register immediately. Third-country nationals at a registered foreign-interest company are substantially faster than the standard route, though still weeks to months, with document preparation the longest element.

### Do I need a car in Cyprus?

Close to essential. Public transport is limited, most professionals drive, and the island uses left-hand traffic, which surprises arrivals from continental Europe.
### What administrative steps follow arrival in Cyprus?

Registration with the Civil Registry and Migration Department, social insurance and tax registration, a local bank account, health insurance arrangements and a driving licence exchange where applicable.
