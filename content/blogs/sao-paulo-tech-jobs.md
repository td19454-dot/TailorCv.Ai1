## Key Takeaways

- Sao Paulo is **Latin America's largest technology market** by a wide margin, with genuine scale problems to work on.
- **Fintech is the standout sector**, with Nubank among the world's largest digital banks and a deep supporting ecosystem.
- **Portuguese is required** for most roles, with a narrow band of English-working international teams.
- **The local talent pool is large and strong**, which sets a real bar for foreign hiring.
- **Remote work for foreign employers** has become a major competitor for Brazilian engineers.

## The sectors

Where technology work concentrates.

**Fintech**, which is the largest and most internationally visible. Nubank, PicPay, Stone, PagSeguro, Creditas, Neon and a long tail of companies built on a market with historically high banking fees and a large underbanked population.

**E-commerce and marketplaces** — Mercado Livre has substantial Brazilian engineering, alongside Magalu, Americanas and the wider sector.

**Delivery and logistics**, with iFood being one of the largest food delivery operations in the world by order volume and a serious engineering organisation.

**Enterprise software**, including TOTVS and a substantial B2B sector serving Brazilian businesses.

**Banking technology** at Itau, Bradesco, Santander Brasil and BTG, all of which run large engineering organisations.

**Agritech**, applying technology to Brazil's largest export sector, with genuine depth in remote sensing, logistics and precision agriculture.

**Healthtech and insurtech**, both growing.

**And international companies** with Sao Paulo engineering centres, drawn by talent and the market.

## Why Brazilian fintech is distinctive

Worth understanding because it is the reason many people target this market.

**Historically high banking fees and concentrated banking** created a large, underserved population and an obvious opportunity.

**Nubank scaled to serve an enormous customer base** across Brazil, Mexico and Colombia, and did it with an engineering culture that became genuinely influential regionally.

**Pix, the central bank instant payment system**, transformed Brazilian payments in a remarkably short period and became one of the most-used instant payment systems in the world.

**Open finance regulation** has moved faster in Brazil than in many markets.

**Which means** the engineering problems are real — high volume, low latency, regulatory complexity and genuine consumer scale — rather than incremental work on a mature market.

**And it produced an ecosystem.** People who built at Nubank and its peers went on to found and staff the next generation, which is what a healthy technology sector looks like.

## Compensation and the currency question

The financial picture, honestly.

**Technology salaries are strong relative to the Brazilian market** and modest converted into dollars or euros, which is the tension that shapes every decision here.

**Currency volatility is a real factor.** The real has moved substantially against major currencies over the years, and a salary calculated at one rate can look very different a year later.

**CLT packages include** paid holiday with a bonus, thirteenth salary, FGTS contributions and typically private health insurance, meal vouchers and transport allowance.

**PJ contracts** show a higher headline figure with none of that and with tax and contributions falling to you.

**Equity exists** at the larger startups and the Brazilian market for it is less mature than the American one, so terms warrant careful reading.

**Costs in Sao Paulo** concentrate in housing in the desirable central neighbourhoods, with much else being reasonable by international standards.

**And if you have obligations in another currency**, whether student debt or family support, model the exchange rate risk properly before committing. It has caught out a great many people.

## Working in Sao Paulo

Daily life in the city.

**It is enormous** — one of the largest metropolitan areas in the world, with the commuting and scale that implies.

**Neighbourhood choice matters more than in most cities.** Vila Madalena, Pinheiros, Itaim Bibi, Jardins and Vila Olimpia each have distinct characters and different commutes.

**Traffic is severe** and the metro, while good on the lines it serves, does not reach everywhere.

**Remote and hybrid working is common** in technology and has genuinely changed where people choose to live.

**The food and cultural scene is exceptional**, and this is one of the more underrated features of the city internationally.

**Security requires practical habits** rather than constant anxiety, and people adapt quickly.

**And the technology community is active**, with genuine meetup and conference culture that is worth using when building a network.

## Getting hired

The practical mechanics for this market.

**LinkedIn is dominant** and genuinely well used by Brazilian recruiters.

**Gupy** is the recruitment platform many large Brazilian employers run their hiring through, and it screens.

**Programathor** and **Geekhunter** are technology-specific and worth using directly.

**Vagas.com** and **Catho** for broader coverage.

**Company career pages** for the fintechs and larger employers, several of which hire primarily through their own sites.

**Referrals carry weight**, and the Brazilian technology community is relationship-driven and genuinely welcoming to people who show up at meetups and events.

**Apply in Portuguese** unless the posting is explicitly English, and a naturally written Portuguese CV outperforms a translated one substantially.

**Run it through an [ATS check](/ats-analysis)** given how much hiring runs through Gupy.

**And publish your work** — a [portfolio site](/portfolio) with deployed projects does more than a description, particularly for the product companies.

## The interview process

What to expect.

**A recruiter screen**, generally in Portuguese, covering background and expectations.

**A technical challenge**, commonly a take-home for the product companies and a live exercise elsewhere.

**Technical interviews** with engineers, practical in orientation and covering system design at senior levels.

**A cultural round**, which the larger fintechs take seriously and which is a genuine filter rather than a formality.

**Sometimes a case or business exercise**, particularly at the product-heavy companies.

**Salary discussion**, where CLT versus PJ should be clarified explicitly rather than assumed.

**And the timeline** is generally reasonable, though the larger companies run longer processes.

## Skills the market rewards

Where demand concentrates.

**Backend engineering** in the mainstream stacks, which carries the most volume by a wide margin.

**Frontend and mobile**, both in steady demand across the consumer platforms.

**Data engineering and analytics**, growing fast at the fintechs and banks.

**Cloud and platform engineering**, where the shortage is clearest.

**Security**, thin locally and increasingly sought given the regulatory environment around financial services.

**And domain knowledge in payments, credit or financial regulation**, which is genuinely differentiating in a market where fintech dominates.

## Alternatives to Sao Paulo

Real options within Brazil.

**Florianopolis** has become a genuine technology hub with a strong quality-of-life argument, and is frequently the first alternative Brazilian engineers name.

**Porto Alegre** has a real technology sector, strong universities and a distinct southern culture.

**Belo Horizonte** combines a technology scene with engineering and mining, at meaningfully lower cost.

**Campinas** for research and technology within reach of Sao Paulo.

**Recife** and its Porto Digital cluster, which has produced a real technology community in the northeast.

**Curitiba** for manufacturing technology and urban quality.

**And fully remote** for a Brazilian company, which is now common and lets you live wherever the cost and quality of life suit you.

**The trade-off** is depth. Sao Paulo has the most companies, the widest range of roles and the strongest network effects, and everywhere else asks you to accept a smaller market for a better daily life.

## Career progression

Where a Sao Paulo technology career leads.

**Within the fintechs and product companies**, from engineer to senior to staff or engineering management, with genuine progression at the better employers.

**Into the ecosystem**, since people who built at the larger fintechs are well placed to join or found the next generation.

**Into regional roles**, as Brazilian companies expand into Mexico, Colombia and the wider region.

**Into foreign remote work**, which pays more and offers less local career depth.

**Abroad**, since Brazilian engineers with fintech scale experience are genuinely employable internationally and Portugal in particular has become a common destination.

**And into founding**, supported by an ecosystem that now has real capital, experienced operators and successful precedents.

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: You want to work in fintech

Sao Paulo is one of the more interesting places in the world to do it, with genuine scale, a transformed payments landscape and a deep ecosystem. It is the strongest single argument for this market.

### Scenario 2: You are a Brazilian engineer weighing a foreign remote offer

The pay difference is substantial and the trade-off is real — remote work for a foreign employer against the ecosystem, mentorship and career depth of a strong local company. Both are legitimate answers.

### Scenario 3: You are a foreign engineer without Portuguese

Your market is a narrow band of international teams. It exists and it is small, and Portuguese study should start before the job search rather than after it.

> **Tip:** If fintech interests you at all, look at Sao Paulo seriously. Pix and the scale of the Brazilian consumer market have produced engineering problems that few places outside the largest markets can offer, and the ecosystem around them is genuinely deep.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Target fintech specifically
- Learn Portuguese seriously
- Understand CLT versus PJ in any offer
- Quantify your work by scale
- Consider the banks alongside the startups
- Look at Florianopolis and Porto Alegre as alternatives

### Don't

- Expect English-only roles outside a narrow band
- Underestimate the local talent pool
- Convert salaries naively without accounting for costs and volatility
- Ignore agritech, which is genuinely deep
- Overlook the large bank engineering organisations
- Assume equity works the way it does in the US

## Common Mistakes

- **Underestimating the talent pool.** Brazilian engineers are numerous and good.
- **Ignoring the banks.** Itau, Bradesco and BTG run large, serious engineering organisations.
- **Naive salary conversion.** Currency volatility makes dollar comparisons misleading.
- **Skipping the CLT versus PJ question.** It changes the real value of an offer substantially.
- **Overlooking agritech.** A genuine Brazilian strength serving its largest export sector.
- **Assuming Sao Paulo is the only option.** Florianopolis, Porto Alegre and Recife have real sectors.
