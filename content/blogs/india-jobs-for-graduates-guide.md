## Key Takeaways

- India produces an enormous number of graduates each year, and the **volume of candidates is the defining feature** of the market.
- **IT services companies hire in bulk** through campus drives and mass recruitment; **product companies and GCCs hire selectively** through a completely different process.
- **Global Capability Centres** — the India offices of international companies — have become one of the best graduate employers in the country and are consistently under-targeted.
- **Tier-1 college placement is not the only route**, and off-campus hiring has grown substantially.
- **Skills and demonstrable projects increasingly beat pedigree**, particularly in technology, which is a genuine change from a decade ago.

## The three hiring markets

India is not one [graduate](/blog/first-resume-for-gen-z-2026) market. It is at least three, and they work so differently that advice for one is actively wrong for another.

**Mass IT services hiring.** TCS, Infosys, Wipro, Cognizant, Accenture, Capgemini and their peers recruit tens of thousands of freshers a year through campus drives and standardised tests. The process is high-volume, largely automated at the front end, and eligibility-criteria driven — percentage cutoffs, no backlogs, specific branches.

**Product and startup hiring.** Companies building their own products — the Indian consumer internet firms, SaaS companies, and the fast-growing startup ecosystem — hire far smaller numbers and assess very differently. Data structures and algorithms, [system design](/blog/campus-placement-system-design-basics), and what you have actually built.

**Global Capability Centres.** The India engineering and operations centres of international companies. These have grown enormously, they pay well above services rates, they hire on capability rather than on volume, and a great many students do not realise how many exist.

**The practical implication:** decide which market you are targeting before you prepare, because the preparation is genuinely different. Aptitude tests and communication rounds for services; DSA and [projects](/blog/projects-in-resume) for product; domain depth and English fluency for GCCs.

## What each market rewards

| | IT services | Product / startup | GCC |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Volume hired** | Very high | Low | Moderate |
| **Entry route** | Campus drive, mass test | Off-campus, referral, portal | Campus and off-campus |
| **What is tested** | Aptitude, basic coding, communication | DSA, system design, projects | Domain skills, coding, communication |
| **Pedigree weight** | Eligibility criteria matter | Lower — skills dominate | Moderate |
| **Starting pay** | Standardised, modest | Wide range, often high | Generally strong |
| **Training** | Extensive structured bootcamp | Minimal, learn on the job | Structured, well funded |

**Services roles are a genuine start.** They provide training, a recognisable name and exposure, and a large number of Indian technology careers begin there and move on after two or three years. Treating them as beneath consideration is a mistake many students make and later regret.

**Product roles are harder to get and pay more.** The preparation required is substantial and specific.

**GCCs are the underrated option.** They combine reasonable accessibility with pay and work quality closer to product companies than to services.

## Beyond technology

Most advice about Indian graduate hiring assumes engineering, which leaves out a large share of graduates.

**[Banking](/blog/resume-matching-insurance-jobs) and financial services.** Bank probationary officer exams, NBFCs, insurance, and the analytics functions inside all of them.

**[Consulting](/blog/case-interview-preparation-guide-2026) and professional services.** The Big Four hire large numbers of commerce and management graduates into audit, tax and advisory.

**FMCG and manufacturing.** Structured management trainee programmes at the large consumer and industrial companies, which are competitive and well regarded.

**Government and public sector.** UPSC, state services, PSUs and banking exams absorb an enormous number of graduates and run on an entirely separate preparation track.

**[Healthcare](/blog/first-job-abroad-practical-checklist), education and pharma**, each with their own entry structures.

**And the analytics layer across all of them**, which has become one of the fastest-growing graduate destinations regardless of sector.

## The hiring calendar

Timing matters more in India than in most markets because so much hiring is seasonal.

**Campus placement season** typically runs from the autumn of the final year, with the large services [recruiters](/blog/how-recruiters-read-resumes) coming earliest and product companies and GCCs following.

**Day zero and day one** at the better colleges are reserved for the highest-paying recruiters, and eligibility is tight.

**Off-campus hiring runs year-round** but peaks alongside the campus cycle as companies fill remaining headcount.

**Internship hiring** happens roughly a year earlier, and a converted internship is one of the cleanest routes into a good company.

**Government exam calendars** are entirely separate, published in advance, and require a preparation timeline measured in years rather than months for the competitive ones.

**Banking exams** run to their own annual schedule.

**Which means:** work backwards from the calendar of whichever market you are targeting. Students who start preparing in the final semester are competing against people who started eighteen months earlier.

**Bengaluru and Hyderabad dominate technology**, and the gap between them has narrowed considerably as Hyderabad grew.

**Delhi NCR is the consulting and corporate centre** with a substantial startup scene in Gurugram.

**And tier-2 cities are growing** — Coimbatore, Kochi, Indore, Jaipur and Bhubaneswar all have real technology employment now, frequently as satellite offices of larger firms, at dramatically lower [living costs](/blog/how-to-negotiate-salary-offer).

## What Indian employers actually screen on

Beyond the obvious, the filters that decide shortlisting.

**Eligibility criteria at large recruiters.** Minimum percentage across tenth, twelfth and [degree](/blog/how-to-list-education-on-resume), no active backlogs, and sometimes a maximum year of passing. These are hard filters applied before anyone reads anything.

**Aptitude and coding tests.** Quantitative, logical and verbal reasoning plus basic programming, used at scale to reduce the pool. These are learnable and practice genuinely improves scores.

**Communication.** Assessed seriously, particularly at services companies and GCCs where the work involves international clients or colleagues. Written and spoken English is a genuine differentiator.

**Projects and demonstrated skill** at product companies and startups, where the technical bar is the filter rather than the criteria.

**[Certifications](/blog/how-to-list-certifications-on-resume)**, which carry more weight in India than in many markets, particularly cloud and platform certifications.

**And [referrals](/blog/networking-messages-for-referrals)**, which move an [application](/blog/how-many-jobs-should-you-apply-to-per-day) past the initial screen at companies where the volume of applicants is otherwise overwhelming.

## Common mistakes

Six that recur across the Indian graduate market.

**Preparing generically.** Aptitude practice does not prepare you for a product-company [interview](/blog/how-to-prepare-for-a-job-interview-in-2026-complete-guide-for-every-industry) and DSA does not prepare you for a services aptitude test.

**Starting late.** The students who clear product-company interviews generally began preparing a year or more earlier.

**Applying only through campus.** Off-campus, GCCs and referrals account for a very large share of good roles.

**Ignoring communication.** It is assessed seriously and it is improvable.

**Chasing package figures without context.** Advertised CTC frequently includes components that are not take-home, and comparing headline numbers across companies is misleading.

**And giving up after campus placement.** A great many strong careers began with an off-campus application after an unsuccessful placement season.

## Building a career after the first job

Where Indian graduate careers typically go.

**The two-to-three year move is standard.** A large share of technology careers begin in IT services and move to a product company, a GCC or a startup after building [experience](/blog/resume-matching-experienced-professionals). This is normal and expected rather than disloyal.

**Lateral hiring is where the money is.** Fresher salaries are compressed; experienced hiring is where the range widens sharply, which means the first job matters less than what you learn in it.

**Specialisation pays.** Generalist engineers are plentiful; people with genuine depth in cloud, data engineering, machine learning, security or a specific domain are not.

**Higher study is a real fork.** A master's abroad, an MBA, or a specialised programme changes the trajectory, and the decision is best made deliberately rather than by default when a job search stalls.

**And international moves** are accessible from India, particularly to the Gulf, Singapore, Germany, Canada and Australia — all of which have routes covered elsewhere in this series.

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: You are from a tier-2 or tier-3 college

Off-campus and GCC routes matter most for you. Build demonstrable projects, contribute publicly, and apply directly — skills-based hiring has genuinely opened up, and the pedigree barrier is lower than it was five years ago.

### Scenario 2: You want the highest starting salary

Product companies and GCCs, and prepare accordingly — data structures, algorithms, system design and real projects. This preparation takes months, not weeks, and starting late is the most common reason strong students miss out.

### Scenario 3: You did not get placed on campus

This is extremely common and not the end of anything. Off-campus hiring, GCCs, startups and lateral entry after a year of experience all exist, and a great many successful careers started this way.

> **Tip:** Research Global Capability Centres by name. Hundreds of international companies run engineering and operations centres in India, they pay well above services rates, they hire on capability rather than college tier — and most students have never made a list of them.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Decide which market you are targeting first
- Prepare specifically for that market
- Research GCCs by name
- Build and deploy real projects
- Use off-campus routes actively
- Consider services as a genuine start

### Don't

- Prepare generically for all three markets
- Assume campus placement is the only route
- Dismiss IT services out of hand
- Ignore GCCs
- Rely on college tier alone
- Start DSA preparation in the final semester

## Common Mistakes

- **Treating India as one market.** Services, product and GCC hiring work completely differently.
- **Ignoring GCCs.** Well paid, capability-based and consistently under-targeted.
- **Dismissing services roles.** They provide training and a launchpad, and many careers begin there.
- **Late preparation.** Product-company preparation takes months of consistent work.
- **No projects.** In skills-based hiring, what you have built is the argument.
- **Assuming placement failure is final.** Off-campus and lateral routes are extensive.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is campus placement the only way to get a job in India?

No. Off-campus hiring has grown substantially, GCCs recruit through their own portals, and skills-based hiring has reduced the weight of college tier considerably.

### What is a GCC?

A Global Capability Centre — the India engineering or operations centre of an international company. They pay well above IT services rates and hire on capability rather than volume.

### Should I take an IT services job?

It is a genuine start with structured training and a recognisable name, and many Indian technology careers begin there. Treating it as beneath consideration is a common regret.

### How different is product company preparation?

Substantially. Data structures, algorithms, system design and real projects, prepared over months rather than weeks. It is a different discipline from aptitude-test preparation.

### Does college tier still matter?

Less than it did. Eligibility criteria still apply at large services recruiters, but skills-based hiring at product companies, startups and GCCs has genuinely opened the market.

### What if I am not from an engineering background?

Banking, consulting, FMCG management trainee programmes, government exams and the analytics functions across every sector all recruit large numbers of non-engineering graduates.
### When does hiring happen in India?

Campus placement runs from the autumn of the final year, with services recruiters earliest and product companies following. Internship hiring is a year earlier, and government exam calendars are entirely separate.

### Which Indian city should I target?

Bengaluru and Hyderabad for technology, Mumbai for finance, Delhi NCR for consulting and corporate, Pune and Chennai for manufacturing and engineering.
### What do Indian recruiters screen on first?

Eligibility criteria at large recruiters — percentage across all levels, no backlogs, year of passing — applied before anyone reads the resume. Then aptitude tests, communication and, at product companies, demonstrated technical skill.

### Why do package figures mislead?

Advertised CTC frequently includes components that are not take-home pay — variable pay, benefits and one-time payments. Comparing headline numbers across companies without breaking them down is misleading.
### Should I stay at my first company?

Most Indian technology careers involve a move after two to three years, frequently from services into a product company or GCC. It is normal and expected rather than disloyal.
