## Key Takeaways

- The **Critical Skills Employment Permit** is Ireland's route for occupations on the critical skills list, with better conditions than the general permit.
- **No labour market needs test** applies, unlike the General Employment Permit, which removes a major employer burden.
- **Family reunification is immediate** rather than delayed, and spouses gain access to the labour market.
- **After two years** holders can typically apply for Stamp 4 permission, which removes the employer tie entirely.
- **A salary threshold applies**, differing by whether the occupation is on the critical skills list and by qualification level.

## How the permit works

The structure.

**It is employer-specific and role-specific**, granted for a job offer of at least two years.

**The occupation must be eligible** — either on the Critical Skills Occupations List, or above a higher salary threshold with a relevant degree.

**No labour market needs test** is required, meaning the employer does not need to advertise the role first, which is a significant practical advantage.

**The employer applies or you apply** through the Employment Permits Online System, depending on circumstances.

**A minimum annual remuneration threshold applies**, set lower for listed critical skills occupations and higher for others.

**The permit runs for two years** initially.

**After that period**, holders are typically eligible to apply to immigration for Stamp 4 permission, which allows you to work without an employment permit at all.

**And that Stamp 4 step is the point** — it is what makes this permit strategically better than the general route rather than merely administratively easier.

## Critical Skills versus General Employment Permit

The comparison that decides your route.

| | Critical Skills | General Employment |
|---|---|---|
| **Labour market needs test** | Not required | Required |
| **Family reunification** | Immediate | After a qualifying period |
| **Spouse labour market access** | Yes, on a dependant permission | More restricted |
| **Path to Stamp 4** | Typically after two years | Longer |
| **Occupation eligibility** | Critical skills list or higher salary | Broader list, with ineligible occupations excluded |
| **Employer change** | Restricted initially, then free on Stamp 4 | More restricted |

**The Critical Skills route is materially better** wherever you qualify for it, and checking whether your occupation is listed should be the first thing you do.

**The General Employment Permit exists** for occupations outside the critical skills list that are not on the ineligible list, and it is a genuine route with slower progression.

**Occupation lists are revised periodically** in response to labour market shortages, so an occupation that was ineligible two years ago may not be now.

## The application in practice

What the process actually involves.

**Applications go through the Employment Permits Online System** operated by the Department of Enterprise, Trade and Employment.

**The employer must be registered** with Revenue and trading in Ireland, and be able to satisfy the requirement that a proportion of the workforce holds EEA nationality, subject to defined exceptions.

**Documents include** the signed job offer or contract, passport details, qualification certificates and evidence of registration.

**Qualification recognition** through NARIC Ireland may be needed where a foreign degree underpins eligibility, and it is worth starting early.

**Processing times are published** and vary with volume, running from weeks to several months.

**Fees apply** and are typically paid by the employer, with a partial refund if an application is refused.

**After approval**, you enter Ireland, register with immigration and receive your Irish Residence Permit.

**And a PPS number** is needed for tax and services, obtained after arrival, which gates payroll and should be arranged in your first days.

## After arrival

The practical sequence.

**Immigration registration** at your local registration office or Burgh Quay in Dublin, producing your IRP card.

**PPS number** from the Department of Social Protection.

**Revenue registration** and your Revenue Payroll Notification, which prevents emergency tax.

**A bank account**, which requires proof of address and can be slower than expected.

**Health cover** — Ireland has a public system with charges and a large private insurance market, and employer schemes are common.

**Accommodation**, which is the genuinely hard part in Dublin and worth beginning before you arrive.

**And the two-year clock** toward Stamp 4, which is worth diarising from the day you register.

## Changing jobs and long-term status

The rules that matter later.

**During the first twelve months** the permit is generally tied to your employer, with change requiring a new permit application.

**After twelve months** a new permit for a different employer becomes more straightforward.

**Redundancy** carries specific provisions allowing time to find new qualifying employment.

**Stamp 4 after two years** removes the tie entirely and allows any employment without a permit.

**Long-term residency and citizenship** follow from continued legal residence, with naturalisation available after a qualifying period of reckonable residence.

**Time on a student permission** generally does not count toward naturalisation, which is a common and expensive misunderstanding.

**And Irish citizenship carries EU free movement**, which is the practical long-term prize for non-EU nationals.

## Common problems

What actually goes wrong with these applications.

**The occupation is misclassified**, with the role title on the contract not matching the occupation being claimed.

**Salary falls just below the threshold**, particularly where a bonus was assumed to count and does not.

**The contract is shorter than two years**, which the Critical Skills route requires.

**Qualification recognition is missing** where the eligibility rests on a degree.

**The employer fails the workforce composition requirement** and has not applied for an exemption.

**Documents are inconsistent** between the application, the contract and the passport.

**And immigration registration is missed** after arrival, which is a separate step from the permit and easy to overlook.

## Comparing Ireland regionally

Where this route sits among European options.

**Against the German EU Blue Card.** Both are skilled routes with salary thresholds; Germany offers a faster settlement timeline with strong language, Ireland offers immediate family reunification and an English-speaking environment.

**Against the Dutch highly skilled migrant scheme.** The Netherlands runs through recognised sponsors with a fast administrative process; Ireland requires no labour market test on this permit and has no integration exam.

**Against the UK Skilled Worker visa.** Ireland is inside the EU and the UK is not, which matters enormously for onward mobility, and Irish citizenship confers EU free movement.

**Against Nordic routes.** Broadly comparable in difficulty, with Ireland substantially easier for English speakers.

**The Irish advantages** are the English-language environment, no integration exam, immediate family reunification and a two-year route to employer-independent status.

**The Irish friction** is housing, not immigration.

## Four things people get wrong

Misreadings that cost time.

**"Any job offer will do."** The occupation must be eligible, the contract must run at least two years, and the salary must clear the threshold for your category.

**"The permit is my immigration status."** It is not. The permit allows the employment; a separate immigration permission and registration allow the residence, and both must be maintained.

**"I can switch employers freely."** Not in the first twelve months, and not without a new permit until you hold Stamp 4.

**"Bonuses count toward the threshold."** Generally the assessment looks at basic remuneration, and assuming otherwise is a common reason applications fall just short.

## A note on dependants

How family arrangements actually work.

**Spouses and partners** can apply for their own permission, which in most cases grants access to the labour market without needing their own employment permit.

**Children** are included in family reunification arrangements.

**Registration applies to each family member** individually after arrival.

**Schooling** is free in the public system, with a significant private and international sector in Dublin.

**Healthcare** operates as a mixed public and private system, and employer-provided private insurance is a common and valuable benefit.

**And the immediacy of family reunification** on this permit is one of its clearest practical advantages over the general route.

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Your occupation is on the critical skills list

This is the route to take. No labour market needs test, immediate family reunification and a two-year path to Stamp 4 make it substantially better than the alternatives.

### Scenario 2: Your occupation is not listed but you have a degree and a strong salary

The Critical Skills route may still be open above the higher remuneration threshold with a relevant qualification. Check that before defaulting to the General Employment Permit.

### Scenario 3: You are already in Ireland on a student permission

Graduates of Irish institutions have their own pathway through the Third Level Graduate Scheme, which gives time to find qualifying employment before applying for a permit.

> **Tip:** Check the Critical Skills Occupations List before you check anything else. The difference between this permit and the General Employment Permit is immediate family reunification and a two-year route to Stamp 4 — which is the difference between a job and a life here.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Check the occupation list first
- Confirm the salary threshold for your category
- Plan the Stamp 4 application from day one
- Register with immigration promptly after arrival
- Keep qualification documents ready
- Track occupation list revisions

### Don't

- Assume the General Permit is your only option
- Overlook the higher-threshold route for unlisted occupations
- Change employer without checking permit conditions
- Miss the immigration registration deadline
- Rely on outdated occupation lists
- Forget that the permit is job-specific

## Common Mistakes

- **Defaulting to the General Permit.** Many people qualify for Critical Skills and never check.
- **Missing the higher-threshold route.** It covers unlisted occupations with a degree.
- **Ignoring the Stamp 4 timeline.** It is the strategic prize and needs planning.
- **Changing employer carelessly.** Conditions apply during the initial period.
- **Using an old occupation list.** They are revised in response to shortages.
- **Late immigration registration.** Required after arrival and easy to overlook.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the Critical Skills Employment Permit?

Ireland's skilled worker permit for occupations on the critical skills list, or for other occupations above a higher salary threshold with a relevant degree.

### How is it better than the General Employment Permit?

No labour market needs test, immediate family reunification, spouse access to the labour market and a typically two-year path to Stamp 4 permission.

### What is Stamp 4?

Immigration permission allowing you to work without an employment permit, removing the employer tie entirely. It is the strategic reason to pursue this route.

### Is there a salary threshold?

Yes, set lower for occupations on the critical skills list and higher for other eligible occupations held with a relevant qualification.

### Can my spouse work?

Yes. Family reunification is immediate on this permit and spouses gain access to the labour market on a dependant permission.

### Do occupation lists change?

Regularly, in response to labour market shortages. An occupation that was ineligible previously may qualify now, so check the current list rather than an old summary.
### How do I apply for the permit?

Through the Employment Permits Online System, with the employer registered and trading in Ireland, a signed job offer, qualification evidence and where needed NARIC recognition of a foreign degree.

### Can I change employers on a Critical Skills permit?

It is generally tied to your employer for the first twelve months, after which a new permit for a different employer is more straightforward. Stamp 4 removes the tie entirely.

### Does student time count toward citizenship?

Generally not. Time on a student permission is usually not reckonable for naturalisation, which is a common and costly misunderstanding.
### What usually goes wrong with permit applications?

Occupation misclassification, salary falling just below threshold, contracts shorter than two years, missing qualification recognition, and inconsistent documents between application, contract and passport.

### Where should I verify current requirements?

The Department of Enterprise, Trade and Employment for permits and occupation lists, Immigration Service Delivery for registration and Stamp 4, NARIC Ireland for qualifications and Revenue for tax.
### How does the Irish permit compare to other European routes?

It offers no labour market test, immediate family reunification, no integration exam and an English-speaking environment, with a two-year route to employer-independent Stamp 4 status.
