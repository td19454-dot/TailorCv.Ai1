## Key Takeaways

- Most submission mistakes are avoidable in under two minutes if you check the right things, not everything.
- The file you attach and the file you edited are not always the same file — this catches more people than expected.
- Contact details and links are the highest-cost, lowest-visibility failure: wrong, and you may never know why you heard nothing.
- Checking the match against the specific posting matters more than proofreading for the tenth time.
- Do this checklist every time, not just when you remember to.

Before you click submit, run through this. It takes under two minutes and catches the mistakes that actually cost interviews — not typos, but structural things that silently break an application.

## 1. Is This the File You Actually Edited?

The single most common last-minute mistake. Open the attached file right now and confirm it is the version with your edits, not an earlier export sitting in your downloads folder.

**Check:** open the file. Does it contain the changes you just made? If you renamed files, confirm the name matches what you intended to send.

## 2. Does It Parse Correctly?

Copy the text out of your final file and paste it into a plain text editor. Read it. Is the order correct? Are your job titles, dates and skills all present?

This catches the layout failures that are invisible on screen but scramble what a system extracts — see [ATS formatting mistakes](/blog/ats-resume-formatting-mistakes). Thirty seconds, and it is the check most people skip because the resume "looks fine."

## 3. Do Your Links Actually Work?

Click your LinkedIn link. Click your portfolio link, if you have one. Confirm both open correctly from the exported file — links sometimes lose their underlying href during export even when the text still looks blue and underlined. See [formatting that broke after export](/blog/resume-formatting-broke-after-export).

## 4. Is Your Contact Information Correct and Findable?

Read your phone number and email character by character. A single digit typo here means every callback fails silently — you will never know why you heard nothing, because there is nothing to hear.

Confirm your contact details are in the **body** of the resume, not a header or footer, since some parsers do not read those regions.

## 5. Does This Resume Actually Match This Posting?

If you have not already, [scan the resume against this specific posting](/ats-analysis) and glance at the missing list. If something required is missing that you genuinely have, this is worth two more minutes before you submit — see [which missing skills actually matter](/blog/which-missing-skills-actually-matter).

If time is genuinely gone, submit anyway — a mostly-matched application on time beats a perfectly-matched one late.

## 6. Does Your Résumé Title Make Sense to Someone Outside Your Company?

Quick glance: is your most recent job title something a stranger would recognise, or an internal code or branded title? If the latter and you have thirty seconds, add the standard equivalent — see [job titles that don't match your industry](/blog/job-titles-dont-match-industry).

## 7. Are You Answering the Application Form Questions Correctly?

Separate from the resume, but decisive. Work authorisation, willingness to relocate, salary expectation, years of experience — these are frequently hard filters. A wrong answer here rejects you regardless of resume quality. Read each question once before submitting; do not autopilot through them. See [rejected within 24 hours](/blog/resume-rejected-in-24-hours) for what happens when these go wrong.

## 8. Is the File Named Sensibly?

`firstname-lastname-resume.pdf`, not `resume_final_v3_ACTUALLY_FINAL.pdf`. This is a small professionalism signal and takes five seconds to fix if it is wrong.

## 9. One Pass for Obvious Errors

Not a full proofread — you do not have time and it is not the highest-value check tonight. Just scan for: the wrong company name (left over from a previous tailored version), a broken sentence from a half-finished edit, or a section that got duplicated during editing.

## 10. Submit

Once the above is done, submit. Do not keep re-reading — diminishing returns set in fast, and the posting will not wait.

## The Two-Minute Version, If That's All You Have

If you genuinely have two minutes and nothing more:

1. Open the attached file and confirm it is current.
2. Read your phone number and email character by character.
3. Click your LinkedIn link once.

Those three catch the failures that are both common and completely silent — you apply, you never hear back, and you never learn why.

## What This Checklist Deliberately Skips

This is not a resume-writing checklist — it assumes the content is largely done and you are in the final minutes before submission. It will not tell you whether your bullets are strong or whether you are applying to the right role. For that, see [resume not passing the ATS](/blog/resume-not-passing-ats-what-to-check-first) and [how many jobs to apply to per day](/blog/how-many-jobs-apply-per-day-realistically) when you have more time to plan ahead of a deadline rather than react to one.

## Make This a Habit, Not a Panic Response

The value of this list compounds if you run it every time, not just when a deadline is looming. Most of these checks take seconds once they are habitual, and they catch failures that are otherwise invisible until weeks of silence force you to go looking for a cause.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Which check matters most if I only have time for one?**
Confirming you attached the file you actually edited. It is the most common and most avoidable mistake.

**Should I proofread before or after checking the match?**
After. A perfectly proofread resume that does not evidence the posting's requirements still gets filtered; a match-checked resume with a small typo usually does not.

**How long should this whole checklist take?**
Under two minutes once you know what to look for. The first few times, budget five.

**What if I find a real problem with three minutes left?**
Fix the contact details and file issues first — those are silent failures. A minor wording issue is lower priority than a wrong phone number.

**Should I do this for every application, even quick ones?**
Yes, particularly items 1, 3 and 4. They take seconds and the failure mode is total silence with no diagnosis.

## Run the Match Check Now

The one item on this list worth doing even under real time pressure is confirming the resume actually fits the posting.

[Scan your resume against the job](/ats-analysis) before you submit — it takes about a minute and tells you whether anything required is missing that you could fix in the time you have left.
