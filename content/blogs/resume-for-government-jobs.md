## Key Takeaways

- **Selection criteria are scored**, frequently one by one, which changes how you must write.
- **Address every criterion explicitly**, since assessors cannot award marks for what you did not claim.
- **Follow the specified format exactly.** Deviating is a common cause of rejection before assessment.
- **Deadlines are genuinely hard** and late applications are usually not accepted.
- **Evidence must be specific**, because vague claims score zero even when true.

## How public sector selection works

The mechanics that differ from private hiring.

**Applications are scored against published criteria**, frequently by a panel using a defined scale rather than by a single reader forming an impression.

**Each criterion is assessed separately**, which means strength in one area does not compensate for silence in another.

**Assessors mark what is written**, not what is implied. Experience you did not explicitly connect to a criterion generally earns nothing.

**Shortlisting is often mechanical**, with a threshold score determining who progresses, and a single unaddressed criterion can put you below it.

**Formats are prescribed**, from application forms to word limits to required sections, and non-compliance is a standard rejection reason.

**Deadlines close absolutely** in most public processes, with systems shutting at the stated time.

**And the process is designed to be fair and auditable**, which is why it is rigid — the rigidity is the point rather than an accident.

## Writing against criteria

The technique that determines your score.

| Approach | Result |
|---|---|
| **One example per criterion, structured** | Scores well |
| **A general career summary** | Scores poorly across all criteria |
| **Reusing one example everywhere** | Weakens each successive use |
| **Describing the team's work** | Assessors cannot credit you |
| **Naming the criterion explicitly** | Makes the assessor's job easy |
| **Vague competence claims** | Scores zero regardless of truth |

**Use the criterion's own words as your heading** where the format allows, so the assessor can locate your evidence immediately.

**Give one specific example per criterion**, structured as situation, your action and the outcome, since this is what most public frameworks are built around.

**Say I rather than we.** Panels score individuals, and a response describing team achievements leaves the assessor unable to award marks.

**And do not assume anything is obvious.** An assessor working through fifty applications will not infer your experience from an adjacent claim.

## The rest of the process

What follows a successful application.

**Panel interviews are standard**, with several assessors scoring against the same published criteria used at application stage.

**Questions map to the criteria directly**, which means your application is effectively your interview preparation and should be reread beforehand.

**Structured answers are expected.** Most public frameworks assess situation, action and result explicitly, and an unstructured answer scores poorly even when the content is strong.

**Tests and exercises are common** — written exercises, presentations, in-tray tasks and numerical assessments depending on the role and grade.

**Timelines are long.** Public recruitment frequently takes months from closing date to offer, and this is normal rather than a sign of disinterest.

**Pre-employment checks are thorough**, covering identity, right to work, references, and in many roles security vetting that can add months.

**And offers are usually at a fixed point** on a published pay scale, with far less negotiation than private sector hiring allows.

## Eligibility and vetting

The requirements that gate everything else.

**Nationality and residency rules apply** to many public roles, particularly in security, defence and central government, and they are absolute where they apply.

**Security clearance levels vary** from basic checks to detailed vetting, and the higher levels require extensive history, referees and time.

**Residency history requirements** are common for clearance, frequently needing several continuous years in the country.

**Criminal record checks** are standard and their scope depends on the role, with certain roles requiring enhanced disclosure.

**Conflicts of interest** must be declared, including family relationships within the organisation and outside financial interests.

**And the checks happen after the offer** in most processes, which means an offer is conditional until they complete.

**Read the eligibility section before applying.** It is the one part of a public application where failing to qualify wastes the entire effort, and it is always stated explicitly.

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: The application asks for a statement against eight criteria

Write eight separate responses with distinct examples. Reusing one example across several dilutes it, and an unaddressed criterion is scored at zero.

### Scenario 2: You meet a criterion through unpaid or voluntary work

Use it. Most public frameworks accept evidence from any context, and volunteering, community roles and study frequently provide strong examples.

### Scenario 3: The form limits you to a short word count per criterion

Cut the context, not the evidence. One sentence of situation, the rest on your action and the result, since the action and outcome are what carry the marks.

> **Tip:** Write your response with the criterion's exact wording as a heading and one clearly structured example beneath it. Assessors work through many applications against a rubric, and making your evidence trivially easy to locate is worth more than elegant prose.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Address every criterion separately
- Use the criterion's own wording
- Give one specific example each
- Write in the first person
- Observe word limits exactly
- Submit well before the deadline

### Don't

- Submit a general career summary
- Reuse one example throughout
- Describe team achievements as your own
- Exceed or ignore stated limits
- Assume implied experience will be credited
- Leave submission until the final hour

## Common Mistakes

- **Ignoring the criteria structure.** It is how the scoring works.
- **A general summary.** It addresses nothing specifically.
- **Saying we.** Panels cannot score a team.
- **Unaddressed criteria.** Each one scores zero.
- **Format non-compliance.** A standard pre-assessment rejection.
- **Late submission.** Public deadlines genuinely close.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How is a government application different?

It is scored against published criteria, frequently by a panel using a defined scale. Each criterion is assessed separately, so unaddressed ones score zero regardless of your overall strength.

### Should I address every criterion?

Yes, every one, with a distinct example. Assessors award marks only for what is explicitly claimed and evidenced, and silence on a criterion cannot be compensated elsewhere.

### Can I reuse the same example?

Avoid it where possible. Each successive use weakens the example and suggests limited experience, so prepare a distinct one for each criterion.

### Do public sector deadlines really close?

Yes. Unlike many private postings, public recruitment systems close at the stated time and late applications are generally not accepted at all.

### Does voluntary experience count?

In most frameworks, yes. Evidence from volunteering, community roles or study is usually acceptable, and it frequently provides strong examples for criteria your paid work does not cover.

### Should I write we or I?

Always I. Panels score individual contribution, and describing a team's achievement leaves the assessor unable to credit you for it.
### What happens after a government application?

Panel interviews scored against the same published criteria, frequently with written exercises or presentations. Timelines run to months, and offers usually sit at a fixed point on a published pay scale.

### What eligibility requirements apply?

Nationality and residency rules for many roles, security clearance with residency history requirements, criminal record checks, and conflict of interest declarations. Read this section before applying.
