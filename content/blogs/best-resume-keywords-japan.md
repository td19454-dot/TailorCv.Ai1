Keywords are what get an English resume noticed in Japan's global and tech job market — both by the **applicant tracking systems (ATS)** many international employers use and by the recruiters who search LinkedIn and resume databases. And in Japan specifically, some keywords (like your JLPT and TOEIC scores) carry unusual weight.

This guide covers the best resume keywords for jobs in Japan in 2026: the categories that matter, examples by field, the Japan-specific credentials to feature, and how to use them naturally.

## The Most Important Keywords: From the Job Description

Your single best source of keywords is the **job posting itself**. Employers list the skills, tools, and qualifications they want — those are the terms the ATS is configured to find. For every application:

1. Read the job description closely.
2. Identify the repeated skills, tools, and terms.
3. Mirror that exact language on your resume where it's genuinely true of you.

## Japan-Specific Keywords to Feature

These credentials and terms carry special weight in the Japanese market:

- **JLPT (Japanese Language Proficiency Test):** state your level explicitly — "JLPT N2", "JLPT N1". This is one of the most valuable signals for any role touching Japanese business.
- **TOEIC:** widely used to certify English ability in Japan — include your score (e.g. "TOEIC 900").
- **[Bilingual](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/bilingual-language-skills-on-resume) / Business-level Japanese / Native English** — clear language-level descriptors.
- **Japan / APAC market experience** — where relevant.
- **Visa status** — noting eligibility can help (confirm details separately).

Feature language credentials prominently — see the [Japanese resume guide](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/japanese-resume-rirekisho-guide) for context on how these are valued.

## How to Use Keywords Naturally

- **Weave them into achievement bullets:** "Built a **Python** data pipeline that reduced reporting time by 40%" beats a bare keyword list.
- **Include a skills section** listing your core tools and competencies — easy for ATS and recruiters to scan.
- **State language credentials clearly** near the top or in a dedicated section.
- **Use exact terms and sensible variations** (e.g. spell out and abbreviate: "Search Engine Optimization (SEO)").
- **Stay honest** — only claim skills you have; you'll be tested in interviews.

## Don't Overdo It

Keyword **stuffing** — cramming irrelevant terms or repeating them unnaturally — hurts you. Modern ATS and recruiters flag it, and it makes your resume unreadable. Aim for natural, relevant, honest usage. Quality and fit beat volume.
