## Key Takeaways

- Kickresume is a resume *builder* first — its template library and design polish are genuinely better than ours.
- Its ATS checker is Premium-only. On the free plan you can build a resume but not find out whether it will parse.
- Kickresume gives Premium away free to verified students through ISIC, ITIC or UNiDAYS. If you qualify, that changes the maths completely.
- TailorCV starts at the ATS score and works backwards into the resume; Kickresume starts at the document and adds checking later.
- Both are cheap. The real question is whether you need a better-looking resume or a better-matched one.

If you are choosing between TailorCV and Kickresume, the honest summary is that these tools are pointed at different moments in a job hunt. We build TailorCV, so read what follows with that in mind — but we would rather tell you where Kickresume wins than have you find out after paying.

Kickresume is a resume and cover letter builder with a large template library, an AI writer, and a career-mapping feature. TailorCV is a tailoring engine: you give it your resume and a specific job description, and it scores the match and rewrites the resume to close the gap honestly.

## What Kickresume Actually Is

Kickresume is a builder. You pick a template, fill in your details, and it produces a well-designed resume — and it is genuinely good at this. The template library is larger and more visually varied than ours, the AI writer produces reasonable first-draft phrasing, and the cover letter templates match the resume designs so an application looks like a set.

It also does things we do not. Career Map shows progression routes between roles. The website templates turn a resume into a personal page. LinkedIn and PDF import save you retyping. If what you need is a resume that looks professional and you are starting from a blank page, Kickresume does that job well.

## What TailorCV Actually Is

TailorCV assumes you already have a resume. The starting point is not a template — it is a job description you are about to apply to.

You paste the posting, upload your resume, and get an [ATS match score](/ats-analysis) showing which of the posting's requirements your resume evidences and which it does not. Then the tailoring step rewrites your bullets to carry the missing language *where your experience actually supports it* — the rule is that it never claims a skill your resume shows no evidence for. From there you can generate a [matching cover letter](/cover-letter), run a [mock interview](/mock-interview) for the role, or publish the whole thing as a [portfolio site](/portfolio).

The difference in one line: Kickresume helps you write a resume. TailorCV helps you send the right version of it.

## Round 1: Pricing — In Your Currency, Not Just Dollars

Most comparison articles quote US prices to everyone, which is quietly useless if you are not in the US. Both of these tools price by region, so the honest answer depends on where you are.

**Kickresume**, from its own pricing page: **$9/month**, **$18 for three months** (about $6/month), or **$48/year** (about $4/month). The free plan is real and permanent — 4 resume templates, 4 cover letter templates, unlimited downloads.

**TailorCV** is region-split: **₹1,999/year in India** (about ₹167/month), **$24.99/year** in lower-income-country pricing, and **$59.99/year** globally. Weekly passes exist too — ₹149 or $4.99 — for people who need a tool for one application round and then stop.

Neither of these is expensive. If price alone decides it, Kickresume's annual plan is cheaper than our global annual plan and roughly comparable to our India pricing. We are not going to pretend otherwise.

**The student exception matters more than the prices.** Kickresume gives Premium away free to students who verify through ISIC, ITIC or UNiDAYS. If you are a student who can verify, you get their full feature set — templates, AI writer, ATS checker — at no cost. That is a better deal than anything we offer students, and you should take it.

## Round 2: The ATS Checker Is Behind the Paywall

This is the substantive difference, and it is worth being precise about.

Kickresume has an ATS Resume Checker. It is **Premium-only** — it is listed among the features the free plan excludes, alongside the AI writer and the premium templates. So on Kickresume's free plan you can build a resume and download it, but you cannot find out whether an ATS will parse it correctly.

That ordering is backwards from how applying actually works. The parse check is the thing you want *before* you send the application, and it is the cheapest possible thing to give away, because it costs one scan to run.

On TailorCV, the [ATS score](/ats-analysis) is free. A guest gets one scan without an account at all — no card, no signup — and once you are logged in, scanning is unlimited and free. We charge for the tailoring, the cover letters and the portfolio builder, not for finding out whether your resume parses.

If you are on Kickresume's free plan and want a parse check without upgrading, you can run your resume through our scanner and take the result back to Kickresume. That is a legitimate way to use both.

## Round 3: Templates vs Matching

Kickresume wins on templates and it is not close: 40+ on Premium against our 22, with more design range and better typography. If your resume needs to look distinctive — design, marketing, creative roles where the document itself is a work sample — that is a real advantage.

But a resume's appearance and its match to a posting are separate problems, and only one of them is why applications get filtered. A beautifully designed resume that does not carry the posting's language still scores badly. Our [guide to what actually moves an ATS score](/blog/ats-score-guide) covers this in more detail, and the short version is that structure and keyword evidence matter far more than font choice.

The honest framing: Kickresume makes the document better. TailorCV makes the *match* better. If your resume already looks fine and is still not getting callbacks, the problem is probably not the template.

## Round 4: One Resume or Many

Kickresume is built around a resume you maintain and update. That fits a job hunt where you send broadly similar applications.

TailorCV is built around the opposite assumption: that every posting is different and the same resume should not go to all of them. The [tailored-vs-generic comparison](/blog/tailored-vs-generic-resume) has the reasoning, but the practical point is that TailorCV produces a *version* per job, keeps them in [My Resumes](/my-resumes), and the [Chrome extension](/extension) does it from the job page itself so you are not copying job descriptions back and forth.

If you apply to three carefully chosen jobs a month, Kickresume's model is fine. If you apply to twenty, versioning becomes the whole problem.

## Round 5: What Else Is in the Box

Kickresume includes Career Map, website templates, a mobile app, LinkedIn/PDF import, and priority support on Premium.

TailorCV includes the [ATS analysis](/ats-analysis), [cover letter generation](/cover-letter), [AI mock interviews](/mock-interview), [interview prep](/interview-prep), the [portfolio site builder](/portfolio), and the browser extension.

These barely overlap. Kickresume's extras are about producing and presenting documents. Ours are about getting through a specific application. Which set is worth more depends entirely on which problem you have this week.

No — 22 against their 40+. We put the work into matching rather than design range, which is the right trade for our users but not for everyone.
