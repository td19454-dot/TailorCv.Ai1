## Step 1: Nail the Basics

## Step 2: Write a Keyword-Rich Headline

Your headline is the most important searchable field after your name. Combine your role, key skills, and value:

> *Financial Analyst | Forecasting & Financial Modelling | Toronto*

This packs in searchable keywords while telling recruiters what you do. Mirror the language of your target roles — for a palette, see [best resume keywords for Canadian jobs](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/best-resume-keywords-canada).

## Step 3: Craft a Compelling About Section

Structure your About (summary) as:

1. A strong opening line stating who you are professionally.
2. A short paragraph on your key strengths and biggest achievements (with numbers).
3. A skills line listing your core competencies and tools — great for search.
4. A closing line on what you're looking for.

Write in the first person, keep it scannable, and weave in your target keywords naturally. Newcomers can briefly note their move to Canada and openness to opportunities.

## Step 4: Optimise Your Experience

Treat each role like a resume entry:

- Use clear, searchable job titles.
- Write achievement bullets with [action verbs](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/best-action-verbs-for-resume) and [measurable results](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/awards-achievements-on-resume).
- Include the tools, skills, and keywords relevant to each role.

## Step 5: Skills, Endorsements, and Recommendations

- **Skills:** add the maximum, prioritising the most relevant to your target roles.
- **Endorsements:** ask colleagues to endorse your key skills.

## Step 6: Complete Every Section

LinkedIn rewards complete profiles ("All-Star") with more visibility. Fill in:

- Education, [certifications](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/best-free-online-certificates-for-resume), and licences (note Canadian credential assessments where relevant).
- Languages — English and French bilingualism is a genuine asset in Canada.
- Volunteer experience — valued in Canada and a great addition for newcomers.
- Projects and publications where relevant.

## Step 7: Signal You're Open and Active

- **"Open to Work":** turn on the recruiter-only signal (or the public badge).
- **Post and engage:** share relevant content and comment thoughtfully to stay visible.
- **Grow your network:** connect with colleagues, alumni, and people at target companies, with a short personal note. A larger, relevant network expands reach and referrals — crucial in Canada.

## Using Skills, Endorsements, and Certifications

LinkedIn's skills section feeds directly into how you're matched to recruiter searches and job recommendations, so treat it strategically. Add up to the maximum, prioritising and pinning your most relevant skills — these are weighted heavily in matching. Seek endorsements from colleagues for your key skills as social proof, take skill assessments where available to earn verification badges, and add completed certifications and courses, which surface as fresh, in-demand keywords. For newcomers and career changers especially, verified skills and certifications help offset a thinner Canadian work history and make your profile more competitive.

## Measuring and Improving Your Profile Over Time

Optimising LinkedIn isn't a one-off task. Check your search appearances and profile views (LinkedIn shows these) to gauge whether your keywords and activity are landing — rising numbers are a good sign. Update your headline and About whenever your target role shifts, refresh your Experience with new quantified achievements, keep your network growing in your field and region, and stay lightly but consistently active. Think of your profile as a living asset: a little maintenance each month keeps you visible to recruiters and ready the moment the right opportunity appears.

## How LinkedIn Decides Who Recruiters See

Getting found isn't only about keywords — LinkedIn weighs several signals when deciding whose profiles surface in recruiter searches:

- **Profile completeness.** Complete ("All-Star") profiles rank higher — another reason to fill every section.
- **Keyword relevance and placement.** Terms in your headline, About, skills, and job titles carry more weight than the same words elsewhere.
- **Activity and recency.** Active users who post, comment, and update appear more often than dormant ones.
- **Network relevance.** A network concentrated in your industry and region makes you more likely to surface for Canadian recruiters searching that space.
- **Engagement.** Content that earns genuine interaction reaches further, keeping your name visible.

You don't need to game anything — be complete, relevant, and consistently active, and visibility follows.

## Building the Right Canadian Network

For newcomers especially, LinkedIn is how you build a professional network from scratch. Do it deliberately:

- **Connect with people in your field and target companies** in your Canadian city — colleagues, alumni, and professionals whose work overlaps yours.
- **Always add a short, personal note** to requests; a one-line reason for connecting dramatically raises acceptance rates.
- **Follow target companies** so their updates and job posts reach you.
- **Reconnect with former colleagues and classmates**, including those abroad — warm ties are the most likely to refer you.
- **Engage before you ask.** Comment thoughtfully on people's posts before reaching out, so you're a familiar name rather than a stranger.

A relevant network of a few hundred well-chosen connections beats thousands of random ones for job-search purposes, and it powers the referrals that fill so many Canadian roles — see [networking to find jobs in Canada](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/networking-jobs-canada).

## Outreach Messages That Get Replies

Direct outreach is much of LinkedIn's value, but generic messages get ignored. Keep them short, specific, and free of a direct job ask.

**Connecting with someone in your field:**

> *Hi [Name], I'm a [your role] building my career in [city/field] and admire your work at [company]. I'd love to connect and learn from professionals in the space. Thank you!*

**Reaching out to a recruiter:**

> *Hi [Name], I noticed you recruit for [field] roles in [city/Canada]. I'm a [your role] with experience in [key skills], actively exploring new opportunities. I'd welcome the chance to connect — happy to share my resume if helpful.*

**Requesting a quick informational chat:**

> *Hi [Name], I admire your path at [company] and I'm keen to grow in [field]. Would you be open to a brief 15-minute chat about your experience and any advice for someone entering the field here in Canada? I'd really appreciate it.*

Lead with genuine interest, respect people's time, and ask for advice or a connection rather than a job — the opportunity often follows the relationship.

## Staying Safe From Scams

As LinkedIn's importance has grown, so have scams. Be wary of "recruiters" asking for payment, personal financial details, or your SIN upfront — legitimate employers never charge you to apply. Verify unfamiliar companies and contacts before sharing sensitive information, treat too-good-to-be-true offers with caution, and keep sensitive personal data off your public profile. Your headline, experience, and skills belong there; your SIN and home address do not.
