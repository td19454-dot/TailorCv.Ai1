Malaysia is one of Southeast Asia's most attractive destinations for foreign professionals: a lower cost of living than Singapore, widespread use of English, a diverse culture, and a growing base of multinationals and regional headquarters. Getting hired as a foreigner takes more planning than for locals — there are work-authorisation rules and employers must justify hiring from abroad — but for the right skills, it's very achievable.

This guide walks you through getting a job in Malaysia as a foreigner in 2026: which sectors hire expats, how work permits and Employment Passes work, where to search, and how to present yourself so Malaysian employers choose you.

> **Note:** Immigration rules and pass categories change. Treat the permit information here as a practical overview and always confirm current requirements with the Malaysian Immigration Department, the Expatriate Services Division (ESD), or your prospective employer before acting.

## Key Takeaways

- Foreigners generally need an employer-sponsored **Employment Pass** to work in Malaysia; you usually secure the job first, then the employer sponsors the pass.
- Expat hiring is strongest in **tech, engineering, finance, oil & gas, education, and senior/specialist roles** where local talent is scarce.
- Your resume should follow **Malaysian conventions**, not your home country's format.
- A clear value proposition — skills the local market needs — is what convinces an employer to sponsor you.
- English is widely used in Malaysian business, which lowers the barrier for many international professionals.

## Can Foreigners Work in Malaysia?

Yes. Foreign nationals can work in Malaysia with the appropriate work authorisation, which is almost always tied to a specific employer. In practice, the sequence is:

1. You find and secure a job offer from a Malaysian employer.
2. The employer applies to sponsor your work pass (commonly an Employment Pass) through the relevant authorities.
3. Once approved, you can legally begin work.

You generally cannot arrive on a tourist visa and simply start working. The offer comes first; the pass follows.

## Work Passes: A Practical Overview

Malaysia has several categories of work authorisation. The most relevant for professionals is the **Employment Pass (EP)**, typically split into tiers based on salary and role seniority, and sponsored by the hiring company. There are also passes for skilled temporary work and dependent/spouse arrangements.

Key practical points:

- The **employer sponsors and applies** for your pass — you can't self-apply as a professional.
- Approval considers your **salary level, qualifications, and the role's need** for foreign expertise.
- Processing takes time, so factor it into your start date.
- Requirements and salary thresholds change — always verify the current rules.

Because of this, employers weigh the effort of sponsorship against the value you bring. Your job is to make that value obvious.

## Which Sectors Hire Foreigners in Malaysia?

Expat hiring concentrates where local supply is tight or specialist expertise is needed:

- **Technology:** software engineering, data, cybersecurity, product, and specialised digital roles.
- **Engineering:** oil & gas, semiconductor, manufacturing, and infrastructure projects (Penang, Johor, and offshore).
- **Finance & shared services:** senior finance, risk, and regional roles in MNCs and GBS centres.
- **Education:** international schools and universities hiring foreign teachers and academics.
- **Executive & specialist roles:** regional leadership and niche expertise that companies bring in from abroad.

If your skills fall into these areas, sponsorship is far more likely.

- A concise 1–2 page document (not the long CV common in some countries).

## Step 2: Target Employers Likely to Sponsor

Not every company hires foreigners. Focus your energy on:

- **Multinationals and regional HQs** with established expat-hiring processes.
- **Companies in shortage sectors** (tech, engineering, oil & gas) where foreign expertise is expected.
- **International schools and universities** for education roles.
- **Firms explicitly open to foreign candidates** in their postings.

Applying to small local SMEs that rarely sponsor is usually a poor use of effort.

## Step 3: Search in the Right Places

- **JobStreet** — Malaysia's largest portal; filter for roles open to foreigners where possible.
- **Company career pages** — MNCs and GLCs list roles and their sponsorship policies.
- **Specialist recruiters** — agencies that place international professionals in tech, finance, and engineering.
- **Your network** — referrals matter everywhere; connect with expats and professionals already in Malaysia.

## Practical Considerations for Moving to Malaysia

- **Cost of living:** lower than Singapore and many Western cities, though KL is pricier than smaller states.
- **Language:** English is widely used in business, which eases the transition; learning some Bahasa Malaysia helps socially.
- **Location:** most expat professional roles are in KL, the Klang Valley, Penang, and Johor. See [how to find a job in Kuala Lumpur](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-find-a-job-in-kuala-lumpur).
- **Timeline:** account for pass processing when planning your move and start date.

## Common Mistakes Foreign Job Seekers Make

- **Using a home-country resume format.** Adapt to Malaysian conventions.
- **Applying to companies that don't sponsor.** Target MNCs and shortage sectors.
- **Being vague about value.** Sponsorship requires a clear, in-demand skill set.
- **Ignoring work-authorisation reality.** Understand the pass process and timelines.
- **Skipping local research.** Know the market, salary ranges, and culture before interviews.
