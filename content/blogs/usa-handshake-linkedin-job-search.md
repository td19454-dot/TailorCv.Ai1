## Key Takeaways

- Handshake surfaces roles filtered to your school and is where employers post when they specifically want your university's students.
- Company career pages carry the most roles and open earliest; aggregators lag and sometimes list expired postings.
- LinkedIn is most valuable for finding people, not roles — its main use in a student search is research and outreach.
- Easy Apply produces volume and very low conversion. Applying through the employer's own system converts better.
- Track what you applied to. By November you will not remember, and recruiters call about roles you forgot.

## Handshake

Handshake is the university-linked platform most US institutions use. Employers post roles targeted at specific schools, and your profile is visible to recruiters who filter by university, major and graduation year.

**Strengths.** Roles here are frequently school-targeted, which means less competition than a public posting. Employers who recruit at your campus post here first. On-campus interview scheduling often runs through it.

**Weaknesses.** Coverage is incomplete — many employers do not use it, particularly smaller companies and startups. And some roles are cross-posted from elsewhere, so it is not exclusive.

**Use it for:** on-campus recruiting, school-targeted roles, career fair logistics, and being found by recruiters filtering for your programme.

Complete the profile properly. Recruiters search it, and an empty profile is invisible to that channel.

## Company career pages

The most reliable source, and the one students under-use.

**They open earliest.** Employers post to their own site before it propagates anywhere else. In [autumn recruiting](/blog/usa-campus-recruiting-timeline), applying the day a posting opens is a genuine advantage — some new grad roles hit an application cap within days.

**They are authoritative.** No expired listings, no duplicated postings, no wrong details.

**They are the actual application system.** Applying elsewhere often just redirects you here anyway, with a worse experience.

The practical habit: build a list of 20-30 target employers and check their career pages weekly during the recruiting window. That is fifteen minutes and it is the highest-return routine in a student search.

## LinkedIn

LinkedIn is where students waste the most effort, because they use it as a job board when its value is as a people directory.

**What it is good for:**

- **Finding alumni** at target companies. The school filter is the single most useful feature for a student.
- **Researching interviewers.** Knowing someone's background before you meet them is genuinely useful.
- **Understanding teams.** Who works on what, how people move between groups.
- **Being contactable.** Recruiters do search it.

**What it is weaker for:** applying. Easy Apply generates enormous volume for employers, which means each application gets less attention. Conversion is poor.

The better use of LinkedIn in a student search is [finding people to contact](/blog/usa-cold-email-networking-students) and then applying through the company's own system. A referral obtained via LinkedIn is worth many times an Easy Apply submission.

## Aggregators and job boards

General boards aggregate listings from many sources. They are broad and shallow.

**Problems:** postings lag behind the source, expired roles persist, duplicates are common, and some listings are not genuine openings.

**Where they help:** discovering employers you did not know existed, particularly smaller companies without campus presence. Use them to *find* a company, then apply on the company's site.

## Choosing by situation

| Situation | Best channel |
|---|---|
| Employer recruits at your school | Handshake, then the career page |
| Large employer, autumn cycle | Career page, the day it opens |
| You want a referral | LinkedIn to find the person, career page to apply |
| Smaller companies you have not heard of | Aggregators to discover, career page to apply |
| [Non-target school](/blog/usa-non-target-school-strategy) | Career pages and referrals — Handshake coverage is thinner |

## Avoiding duplicate applications

A real problem once you are applying at volume. Applying twice to the same role through different channels looks careless, and some systems flag it.

Keep a simple tracker: company, role, requisition number if there is one, date, channel, status. A spreadsheet is fine.

This also matters when a recruiter calls in November about a role you applied for in September. Being unable to remember which role is a bad start to a conversation.

## What actually decides the outcome

The channel affects whether your application is seen. What happens next is the same everywhere.

Your resume is parsed and matched before a human reads it. A document that does not [survive parsing](/ats-analysis) fails in every channel equally, and a generic document matches nothing specifically. [Tailoring per posting](/blog/how-to-tailor-resume-for-every-job) does more for your interview rate than choosing between platforms.

If you are applying at volume — which [autumn recruiting](/blog/usa-campus-recruiting-timeline) requires — the practical difficulty is keeping tailoring up while raising volume. A browser [extension that tailors against the posting you are viewing](/extension) removes most of that friction.

## Common Mistakes

- **Relying only on Handshake.** Coverage is partial; many employers never post there.
- **Using LinkedIn as the primary application channel.** High volume, low conversion.
- **Applying to aggregator listings without checking the source.** Expired and duplicate postings are common.
- **Not checking career pages weekly during the window.** Some roles cap applications within days.
- **No tracking.** Duplicate applications and forgotten conversations both follow.
- **Optimising channel choice while sending an untailored resume.** The screen is the same in every channel.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Handshake worth using if my school is small?

Yes. Employers filtering for your school are specifically looking for students like you, which is less competitive than a public posting. Coverage may be thinner, so pair it with career pages.

### Does Easy Apply actually work?

Occasionally, but conversion is low because volume is enormous. Use it as a supplement, not a strategy, and prefer the employer's own system where it exists.

### Should I apply through Handshake or the company site if both list it?

If the Handshake listing is school-targeted, use Handshake — that targeting is the advantage. Otherwise the company site.

### How do I find alumni on LinkedIn?

Use the school filter on your university's page, then narrow by company or role. It is the most useful student feature on the platform.

### How many roles should I be tracking?

However many you have applied to — all of them. The tracker exists so you can answer a recruiter's call competently, not to hit a number.

### Do recruiters really search these profiles?

Yes, particularly on Handshake for campus roles and LinkedIn for everything else. A complete profile is a passive channel that costs one evening to set up.
