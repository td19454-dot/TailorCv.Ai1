A resume tells a recruiter you hit 130% of quota. A [portfolio](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-build-professional-portfolio) shows *how* - the territory you inherited, the deals you built, and the numbers behind them. Sales is one of the few fields where a portfolio is still rare, which makes having one a real edge over other candidates. Pair this with [How to Build a Professional Portfolio](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-build-professional-portfolio) and [How to Quantify Resume Achievements](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-quantify-resume-achievements).

## What to Include
- **Quota attainment history** - a simple chart or table by quarter or year
- **2-4 deal case studies** - the account, the problem, your approach, the outcome
- **Pipeline and process evidence** - how you prospect, qualify, and forecast (frameworks like MEDDIC, SPIN, or your own)
- **Testimonials** - a line or two from a manager or client, with permission
- **Tools and stack** - CRM (Salesforce, HubSpot), sales engagement tools, and any certifications

## Frame Every Deal as a Story
Recruiters and sales leaders do not want a list of closed-won deals - they want to see judgment. For each case study, cover:

1. **Territory or account context** - size, industry, competitive landscape
2. **The obstacle** - budget freeze, incumbent vendor, long sales cycle, technical objection
3. **Your approach** - how you built the relationship, handled objections, and moved the deal
4. **The result** - deal size, cycle time, expansion or renewal outcome

If deal specifics are confidential, describe them without naming the client: "Closed a $340K annual contract with a mid-market logistics company after a 4-month cycle, beating an incumbent vendor on implementation speed."

Your resume, [LinkedIn](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-use-linkedin-for-job-search), and portfolio should tell the same story - same quota numbers, same specialty (SaaS, enterprise, SMB), same territory focus - just at different levels of depth. ## Common Mistakes
- Listing quota numbers with no context on team size, ramp time, or territory difficulty
- No deal narrative - just a spreadsheet of numbers
- Sharing confidential contract values or client names without permission
- A stale portfolio that stops at your last role - see [Portfolio Checklist Before Applying](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/portfolio-checklist-before-applying)

## Pro Tips
- Lead with your best quarter or your most complex deal, not chronologically
- Include one case study where you lost then won the deal back - it shows resilience
- Generate your portfolio shell from your resume with the [portfolio builder](https://thetailorcv.com/portfolio) and drop in your deal stories

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a portfolio unusual for sales roles?
Yes, which is exactly why it stands out. Sales leaders rarely see one, so a clear, numbers-backed [portfolio](https://www.thetailorcv.com/portfolio) signals initiative before the first interview.

### What if my results are all under NDA?
Describe outcomes without naming the client or exact figures where required - percentage growth, relative deal size, and cycle time are usually safe to share.

### How do I align my resume?
Use consistent quota and revenue framing across both - see [How to Quantify Resume Achievements](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-quantify-resume-achievements) for the exact phrasing patterns recruiters respond to. Additionally, consider using [resume templates](https://www.thetailorcv.com/templates) to maintain a professional look.
