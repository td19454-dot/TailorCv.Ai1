## Key Takeaways

- **Ho Chi Minh City is the commercial centre** with the largest startup and product scene; **Hanoi** has a strong engineering base and government-adjacent technology.
- **Software outsourcing is the largest employer** — FPT, TMA, NashTech and a substantial tier of firms serving international clients.
- A **domestic product sector** exists around VNG, MoMo, Tiki, VNPay and a growing startup ecosystem.
- **The local engineering talent pool is strong and inexpensive**, which sets a high bar for foreign hires.
- **Da Nang** has emerged as a third centre with lower costs and a growing technology presence.

## The outsourcing industry

Vietnam's largest technology employer and the foundation of the sector.

**FPT Software** is the largest, with tens of thousands of engineers serving clients across Japan, the US and Europe.

**TMA Solutions, NashTech, KMS Technology, Axon Active** and a substantial tier of firms operate similar models.

**Japanese clients are a particular specialism.** Vietnam has built deep relationships with Japanese firms, and Japanese language capability is genuinely valuable in this market — considerably more so than in most outsourcing destinations.

**The work is real engineering** — application development, maintenance, testing, embedded systems and increasingly data and cloud work.

**It trains a great many people**, and a large share of Vietnam's senior engineers came up through it.

**For a foreigner**, these firms hire mainly for client-facing, delivery management and specific technical leadership roles rather than for general engineering, since the local pool covers that.

## The product and startup scene

Smaller but growing and increasingly where the interesting work is.

**VNG** is Vietnam's best-known technology company, spanning gaming, messaging and payments.

**MoMo and VNPay** dominate digital payments in a market that moved quickly to mobile money.

**Tiki and the e-commerce sector**, competing with regional players.

**A growing startup ecosystem**, concentrated in Ho Chi Minh City, with increasing venture funding.

**Regional companies with Vietnamese engineering** — several Southeast Asian technology firms run substantial development teams there.

**And international companies** establishing engineering centres, drawn by the talent pool and costs.

**The pattern:** Vietnam is transitioning from purely outsourced work toward product ownership, and the product side is where the more interesting engineering increasingly sits.

## The two cities and Da Nang

| City | Character |
|---|---|
| **Ho Chi Minh City** | Commercial capital, largest startup and product scene, biggest international community, faster pace |
| **Hanoi** | Political capital, strong engineering universities, government-adjacent technology, more traditional |
| **Da Nang** | Growing third centre, coastal, much lower costs, increasingly popular with remote workers |

**Ho Chi Minh City is where most foreign professionals are** and where the commercial technology sector concentrates.

**Hanoi has excellent technical universities** and a deep engineering pool, with a somewhat more formal business culture.

**Da Nang has grown deliberately** as a technology and lifestyle destination, with lower costs and a coastal setting that has attracted both companies and remote workers.

## Compensation and costs

The financial picture for technology work.

**Local engineering salaries are low** by international standards and have risen steadily as demand grew.

**Senior and lead roles** at product companies and international employers pay considerably more.

**Foreign-linked packages** at international companies are a different category and are what make Vietnam financially attractive for expatriate professionals.

**Outsourcing firms pay less than product companies** for equivalent seniority, which is the usual pattern.

**Personal income tax is progressive** for residents on worldwide income, with residency determined by days present and other factors.

**Costs are genuinely low.** Rent, food, transport and daily life all cost a fraction of Western equivalents, and even a modest salary supports a comfortable standard of living.

**Ho Chi Minh City is the most expensive** of the three cities, with Da Nang considerably cheaper.

**And the savings rate can be high** for someone on a foreign-linked salary, which is a large part of why people come.

## Getting hired

Practical mechanics.

**ITviec is the technology-specific platform** and is well used by the local market.

**VietnamWorks** covers professional roles broadly.

**LinkedIn** works for international employers and senior roles.

**Company career pages** for FPT, VNG and the larger employers.

**Technology meetups and communities** exist in both cities and are genuinely accessible.

**Recruiters** are active, particularly for senior and specialist positions.

**And be specific about your value.** In a market with strong local talent, an application that does not clearly state what you bring beyond general engineering competence will not progress. Lead with the specialism, the language, the leadership experience or the client-facing capability that makes hiring you make sense.

## The broader technology landscape

Beyond software, where technical work sits.

**Embedded systems and hardware**, tied to the electronics manufacturing base and genuinely substantial.

**Fintech**, driven by rapid mobile payment adoption — MoMo, VNPay, ZaloPay and the banking sector's digital efforts.

**E-commerce and logistics technology**, serving a market that moved online quickly.

**Gaming**, with VNG a significant regional player and a broader studio presence.

**AI and data**, growing from a low base with government interest and university programmes.

**EdTech and HealthTech**, both expanding.

**And IT infrastructure and cloud**, as Vietnamese enterprises modernise.

**The pattern:** Vietnam's technology sector has breadth beyond outsourcing, and the domestic product side has grown quickly enough that it is now a genuine career option rather than a smaller alternative.

## Career progression

Where a Vietnamese technology career leads.

**Within outsourcing**, from engineer to lead to delivery management, with client-facing roles being the differentiator.

**Into product companies**, which increasingly recruit from the outsourcing pool.

**Regionally**, since Southeast Asian technology companies operate across borders and Vietnamese experience is recognised.

**Into international companies' Vietnamese operations**, which pay more and offer global exposure.

**And out**, since Vietnamese engineering experience is increasingly recognised internationally, particularly for people who worked on international client projects.

**For a foreigner specifically**, the path is usually leadership or specialist rather than individual contribution, and progression tends toward regional responsibility rather than deeper local roles.

## Remote work and the alternative route

An increasingly common arrangement worth treating properly.

**Vietnam is an attractive base for remote workers** on cost grounds, and Da Nang in particular has built around this.

**The visa position needs care.** Vietnam does not currently have a dedicated digital nomad visa comparable to Thailand's or Portugal's, and working remotely on a tourist visa is not a lawful arrangement.

**Some people use business visas or other categories**, and the position should be verified properly rather than assumed from forum advice.

**Tax residency is a separate question** from immigration status, and spending significant time in Vietnam can create obligations regardless of your visa type.

**The infrastructure is good.** Internet is reliable and fast in the major cities, coworking spaces are plentiful and inexpensive, and the practical experience of remote working there is straightforward.

**And the community exists.** Ho Chi Minh City and Da Nang both have substantial remote-working populations, which makes arriving socially easier than in many places.

**The honest advice:** the lifestyle case is strong and the legal position requires proper attention rather than the casual approach many people take.

## Practical steps for a technology search

Concrete actions.

**Create profiles on ITviec and VietnamWorks**, both of which are searched directly by recruiters.

**Build a target list** across outsourcing firms, domestic product companies and international employers with Vietnamese operations.

**State your differentiator in the first line** — the specialism, the language, the leadership experience. In a market with strong local talent, a generic profile does not progress.

**Attend the meetups.** Both cities have active technology communities and they are genuinely accessible to foreigners.

**Join the bilateral chamber** relevant to your nationality, which provides networking and job listings.

**Understand the permit position before applying** so you can answer it clearly when asked.

**And consider the remote alternative honestly.** For many foreign engineers, working remotely for a foreign employer while living in Vietnam is a better arrangement than seeking local employment — provided the visa position is handled properly.

## Vietnam versus the region for technology

How it compares as a technology destination.

**Against India.** India's market is vastly larger with a deeper product ecosystem. Vietnam is smaller, growing faster from a lower base, and considerably cheaper.

**Against the Philippines.** The Philippines has the English-language advantage and a larger services sector; Vietnam has stronger engineering depth and a manufacturing-linked hardware base.

**Against Thailand.** Thailand's technology sector is smaller relative to its economy; Vietnam's engineering output is stronger.

**Against Indonesia.** Indonesia has the larger domestic market and consumer technology scene; Vietnam has the stronger outsourcing and engineering base.

**Against Singapore and Malaysia.** Both are considerably more developed with higher salaries and more established foreign employment; Vietnam is earlier-stage and cheaper.

**The summary:** Vietnam is a strong engineering market with rising quality, a manufacturing-linked hardware advantage, and a domestic product scene growing quickly — and it is a difficult market to enter as a general foreign engineer precisely because the local talent is good.
