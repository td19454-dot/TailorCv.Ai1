## The Short Answer

**A good [ATS score](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/ats-score-guide) is 80 or higher.** At that level, your resume strongly matches the job description and is very likely to pass automated filtering and reach a recruiter.

Below is the full breakdown.

| ATS Score | Rating | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| 90-100 | Excellent | Apply now - your resume is highly aligned |
| 80-89 | Strong | Apply; minor polish optional |
| 70-79 | Good | A few tweaks will push you over 80 |
| 60-69 | Moderate | Optimize keywords and formatting first |
| Below 60 | At risk | Likely filtered out - needs real work |

## Why 80 Is the Target

[ATS](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/what-is-an-ats-score-and-why-does-it-decide-your-job-application-before-any-human-reads-it) software ranks every applicant. Recruiters often review only the top-ranked resumes for a role that may have hundreds of applicants. A score of 80+ generally means:

- Your skills and keywords closely match the posting
- Your formatting parses cleanly
- Your job titles and experience align with the role

A score in the 60s might still be qualified on paper, but it risks being ranked below other candidates and never reviewed.

## How ATS Scores Are Calculated

Different tools weight factors slightly differently, but the core components are consistent:

| Factor | Approximate Weight |
|---|---|
| Keyword & skills match | 30-40% |
| Skills alignment | 20-25% |
| Formatting & parseability | 10-15% |
| Job title relevance | 10-15% |
| Experience quality | 10-20% |
| Education & certifications | 5-10% |

This is why a generic resume rarely scores well - it is not tailored to the specific keywords and titles in the job description. Learn how to fix that in [how to tailor your resume for every job](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-tailor-resume-for-every-job).

## A "Good" Score Is Relative to the Job

There is no universal [ATS score](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/ats-score-guide). The same resume can score 90 for one role and 55 for another, because the score measures the **match** between your resume and a **specific** job description.

That means:

- Always check your score against the exact job you are applying to
- Re-tailor and re-check for each application
- Do not assume one strong score covers every job

## Common Mistakes That Lower Your ATS Score

When optimizing your resume for Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS), it's crucial to avoid common pitfalls that can negatively impact your score. Here are some frequent mistakes to steer clear of:

1. **Using Non-Standard Formatting**: ATS may struggle to read resumes with unusual fonts, graphics, or complex layouts. Stick to traditional formats like .docx or .pdf and use standard fonts like Arial or Times New Roman.

2. **Overlooking Keywords**: Failing to include relevant keywords from the job description can lead to a low score. Analyze the job posting and incorporate those terms naturally throughout your resume.

3. **Including Irrelevant Information**: Adding unrelated work experience or skills can dilute your ATS score. Focus on what is pertinent to the job you’re applying for.

4. **Ignoring Job Titles**: If your previous job titles were unique to your company, consider using standard titles that align with industry norms to ensure the ATS recognizes your experience.

5. **Neglecting to Tailor Your Resume**: Sending out a generic resume can significantly reduce your chances of passing the ATS filter. Customize your resume for each application, focusing on the specific requirements listed in the job description.

For a deeper understanding of how to avoid these pitfalls, check out our guide on [How ATS Detects a Generic Resume - And Why It Filters You Out](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-ats-detects-generic-resume).

## Step-by-Step: How to Tailor Your Resume for Better ATS Performance

To ensure your resume meets ATS requirements and achieves a good score, follow this step-by-step guide:

1. **Analyze the Job Description**: Highlight keywords and phrases that are repeated or emphasized. This includes required skills, qualifications, and specific job titles.

2. **Choose the Right Format**: Use a simple, clean format. Avoid tables and text boxes that ATS might misinterpret. A chronological or combination format is often best.

3. **Incorporate Keywords**: Integrate the identified keywords into your resume. Use them in your summary, work experience, and skills sections, maintaining a natural flow.

4. **Use Standard Headings**: Stick to conventional section titles like "Work Experience," "Education," and "Skills." This helps ATS easily categorize your information.

5. **Edit for Clarity**: Proofread your resume to remove any typos or errors. ATS can misread misspelled words, which could cost you points.

6. **Test Your Resume**: Before submitting, use an [ATS Resume Checker - How It Works and How to Pass](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/ats-resume-checker-how-it-works) to evaluate your score and make necessary adjustments.

## Do's and Don'ts for ATS Optimization

### Do's

- **Do Use Relevant Keywords**: Tailor your resume to match the specific terminology used in the job description.
- **Do Keep It Simple**: Use a straightforward layout and avoid complicated graphics and fonts.
- **Do Include Action Verbs**: Start bullet points with strong action verbs to convey your achievements effectively.
- **Do Save in the Right Format**: Save your resume in a .docx or .pdf format unless otherwise specified.

### Don'ts

- **Don’t Stuff Keywords**: Avoid overloading your resume with keywords; this can come off as unnatural and may even lead to disqualification.
- **Don’t Use Uncommon Abbreviations**: Always spell out acronyms the first time you use them to ensure clarity.
- **Don’t Include Personal Information**: Avoid putting sensitive information like your age, gender, or marital status, as these can lead to bias.
- **Don’t Submit Without Testing**: Always check your ATS score before sending your resume out to potential employers.

No. A perfect score is not necessary. Anything 80 and above is competitive. Chasing 100 often leads to unnatural keyword stuffing, which can hurt you with human reviewers.
