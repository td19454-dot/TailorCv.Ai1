## Key Takeaways

- The **Temporary Graduate visa (subclass 485)** is the bridge — it gives you full working rights after graduating, but it expires, and it is not permanent residence.
- Permanent skilled visas run on a **points test**, and you must reach a minimum score before you can even submit an Expression of Interest.
- **English test results are the single most improvable factor** — moving from Proficient to Superior English is worth a large jump for one weekend of preparation and a test fee.
- Your occupation must be on a **skilled occupation list** and you need a positive **skills assessment** from the relevant assessing authority before you can claim points.
- **State nomination (190) and regional (491)** routes add points on top of your base score and frequently decide the outcome.

## The two-stage picture

Almost every confusion about Australian immigration comes from collapsing two separate things into one.

**Stage one is the 485.** You [graduate](/blog/first-resume-for-gen-z-2026) from an Australian institution, you apply for a Temporary Graduate visa, and you get full working rights for a defined period. No [employer](/blog/how-recruiters-spot-generic-resumes) sponsorship, no points test, no [job offer](/blog/how-to-negotiate-a-job-offer-email) required. Its length depends on your qualification level, and studying or living in a designated regional area can extend it.

**Stage two is permanent residence**, which runs on an entirely different mechanism: the points test. The 485 does not convert into permanent residence automatically. It gives you a window in which to build the points and the work [experience](/blog/resume-matching-experienced-professionals) that a permanent visa requires.

Graduates who treat the 485 as the destination rather than the runway are the ones who find themselves scrambling in its final year.

## What the points test actually scores

| Factor | How it behaves |
|---|---|
| **Age** | Peaks in the 25–32 band, and falls away after 32 |
| **English ability** | Competent scores nothing extra; **Proficient** and **Superior** add substantially |
| **Skilled employment** | Both Australian and overseas experience count, in bands by years |
| **Education** | A doctorate, then a bachelor's or master's, then a diploma |
| **Australian study requirement** | Points for a qualification obtained in Australia |
| **Specialist education** | An additional award for a relevant Australian master's by research or doctorate |
| **Regional study** | Additional points for study in a designated regional area |
| **Community language** | Credentialed translator/interpreter accreditation |
| **Partner skills** | Points where your partner also meets criteria, or where you are single |
| **Professional Year** | An accredited Professional Year program in certain fields |
| **State nomination / regional sponsorship** | A substantial addition, and often decisive |

The individual point values and the pass mark are set by the Department of Home Affairs and are revised, so check the current table on their site rather than any secondary source — including this one.

## Why English is the lever to pull first

This is the practical heart of the whole thing for a new graduate.

English is scored in bands: **Competent**, **Proficient**, and **Superior**. Competent is the eligibility baseline and earns no additional points. Proficient earns a meaningful number. Superior earns considerably more.

The gap between Competent and Superior is one of the largest single point swings available to any applicant — and unlike age, education or years of experience, it is entirely within your control and improvable in weeks.

**Accepted tests include IELTS, PTE Academic, TOEFL iBT, Cambridge C1 Advanced and OET.** They are not interchangeable in difficulty for every candidate. Many applicants find PTE Academic's computer-scored format easier to optimise for than IELTS's human-marked speaking and writing, and switching test is a legitimate strategy rather than a trick.

**Every band is scored separately.** Your lowest section determines your level — a Superior score in three sections and a Proficient in one gives you Proficient. Target the weakest band specifically rather than studying generally.

## Skills assessment and the occupation list

Two gates before points matter at all.

**Your occupation must be on a skilled occupation list.** The lists are reviewed and occupations are added and removed, which is why an occupation that qualified a friend three years ago may not qualify you now.

**You need a positive [skills](/blog/resume-skills-match-job-description) assessment** from the assessing authority for your occupation — Engineers Australia for engineers, ACS for ICT roles, CPA Australia or CAANZ for accountants, VETASSESS for many others. Each has its own requirements, its own fee and its own [processing time](/blog/how-long-to-hear-back-after-applying).

**Start this early.** A skills assessment takes real time, it must be valid when you submit, and it is the step most likely to delay an otherwise ready [application](/blog/how-many-jobs-should-you-apply-to-per-day). For ICT graduates in particular, the ACS has specific requirements about how much of your [degree](/blog/how-to-list-education-on-resume) is ICT-relevant and how work experience is counted.

## The three main permanent pathways

**Subclass 189 — Skilled Independent.** No sponsor required. You submit an Expression of Interest through SkillSelect and are invited if your score is high enough. The most flexible visa and the hardest to reach on points alone.

**Subclass 190 — Skilled Nominated.** A state or territory nominates you, which adds points to your score. In exchange you commit to living and working in that state. Each state publishes its own occupation list and criteria, which differ from the federal list and from each other.

**Subclass 491 — Skilled Work Regional (Provisional).** Nomination by a state or by an eligible relative in a designated regional area, adding a larger number of points than 190. It is provisional rather than permanent, leading to permanent residence after meeting residence and income requirements.

**The strategic read:** most graduates do not reach an invitation on base points alone. State nomination or the regional route is what closes the gap, and it means researching individual state lists rather than only the federal one.

## How SkillSelect and invitation rounds work

Submitting an Expression of Interest is not an application, and the distinction matters.

You lodge an EOI through **SkillSelect**, the online system, stating your occupation, your claimed points and your visa preferences. It sits in a pool. The Department then runs **invitation rounds**, inviting the highest-ranked candidates in each occupation until that round's allocation is filled.

Three consequences follow from that design.

**You are ranked against other people, not against a fixed bar.** Meeting the minimum score gets you into the pool; it does not get you invited. In competitive occupations the score that actually receives an invitation sits well above the minimum.

**Ties are broken by how long you have been in the pool.** Two candidates on the same score are separated by EOI submission date, which is a quiet argument for lodging as soon as your claims are genuinely supportable.

**Your claims must be provable at invitation, not aspirational.** Everything you claim points for - skills assessment, English result, employment - has to be documented if you are invited. Overstating a claim and hoping to fix it later is how applications get refused and applicants get barred from reapplying for a period.

## Building points across your 485 years

The most useful way to think about the graduate window is as a deliberate points-building period rather than simply a job.

**Skilled employment counts in bands.** Australian skilled work experience accrues toward point bands, so the classification of your role matters, not just that you are employed. Work that is not in your nominated occupation, or not at a skilled level, may not count toward the claim you eventually make.

**Age works against you the whole time.** Points peak in the 25-32 band and fall afterwards. For someone at the upper end of that range, a year spent waiting costs points that no amount of extra experience recovers.

**A Professional Year** in accounting, ICT or engineering is an accredited program combining structured learning with a workplace placement, and it carries points. It costs money and takes months, so weigh it against what an English retake would deliver for a fraction of the effort.

**Partner points are real.** If your partner also has an assessed occupation and Competent English, that adds points; being single also attracts points in its own right. Couples should calculate both scores before deciding who is the primary applicant, because the answer is sometimes counterintuitive.

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: You are a few points short

Book an English test and target Superior. It is the fastest and largest single improvement available, and it typically outweighs waiting another year for an experience band.

### Scenario 2: Your occupation is not on the federal list

Check the individual state and territory lists, which differ from the federal one and from each other. An occupation closed federally is sometimes open under a specific state's nomination stream.

### Scenario 3: Your 485 is running out

Do not wait for the final months. Skills assessments, English tests and EOI invitation rounds all run on their own [timelines](/blog/campus-placement-preparation-timeline), and stacking them at the end is how people run out of visa before they run out of options.

> **Tip:** Sit the English test early, even if you feel you are not ready. The result tells you exactly which band is holding your score down, and you can retake targeting only that section — far more efficient than studying everything and hoping.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Treat the 485 as a runway, not a destination
- Start the skills assessment early
- Target Superior English specifically
- Check current lists and point values on Home Affairs
- Research individual state nomination lists
- Consider regional study and work seriously

### Don't

- Assume the 485 converts to permanent residence
- Rely on point values quoted in an article or forum
- Study English generally instead of targeting your weakest band
- Assume the occupation list is the same as it was last year
- Leave the skills assessment until your visa is nearly up
- Ignore the 190 and 491 routes because 189 sounds better

## Common Mistakes

- **Treating the 485 as permanent residence.** It is a time-limited bridge, and the points work has to happen inside it.
- **Using stale point figures.** The table and the pass mark are revised; check Home Affairs directly.
- **Leaving the skills assessment late.** It takes real time and it is the most common cause of a delayed application.
- **Studying English generally.** Your lowest band sets your level, so target that band specifically.
- **Ignoring state nomination.** Most graduates close the gap through 190 or 491, not on base points alone.
- **Assuming the occupation list is static.** Lists are reviewed and occupations move on and off them.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does the 485 visa lead to permanent residence?

Not automatically. It gives you full working rights for a defined period, and that window is when you build the points, work experience and skills assessment a permanent visa requires.

### How many points do I need?

There is a minimum to submit an Expression of Interest, and a separate, higher score at which invitations are actually issued in a given round. Both move — check the current position on the Department of Home Affairs site.

### Which English test should I take?

IELTS, PTE Academic, TOEFL iBT, Cambridge C1 Advanced and OET are accepted. They suit different candidates; many find PTE's computer-scored format easier to optimise. Switching test is a legitimate strategy.

### What is a skills assessment and who does it?

A formal assessment by the authority for your occupation — Engineers Australia, ACS, CPA Australia, VETASSESS and others. It is required before you can claim points, and it takes real time.

### What is the difference between 189, 190 and 491?

189 needs no sponsor but demands the highest score. 190 is state-nominated and adds points in exchange for committing to that state. 491 is regional and provisional, adding more points, leading to permanent residence later.

### Does studying regionally actually help?

Yes — study in a designated regional area can extend your 485 and contribute additional points, and regional nomination under 491 adds the largest single boost available to most graduates.
### What is SkillSelect and how do invitation rounds work?

You lodge an Expression of Interest stating your points and occupation; it sits in a pool, and the Department invites the highest-ranked candidates each round. Meeting the minimum gets you into the pool, not invited.

### Does it matter when I lodge my EOI?

Yes - candidates on the same score are separated by how long they have been in the pool, so lodging as soon as your claims are genuinely provable is worth doing.

### Is a Professional Year worth it?

It carries points and combines study with a workplace placement, but it costs money and months. Compare it honestly against what an English retake would add for far less effort.

### Should my partner or I be the primary applicant?

Calculate both scores before deciding. Partner skills attract points, being single attracts points, and the stronger primary applicant is sometimes not the one you would assume.
