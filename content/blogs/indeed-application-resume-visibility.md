## Key Takeaways

- Indeed has a separate resume database that recruiters can search directly — distinct from applying to any specific posting.
- Your resume's visibility in that database depends on a privacy setting, which many people leave on the less visible default without realising it.
- Indeed's resume search is keyword-based, similar in principle to how a job posting's ATS matches your resume, but operating independently of any application.
- Recruiters searching Indeed's database are looking for exact terms and specific job titles, not broad descriptions.
- These are genuinely two different opportunities: applying to a posting, and being found through the resume database.

Most people think of Indeed purely as a job board — search postings, click apply. It also runs a separate resume database that recruiters actively search, and most job seekers never adjust the setting that controls whether they show up in it.

## Two Separate Things: Applying and Being Found

**Applying to a specific posting** works how you would expect — you find a listing, submit your resume, and it enters that employer's application process, generally through whatever ATS the employer uses on their end.

**Being findable in Indeed's resume database** is a separate, independent feature. If you have uploaded a resume to your Indeed profile, recruiters with database access can search across stored resumes directly — completely apart from any specific posting or application you have made. This is closer to a searchable candidate pool than to a job-application queue.

These are genuinely different opportunities. You can be well-positioned for one and invisible in the other, depending on your settings and resume content.

## The Privacy Setting Most People Never Check

Whether your resume is discoverable in this searchable database depends on a visibility setting on your profile — and many job seekers leave it at whatever the default happens to be, without realising this determines whether you can be found this way at all.

**If you are actively job hunting and want inbound opportunities**, setting your resume to publicly visible (searchable) makes sense — it opens a channel for recruiters to find and reach out to you that has nothing to do with actively applying anywhere.

**If you are not actively looking, or are concerned about your current employer seeing your resume**, keeping it private is the more cautious choice — check your specific privacy controls, since some platforms offer more granular options like hiding your resume from your current employer specifically while remaining visible more broadly.

## How Recruiters Actually Search

Recruiters using Indeed's resume search typically filter by specific hard skills, job titles, and location — a keyword-based search functioning on similar underlying logic to how an ATS matches a resume against a posting, covered generally in [how to match resume keywords to a job description](/blog/how-to-match-resume-keywords-to-job-description).

The practical implication: for your resume to surface in these searches, it needs to contain the specific, exact terms a recruiter is likely to search for — the actual tool names, the actual job titles, the actual certifications — rather than only broader, more general descriptions of your work. This is the same principle behind [naming what you already did](/blog/improve-score-without-lying) applied to a searchable database rather than a single application.

## Keeping Your Resume Current Matters Here Specifically

Because this is an ongoing, searchable presence rather than a one-time submission, an outdated resume sitting in the database indefinitely is a missed opportunity in a way that does not apply the same way to a single job application. Periodically updating your resume — even minor refreshes — is worth doing if you want to maintain visibility and relevance in ongoing searches, separate from any specific application you might also be making.

## This Does Not Replace Tailored Applications

Being discoverable in Indeed's resume database is a genuinely useful inbound channel, but it works differently from applying directly to a specific posting with a resume tailored to that posting's requirements. A resume optimised broadly for database discoverability — covering your general skill set with the exact terms recruiters commonly search — is not the same document as one tailored precisely to a single job description's specific requirements.

If you are relying on the database for inbound interest, keep a strong, keyword-rich general resume there. When you find and apply to a specific posting, that is a separate moment worth its own tailoring pass — see [why one resume cannot serve every application](/blog/same-resume-every-job-why-failing) for why a single document serves both purposes poorly.

## A Practical Checklist

1. **Check your resume visibility/privacy setting** — confirm it matches whether you actually want to be found through search right now.
2. **Ensure your uploaded resume contains specific, exact terms** — real job titles, real tool names, real certifications — not just general descriptions.
3. **Update your resume periodically** if you want to maintain visibility in ongoing searches.
4. **Do not treat your database resume and your per-application tailored resume as the same document** — keep the database version strong and general; tailor separately when applying to something specific.
5. **Consider hiding your resume from your current employer specifically**, if that option exists and is relevant to your situation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need to upload a resume to Indeed to apply for jobs there?**
Application requirements vary by posting — some accept your uploaded profile resume, others allow or require a specific tailored document. Check what a given posting requests.

**Is it risky to make my resume publicly searchable on Indeed?**
The main practical risk most people worry about is a current employer discovering it — check the platform's specific privacy controls, since options for hiding it from your current employer specifically often exist separately from full privacy.

**Does having my resume searchable guarantee recruiter outreach?**
No — it opens a channel, but outreach still depends on your resume matching what recruiters are actually searching for.

**Should my Indeed database resume be different from my tailored application resumes?**
It is reasonable for them to differ somewhat — the database version should be strong and broadly keyword-rich for discoverability, while application-specific versions should be tailored to individual postings.

**How often should I update my Indeed resume for visibility purposes?**
There is no fixed rule, but periodic updates — every few weeks while actively searching — are a reasonable practice if inbound visibility matters to you.
