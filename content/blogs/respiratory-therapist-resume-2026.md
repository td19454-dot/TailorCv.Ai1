## Key Takeaways

- **RRT beats CRT** in screening. Write which one you hold, with the NBRC credential and your state licence.
- Ventilator experience is the core keyword. Name the modes and the vendors.
- Specialty credentials — ACCS, NPS, RRT-ACCS, AE-C — are strong differentiators and frequently required.
- Unit type is a matching keyword: MICU, SICU, NICU, ED, step-down. Postings screen on these.
- Patient load and vent count per shift are the numbers that separate RT resumes.

Respiratory therapy postings are specific about credentials and setting because the work differs sharply between a NICU, an adult ICU and an outpatient clinic. A resume that does not name the unit type and the ventilator experience is hard to place.

## Best Respiratory Therapist Resume Format for 2026

Reverse-chronological, one to two pages, single column.

Single column, no tables or text boxes — hospital ATS installations handle them badly. See [ATS formatting mistakes](/blog/ats-resume-formatting-mistakes) and, on length, our [resume length guide](/blog/ats-resume-length-guide).

Section order:

1. Header with credentials
2. Licensure and certification
3. Summary
4. Clinical skills and equipment
5. Experience
6. Education

## Header and Credentials

```
Priya Raman, RRT, RRT-ACCS
Phoenix, AZ · (602) 555-0173 · p.raman@email.com
```

The NBRC credentials:

- **CRT** — Certified Respiratory Therapist (entry level)
- **RRT** — Registered Respiratory Therapist (advanced; the standard for hospital roles)
- **RRT-ACCS** — Adult Critical Care Specialty
- **RRT-NPS** — Neonatal/Pediatric Specialty
- **RRT-SDS** — Sleep Disorders Specialty
- **AE-C** — Certified Asthma Educator

Write the highest you hold plus any specialty. Most acute-care postings require or strongly prefer RRT.

## Licensure and Certification Section

```
LICENSURE & CERTIFICATION
Registered Respiratory Therapist (RRT), NBRC — credential #98211, current
Adult Critical Care Specialist (RRT-ACCS), NBRC — 2023
Respiratory Care Practitioner License, Arizona, #RCP-4471, active through 05/2028
BLS / ACLS / PALS / NRP — all current through 2027
```

NRP matters for anyone touching deliveries or neonatal transport; PALS for pediatric coverage. These are frequently hard requirements, and each takes one line.

## Ventilator Experience Is the Core Keyword

This is the section hiring managers read first, and vague versions fail.

> **Ventilation:** Invasive and non-invasive ventilation · Volume and pressure control · PRVC · APRV · SIMV · Pressure support · High-frequency oscillatory ventilation (HFOV) · BiPAP/CPAP · High-flow nasal cannula
> **Vendors:** Puritan Bennett 980 · Hamilton G5 · Servo-i / Servo-u · Dräger Evita · Trilogy · V60
> **Adjuncts:** Inhaled nitric oxide · Heliox · ECMO circuit support · Prone positioning protocol

Name modes *and* vendors. A unit running Hamilton G5s wants someone who has used them, and postings say so. Generic "ventilator management" matches far less than the specifics.

## Unit Type Is a Matching Keyword

Postings screen on setting because the skill sets genuinely differ:

MICU · SICU · CVICU · NICU · PICU · Neuro ICU · Emergency Department · Step-down / progressive care · Long-term acute care (LTACH) · Pulmonary function lab · Sleep lab · Home care / DME

Write the units you have covered, with duration. If you are moving between them — adult to neonatal, or ICU to outpatient — name the target explicitly and connect what transfers.

## Best ATS Keywords for an RT Resume

**Clinical:** mechanical ventilation, airway management, intubation assist, extubation, weaning protocol, ABG draw and interpretation, arterial line sampling, bronchoscopy assist, tracheostomy care, chest physiotherapy, aerosol therapy, nebuliser treatment, oxygen titration, suctioning

**Assessment:** ventilator weaning, spontaneous breathing trial, RSBI, capnography, pulse oximetry, pulmonary function testing, spirometry, six-minute walk test

**Emergency:** rapid response, code blue, RSI support, transport ventilation, neonatal resuscitation, stroke and trauma response

**Credentials:** RRT, CRT, NBRC, ACCS, NPS, AE-C, ACLS, PALS, NRP

## Writing Experience Bullets

**Weak:**
> Managed ventilators and provided respiratory treatments to patients.

**Strong:**
> Manage 8-12 ventilated patients per shift across a 24-bed MICU, including APRV and HFOV, with independent weaning under protocol and daily spontaneous breathing trials.

**Weak:**
> Participated in the ventilator weaning programme.

**Strong:**
> Co-authored the unit's spontaneous breathing trial protocol, reducing mean ventilator days from 6.4 to 4.9 across 12 months without an increase in reintubation rate.

Numbers most RTs have: vented patients per shift, total patient load, ABGs per shift, intubations assisted, codes responded to, ventilator days, reintubation rate, transport runs, students precepted. > - Manage 8-12 ventilated patients per shift in a 24-bed MICU; independently titrate and wean per protocol.
> - Respond to all code blues and rapid responses for the adult tower; assist RSI and manage post-intubation stabilisation.

## New Graduate Respiratory Therapists

Lead with clinical rotations and credential status.

> **CLINICAL ROTATIONS — 800+ hours, 2025-2026**
> **Adult ICU**, St. Joseph's (240 hrs). Managed up to 4 ventilated patients under supervision; ABG draws, weaning trials, code response.
> **NICU**, Children's Medical (160 hrs). CPAP and conventional ventilation, surfactant administration support, NRP-certified deliveries.
> **Emergency Department** (120 hrs). Airway management, nebuliser therapy, BiPAP initiation.
> **Pulmonary Function Lab** (80 hrs). Spirometry, DLCO, six-minute walk testing.

Our [no-experience resume guide](/blog/how-to-write-resume-with-no-experience-2026) covers the general framing. State credential status plainly: *"CRT earned 06/2026; RRT clinical simulation exam scheduled 09/2026."* Hospitals hire on that basis regularly, and being explicit is better than leaving it ambiguous.

## Common Respiratory Therapist Resume Mistakes

**Writing "respiratory therapist" without RRT or CRT.** The credential is the filter.

**Generic ventilator language.** Name the modes and vendors.

**Omitting unit type.** MICU and NICU experience are not interchangeable and postings know it.

**Leaving out ACLS/PALS/NRP.** Frequently hard requirements; one line each.

**No patient or vent numbers.** Load is the operational fact hiring managers need — see [weak resume bullets](/blog/weak-resume-bullets-fix).

**Hiding float or cross-unit coverage.** Flexibility across units is valuable — say it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is RRT required, or will CRT do?**
Most acute-care hospital roles require or strongly prefer RRT. Outpatient and some long-term settings accept CRT.

**Do specialty credentials matter?**
Yes. ACCS and NPS are often listed as preferred and sometimes required, and they are strong differentiators in a competitive posting.

**Should I list ventilator vendors?**
Yes, where you have used them. Postings name platforms and the workflow differences are real.

**How do I move from adult to neonatal?**
Name the target in the summary, list NRP and any neonatal clinical hours, and connect transferable airway and ventilation skills honestly.

**How long should the resume be?**
One page for new graduates; two once you have multiple units, specialty credentials and protocol work worth listing.
