## Key Takeaways

- The extension covers the whole application object, not just the resume: **tailored resume, matching cover letter, and answers to application form questions**.
- Your **apply profile** stores the standard answers every form asks for, so deterministic autofill handles them without AI.
- The **open-ended questions** — "why do you want to work here", "describe a time you..." — are answered from your base resume and the job description.
- Everything downloads as **ATS-ready PDFs** and is saved to your TailorCV account.
- The workflow deliberately **stops short of submitting for you**. You read, you edit, you send.

## What the workflow covers

An application is rarely just a resume, and the parts around it are where the time actually goes.

**The tailored resume.** Your base resume rewritten against this posting's description, downloaded as a PDF.

**The cover letter.** Generated against the same job description and your same base resume, so the two documents agree with each other rather than contradicting themselves — which happens more often than people think when the resume and letter are written weeks apart.

**The apply profile.** The standard fields every application form asks: contact details, work authorisation, notice period, links, education. Stored once, filled deterministically.

**The open questions.** The free-text prompts that make people abandon applications halfway through. These are answered from your base resume and the job description, with the question's own field type and character limit respected.

**The record.** Each application is logged, so you have a history of what you applied to and with which version.

## Deterministic first, AI second

This distinction matters and it is deliberate.

**Structured fields are filled deterministically.** Your name, email, phone, education, work authorisation and links do not need a language model — they need a stored profile and exact matching. That part is fast, free and predictable.

**Only the genuinely open questions go to AI.** "Why are you interested in this role?" with a 500-character limit is a writing task; "What is your phone number?" is not.

**The system respects the field.** Character limits, field types and multiple-choice options are all passed through, so a 200-character answer comes back at 200 characters rather than at 800 that you then have to cut.

**Why this matters to you:** it is faster, it costs less of your quota, and the answers that come back are the ones that actually needed writing.

## The skill match, before you decide to apply

There is a step before all of this that is easy to miss.

**The extension calculates a skill-match score** between your base resume and the job description you are looking at. It is deterministic — pure text matching, no language model — which means it is instant and it refires as you scroll through jobs.

**Use it as a filter.** A very low match usually means either the job is genuinely a poor fit, or your resume does not evidence things you actually have. Both are worth knowing before you spend an application.

**A low score is not always a no.** Sometimes it reveals that your base resume is understating you, which is a fixable problem and a more valuable insight than the score itself.

## What it deliberately does not do

Three limits, each on purpose.

**It does not submit for you.** You read the generated documents, you edit what you want to change, and you press the button. An automated system that fires off applications without you reading them produces exactly the outcome you are trying to avoid.

**It does not invent qualifications.** Answers and resume content are grounded in your base resume. If you have not done something, the system does not claim you have — because you will be asked about it.

**It does not replace judgement about fit.** Making applications cheap makes it tempting to apply to everything. Cheap applications to jobs you do not want are still wasted applications, and a recruiter who reads a mismatched application remembers it.

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: The form has six open questions and a 300-character limit each

This is the case that makes people abandon applications. The extension answers them from your own material within the limits, and you edit rather than starting from a blank box six times.

### Scenario 2: You are unsure whether a job is worth applying to

Check the skill match first. It is instant and free of quota, and it tells you whether your resume evidences what the posting asks for before you spend anything.

### Scenario 3: You want the cover letter to sound like you

Generate it, then rewrite the middle paragraph in your own words. The generated version solves the blank page and the structure; the voice should be yours on any job you actually want.

> **Tip:** Fill in your apply profile properly, once. Every hour you spend on it is repaid across every application afterwards, because the deterministic autofill covers the boring fields without touching your AI quota.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Complete the apply profile fully
- Use the skill match as a pre-filter
- Read and edit everything before submitting
- Rewrite the cover letter's voice on jobs you care about
- Keep the base resume current
- Check the application record periodically

### Don't

- Submit anything you have not read
- Treat a low skill match as automatically a no
- Apply to everything just because it is cheap
- Expect the system to claim skills you lack
- Leave the apply profile half-finished
- Assume one cover letter fits a category of jobs

## Common Mistakes

- **Submitting unread.** The generated documents are drafts, not final submissions.
- **Half-finished apply profile.** It is the piece that makes everything downstream fast.
- **Ignoring the skill match.** It is instant, free and genuinely informative.
- **Applying indiscriminately.** Cheap applications to bad-fit jobs are still wasted.
- **Never editing the voice.** On jobs that matter, the letter should sound like you.
- **Stale base resume.** Everything generated is only as current as its source.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does the extension submit applications for me?

No, deliberately. It generates the documents and answers; you read them, edit what you want, and submit yourself.

### How are application form questions answered?

Standard structured fields are filled deterministically from your stored apply profile. Only genuinely open-ended questions are answered from your base resume and the job description.

### Does it respect character limits?

Yes. The question's field type, options and character limit are passed through, so answers come back at the right length rather than needing to be cut.

### What is the skill match score?

An instant, deterministic text match between your base resume and the job description. It uses no AI, so it is fast and free of quota, and it works as a pre-filter.

### Will the cover letter contradict my resume?

It should not — both are generated from the same base resume against the same job description, which is precisely the problem that writing them separately creates.

### Should I edit the generated cover letter?

On jobs you genuinely want, yes. The generated version solves structure and the blank page; the voice should be yours.
