Insurance job descriptions are dense with regulatory and licensing requirements that most other industries do not have to deal with. A [job description](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/job-description-keyword-extraction-guide) for an underwriter, a claims adjuster, and an insurance sales agent will each specify different licenses, software systems, and lines of business, and a resume that does not reflect these specifics precisely can be filtered out before a human ever reads it.

Insurance carriers and large agencies commonly use [ATS](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/ats-score-guide) software to manage high applicant volumes, particularly for claims and underwriting roles.

## Key Takeaways

- Insurance job descriptions require precise matching of licenses, lines of business, and systems to avoid being filtered out by ATS software.
- Key resume elements include specific licensing requirements, domain knowledge for different lines of business, and familiarity with designated software tools.
- For underwriting roles, highlight relevant licenses, exact lines of business, and quantify achievements like book size or loss ratios.
- Claims adjuster resumes should specify claims management systems used, track metrics like claim volume, and mirror licensing requirements.
- Sales resumes must emphasize production numbers, match product lines, and clearly state licensing status to align with job postings.

## Why Insurance Resumes Need Precise Matching

Three factors make insurance resume matching especially detail-sensitive:

1. **Licensing requirements** — Property & Casualty, Life & Health, or state-specific licenses are often hard requirements, not preferences
2. **Line of business specificity** — commercial, personal, life, health, and specialty lines each require different domain knowledge
3. **Systems and tools** — claims management and policy administration systems vary widely by carrier and are frequently named explicitly in postings

Missing any of these three in your resume, even with strong general insurance experience, can weaken your match significantly.

## Matching an Underwriting Resume

- Match the exact line of business named in the posting (commercial property, auto, life, specialty)
- Reflect your licensing status clearly and match it to the posting's stated requirement
- Name the underwriting or policy administration system you use, if specified in the posting
- Quantify book of business size, premium volume, or loss ratio improvements where possible

Read [how to quantify resume achievements](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-quantify-resume-achievements) for guidance on presenting underwriting results with real numbers.

---

## Matching a Claims Adjuster Resume

- Name the specific claims management system required (Guidewire, Duck Creek, Xactimate)
- Reflect claim volume handled, average cycle time, or settlement accuracy if you track these metrics
- Match the exact claim type language used in the posting (property, auto, workers' comp, liability)
- Mirror any licensing or certification requirements (adjuster license by state, AIC designation)

## Matching an Insurance Sales Resume

- Lead with production numbers: policies sold, premium volume, retention rate
- Match the exact product lines named in the posting (auto, home, commercial, life)
- Reflect any required licenses explicitly, since sales roles often require active state licensing
- Name the CRM or agency management system you use if the posting specifies one

If you are transitioning from a related financial services role into insurance, read [resume matching for career changers](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/resume-matching-with-job-description-complete-guide) for guidance on translating adjacent experience.

## Common Mistakes in Insurance Resume Matching

### Leaving Out License Status

If a posting requires an active P&C or Life & Health license and your resume does not clearly state your licensing status, you risk being filtered out even with relevant experience. State it explicitly and keep it current.

### Using Vague Line-of-Business Language

"Insurance experience" alone is too broad. Specify the exact line of business, commercial property, personal auto, group health, that matches the posting.

### Omitting Named Systems

Claims and underwriting postings frequently name specific platforms as requirements. Listing "claims software" generically instead of naming Guidewire or Duck Creek directly can cost you a keyword match.

### Formatting That Fails ATS Parsing

Review [ATS resume formatting mistakes](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/ats-resume-formatting-mistakes) and use a [clean, ATS-friendly template](/templates) for carrier and agency applications that go through high-volume screening.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid When Tailoring Your Resume

1. **Using a Generic Resume**: Avoid submitting the same resume for every job application. Tailoring your resume significantly increases your chances of being noticed.

2. **Ignoring Soft Skills**: While technical skills are crucial, don’t overlook soft skills like communication and teamwork, which are often emphasized in insurance roles.

3. **Overloading with Keywords**: While it's important to include keywords, avoid keyword stuffing. Ensure that your resume reads naturally.

4. **Neglecting Job Titles**: Ensure that your previous job titles accurately reflect the roles you've held. If your title was different from the job you're applying for, you can include a brief explanation in parentheses.

5. **Failing to Update Your Resume Regularly**: Regularly updating your resume with new experiences and skills ensures you’re always ready to apply when a suitable opportunity arises.

## Do's and Don'ts of Resume Matching in the Insurance Industry

### Do's
- **Do Highlight Relevant Certifications**: If you have certifications relevant to the insurance industry (like CPCU or AIC), make sure they are prominently displayed on your resume.
- **Do Use Action Verbs**: Start bullet points with strong action verbs (e.g., "Analyzed," "Developed," "Implemented") to convey your contributions effectively.
- **Do Tailor Your Cover Letter**: Complement your resume by tailoring your cover letter to match the job description as well. Visit our [cover letter resource](https://www.thetailorcv.com/cover-letter) for tips.

### Don'ts
- **Don’t Include Irrelevant Work Experience**: Focus on experiences and skills that directly relate to the insurance position you're applying for.
- **Don’t Use a One-Size-Fits-All Approach**: Every insurance job may require different qualities. Tailor your resume for each application.
- **Don’t Forget to Proofread**: Spelling and grammatical errors can lead to a negative impression. Always proofread before submitting your application.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do insurance carriers use ATS software to screen resumes?

Most large carriers and many agencies do, particularly for underwriting and claims roles that receive high application volumes. Matching your resume's licensing and systems language to the posting improves your odds of passing the initial screen.

### How important is stating my license status explicitly?

Very important. Licensing is often a hard requirement, and an ATS or recruiter scanning quickly may skip a resume that does not clearly confirm active licensing status.

### Should I list every insurance system I have ever used?

List the systems relevant to the posting, prioritizing exact matches to what is named as required. Irrelevant systems dilute your match signal.

### Can I use one resume for underwriting and claims roles?

Not effectively. The two roles require different skills, systems, and metrics. Maintain separate tailored versions, following [managing multiple resume versions](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/managing-multiple-resume-versions).
